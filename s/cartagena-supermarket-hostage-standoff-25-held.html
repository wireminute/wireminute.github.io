Gunmen Hold 25 Hostage in Cartagena Supermarket After Botched Robbery

Gunmen are holding 25 people hostage inside a supermarket in Cartagena, a Caribbean tourist city in Colombia, after a botched robbery of an armoured vehicle turned into a standoff on Tuesday.

About 30 shoppers and staff were seized when armed men fled into an Exito supermarket in the La Castellana area after police were called to an attempted robbery of an armoured truck at the shopping centre. Five elderly women were later freed, leaving 25 people inside, President Abelardo de la Espriella said.

Police snipers took positions on nearby rooftops and special units cordoned off the area as talks with the gunmen continued. Landings at the city's Rafael Nunez airport were briefly suspended, then resumed after about 45 minutes. Local media reported the gunmen were asking for nearly $2 million, two armoured vehicles and safe passage to Barranquilla, about 130 kilometres away.

The president promised the hostages would be freed and described the gunmen as common criminals. The standoff came hours after a car bombing in Villavicencio wounded 25 people; officials have reported no link between the two incidents.

Key points

  • Gunmen seized about 30 people in an Exito supermarket in Cartagena's La Castellana area after a failed attempt to rob an armoured truck; five elderly women were freed and 25 remain held.
