Colombian Forces Storm Cartagena Supermarket, Free All Hostages; One Gunman Killed

Colombian special forces stormed a supermarket in Cartagena early on Wednesday and freed all the remaining hostages after a siege of almost 20 hours, the president said, with one gunman killed and four others detained.

Army and police commandos entered the Exito store in the La Castellana area at about 1 a.m. local time, setting off stun grenades before moving in, about half an hour after a deadline to surrender had expired. About 20 people were still inside when the raid began. President Abelardo de la Espriella, who had travelled to the city to oversee the operation, said he authorised the assault himself after meeting military and police commanders, and called it “absolutely impeccable”.

“In an extraordinary joint operation between the National Police and the Armed Forces, all the hostages were rescued,” the president said in a video message. One of the gunmen was killed in the operation and four others were detained, according to a preliminary tally. At least one member of the security forces was reported wounded. No official account has yet been given of the condition of the freed hostages.

The standoff began at about 8 a.m. on Tuesday, when armed men tried to rob an armoured van collecting cash from the store. When police arrived, the men fled inside the supermarket and seized about 30 shoppers and workers. Ten people, including five elderly women, were released during the day as talks continued, while police snipers took positions on nearby rooftops and special units cordoned off the area. Landings at the city’s Rafael Nunez airport were briefly suspended, then resumed after about 45 minutes.

Local media reported the gunmen had demanded nearly $2 million, two armoured vehicles and safe passage to Barranquilla, about 130 kilometres away, though police had earlier said claims circulating online about the captors’ demands were false. The president described the gunmen as common criminals and ruled out any link to armed groups. The standoff came hours after a car bombing in Villavicencio wounded 25 people; officials have reported no link between the two incidents.

Key points

  • Special forces stormed the Exito supermarket in Cartagena at about 1 a.m. on Wednesday, freeing all remaining hostages and ending a siege of almost 20 hours.
  • One gunman was killed in the raid and four others were detained; at least one member of the security forces was reported wounded.
  • About 30 shoppers and workers were seized on Tuesday after gunmen fled into the store following a failed robbery of an armoured cash van; 10 were released during the day.
  • The president, who authorised the assault, called the operation “absolutely impeccable” and described the gunmen as common criminals.
