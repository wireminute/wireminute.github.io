Nigeria Funeral Boat Toll Rises to 29 as Search for Trapped Passengers Goes On

The death toll from a boat that capsized while carrying mourners to a funeral in southern Nigeria has risen to 29, the Bayelsa State governor said on Sunday, as rescuers kept working to recover the vessel, with some passengers feared still trapped inside.

Bayelsa State Governor Douye Diri said on Sunday that the death toll from the boat accident had risen to 29, with more than 51 survivors accounted for, as efforts continued to recover the capsized vessel. Diri disclosed the new figure during a condolence visit by Vice President Kashim Shettima to the state, and said the final casualty figure had yet to be established, with some passengers feared still trapped inside the boat.

Shettima announced that President Bola Tinubu had approved 250 million naira in support for the families of the victims. Speaking at the state government house in Yenagoa, the vice president said he had come on the president's directive to convey the federal government's condolences and obtain further details. He said Tinubu had also directed the federal ministry of marine and blue economy to put safety measures in place for people travelling through riverine communities, and that the governor had set up a commission of inquiry into the accident whose findings must be implemented.

Diri described the accident as avoidable, saying the boat was not fit for river travel and had operated from an unauthorised loading point. A survivor had earlier said the wooden passenger boat, known locally as a love boat, was overloaded with passengers and their belongings. The state government said it had taken responsibility for the medical bills and feeding of the survivors.

The boat was travelling from the community of Ayama to Ukubie for a burial ceremony when it overturned at about 11 a.m. local time on Friday, near the Oweikorogha and Otuan communities in the state's Southern Ijaw district, where two rivers meet. Police said the vessel developed a mechanical fault. The emergency agency had earlier put the confirmed toll at 24, with 24 people rescued, and officials have still not said how many people were on board, though residents told reporters there were at least 300.

The accident is the latest deadly boat mishap on Nigeria's waterways, where overcrowded vessels and poor maintenance are frequently blamed when boats go down. Safety officials have repeatedly warned operators against overloading, but enforcement on remote river routes remains weak, and families in river communities often have no other way to travel between settlements. The story is still developing.
