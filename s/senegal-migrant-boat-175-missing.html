About 175 Migrants Missing After Boat From Senegal Vanishes En Route to Europe

Around 175 people are unaccounted for after a migrant boat that sailed from Dakar about two weeks ago fell silent on its way to Europe, local officials and a rights group said.

The wooden fishing boat, known locally as a pirogue, sailed from Yoff, a neighbourhood of Dakar, around November 9-10 carrying about 175 people, Yoff mayor Seydina Issa Laye said. The rights group Action for Human Rights and Friendship put the number believed missing at 172 and urged a bigger search effort.

Families in Yoff said they have heard nothing from their relatives since the boat departed, and some have stopped eating and sleeping as they wait for news. Community leaders said many of those on board were young fishermen from Dakar and nearby coastal towns, and local people have begun organising their own search efforts after two weeks without word.

Senegal's navy said it picked up 974 migrants from five pirogues on Monday, in a sign of how many people are attempting the crossing. The boat that vanished was heading for Europe, with Spain's Canary Islands the usual destination for vessels leaving the Senegalese coast.

The Atlantic crossing from West Africa to the Canary Islands is one of the world's deadliest migration routes, and departures from Senegal have risen sharply this year. No wreckage has been reported and the fate of the 175 people on board remains unknown, with the search continuing.

Just last week, four bodies were recovered after another migrant boat capsized off Yenne, south of Dakar, with dozens still missing. That wreck came ten days after 43 people died when a boat went down off the north-western town of Thieppe, underlining how deadly the crossing has become as departures climb.

  • Families in Yoff have heard nothing for about two weeks; many of those aboard were young local fishermen
