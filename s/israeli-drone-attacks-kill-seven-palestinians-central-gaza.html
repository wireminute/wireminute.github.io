Seven Killed in Israeli Drone Strikes Across Central Gaza as Ceasefire Frays

At least seven Palestinians were killed and others wounded in Israeli drone strikes across central Gaza on Friday, medical sources said, as attacks continued despite a ceasefire that has formally been in force for more than a year.

Four of the dead were killed on Friday when a drone struck a group of civilians in the Bureij refugee camp in Deir el-Balah, according to medical sources. A fifth person was killed in a drone strike in the Nuseirat refugee camp, and two more died when a drone hit civilians near the entrance to Deir el-Balah city. Others were wounded and taken to hospitals in central Gaza.

Hospitals in central Gaza are operating at reduced capacity, short of medical supplies and staff, and the injured were brought in through the day. The constant buzzing of drones has left people afraid to leave their homes or move along roads, with residents describing fear and panic as the strikes continue alongside the sound of explosions from demolitions and fighter jets overhead.

Israeli forces also carried out demolition operations on Friday near areas under their control in the al-Shuja'iya neighbourhood of eastern Gaza City, using explosive-laden drones, and three large-scale demolitions near the ceasefire line east of Khan Younis in the south. By mid-June this year, about 82 percent of all structures in Gaza, more than 201,000 buildings, had been damaged or destroyed, according to United Nations satellite analysis.

The attacks came despite a ceasefire that has formally been in force since October 10, 2025. More than 1,600 Palestinians have been killed since it took effect, and more than 74,000 since the war began in October 2023, with 176,000 injured, according to Gaza's Health Ministry. Friday also marked World Children's Day, and Palestinian prisoner groups said about 350 Palestinian children are being held in Israeli prisons, where they face torture, starvation and denial of medical care, education and family visits.
