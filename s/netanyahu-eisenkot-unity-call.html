Eisenkot Says Netanyahu Cannot Stay 'a Minute Longer' Over Alleged Vote Meddling

Gadi Eisenkot, the man tasked with forming Israel's next government, has said caretaker prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu cannot stay in office for another minute, after a television investigation accused the prime minister's office of meddling in last month's election.

Eisenkot, leader of the Yashar party, made the charge on Sunday, hours after aides to both men sat down to discuss a national unity government. He said the conduct of Netanyahu and his inner circle directly threatened Israeli democracy, warning that as long as Netanyahu remained in office, Israel would no longer hold free and fair elections. Netanyahu had urged Eisenkot to join a unity government the day before, saying only a broad coalition could face the threat from Iran; Eisenkot had called that appeal political spin.

The row follows a Channel 12 investigation, broadcast on Sunday, which reported that Netanyahu's chief of staff, Ido Norden, telephoned the police commissioner's office the day before the October 27 election to complain about three members of Ra'am, the Arab-led United Arab List. Norden is said to have directed officers to a supposed intelligence source said to be a former employee of the prime minister's office.

About 14 officers raided the homes of the three men, arrested them without a warrant on suspicion of paying voters to support Ra'am, the report said. The three were later released after police found the information from the source was unfounded. Netanyahu then made unproven accusations of electoral fraud on polling day, after the Arab-led parties won a record 19 seats.

The same investigation reported that David Zini, head of the Shin Bet internal security agency, had asked the attorney general's office for permission to tap the phones of five Knesset members from Palestinian-Israeli parties on election day, over suspected interference. The request was turned down.

Eisenkot demanded an immediate investigation and promised an inquiry commission to examine the prime minister's office and the security agencies before and during the vote. His bloc has called on the attorney general to open a criminal investigation, and opposition leader Yair Lapid likewise accused Likud of trying to steal the election from inside the prime minister's office.

Netanyahu's office called the report false, saying Norden had passed on information about suspected criminal activity and had done his civic duty. Likud accused Eisenkot of outrageous incitement. The row has deepened the deadlock: Netanyahu's bloc has 53 of the Knesset's 120 seats and Eisenkot's has 48, with Arab parties holding the remaining 19, leaving both short of the 61 seats required for a majority in the 120-seat parliament. Herzog asked Eisenkot to form a government on November 8, giving him 28 days.

Key points

  • Eisenkot says Netanyahu cannot stay in office 'a minute longer', calling his conduct a direct threat to Israeli democracy after a TV report alleged election meddling by the prime minister's office.
  • Channel 12 reported that Netanyahu's chief of staff sent police to raid the homes of three Ra'am members on election eve on unfounded information; the three were arrested, then released.
  • The report also said the Shin Bet chief sought permission to wiretap five Arab-Israeli lawmakers on election day; the request was rejected.
  • Eisenkot has demanded an investigation and promised a commission of inquiry; Netanyahu's office calls the report false. Neither bloc holds the 61 seats needed to govern.
