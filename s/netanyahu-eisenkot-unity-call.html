Eisenkot Bloc Rules Out Unity Government With Netanyahu, Calling Vote Meddling a 'Coup Attempt'

The opposition bloc led by Gadi Eisenkot has ruled out forming a unity government with Benjamin Netanyahu, after the public broadcaster KAN reported that the alleged election interference by the prime minister's office amounted to an attempted coup.

Eisenkot, leader of the Yashar party, made the charge on Sunday, hours after aides to both men met He said the conduct of Netanyahu and his inner circle directly threatened Israeli democracy, warning that Israel would no longer hold free and fair elections while Netanyahu remained in office. Netanyahu had urged Eisenkot to join a unity government the day before.

On Monday, the bloc hardened its position. Sources in Eisenkot's camp told the public broadcaster KAN that the reports of interference had closed the door to any unity cabinet with Netanyahu's Likud party, describing the episode as a coup attempt. The sources called for both intelligence chiefs, David Zini of the Shin Bet and Roman Gofman of the Mossad, to be removed.

The row follows a Channel 12 investigation, which reported that Netanyahu's chief of staff, Ido Norden, telephoned the police commissioner's office the day before the October 27 election about three members of Ra'am, the Arab-led United Arab List. Norden is said to have pointed officers to a supposed intelligence source said to be a former employee of the prime minister's office.

About 14 officers raided the homes of the three men and arrested them without a warrant on suspicion of paying voters to support Ra'am. The three were later released after police found the information unfounded. Netanyahu then made unproven accusations of electoral fraud on polling day, after the Arab-led parties won a record 19 seats.

The same investigation reported that David Zini, head of the Shin Bet internal security agency, had asked the attorney general's office for permission to tap the phones of five Knesset members from Palestinian-Israeli parties on election day, over suspected interference. The request was turned down.

Eisenkot demanded an immediate investigation and promised a commission of inquiry into the prime minister's office and the security agencies before and during the vote. His bloc has called on the attorney general to open a criminal investigation, and opposition leader Yair Lapid accused Likud of trying to steal the election from inside the prime minister's office.

Netanyahu's office called the report false, saying Norden had passed on information about suspected criminal activity and had done his civic duty. Likud accused Eisenkot of outrageous incitement. The deadlock stands: Netanyahu's bloc has 53 of the Knesset's 120 seats and Eisenkot's has 48, with Arab parties holding the remaining 19, leaving both short of the 61 needed.

Key points

  • Eisenkot's bloc has ruled out a unity government with Netanyahu, calling the alleged election interference a coup attempt and seeking the removal of both intelligence chiefs.
  • Channel 12 reported that Netanyahu's chief of staff sent police to raid the homes of three Ra'am members on election eve on unfounded information; the three were arrested, then released.
  • The report also said the Shin Bet chief sought permission to wiretap five Arab-Israeli lawmakers on election day; the request was rejected.
  • Eisenkot has demanded an investigation and promised a commission of inquiry; Netanyahu's office calls the report false. Neither bloc holds the 61 seats needed to govern.
