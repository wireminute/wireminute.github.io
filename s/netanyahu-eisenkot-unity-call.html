Netanyahu Presses Rival Eisenkot for Unity Government as Iran Fears Dominate Coalition Talks

Israel's caretaker prime minister has publicly urged his chief rival to join a national unity government, arguing that only a broad coalition can face the threat from Iran — but the man he is courting dismissed the appeal as a political trick and said talks were already planned.

Benjamin Netanyahu made the call in a video statement on Saturday evening, days after Israel's October 27 election left no clear path to a government. He said his meeting with Gadi Eisenkot, the leader of the Yashar party, on Thursday had only deepened his belief that a unity cabinet was essential. He urged Eisenkot to sit down without conditions and settle their differences, including the long-running dispute over military service for ultra-Orthodox men.

Netanyahu framed the appeal around Iran. He said Tehran was working to restart its nuclear programme and would use atomic weapons against Israel if it managed to resume uranium enrichment and speed up weapons work. A narrow coalition, or one that depended on Arab parties, would be too weak for the task, he said, while a unity government bringing together parties that back Israel as a Jewish state could deal with the danger for good. He said Israel had to keep acting to stop Iran reaching that point.

Eisenkot answered within hours, rejecting the offer. He called the appeal political spin, noting that negotiating teams from Yashar and Likud were already due to meet on Sunday. He accused the caretaker premier of claiming to want unity while sowing division, and said Netanyahu knew he had lost the election and that a new government would indeed be formed.

Eisenkot was asked by President Isaac Herzog to form the next government and has 28 days to do it, with a possible two-week extension. He has said he is working on a national emergency government and wants a break with far-right figures, including minister Itamar Ben Gvir. His bloc also plans to vote this week to replace the parliament's speaker with one of its own. Within Netanyahu's Likud, pressure for unity is growing: one lawmaker said he would pitch a protest tent outside parliament until a unity government is formed, and the finance minister backed Netanyahu's call.

Key points

  • Netanyahu urged Eisenkot on Saturday to enter unity government talks without preconditions, saying only a broad coalition can guarantee Israel's security.
  • He warned Iran is rebuilding its nuclear programme and said a narrow government, or one backed by Arab parties, could not meet the challenge.
  • Eisenkot dismissed the appeal as political spin, saying Yashar and Likud negotiating teams are already set to meet on Sunday.
  • Eisenkot, tasked with forming a government after the October 27 election, says he is building a national emergency government and has 28 days to do it.
