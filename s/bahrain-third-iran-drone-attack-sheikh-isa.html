US Denies Iranian Claim of Radar Hit in Bahrain as Qatar Meets Tehran to Push Mediation

The United States says Iranian drones aimed at an American radar site in Bahrain were all intercepted, denying Tehran’s claim of a hit, as Qatar opened a new round of talks with Iranian officials to try to stop the fighting.

Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said their drones carried out a precise operation at dawn on Tuesday against a radar system used by American forces at the Sheikh Isa air base. The Guards said the strike answered what they called continued violations of Iranian airspace by American drones, and claimed their electronic warfare units had intercepted and seized American drones. It was the third time in five days that Iran has claimed a strike on an American radar site in the kingdom, after attacks on Friday and Sunday that Bahrain also said it stopped.

Bahrain’s state television said the attack had been thwarted, and the defence force said its air defences intercepted several incoming attacks. Earlier Bahrain warned of an unspecified threat and told residents to move to the nearest safe place, and explosions were heard in the capital, Manama. No casualties or damage have been confirmed inside Bahrain.

American Central Command said multiple one-way attack drones launched by the Guards towards Bahrain were intercepted before they could cause any damage. It said there was no damage to American equipment and no injury to American personnel, and it called the claim that the radar had been hit false. The United Arab Emirates condemned the renewed attack, and Qatar called for restraint from all sides.

Qatar’s foreign ministry said its officials would meet Iranian counterparts on Tuesday as part of mediation efforts, aiming to narrow the gap between the positions on the table and urging Washington and Tehran to avoid any escalation. Iran’s foreign minister was in Oman on Tuesday for talks with his Omani counterpart on safeguarding shipping lanes. The Guards’ chief warned that any fresh attack on Iran would draw a devastating response and claimed Iran had dealt a heavy blow to American and Israeli plans. Bahrain is home to the headquarters of the American navy’s Fifth Fleet, and the fighting between the United States, Israel and Iran, which began in late February, has now run for nearly nine months.

Key points

  • The United States says multiple Iranian drones aimed at Bahrain were intercepted, with no damage to American equipment and no injury to American personnel.
  • Iran’s Revolutionary Guards claimed a precise dawn strike on a radar system at the Sheikh Isa air base, the third such claim in five days.
  • Qatar said its officials would meet Iranian counterparts on Tuesday to advance mediation and urged both sides to avoid escalation.
  • Bahrain said its air defences stopped the attack, and explosions were heard in the capital, Manama, with no casualties or damage confirmed.
