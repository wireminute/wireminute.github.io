Iran Threatens Civilian Sites Near US Bases After Bahrain Radar Claim the US Denies

Iran's parliament speaker and lead negotiator has warned that dams, ports and power plants near American or Israeli bases are now fair game, hours after the Revolutionary Guards claimed a drone strike on a US radar site in Bahrain that Washington says never landed.

Iran's parliament speaker and lead negotiator with the United States, Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, said on Tuesday that any dam, port or power plant near an American or Israeli base should now be treated as dual-use, and therefore a legitimate target. "New rule: Any dam, port, or power plant near a US or Israeli base is dual-use," he wrote online, adding: "We'll apply your logic consistently and even-handedly." He was responding to a report that some of Iran's dams may have been built partly to support underground military facilities. The report did not say Washington or Tel Aviv was considering strikes on Iranian dams, but the American president has threatened civilian sites, including power plants, since the war began in late February.

Hours after Ghalibaf's post, the Revolutionary Guards said their drones carried out a precise operation at dawn on Tuesday against a radar system used by American forces at the Sheikh Isa air base, answering what they called continued violations of Iranian airspace by American drones. It was the third time in five days that Iran has claimed a strike on an American radar site in the kingdom, after attacks on Friday and Sunday that Bahrain also said it stopped.

Bahrain's state television said the attack had been thwarted, and the defence force said its air defences intercepted several incoming attacks. Bahrain had earlier warned residents to move to the nearest safe place, and explosions were heard in the capital, Manama. No casualties or damage have been confirmed inside Bahrain. American Central Command said multiple one-way attack drones launched by the Guards were intercepted before they could cause any damage, with no damage to American equipment and no injury to American personnel, and it called the claim that the radar had been hit false. The United Arab Emirates condemned the renewed attack, and Qatar called for restraint.

Qatar's foreign ministry said its officials would meet Iranian counterparts on Tuesday, aiming to narrow the gap and urging Washington and Tehran to avoid any escalation. Iran's foreign minister was in Oman on Tuesday for talks on safeguarding shipping lanes. The Guards' chief warned that any fresh attack on Iran would draw a devastating response. Bahrain is home to the headquarters of the American navy's Fifth Fleet, and the fighting between the United States, Israel and Iran, which began in late February, has now run for nearly nine months.

Key points

  • Iran's parliament speaker and lead negotiator said dams, ports and power plants near US or Israeli bases are now "dual-use" and threatened tit-for-tat strikes on civilian infrastructure.
  • The Revolutionary Guards claimed a precise dawn strike on a radar system at the Sheikh Isa air base, the third such claim in five days.
  • The United States says multiple Iranian drones aimed at Bahrain were intercepted, with no damage to American equipment and no injury to American personnel.
  • Qatar said its officials would meet Iranian counterparts on Tuesday to advance mediation and urged both sides to avoid escalation.
