Guadalajara Cartel Founder Miguel Angel Felix Gallardo Dies at 80

Miguel Angel Felix Gallardo, the founder of the Guadalajara cartel and one of the most powerful drug traffickers in Mexican history, has died in hospital at the age of 80, nearly four decades after his arrest ended his reign over the cocaine trade.

Felix Gallardo was moved from the Puente Grande prison complex to a private hospital in Guadalajara on Friday after his health deteriorated, and died of multiple organ failure at about 2pm local time on Sunday, security officials in the state of Jalisco said. He had been in custody since his arrest in 1989, and his lawyers had pressed for years for him to serve the rest of his sentence at home because of his failing health.

Born in 1946 in Culiacan, in the north-western state of Sinaloa, Felix Gallardo worked as a police officer and a bodyguard for a local governor before turning to trafficking. In the late 1970s he helped found the Guadalajara cartel, uniting Mexico's rival traffickers into a single federation that became the first to move cocaine into the United States on a massive scale. He built an alliance with Colombian groups, including the Medellin cartel, and at the height of the cocaine boom in the 1980s he was known as the Boss of Bosses and the Godfather of the trade.

His fall came after the 1985 kidnapping and murder of Enrique Camarena, an agent of the United States Drug Enforcement Administration who had infiltrated the cartel, and of Mexican pilot Alfredo Zavala. Felix Gallardo was arrested in April 1989 and sentenced to 40 years in prison on drug-trafficking and weapons charges. In 2017 a court imposed a further 37-year sentence over the Camarena killing, a crime he always denied ordering. His arrest broke up the organisation he had built.

In his final years his health had collapsed. By the time he gave a rare interview from prison in 2021 he was blind in one eye, practically deaf and unable to walk without help. He spent 37 years in custody and died still a prisoner, the last of the cartel's founders to pass from the scene.

  • He built the first great federation of Mexican trafficking and moved cocaine into the United States on a massive scale in the 1980s.
