Hundreds of Men March in Bern to Demand Men Do More Against Violence Towards Women

Hundreds of men marched through the Swiss capital, Bern, on Saturday to denounce misogyny and violence against women, urging other men to take a bigger and more vocal role in confronting both.

The rally outside parliament on Bundesplatz, held under the banner "Men Against Violence Against Women", followed growing concern that misogyny is too widespread online among teenage boys and young men, and that other men need to build a stronger counter-narrative. It was co-organised by Christoph Gosteli, a 42-year-old social worker, together with the men's group maenner.ch.

The march came after a survey released in June by the University of Zurich and MenCare, an umbrella group of Swiss men's and fathers' organisations, found that half of men aged 18 to 24 held a restrictive and dominance-based view of masculinity. Many of those surveyed said they felt real men were increasingly marginalised in society.

Justice Minister Beat Jans attended a conference earlier this month on the dangers of violent abuse of women online. In a speech on 2 November, he cited figures showing that four-fifths of violent crimes in the country are committed by men, a share that rises to 99 percent in rape cases. Organisers also pointed to figures showing that more than half of women in Switzerland have experienced sexual harassment, and one in five has faced unwanted sexual acts.

Organisers said the aim was not only to condemn violence, but to push men to speak out against it in everyday life, at home, at work and online, rather than leaving the issue for women to raise alone.
