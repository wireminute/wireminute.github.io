Senior Erdogan Ally Quits Ruling Party Post as Fund Probe Reaches His Son

Hayati Yazici, a founder of Turkey's ruling AK Party and a former minister, has resigned from the party leadership after a criminal probe into alleged stock-market manipulation widened to include his son.

Yazici, who served as a minister from 2011 to 2014 and was President Tayyip Erdogan's personal lawyer before that, announced the decision on Tuesday. His son Mustafa was recently questioned by the authorities over suspected manipulative trades on the Borsa Istanbul stock market. The authorities have imposed a travel ban on the son and appointed trustees to run companies linked to him. Yazici said neither he nor his son had done anything wrong or profited from the trading, and said he was stepping down because his position had been turned into an instrument in a social media campaign against him and the party.

The case sits inside a wider crisis that began in September, when investment funds that had concentrated their holdings in thinly traded stocks could no longer meet requests from investors to withdraw their money. Regulators then ordered 131 funds into liquidation and opened a criminal investigation that has reached senior officials and members of the government. More than 455,000 small investors have been affected. A former minister and senior party figure, Fatma Betul Sayan Kaya, was detained this month over suspicious share trades made just before the crisis broke, and was freed to house arrest this week after 18 days in jail. A former central bank governor, Sahap Kavcioglu, resigned this month and is under investigation, and more than 130 people remain in jail, including senior figures from the ruling party and the financial bureaucracy, the chief executive of the stock exchange and a former head of the securities custodian.

The scandal has become a political liability for the ruling party ahead of elections scheduled for 2028, with pollsters saying it has eaten into support for the party as the government tries to contain the fallout and get investors their money back. Finance Minister Mehmet Simsek has dismissed rumours that he plans to resign as baseless. The investigation is continuing, and further arrests and resignations have not been ruled out.

Key points

  • Hayati Yazici, an AK Party founder and former minister, resigned from the party leadership as a market-manipulation probe widened to include his son.
  • His son Mustafa was questioned over suspected manipulative trades on the Borsa Istanbul; he faces a travel ban and trustees now run his companies.
  • The probe follows the collapse of 131 investment funds, leaving more than 455,000 investors unable to withdraw their money.
  • More than 130 people remain jailed in the investigation, including senior party and financial officials; a former minister was freed to house arrest this week.
