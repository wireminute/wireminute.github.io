Kirkland & Ellis Gave $11.8m in Free Legal Work to Trump Administration, Democrats Say

The law firm Kirkland & Ellis has provided at least $11.8 million in free legal services to federal agencies since striking a deal with President Donald Trump's administration, three senior Democrats said on Thursday, calling the arrangement evidence of a corrupt bargain.

The largest shares went to the Commerce Department, which received about $4 million in work, and the Defense Department, which received about $2.2 million. The firm also did about $1.6 million of work for the Postal Service, $2 million for the US International Development Finance Corporation and $1.8 million for an investment vehicle negotiated between the Trump administration and Japan to create a $550 billion fund.

The figures come from a letter the firm sent to the lawmakers on November 5. In it, Kirkland said it had billed more than $18.7 million in pro bono work connected to the deal, amounting to over 15,000 hours of lawyers' time. President Trump said in April 2025 that the firm had pledged $125 million in free legal work to causes he supported.

Kirkland was one of ten major firms that agreed to provide almost $1 billion in free legal services after the White House issued executive orders early in Mr Trump's second term targeting firms over their legal work, their diversity policies and their political ties. The firm said it began negotiating after rival firm Skadden reached its own agreement in March 2025, following a federal inquiry into Kirkland's employment practices.

The three Democrats behind the disclosure, Representative Jamie Raskin and Senators Richard Blumenthal and Adam Schiff, said it showed the deal went much further than first understood and described it as unprecedented official pressure on major law firms. They have now demanded records from the nine other firms that struck similar deals and vowed to keep investigating in the next Congress.

The lawmakers also said the administration had unsuccessfully tried to involve Kirkland in work on renaming the Kennedy Center and building a triumphal arch, and that the firm declined both. Kirkland is represented by the Washington litigator Beth Wilkinson.
