Thousands March Across Europe as Cost-of-Living Protests Hit Sixth Weekend

Thousands of people marched in France and Portugal on Saturday as protests over the rising cost of living, unaffordable housing and cuts to public services entered a sixth consecutive weekend, a day after young people staged a coordinated day of action across more than 25 countries.

In France, the Interior Ministry said 82,500 people joined demonstrations nationwide, including 21,500 in Paris, down from 106,000 the previous weekend. Organisers put the nationwide figure higher, at 150,000, including 45,000 in the capital. The marches were largely peaceful, and about 70,000 security officers were deployed across the country. In Paris, crowds gathered at the Place de la Republique before marching through the city, while demonstrations also took place in cities from Lille in the north to Marseille in the south and Lyon in the south-east.

The French protests started in September, when students marched for better schools, and have since grown into a broader movement over living costs, public services and police violence, drawing in trade unions and the Yellow Vests. Saturday was the sixth weekend in a row of demonstrations. Turnout has fallen from the peak on October 17, when the ministry said about 565,000 people protested, but organisers say the movement continues, and demonstrators have also objected to new rules giving officers who fire their weapons stronger legal protection.

In Portugal, thousands marched through Lisbon in heavy rain, calling for higher wages, affordable housing and relief from rising prices. The demonstration was organised by the CGTP, Portugal’s biggest trade union federation, which also demanded higher pensions and cheaper essential goods. Portugal is facing one of Europe’s worst housing affordability crises, with home prices now more than twice their 2017 level and rents on new leases almost doubled, while average earnings in 2025 ran 37 percent below the EU average.

The weekend’s marches followed Friday’s youth-led day of action, when tens of thousands of young people demonstrated in more than 25 countries over living costs, school funding and military spending. Italy saw the biggest turnout, where a 24-hour general strike called by the CGIL union brought marches to more than 100 cities, with the union’s leader claiming more than one million people took part. About 4,000 people gathered in Berlin, hundreds rallied in London, and a youth campaign in Germany announced demonstrations in 73 locations. Organisers say the protests reflect shared worries about living standards, schooling and future prospects, even though their demands differ from country to country.

Key points

  • France’s Interior Ministry counted 82,500 protesters nationwide, including 21,500 in Paris, while organisers claimed 150,000; turnout was down from 106,000 a week earlier.
  • Thousands marched through Lisbon in heavy rain as Portugal’s largest union federation demanded higher wages, pensions and cheaper essentials.
  • The protests are in a sixth consecutive weekend, growing from September student demonstrations into a wider movement with unions and Yellow Vests.
  • Friday’s youth day of action spanned more than 25 countries, with Italy’s general strike bringing marches to over 100 cities.
