Young People March Across Europe in Day of Action Over Living Costs and War Spending

Tens of thousands of young people marched across Europe on Friday in a coordinated day of action spanning more than 25 countries, demanding relief from the rising cost of living, more funding for schools and universities, and an end to higher military spending.

Tens of thousands of young people marched across Europe on Friday in a coordinated day of action spanning more than 25 countries, demanding relief from the rising cost of living, more funding for schools and universities, and an end to higher military spending. The marches were largely peaceful, drawing crowds of hundreds or thousands in most cities, with the biggest turnout in Italy, where a nationwide general strike brought demonstrations to more than 100 cities.

In Italy, the CGIL, the country's largest trade union, called a 24-hour general strike covering the private and public sectors, including schools and healthcare. Its leader, Maurizio Landini, told a rally in Rome that more than one million people had taken part across the country, a figure that could not be independently verified. In Rome, students chanted "Money for schools, not war" as they marched through the city centre.

About 4,000 people gathered at Alexanderplatz in Berlin, and thousands more marched in Cologne, where protesters voiced concern about Germany's rapid rearmament and fears of a return to compulsory military service. Hundreds rallied at Parliament Square in London, where organisers said students from more than 350 schools, colleges and universities walked out. Many marchers waved Palestinian flags and carried signs calling for less spending on the military.

The action was organised by a coalition of more than 50 youth groups and was inspired by student protests in France that began in mid-September over school funding and living costs. Unions and housing activists put forward six demands, including a guaranteed right to housing, higher taxes on the rich and an end to rearmament. Organisers say further rallies are planned over the weekend, and a separate rail strike in Italy is due to run from Friday night into Saturday evening.

Key points

  • Tens of thousands of young people marched in more than 25 countries in a coordinated day of action over living costs, school funding and military spending.
  • Italy saw the biggest turnout, with a 24-hour general strike and marches in more than 100 cities; the CGIL union claimed over one million participants.
  • About 4,000 rallied in Berlin and hundreds in London, where students from more than 350 schools, colleges and universities walked out.
  • Organisers say more rallies are planned over the weekend, with an Italian rail strike due to run from Friday night into Saturday evening.
