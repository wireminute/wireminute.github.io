Aoun Demands Israeli Withdrawal and an End to Iranian Meddling in Lebanon

Lebanon's President Joseph Aoun said on Saturday that his country cannot fully celebrate its independence while Israeli forces hold Lebanese land and foreign powers interfere in its affairs, in a speech that pressed both Israel to withdraw and Hezbollah to give up its weapons.

Speaking on the eve of Lebanon's 83rd independence anniversary, Aoun said the country faces two fundamental obstacles that are inseparably linked: the Israeli occupation of parts of its land, and foreign interference from influential parties in the Iranian regime through weapons and decisions outside the authority of the Lebanese state. He said Lebanon cannot celebrate independence as if its sovereignty were complete while parts of its territory remain occupied, displaced people have not returned to their homes, and the decision of war and peace does not rest exclusively with the state.

Addressing Israel, Aoun demanded a complete withdrawal, an end to attacks and the return of Lebanese prisoners. "Withdraw from every inch of Lebanese territory. Stop your attacks. And return our captives," he said. He argued that occupation does not create security and destruction does not create peace, and that withdrawal is the only sure path to stability along Lebanon's internationally recognised southern border. On Saturday, Israeli strikes hit the districts of Nabatieh, Bint Jbeil and Marjayoun in southern Lebanon.

Turning to Iran, Aoun declared that the era of weapons outside the authority of the state is over. He said there would be no weapons outside the state that take orders from, or serve the interests of, foreign powers, and told Hezbollah it must hand over its arms. He acknowledged that armed resistance had played a role in an earlier period, but said it later inflicted heavy losses on Lebanon and its people once it became devoted to causes that were not Lebanese. He promised there would be no persecution, no revenge and no retribution when the weapons are handed over.

Lebanon's government has committed to disarming Hezbollah, whose arsenal has long outstripped that of the national army. A framework agreement signed in June in Washington requires Israel to withdraw its troops and Hezbollah to disarm, with the Lebanese army deploying in the south, but progress has stalled while Israel continues its strikes. Israel still holds around five percent of Lebanese territory. Aoun also spoke of the hardship at home, with citizens unable to reach their bank deposits and young people seeking their futures abroad, and said his government had embarked on a national rescue plan that tackles Israel and Iran in parallel. He said diplomacy is not surrender, negotiations do not mean abandoning national rights, and peace is compatible with dignity.

Key points

  • President Joseph Aoun said Lebanon faces two linked obstacles to full independence: Israeli occupation and Iranian interference.
  • He told Israel to withdraw from every inch of Lebanese territory, stop its attacks and return Lebanese prisoners.
  • He declared the era of weapons outside state control over and told Hezbollah to hand over its arms, promising no revenge.
  • A June agreement requires Israeli withdrawal and Hezbollah disarmament, but progress has stalled while strikes continue.
