Waymo Pulls Driverless Taxis Off Freeways Across the US After California Calls Them a Safety Hazard

Waymo has pulled its driverless taxis off freeways across the United States after California's motor vehicles department barred the cars from state highways, saying they pose an imminent hazard to public safety.

California's Department of Motor Vehicles said on Tuesday that Waymo's driverless freeway operations posed an imminent hazard to public safety, and barred the cars from the state's freeways with effect from November 11. The department cited recurring incursions by the vehicles into active construction zones where workers, vehicles and equipment were present, despite an earlier restriction and corrective action after similar incidents in May. It pointed to an incident on November 5 in which three Waymo cars entered an active construction closure on Route 101 near Burlingame, and a separate incident in which a car tried to pass a California Highway Patrol vehicle that was slowing traffic on Highway 280 in San Francisco.

Waymo said it had temporarily restricted freeway rides in all of its markets where the service runs, to evaluate its performance. The pause covers the San Francisco Bay Area and Los Angeles County in California, as well as Phoenix, Miami and Austin, the other cities where it offered freeway rides. The company said it would keep running its robotaxis on surface streets and continue freeway testing with trained specialists behind the wheel, while it works with regulators to resume the service.

The restriction will stay in place until Waymo can show it has taken effective corrective action, the department said. Waymo began running robotaxis on California freeways in November 2025 and expanded the service across Sacramento, the Bay Area and San Diego in August 2026. It had already paused freeway service voluntarily in May, after earlier examples of its vehicles entering freeway construction sites, before resuming it.

Waymo, owned by Alphabet, operates in 15 American cities but offered freeway rides in only a few of them, including San Francisco and Los Angeles. The company said the recent construction-zone encounters involved no injuries or collisions. The ban is the sharpest regulatory rebuke yet for Waymo, and it lands as rival operators and regulators watch closely how quickly the company can satisfy the state's safety demands.

Key points

  • California's motor vehicles department barred Waymo's driverless cars from state freeways from November 11, calling them an imminent hazard to public safety.
  • Waymo has temporarily stopped freeway rides in every American market where it offered them, including Phoenix, Miami and Austin, while street rides continue.
  • Regulators cited three Waymo cars entering an active construction zone on Route 101 and one trying to pass a highway patrol car slowing traffic.
  • The ban stays until Waymo shows it has taken effective corrective action; the company began freeway rides in California in November 2025.
