Man Convicted of Starting Deadly Palisades Fire That Killed 12

A federal jury in Los Angeles has convicted Jonathan Rinderknecht, 30, of starting the Palisades Fire, the January 2025 blaze that killed 12 people and destroyed thousands of homes.

A federal jury in Los Angeles has convicted Jonathan Rinderknecht, 30, of starting the Palisades Fire, the January 2025 blaze that killed 12 people and destroyed thousands of homes. Jurors found him guilty on Friday of two federal counts, destruction of property by means of fire and arson affecting property used in interstate commerce, after a retrial that followed a hung jury in his first case.

Prosecutors said Rinderknecht deliberately lit a small fire with a lighter in the early hours of 1 January 2025. That blaze, known as the Lachman Fire, burned unnoticed deep in root systems for days before powerful Santa Ana winds drove it back to life on 7 January, when it grew into the Palisades Fire and tore through Pacific Palisades, Topanga and Malibu. The fire scorched 23,448 acres, destroyed about 6,800 structures and forced about 30,000 people to flee. It caused more than $200 billion in economic losses, according to state fire officials.

The verdict came after a 14-day retrial that began on 2 November. Rinderknecht's first trial, in June, ended in a mistrial after jurors deadlocked 10-2 in favour of acquittal. He declined to testify in his own defence in either trial and has remained in custody since his arrest in Florida in October 2025. He is due to be sentenced on 17 March 2027; each count carries a minimum of five years and a maximum of 20 years in federal prison.

"Twelve people lost their lives and thousands lost their homes because of Jonathan Rinderknecht's malicious conduct," Attorney General Todd Blanche said after the verdict. The Palisades Fire was one of the most destructive wildfires in Los Angeles history, and the conviction closes a case that began with a fire lit on New Year's Day and ended, a week later, in neighbourhoods reduced to ash.
