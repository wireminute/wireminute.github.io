Pregnant US Citizen Pepper-Sprayed and Pinned to Ground by ICE Agents in California

A pregnant American woman was pepper-sprayed and forced face down onto the pavement by immigration agents outside a county jail in California, after telling them she was pregnant, video of the encounter shows. She was held for several hours and released without charge.

Lizbeth Vargas Andrade, 23, from Ceres, who is 12 weeks pregnant, was among local residents watching immigration agents outside the Stanislaus County Jail in Modesto on Sunday. She was filming on her phone as the agents moved to detain a man who had just been released from the jail. Footage recorded by her husband, Adolfo Talavera, shows a chaotic scene in the parking lot.

Vargas Andrade said an agent backed into her as the arrest unfolded. She said she told the agent she was pregnant and asked him not to touch her. The video then shows another agent pointing a pepper-spray canister in her direction, and two agents taking her to the ground, where she was handcuffed with her stomach against the pavement before being rolled onto her side. She told reporters she could not breathe as the spray burned her nose and eyes.

She was taken to an immigration field office in Stockton and held for several hours. Her lawyer, Patrick Kolasinski, said a hospital check found the baby unharmed, and she was freed on Sunday evening without any charge being filed. She was not fingerprinted. She said officers had accused her of assaulting an agent, an accusation she denies.

On Tuesday, the immigration agency gave its account. It said the agents had been detaining Fabian Santiago Martinez, a Mexican national who, it said, faced several drink-driving charges and had agreed to go back to Mexico of his own accord. It said Vargas Andrade had interfered with the arrest and obstructed officers, which it called a federal crime, and said she received medical care at once and was taken to hospital for checks. The incident comes amid a rise in immigration enforcement in California's Central Valley, a farming region where residents have begun tracking agents' movements.
