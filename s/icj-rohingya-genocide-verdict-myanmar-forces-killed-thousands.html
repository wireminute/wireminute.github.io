Judges Say Myanmar Troops Killed Thousands of Rohingya as Landmark Ruling Is Read Out

Judges at the United Nations' highest court have found that Myanmar's security forces killed thousands of Rohingya civilians and carried out widespread sexual violence during the 2017 crackdown, as the court reads out its judgment in the genocide case against Myanmar.

The court said the forces also inflicted extreme violence on children and attacked Rohingya families as they fled in large numbers toward Bangladesh. The reading of the judgment was still under way when the findings were reported, and the court's final answer on whether Myanmar committed genocide had not yet been delivered.

The Gambia brought the case in November 2019 under the 1948 Genocide Convention, with the backing of the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation. More than 740,000 Rohingya fled to Bangladesh during the 2017 crackdown, and about 1.2 million now live there as refugees. Myanmar denies the genocide charge, saying its forces were responding to armed attacks. The judgment is final and cannot be appealed, although the court has no means of enforcing it.

Key points

  • The World Court found Myanmar's security forces killed thousands of Rohingya civilians and committed widespread sexual violence in the 2017 crackdown.
  • The reading of the judgment was still under way, with the court's final finding on whether Myanmar committed genocide yet to be delivered.
  • The Gambia filed the case in 2019 under the Genocide Convention; more than 740,000 Rohingya fled to Bangladesh, where about 1.2 million now live as refugees.
