Student Deaths at India's IITs Expose Gaps in Campus Mental Health Support

A student who won a place at one of India's elite technology institutes died months later in a suspected suicide, a case that has revived hard questions about how the country's most celebrated campuses look after their brightest students.

Rishikesh Kumar had fulfilled his dream of studying at an Indian Institute of Technology, one of the country's premier tech institutions. Months later, he died in a suspected suicide. His story, examined in a report published on Saturday, has put a fresh spotlight on the pressures inside the IITs and the support available to students who struggle there.

Official figures show the scale of the problem. Forty-five students died by suicide across 23 IITs between the 2020-21 and 2024-25 academic years, according to data reported in September 2026. Sixteen of those deaths came in 2023-24, against six the year before, followed by 13 in 2024-25. Twenty-eight of the 45 students belonged to Scheduled Caste, Scheduled Tribe and Other Backward Classes communities. IIT Hyderabad recorded seven deaths, while Kharagpur, Delhi and Kanpur recorded six each. Responses to right-to-information requests show IIT Kharagpur alone reported 10 student deaths by suicide between 2022 and October 2026, and IIT Bombay reported three in 2026, including second-year student Sahil Wakode in September, whose death was followed by protests on campus.

The support on offer falls far short of official norms. Only three of 16 IITs that answered information requests meet the University Grants Commission's recommended ratio of one mental health professional for every 500 students. At IIT Kharagpur there is one professional for every 1,135 students, at Roorkee one for every 1,317, at Patna one for every 1,950, and at IIT-BHU one for every 4,750. The gap reflects a wider national shortage: India has about 0.75 psychiatrists per 100,000 people, against a benchmark of three set by the World Health Organization.

The courts have already intervened. In March 2025 the Supreme Court set up a national task force under former judge Justice S Ravindra Bhat to examine student mental health and recommend preventive steps, and in July it issued guidelines for educational institutions to safeguard student well-being. An interim report from the task force was submitted in November 2025. But student deaths have continued, and the evidence points to a gap between counselling on paper and coordinated, individual attention in practice, with academic pressure, isolation and unaddressed distress combining in ways routine check-ins do not catch.

Key points

  • Rishikesh Kumar won a place at an IIT and died months later in a suspected suicide, reviving scrutiny of student support at India's top technology institutes.
  • Official data shows 45 students died by suicide across 23 IITs between 2020-21 and 2024-25, with 16 deaths in 2023-24 alone.
  • Only three of 16 IITs meet the recommended one counsellor per 500 students; at IIT-BHU the ratio is one professional for every 4,750 students.
  • The Supreme Court set up a national task force in March 2025 and issued guidelines that July, but student deaths have continued.
