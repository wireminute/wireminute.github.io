Sinner Ruled Out of Australian Open After Knee Surgery

Jannik Sinner will miss the Australian Open in January after surgery on his right knee, tournament organisers confirmed on Thursday, ending any hope that the two-time champion would be fit for the first Grand Slam of the season.

The 25-year-old Italian, the world number two, had the operation on Tuesday, describing it as a minor procedure on the band of tissue that runs down the outside of the thigh to the knee. The operation was carried out by knee specialist Lasse Lempainen at a clinic in Finland, after months in which Sinner and his team tried to treat the injury without surgery.

Tennis Australia chief executive Andrew Abdo said organisers had stayed in regular contact with Sinner's team and had been told he would not play in Melbourne. He said Sinner loved competing at the tournament and was looking forward to returning for the 2028 edition. The Australian Open, which Sinner won in 2024 and 2025, starts on January 17.

Sinner has been out of action since lifting his second Wimbledon title in July, the fifth Grand Slam singles crown of his career. He reached the semi-finals in Melbourne this year, losing to Novak Djokovic, and he ended his 2026 season last month to treat the inflamed knee. Alexander Zverev has moved to the top of the rankings in his absence. Before the injury stopped him, Sinner had been enjoying the best season of his career, claiming five Masters 1000 crowns as well as his Wimbledon triumph.

Sinner said on Tuesday that the knee had not responded as hoped after some encouraging early progress with non-surgical treatment, which was why he had decided on the operation. He gave no date for his return to the tour. One knee specialist told Italian television that his absence could last at least three to four months, which would point to a return around the March events in the United States.

Key points

  • Sinner, 25, will miss the Australian Open in January after surgery on his right knee; organisers confirmed it on Thursday.
  • He had a minor operation on Tuesday on his iliotibial band after months of non-surgical treatment failed; the procedure was done by a knee specialist in Finland.
  • The two-time champion in Melbourne has not played since winning Wimbledon in July; he ended his 2026 season last month and has lost the world number one ranking to Alexander Zverev.
  • He gave no return date; one specialist suggested an absence of at least three to four months, which would point to a comeback around the March hard-court events in the United States.
