Duran Duran Guitarist Andy Taylor Dies at 65 After Prostate Cancer Battle

Andy Taylor, the original guitarist of Duran Duran whose riffs powered some of the biggest hits of the 1980s, has died at the age of 65.

Taylor died at home on Monday, with his family around him, after living with metastatic prostate cancer, his family and former bandmates said in a joint statement released on Wednesday. He was diagnosed with the disease in 2018 and made the diagnosis public four years later, when ill health kept him from the band's induction into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame. He revealed the diagnosis in a letter read out at the 2022 ceremony, saying he had been diagnosed four years earlier.

Born in Tynemouth in north-east England in 1961, Taylor joined Duran Duran in 1980 after answering a newspaper advert placed by the band. His guitar work drove era-defining singles including Rio, Hungry Like the Wolf, Girls on Film and The Reflex, songs that helped make the Birmingham band one of the defining acts of the MTV era. He left the group in 1986, returned in 2001 for a five-year reunion run, and also played in the supergroup The Power Station.

Tributes poured in from across the music world. Lead singer Simon Le Bon called Taylor "a bombshell, a firebrand and a guitar genius", while Rod Stewart and Belinda Carlisle were among those paying tribute. The band won two Grammy awards, and Taylor's last solo album, Man's a Wolf to Man, was released in 2023.

Taylor is survived by his wife Tracey, whom he married in 1982, their four children Georgina, Isabella, Bethany and Andrew, and a grandson, Albie. His family said they were grateful for the support shown since his illness became known.
