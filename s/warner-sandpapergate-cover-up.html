Warner Reignites Sandpapergate Row, Accusing Cricket Australia of a Cover-Up

Former Australia opener David Warner has accused Cricket Australia of a cover-up in its investigation of the 2018 ball-tampering scandal, saying the board never questioned three of the team's leading bowlers about what they knew.

In a post on Instagram on Saturday, Warner said that, as far as he was aware, Josh Hazlewood, Mitchell Starc and Nathan Lyon were not interviewed during the original inquiry. He argued that the board could not fairly claim the bowlers knew nothing if it had never asked them, and described the investigation as shoddy and shaped to protect a version of events decided in advance.

The row has flared again after the release of Usman Khawaja's autobiography, Sabr, in which the former opener suggested knowledge of the plan went wider than the three players who were punished. Warner backed Khawaja's account earlier in the week, and his latest post turns the focus from the players to the board's own inquiry.

Cricket Australia has pushed back. A spokesperson said every player had been given the chance to speak when the investigation took place and had been able to come forward with new evidence in the eight years since. The statement did not say whether the three bowlers named by Warner had been interviewed. Chief executive Todd Greenberg has said there are no grounds to reopen the case.

The scandal broke during the 2018 Test in Cape Town, when Cameron Bancroft was caught using sandpaper on the ball. Warner and then-captain Steve Smith were banned for a year and Bancroft for nine months, after the board's inquiry concluded only the three had known of the plan. The bowlers denied any knowledge in a joint statement in 2021, and Starc repeated that position this week. Warner's claim about who was interviewed has not been independently established.

Key points

  • Warner says Hazlewood, Starc and Lyon were never interviewed in the 2018 inquiry, and calls the probe a cover-up.
  • He argues the board cannot claim the bowlers knew nothing if it never questioned them.
  • Cricket Australia says all players had the chance to speak and to bring new evidence since, and has ruled out reopening the case.
  • The row follows Khawaja's autobiography, which suggested wider knowledge of the plan; the bowlers denied knowing anything in 2021 and again this week.
