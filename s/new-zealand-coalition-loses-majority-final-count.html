New Zealand's Ruling Coalition Loses Its Majority in Final Count, Leaving a Hung Parliament

New Zealand's ruling coalition has lost its parliamentary majority in the final election count, leaving Prime Minister Christopher Luxon dependent on a small centrist party to stay in power.

The Electoral Commission released the final results on Friday, three weeks after the 7 November election. The three-party coalition of National, ACT and New Zealand First holds 65 seats in the 130-seat parliament, one short of the 66 needed for a majority. On election night it had appeared to win 67 seats, but the counting of special votes, including ballots cast overseas and outside voters' home electorates, erased that lead.

National won 40 seats, ACT 13 and New Zealand First 12. Labour, the main opposition party, took 36 seats, the Greens 17, Te Pāti Māori five, and the Opportunity Party seven. Two electorates flipped from National to Labour in the final count, and the Greens picked up an extra list seat. A total of 2,931,604 votes were cast, including 484,865 special votes, and turnout was 77.2 percent, down from 78.2 percent at the last election.

The result puts the Opportunity Party, which entered parliament for the first time since its founding a decade ago, in the decisive position. Its leader, Qiulae Wong, has said the party will negotiate first with the largest party, and Luxon must now win its support to form a government. He had ruled out working with the party during the campaign, then signalled after election night that he would talk to it if that was what it took to govern. The party's flagship policy is a universal basic income paid for by a tax on land values.

The centre-left bloc of Labour, the Greens and Te Pāti Māori holds 58 seats and cannot form a government even with Opportunity's seven, so the only path to a government runs through Luxon. If he cannot reach a deal, the country faces a hung parliament and possibly another election. Recounts can be sought within three working days, and the caretaker government stays in place until a new administration is sworn in. National's share of the party vote fell to 28.35 percent, down from 38.1 percent at the 2023 election.

Key points

  • The final count gives Luxon's coalition 65 of 130 seats, one short of a majority; on election night it had appeared to hold 67.
  • National won 40 seats, ACT 13 and New Zealand First 12; Labour took 36, the Greens 17, Te Pāti Māori five and Opportunity seven.
  • The newcomer Opportunity Party now holds the balance of power; the centre-left bloc cannot govern even with its support.
  • If Luxon cannot strike a deal with Opportunity, New Zealand faces a hung parliament and possibly another election.
