European Commission Confirms AI-Tool Cyberattack Disrupted Its Systems for Over Ten Days

The European Commission has confirmed it was targeted by a cyber campaign carried out with the help of artificial intelligence tools, disrupting staff systems for more than ten days.

The European Commission said on Thursday it had been hit by a cyber campaign carried out with the help of artificial intelligence tools, in what news agency reports described as the first time the bloc's executive has publicly reported an attack of this kind.

Commission spokesman Thomas Regnier confirmed the incident at the regular midday briefing in Brussels, telling reporters the Commission had taken the necessary steps after detecting the campaign. He called it an AI-generated cyber campaign and said the inquiry was still open, so the full scale could not yet be judged.

The campaign was first spotted on 22 October and confirmed after a first investigation on 26 October. Internal systems were disrupted for more than ten days. Staff could not use Sysper, the platform for payslips, working hours and leave, or MyPMO, which handles medical reimbursements, and translation services were also hit. Most of the affected systems are back online, although Sysper went down again on Wednesday and the staff health insurance portal was still unavailable on Thursday, according to reports.

The Commission's cyber defence team, CERT-EU, found traces of ARTEX, a publicly available tool that uses AI agents to probe computer systems for weaknesses. Officials speaking to reporters said hackers had used the tool against the Commission, but it has not been established whether it helped them break in or was only used to look for openings, and no attacker has been named. The tool's developer withdrew it from public view last month.

The biggest open question is whether staff data was taken. Regnier said there was no evidence so far of a breach affecting Commission staff as a whole, a careful wording that stops short of ruling out any exposure of personal details. The Commission's top internal crisis body met urgently over the incident, and technology commissioner Henna Virkkunen briefed her fellow commissioners on Wednesday.

Key points

  • The European Commission confirmed it was targeted by an AI-generated cyber campaign, first detected on 22 October.
  • Staff systems for payslips, leave and medical reimbursements were disrupted for more than ten days; most are back, but some were still down on Thursday.
  • Cyber defenders found traces of ARTEX, a publicly available AI penetration-testing tool, but it is not confirmed how the attackers got in or who they were.
  • The Commission says there is no evidence so far of a data breach affecting staff as a whole, but it has not ruled out that personal data was exposed.
