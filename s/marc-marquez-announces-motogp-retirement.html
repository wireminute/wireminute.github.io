Marquez Bows Out at the Top With Shock MotoGP Retirement After Eighth Title

Eight-time MotoGP champion Marc Marquez will retire after this weekend's Valencia Grand Prix, his Ducati team announced on Friday, bringing one of motorcycle racing's greatest careers to a close at the very top.

The announcement came on Friday morning at the Ricardo Tormo circuit in Valencia, ahead of the season's final round. Ducati confirmed that the Valencia finale would be his last race as a professional rider with the factory team. Marquez shared the news himself in a video recorded in the paddock, holding up a series of placards that told fans this weekend's race would be his last and that they had made history together.

The decision stunned the paddock. Marquez sealed a record-equalling eighth premier-class title only last weekend at Portimao, finishing eighth to draw level with Giacomo Agostini, the Italian great whose eight premier-class crowns were won between 1966 and 1975. It is his tenth world title across all classes, moving him past Valentino Rossi's nine, and his second championship in a row since joining Ducati.

Only in June, Marquez had extended his contract with Ducati until the end of 2028, and his team had insisted as recently as last weekend that he would see it out. He has given no reason yet for walking away. His career has been shaped by serious injury: he went under the knife for a seventh time on his right arm and shoulder in May, when surgeons removed two bent screws that had been pressing on a nerve, and he missed two race weekends this season before fighting back to the title.

His departure leaves Ducati searching for a new factory rider to partner Pedro Acosta from 2027, when the sport moves into a new era of smaller 850cc machines. Sunday's race in Valencia now becomes a farewell for a rider who won six of his premier-class titles with Honda before switching to Ducati and rebuilding his career after injury.

Key points

  • Marc Marquez will retire after Sunday's Valencia Grand Prix, Ducati confirmed on Friday.
  • He sealed a record-equalling eighth premier-class title last weekend at Portimao, his tenth world title across all classes.
  • Marquez had a Ducati contract until the end of 2028 and has given no reason yet for stepping away.
  • Ducati must now find a new factory rider to partner Pedro Acosta from 2027.
