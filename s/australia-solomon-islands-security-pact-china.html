Australia and Solomon Islands Sign Sweeping Security Treaty in Shift Away From China

Australia and Solomon Islands have signed a sweeping treaty that names Canberra as the Pacific nation's primary security partner and rules out any foreign military base on its soil, in a deal widely seen as a setback for China's ambitions in the region.

Prime Ministers Anthony Albanese of Australia and Matthew Wale of Solomon Islands signed the treaty at Parliament House in Canberra on Wednesday, with Wale leading a delegation of 15 ministers for talks covering security, infrastructure, education and health. The agreement, formally called the Australia-Solomon Islands Treaty, declares Australia the island nation's primary security partner and states that there will be no foreign military or security base on its soil.

Under the treaty, Solomon Islands also commits to keeping its sensitive infrastructure free from militarisation, foreign interference and unauthorised access, covering airports, ports, energy and telecommunications. Honiara must consult Canberra before striking deals with other countries on critical infrastructure or security cooperation outside the Pacific Islands Forum, the regional bloc of which China is only a dialogue partner.

Australia will commit 740 million Australian dollars, about 490 million US dollars, over a decade, including 670 million dollars in grants and 70 million in concessional loans, to support free education, health, policing, resources, energy and climate resilience. The funding for free education is due to be in place by January. The treaty also covers road and bridge building, renewable energy, sport, help with a sovereign wealth fund and improved visa arrangements.

The signing follows reports that Honiara told Beijing it would not renew a secretive security pact struck with China in 2022, which is due to expire in April 2027. Wale would neither confirm nor deny those reports at the news conference, saying a non-disclosure clause in the China deal prevented him from discussing it and that talks with Beijing were continuing. China's foreign ministry said on Tuesday that its ties with Pacific nations were not a matter of geopolitical rivalry. Wale, elected in May, has moved to rebuild relations with traditional partners after his predecessor built close ties with Beijing, including the switch of diplomatic recognition from Taiwan to China in 2019.

Key points

  • Australia and Solomon Islands signed a treaty in Canberra on Wednesday naming Australia as Honiara's primary security partner.
  • The treaty rules out any foreign military or security base on Solomon Islands soil and protects sensitive infrastructure from militarisation and foreign interference.
  • Australia will provide A$740 million over a decade, mostly grants, for education, health, policing, energy and climate resilience.
  • The deal follows reports Honiara will not renew its secretive 2022 security pact with China; Wale would neither confirm nor deny them, citing a non-disclosure clause.
