Magnitude 6.9 Earthquake Brings Rockfalls and Damage to Peru's Andes, No Deaths Reported

A strong earthquake in central Peru has brought down landslides and rockfalls, blocked a main highway and cut power in parts of the Andes, officials said, though no deaths or injuries have been reported.

The tremor measured magnitude 6.9 and struck at 7:09 p.m. local time on Friday, the United States Geological Survey said. The survey placed the epicentre in the Andes, 34 kilometres north of the city of Junin, and put the depth at 108 kilometres. Peru's own Geophysical Institute measured the quake at magnitude 6.7 and put the depth at 129 kilometres. The shaking was felt in the capital Lima, where residents ran into the streets and lawmakers walked out of Congress, and in the regions of Huanuco and Callao.

No deaths or injuries have been reported, the Geophysical Institute and the national emergency institute said. But damage has now been reported in four regions. The head of the Geophysical Institute, Hernando Tavera, told reporters that the shaking was intense and that landslides and rockfalls had hit hillsides. Rockfalls blocked part of the Carretera Central highway near Jauja, in Huanuco, and landslides fell from the cliffs of the Costa Verde onto a highway in Lima. Early local reports also spoke of power cuts in Satipo and cracks at a university campus in La Merced, and officials said teams were still checking those reports.

Peru's National Emergency Operations Centre said the quake was felt with moderate to strong intensity and that monitoring was continuing in vulnerable areas. No tsunami warning was issued. Emergency teams have been sent to the affected areas to assess the damage, and officials have urged people to follow official bulletins as aftershocks remain possible.

Peru lies on the Pacific Ring of Fire, one of the most seismically active zones on Earth, and strong quakes are common in the Andean region. A smaller quake in the same region in July killed six people, and a magnitude 6.7 quake in the Andes in August injured two people and damaged homes, health centres and schools. The story is still developing.
