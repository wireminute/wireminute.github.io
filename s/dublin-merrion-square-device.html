Viable Bomb Found in Dublin Office as European Leaders Gather Nearby

Army bomb experts have made safe a crude but workable explosive device found in an office building on Merrion Square in Dublin, a short distance from where European leaders dined the night before while they met in the Irish capital for a two-day summit.

Police were called shortly after 2:20 p.m. on Friday, after a suspicious device was reported in the building. A cordon went up, and the Defence Forces bomb disposal team was called in after a police assessment. The team made the device safe, and police said an investigation had begun, with no word on when it was planted or by whom.

The find came as about 50 European leaders gathered in Dublin for a two-day summit. They had dined at the National Gallery of Ireland on Thursday night — the gallery stands on the square's west side, the device in an office on its north side. The summit itself met at a separate venue about 1.5 kilometres away.

Hoax bomb threats had already swept Ireland that week, including emails to more than 300 schools, during the largest security operation in the country's history. Police have not linked it to those threats.
