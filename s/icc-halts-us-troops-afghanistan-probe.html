International Court Ends Afghanistan Case Covering US Forces

Judges at the international court in The Hague have closed the part of its Afghanistan investigation that could have examined alleged crimes by American troops, and will now focus only on the Taliban's actions since 2021.

The judges' decision, taken on Thursday and made public on Friday, formally ends a line of inquiry that had already been dormant since 2021. In their ruling, the judges pointed to serious limits on the prosecution's ability to carry out investigations in Afghanistan and to gather evidence there. The same decision also closes a separate inquiry into the Islamic State affiliate in Khorasan Province.

The prosecutor's office said it had already appealed the ruling, though it gave no further details. The Afghanistan investigation began in 2020 and covered alleged offences dating back to 2002, involving Afghan government forces, the Taliban, American troops and American intelligence operatives. In 2021 the then prosecutor said he would give lower priority to the part concerning American forces, citing the difficulty of obtaining evidence. He was removed from the court earlier this year over sexual misconduct allegations.

The ruling comes after years of friction between Washington and the Hague-based court. The United States has imposed sanctions on 13 current and former court staff over the investigation into alleged crimes by American personnel and over arrest warrants issued against Israeli officials. The American secretary of state has said the president will never allow the court to prosecute Americans, and has warned that Washington will keep working to dismantle the court. The United States is not a member of the court and does not accept its jurisdiction, although the court's founding statute allows it to prosecute atrocity crimes committed on the territory of member states.

The court will now concentrate on alleged crimes by the Taliban since 2021. It issued four arrest warrants for Taliban leaders last year. It remains unclear whether the decision to end the American-troops inquiry will change Washington's stance towards the court.

Key points

  • Judges have closed the part of the Afghanistan investigation that could have examined alleged crimes by American troops, citing limits on investigating and gathering evidence.
  • The same decision also ends the inquiry into the Islamic State affiliate in Khorasan Province, and the prosecutor's office has appealed.
  • The American-troops line had been dormant since 2021, when the then prosecutor deprioritised it over evidence difficulties.
  • The court will now focus only on the Taliban's actions since 2021, after issuing four arrest warrants for Taliban leaders last year.
