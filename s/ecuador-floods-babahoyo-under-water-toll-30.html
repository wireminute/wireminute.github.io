Babahoyo Under Water as Ecuador Flood Toll Climbs to at Least 30

Floods driven by heavy El Niño rains have left much of the Ecuadorian city of Babahoyo under water, with at least 30 people now dead and more than 121,000 affected across the country.

Babahoyo, the capital of Los Ríos province, is the worst-hit city. About 80 percent of the canton is flooded after eight rivers burst their banks, and the local emergency committee has declared a 90-day emergency. Streets, homes and businesses are under water, residents are wading through flooded roads, and small boats are moving through neighbourhoods where cars once drove.

The death toll has risen sharply in the past two weeks. Ecuador's risk agency counted 14 deaths and about 77,500 people affected by mid-November, but the latest reports put the toll at 30 or more, with the number affected above 121,000. In Los Ríos alone, nearly 15,000 people have been affected, and a landslide in the town of Mocache killed two people and put the area under a state of emergency.

The flooding has spread well beyond Babahoyo. Dozens of neighbourhoods in Guayaquil, the country's largest city, are under water, and classes on the coast and in the Galápagos Islands have moved online. Highways linking Babahoyo with neighbouring towns are closed or flooded, and emergency teams have opened shelters and moved supplies to families forced from their homes.

The rains are part of an El Niño emergency that Ecuador declared at the end of August, and forecasters warn the event could grow stronger before the end of the year. Since then, the risk agency has recorded about 1,500 rain-related emergencies across all 24 provinces, from floods and landslides to damaged homes, roads and crops, and the toll is expected to rise as waters recede and more areas are reached.

Key points

  • At least 30 people have been killed and more than 121,000 affected by El Niño-linked flooding across Ecuador.
  • Babahoyo, the capital of Los Ríos province, is the worst hit, with about 80 percent of the canton flooded and a 90-day local emergency in force after eight rivers overflowed.
  • The toll has more than doubled since mid-November, when the official count stood at 14 dead and about 77,500 people affected.
  • A landslide in Mocache killed two people, schools on the coast have moved online, and forecasters warn the El Niño event could strengthen further.
