Ecuador Flood Toll Rises to 34 as El Niño Rains Displace About 47,000

The death toll from El Niño-linked flooding across Ecuador has risen to 34, the country's risk agency said on Friday, with about 47,000 people displaced and more than 126,000 affected in total.

Babahoyo, the capital of Los Ríos province, remains the worst-hit city. About 80 percent of the canton is flooded after eight rivers burst their banks, and the local emergency committee has declared a 90-day emergency. Streets, homes and businesses are under water, residents are wading through flooded roads, and small boats are moving through neighbourhoods where cars once drove.

Ecuador's National Secretariat for Risk Management said on Friday that the floods have now killed 34 people, left about 47,000 displaced and injured 23, with roughly 31,000 homes damaged. The total number of people affected stands at 126,935, and the agency has recorded 2,227 rain-related emergencies across all 24 provinces since the emergency began at the end of August. Two of the latest deaths were reported on Thursday in San Vicente, in Manabí province, where rain-loosened earth buried homes, and a bridge over the Cristal River in Montalvo collapsed, with no injuries reported.

The flooding has spread well beyond Babahoyo. Dozens of neighbourhoods in Guayaquil, the country's largest city, are under water, and classes on the coast and in the Galápagos Islands have moved online. Highways linking Babahoyo with neighbouring towns are closed or flooded, and emergency teams have opened shelters and moved supplies to families forced from their homes.

The death toll has risen sharply in the past two weeks. The risk agency counted 14 deaths and about 77,500 people affected by mid-November, but the latest reports put the toll at 30 or more by November 25, and it has climbed again since. The rains are part of an El Niño emergency that Ecuador declared at the end of August, and forecasters warn the event could grow stronger before the end of the year. The toll is expected to rise further as waters recede and more areas are reached.

  • Two more people died on Thursday in San Vicente, Manabí, where rain-loosened earth buried homes, and a bridge over the Cristal River in Montalvo collapsed.
