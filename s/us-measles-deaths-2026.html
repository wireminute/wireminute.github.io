US Measles-Free Status Ends After 26 Years as Virus Keeps Spreading

After 26 years, the United States no longer holds its measles-free designation: health experts have concluded the virus is once again spreading without interruption inside the country, during its worst outbreak since 1991.

The designation, in place since 2000, was withdrawn after an independent panel of health experts found the virus had been passing from person to person in the country without a break for more than a year. The finding, reported on Friday, closes a public-health chapter that lasted 26 years and lands in the middle of the country's worst outbreak since 1991.

The panel gathered in Washington over four days in mid-November to weigh the evidence. Under the rules it applies, a country loses its elimination standing when one strain of the virus keeps circulating without interruption for at least 12 months. Genomic evidence pointed to a single lineage, known as D8-9171, behind the major outbreaks in Texas, Utah and South Carolina, with Utah's own laboratory work tracing one strain through the state for well over a year.

The formal announcement is still to come. The health body has not published the decision in its own name, and its director makes the final call; reports say the outcome is expected to be made public on Tuesday, November 17. Losing the status carries no formal sanctions, and it can be regained, but only after transmission is interrupted and the country stays free of endemic spread for a full year, alongside a remedial plan.

Case counts underline the scale of the outbreak behind the decision. States have logged over 5,400 infections in 2026, the most in any year since 1991, and the death toll reported by the states stands at 10 this year, seven in Pennsylvania, two in Wisconsin and one in New York. The national health agency's own confirmed count stands at 5,182 cases through November 5, and its official death tally lists six. Kindergarten vaccination coverage against measles has fallen to 92.4 percent, down from 95.2 percent in the 2019-2020 school year and below the 95 percent level the health body says is needed to keep measles eliminated.

Key points

  • An international health panel has concluded the US no longer qualifies as having eliminated measles, ending a status held since 2000.
  • The same measles strain has circulated without a break for more than 12 months, driven by outbreaks in Texas, Utah and South Carolina.
  • States have logged more than 5,400 cases in 2026, the most since 1991, and 10 measles-associated deaths.
  • The formal announcement is expected on Tuesday, November 17; the status can only be regained after a full year without endemic spread.
