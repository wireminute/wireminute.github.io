Child Under Five Dies of Measles in Virginia, the State's First Death of 2026

A child under five has died after testing positive for measles in Virginia, the state's first measles-associated death of 2026, health officials have announced, days after the United States lost its measles elimination status.

The Virginia Department of Health announced the death on Friday, saying the child was between birth and four years old. The department said it would give no further details about the child, including where the child lived or whether the child had been vaccinated, to protect the family's privacy.

Virginia has recorded 220 measles cases in 2026, according to state health department figures as of November 19, with most cases in the central part of the state. Forty of the cases are in children aged four and under, and 75 are in children aged five to 12. The department said the vast majority of the state's cases have been in people who were not vaccinated against the virus. Its public dashboard is due to be updated on Tuesday, November 24, to include the death.

The death follows the eighth measles fatality of the year in Pennsylvania, where an unvaccinated resident of Clinton County died after contracting the virus. Pennsylvania's outbreak has reached 1,589 confirmed cases across 46 of its 67 counties, with 315 patients admitted to hospital, close to one in every five confirmed cases. The state's death toll has risen from six at the start of the month.

The two deaths come days after the Pan American Health Organization confirmed the United States had lost its measles elimination status, a standing it had held since 2000. The health body's expert commission ruled that the same strain of the virus has circulated from person to person inside the country for more than 12 months, the threshold at which a country loses the standing. Bolivia and Mexico were told they have lost the status as well.

The national health agency has confirmed 5,412 measles cases in 2026 as of November 12, the highest annual total in 35 years. Losing the elimination status carries no formal sanctions and changes no law or travel rule, but it can only be regained after transmission is interrupted and the country stays free of endemic spread for a full year. Kindergarten vaccination coverage against measles has fallen to 92.4 percent, down from 95.2 percent in the 2019-2020 school year and below the 95 percent level the health body says is needed to keep measles eliminated.
