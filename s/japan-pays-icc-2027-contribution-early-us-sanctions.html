Japan to Pay ICC Dues Early to Shield Court From US Sanctions

Japan will pay its full 2027 contribution to the International Criminal Court by the end of the year, Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi said on Tuesday, stepping in to shield the court from American sanctions that threaten its funding.

Takaichi told the court's president, Tomoko Akane, of the decision during a meeting at her office in Tokyo on Tuesday. The court had asked Japan, its largest financial backer, to pay early because Washington's sanctions could cut off its access to the dollar-based payments system.

Japan provides about 15 percent of the money the court needs, roughly 4.6 billion yen of a budget of about 34.5 billion yen, making it the biggest contributor ahead of Germany, France, Britain, Italy, South Korea and Canada. Paying the 2027 contribution months ahead of schedule gives the court a cash buffer before the sanctions take full effect in April 2027, and the money will go toward staff salaries and running costs.

Washington sanctioned Akane, a Japanese national and the first from her country to lead the court, in August, freezing her assets in the United States and barring her entry. It imposed sanctions on the court itself in October, with a 180-day grace period. The administration accuses the court of overstepping state sovereignty and objects to its investigations into alleged war crimes involving American military personnel and Israel.

Takaichi said Tokyo and Washington disagree over the court and that Japan would work with other countries to keep it running. The decision came days after the leaders of the United States, China and Russia said in a joint declaration after their first three-way summit that the court must not abuse its jurisdiction. Akane said she and Takaichi did not discuss that declaration.

The early payment is the clearest sign yet that Japan intends to stand by the court even as its closest security ally tries to weaken it. Analysts say the move could encourage other large contributors to pay early as well, building a buffer that keeps the court operating into next year.

Key points

  • Japan will pay its full 2027 contribution to the International Criminal Court by the end of this year, Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi said on Tuesday.
  • Japan is the court's largest contributor, providing about 15 percent of its budget, roughly 4.6 billion yen.
  • The United States sanctioned the court's president in August and the court itself in October; the sanctions take full effect in April 2027.
  • The early payment gives the court access to its funding before the sanctions bite, covering staff salaries and running costs.
