Musk Accuses Zelenskyy of Lying Over Starshield Limits as Kyiv Warns Drones Are Losing Their Link

Elon Musk has accused Volodymyr Zelenskyy of lying about restrictions on the military satellite service Starshield, saying Ukraine is really trying to use the civilian Starlink network for offensive drone strikes, as Kyiv warns the limits are letting Russian attacks on its power grid through.

Elon Musk has accused Volodymyr Zelenskyy of lying about restrictions on the military satellite internet service Starshield, telling a New York audience that Ukraine is trying to use the civilian Starlink network for offensive drone strikes. Asked about the Ukrainian president's claim that Starshield had been slowed, Musk replied that it was false and that Zelenskyy was not always truthful, adding that Kyiv really wanted the commercial satellites for offensive military operations, something SpaceX had refused from the start of the war.

Zelenskyy has said for days that speed limits on Starshield terminals are letting Russian strikes on Ukraine's energy network get through. Ukrainian interceptor drones lose their link to their operators once they pass 161 kilometres per hour, he said, so they cannot keep pace with the jet-powered Shahed drones Russia is now using against substations. Russian forces struck energy and railway infrastructure in eight regions overnight into Sunday, and an energy facility hit in Odesa left 41,000 households without electricity. Russia launched more than 2,300 attack drones, 1,590 guided bombs and 15 missiles against Ukraine in the past week alone, the president said.

The row has been running for weeks. The limits have been in effect since last week, according to the president, who said the service provider had pointed to technical problems, an explanation Kyiv does not accept. Starlink has denied imposing any speed limits, and Musk has said the United States government controls Starshield. A newspaper reported in October, citing American officials, that Musk himself had ordered a slowdown of the service, and later that month Musk said SpaceX had cut back Ukraine's Starlink access after Kyiv used the service for offensive drone attacks in breach of its terms, an account Ukrainian officials rejected. Ukraine's defence ministry said the limits had been detected only in occupied territory, not in areas under Kyiv's control.

Musk's remarks came as Ukraine struck the Crimean Bridge for a third time in a week, hitting its railway section overnight and halting train services. Reports from Kyiv said Ukrainian forces did not use either satellite system in that strike, relying on their own equipment instead. Zelenskyy has ordered his foreign ministry, the defence ministry and his own office to work with the American side and SpaceX to get the limits lifted, warning that protecting lives and keeping the energy system running through the winter depended on it. The story is still developing.

Key points

  • Musk told a New York summit audience that Zelenskyy was lying about Starshield slowdowns and said Kyiv wants to use civilian Starlink for offensive strikes
  • Zelenskyy says a 161 km/h speed limit is cutting interceptor drones off from their operators and letting Russian strikes hit power sites
  • The dispute has run for weeks: SpaceX blamed technical faults for an October slowdown, while a newspaper report said Musk himself ordered it
  • Ukraine says its forces did not use either satellite system in this week's Crimean Bridge strikes, relying on its own equipment
