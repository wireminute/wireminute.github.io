Zelenskyy Says Starshield Speed Limits Are Letting Russian Strikes Through

Ukraine's president has blamed speed limits on the military satellite internet service Starshield for allowing Russian attacks on the country's energy network to succeed, saying interceptor drones are losing contact with their operators mid-flight.

Russian forces struck energy and railway infrastructure in eight regions of Ukraine overnight into Sunday, according to President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. Energy sites were hit in the Zaporizhzhia, Sumy, Odesa, Zhytomyr and Kirovohrad regions, and two people were killed on Saturday when a drone struck infrastructure in the northern town of Koriukivka. An energy facility hit in Odesa overnight left 41,000 households without electricity, the country's largest private energy provider said.

Zelenskyy said the problem was the operating speed of Starshield terminals used along the front line and in occupied territory. Ukrainian interceptor drones lose their link to their operators once they pass 161 kilometres per hour, he said, so they cannot keep pace with the jet-powered Shahed drones Russia is now using against substations. He said many Russian strikes were reaching their targets because the limits were still in place.

The limits have been in effect for five days, according to the president, who said the service provider had pointed to technical problems — an explanation Kyiv does not accept. Starlink has denied imposing any speed limits, and its owner, Elon Musk, has said the United States government controls Starshield. It is the second such disruption in recent weeks, and Ukraine's defence ministry said on Friday that limits had been detected only in occupied territory, not in areas under Kyiv's control.

Zelenskyy said he had ordered the foreign ministry, the defence ministry and his own office to work with the American side and SpaceX to get the limits lifted, warning that protecting lives and keeping the energy system running through the winter depended on it. Russia launched more than 2,300 attack drones, 1,590 guided bombs and 15 missiles of various types against Ukraine in the past week alone, he said.

Key points

  • Zelenskyy says speed limits on Starshield terminals are letting Russian strikes on Ukraine's energy network get through.
  • Ukrainian interceptor drones lose contact with their operators above 161 km/h and cannot catch jet-powered Shahed drones.
  • Russia struck energy and railway sites in eight regions overnight, and 41,000 households in Odesa lost power.
  • Kyiv has asked the US and SpaceX to lift the limits; Starlink denies imposing them and says Washington controls Starshield.
