Six Killed, Including Two Children, as Suspected Gas Blast Collapses Tbilisi Apartment Block

Six people, including two children, have been killed after a suspected gas explosion tore through part of a five-storey apartment building in Georgia's capital, Tbilisi, with a three-year-old girl pulled alive from the rubble after hours of rescue work.

The blast struck the residential building in the Ponichala district on Thursday morning, at around 9 a.m., trapping residents beneath the wreckage. Georgia's interior minister, Sulkhan Tamazashvili, said rescuers recovered six bodies from the rubble, including a seven-year-old child, and that search and rescue operations were completed around midnight.

A three-year-old girl was found alive under the rubble after several hours of digging. Doctors at the Ingorokva University Clinic said she was in a stable condition and scans had shown no serious traumatic injuries. Four people were pulled from the wreckage and taken to hospital, and the minister said all four were conscious when rescuers reached them.

Tbilisi's mayor, Kakha Kaladze, said 28 families, more than 100 residents, would be temporarily moved to hotels after structural checks found two sections of the building unsafe. A prosecutor said a criminal investigation had been opened into possible violations of safety regulations.

Early findings point to a gas heater on the fifth floor. The minister said the resident told investigators he had used the heater the night before and switched it on again in the morning, when the blast happened. But the gas supplier, Tbilisi Energy, said the flat identified as the likely source had been registered as unoccupied and had recorded no gas use over the past year, leaving the exact cause still to be established.

Key points

  • Six people, including two children, were killed when a suspected gas explosion partly collapsed a five-storey apartment block in Tbilisi's Ponichala district.
  • A three-year-old girl was pulled alive from the rubble after hours of rescue work and is in a stable condition in hospital.
  • Search and rescue operations ended around midnight; 28 families, more than 100 people, were moved to hotels after two sections of the building were found unsafe.
