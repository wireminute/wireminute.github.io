Costa Rica Bars UN Rapporteur Francesca Albanese Over Alleged Terror-Links Alert

Costa Rica has refused entry to Francesca Albanese, the United Nations special rapporteur for the occupied Palestinian territories, saying she is named in an international alert over alleged links to terrorist acts, a claim she has dismissed as a disgrace.

Albanese arrived on Thursday at Juan Santamaria International Airport, outside the capital San Jose, to collect an honorary doctorate awarded by the University of Costa Rica. Immigration officers stopped her at the airport and held her there overnight. Omer Badilla, director general of the immigration authority, said in a recorded video message that she was refused entry because an international alert named her over alleged links to terrorist acts. He did not say which country issued the alert.

Speaking while held at the airport, Albanese called her detention a disgrace. In a post from the airport, she wrote that people see the face of empire on the day they touch a nerve or cross a line. She told the student outlet Semanario Universidad that she knew her name was on a US sanctions list, but said that gave Costa Rica no reason to hold her. She asked whether Costa Rica was a sovereign country, noting that they were not in the United States. A Costa Rican court has since put her removal on hold while it reviews a challenge to the decision.

The University of Costa Rica said immigration authorities were blocking her entry and called on the government to justify the move. The university was awarding her the honorary doctorate, which it bestows each year on a leading figure, in recognition of her scholarship, publications and human rights work.

The UN Human Rights Council named Albanese to the post in 2022. As an independent expert, she does not speak for the United Nations itself. She has drawn harsh criticism from Israel and from the United States and its allies over her conclusions that Israel carried out genocide and other war crimes in Gaza and the occupied West Bank. Washington imposed sanctions on her after she criticised its policy on Gaza, and Israel declared her persona non grata in 2024.

The decision drew praise from Israel's envoy to the United Nations, Danny Danon, who welcomed the move. President Laura Fernandez, a staunch supporter of US President Donald Trump, has moved to deepen trade ties with Israel since taking office in May. Last December Albanese spoke against a Costa Rica-Israel free trade agreement and urged lawmakers to pause its ratification, drawing a sharp rebuke from San Jose.

Key points

  • Costa Rica refused Albanese entry at San Jose airport, citing an international alert over alleged links to terrorist acts, without naming the country behind it.
  • She was held at the airport overnight after arriving to collect an honorary doctorate awarded by the University of Costa Rica, which demanded an explanation.
  • Albanese called the detention a disgrace and asked why a US sanctions listing would justify her detention in Costa Rica; a court has put her removal on hold.
  • She was named to the UN post in 2022 and has faced US sanctions and Israeli criticism over her findings on Gaza and the occupied West Bank.
