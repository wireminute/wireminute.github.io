UN Rapporteur Albanese Leaves Costa Rica Airport After Court Orders Her Release

Francesca Albanese, the United Nations special rapporteur for the occupied Palestinian territory, has left the airport in San Jose after two nights held there, following a court order, and is due to travel on to Mexico after Costa Rica refused her entry over alleged terror links she calls false.

Albanese confirmed her release in a post on Saturday: "I'm outside the airport. Finally. Thank you to everyone who helped me." She had spent a second night held at Juan Santamaria International Airport, where she arrived on Thursday to collect an honorary doctorate. Late on Friday, the Constitutional Chamber of the Supreme Court issued a second interim order directing that she be moved to a hotel, and confirmed Mexico as her destination.

Hours before her release, she said the order was not being carried out. In a post at about 4am GMT she wrote that the court had ordered her to be let out to a hotel to rest, but immigration police would not let her leave, and that airline staff were ignoring the ruling after more than 50 hours awake. A court had also ordered officials on Friday to return her passport immediately.

Immigration officers stopped her on Thursday. Omer Badilla, head of the immigration authority, said she was refused entry because an international alert named her over alleged links to terrorist acts, without saying which country issued it. Albanese has called the claim defamation and demanded the government prove it or retract it and apologise.

Speaking from the airport, she said officers took her passport and offered her a room she said smelled so badly she nearly felt sick, and threatened to force her onto a flight back to Paris when she refused to board. On Friday the university went ahead with the ceremony, and Albanese joined it by video call. She never formally entered the country, and the Italian embassy provided consular assistance.

The UN human rights chief, Volker Turk, said he was deeply concerned and urged Costa Rica to reconsider urgently. Human Rights Watch and Costa Rican lawmakers criticised the refusal, while Israel's envoy to the United Nations welcomed it. President Laura Fernandez has deepened trade ties with Israel since taking office in May, and Albanese had previously spoken against a Costa Rica-Israel free trade agreement.

The UN Human Rights Council named Albanese to the post in 2022. She has drawn harsh criticism from Israel and from the United States and its allies over her conclusions that Israel carried out genocide and other war crimes in Gaza and the occupied West Bank. Washington imposed sanctions on her after she criticised its policy on Gaza, and Israel declared her persona non grata in 2024.

Key points

  • Albanese left San Jose airport on Saturday morning after the Constitutional Court ordered her release to a hotel; she is due to travel on to Mexico.
  • She says immigration police at first refused to carry out the court order and airline staff ignored it, after she spent more than 50 hours awake at the airport.
  • Costa Rica refused her entry over an unnamed international alert alleging terror links; she calls the claim defamation and demands proof or an apology.
  • She arrived to collect an honorary doctorate and joined the ceremony by video; the UN human rights chief urged Costa Rica to reconsider, while Israel's envoy welcomed the refusal.
