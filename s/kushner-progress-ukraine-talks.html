Kushner Says Progress Made Toward Ukraine Deal, Warns Hard Choices Remain

The United States is making headway toward an agreement to end the war in Ukraine, envoy Jared Kushner said on Monday, while cautioning that serious obstacles remain and any final deal will demand difficult decisions.

Kushner said he and fellow envoy Steve Witkoff had spoken with Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov on Monday morning about the progress made so far and the next steps in the peace process. He said the two Americans had also spoken with Ukraine and its European partners, including the national security advisers of Germany, France, Britain, Italy and Poland, along with representatives of the European Union and NATO.

Writing on X, Kushner set out three elements on which he said the United States, working with Ukraine and its European partners over the past year, had made meaningful headway: strong and credible security guarantees to protect Ukraine from future attack, a path to membership of the European Union, and a broad post-war prosperity plan drawn up with European partners, the World Bank and others to support the country's finances, reconstruction and long-term growth. He said the aim was an outcome that ends the war, strengthens European security and lays the foundations for lasting peace.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said the online discussion with the American envoys and European representatives had not been easy, but that it had produced a result. He said the participants had agreed to meet in Berlin with the Coalition of the Willing, the group of countries backing Ukraine, so Kyiv and its supporters can settle their next negotiating steps. Zelenskyy said the French, German, British, Italian and Polish representatives had taken full part in the talks and would have the same role in future meetings with Russian negotiators.

Moscow, however, pushed back on the idea of European involvement. Ushakov said Moscow had not signed off on bringing European delegates into the talks, which so far have involved only Russian, Ukrainian and American teams, after Kushner and Witkoff had said following their Moscow meeting that European representatives would join the next round. The exchanges followed talks in Abu Dhabi last week, the first time American, Ukrainian and Russian officials had met together in nine months. Zelenskyy also said Kyiv had intelligence pointing to heavy Russian strikes on Ukrainian targets in the coming hours, and that North Korea was now supplying Russia with dozens of ballistic missiles every month.

Key points

  • Kushner said the US was making headway toward a deal to end the war, after calls with Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov and with Ukraine and European partners
  • He set out three elements: credible security guarantees for Ukraine, a path to EU membership, and a post-war prosperity plan
  • Zelenskyy called the talks difficult but productive, and said the sides had agreed to meet in Berlin with the Coalition of the Willing
  • Moscow said it had not agreed to European participation in the next round of talks with Russian negotiators
