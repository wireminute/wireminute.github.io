Trump Posthumously Pardons Baseball Hit King Pete Rose

President Donald Trump has posthumously pardoned Pete Rose, baseball's all-time hits leader, more than two years after the star's death, clearing the tax convictions that followed his lifetime ban from the game.

Trump announced the pardon on Thursday in a social media post from Air Force One as he flew home from a trip to Asia, calling Rose a true baseball superstar and legend. The pardon covers the two counts of filing false tax returns to which Rose pleaded guilty in 1990.

Rose, who died in September 2024 at the age of 83, holds baseball's career record with 4,256 hits over 24 seasons from 1963 to 1986, most famously as part of the Cincinnati Reds side known as the Big Red Machine. He was a three-time World Series champion, the 1973 league most valuable player and the 1975 World Series most valuable player.

His playing career was overshadowed by gambling. In August 1989 Rose agreed to a permanent ban from baseball after an investigation led by John Dowd concluded he had bet repeatedly on the Cincinnati Reds while playing for and managing his hometown team between 1985 and 1987. He later admitted he had bet on baseball for years.

The pardon follows a separate decision last May by Major League Baseball Commissioner Rob Manfred, who ruled that people on the permanently ineligible list have their status restored at death. That ruling, which Manfred acknowledged Trump had pushed for, made Rose eligible for consideration for the Hall of Fame, with the next vote by the Classic Baseball Era committee due in December 2027 and possible induction in 2028. The pardon removes the criminal conviction from Rose's record, but neither step guarantees he will be voted in.

Key points

  • President Donald Trump has posthumously pardoned Pete Rose, clearing the two 1990 convictions for filing false tax returns.
  • Rose, who died in September 2024 aged 83, is baseball's career hits leader with 4,256 hits over 24 seasons.
  • He was banned from baseball for life in 1989 after an investigation found he had bet on the Cincinnati Reds while playing for and managing the team.
  • Baseball reinstated Rose after his death in May 2025, making him eligible for Hall of Fame consideration, with the next vote due in December 2027.
