Trump's Election Security Pick Says Jan 6 Was Not an Attack at Senate Hearing

The nominee chosen to lead the agency that helps protect American elections told senators on Thursday that he does not believe the storming of the US Capitol on January 6, 2021 was an attack, and he refused to say who won the 2020 presidential election.

Michael LoSapio, nominated to head the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, was questioned by Senator Richard Blumenthal of Connecticut during his confirmation hearing before the Senate Homeland Security Committee. Asked directly whether an attack on the Capitol had taken place that day, LoSapio first said he did not know, then answered: "I don't believe it was an attack." When Blumenthal pressed him again, he repeated that it was not an attack.

LoSapio said he had not been involved in politics at the time and knew only what he had seen on the news. Blumenthal, who asked him "Were you alive on the planet?", said the evasions showed a lack of independence and objectivity. On the 2020 result, LoSapio said he would not answer a question about an election six years ago, adding that his only involvement in that vote was casting a ballot for the president.

LoSapio is currently the chief information security officer at Palantir, a data and software company. The agency he has been picked to lead helps defend election systems and critical infrastructure from cyber threats, and it has had no Senate-confirmed director since Jen Easterly left the post in January 2025. He was one of 13 nominees who appeared before the committee on Thursday.

The answers drew a swift backlash. Shortly after the hearing, Senator Thom Tillis, a Republican on the committee, said he would not support the nomination and would place a hold on it, while Senator Ron Wyden said he would block expedited consideration. The nomination must first be advanced by the committee before the full Senate can vote on it. A day earlier, another nominee, Cody Hiland, had also declined to say who won the 2020 election when Blumenthal put the same question to him.

Key points

  • Michael LoSapio, Trump's pick to lead the election security agency CISA, told senators he does not believe the January 6 Capitol riot was an attack.
  • He also refused to say who won the 2020 election, declining to answer a question about an election six years ago.
  • LoSapio, the chief information security officer at Palantir, was pressed by Senator Richard Blumenthal; CISA has had no confirmed director since January 2025.
  • Republican Senator Thom Tillis said he will block the nomination, putting its confirmation in doubt.
