Pope Leo Demands Justice for Indigenous Leaders Murdered Defending the Amazon

Pope Leo XIV demanded justice on Sunday for Indigenous leaders murdered while defending their land in Peru's Amazon, telling a largely Indigenous audience in the jungle city of Pucallpa that the fate of the rainforest and the fate of its peoples could not be separated.

Speaking in Pucallpa, a city on the Ucayali River about 200 kilometres west of the Brazilian border, Leo said many Indigenous leaders had been killed for standing up for their territories. "Many Indigenous leaders have been murdered for defending their territories," he told missionaries and representatives of Amazonian peoples gathered in the city. "The blood of these brothers cries out to God and demands justice!" The visit came on the penultimate day of his 12-day tour of South America, and he is due to return to Rome on Monday.

The pope urged Catholics to oppose "with perseverance" the deforestation and pollution caused by gold and oil production in the world's largest rainforest. He warned that patterns of exploitation and economic activity threatened the balance of creation, and told his audience they knew well the damage done by indiscriminate deforestation and the pollution of rivers and other waters through illegal mining and oil spills. "The cry of the earth and the cry of the poor are inseparable," he said, echoing a teaching of his predecessor Francis that links care for the most vulnerable with care for creation.

The scale of the violence he described is stark. Thirty-five Indigenous leaders have been killed in Peru over the past 15 years, more than two-thirds of them this decade, according to the advocacy group Amazon Watch. Their communities face mounting pressure from illegal logging, gold mining, coca cultivation and agricultural expansion, and most killings of land defenders go unpunished. Peru lost about 200,000 hectares of natural forest in 2025 alone, and 3.2 million hectares between 2001 and 2024, an area roughly the size of Belgium.

Earlier on Sunday, Leo had celebrated Mass in Cusco, the former Inca capital more than 3,400 metres above sea level, at the fortress of Saqsaywaman. There he denounced what he called the abandonment of Indigenous peoples by the authorities, saying it was unacceptable that many communities lacked healthcare and education because of geographical isolation and institutional neglect. He also spoke of a rise in sexual violence against Indigenous Awajun and Wampis schoolgirls in the northern Amazon province of Condorcanqui. The journey comes a month after surgery in which doctors removed the lower lobe of his right lung to treat cancer.

Key points

  • Pope Leo, in Pucallpa on Sunday, demanded justice for Indigenous leaders killed defending their territories in Peru's Amazon.
  • He urged Catholics to resist deforestation and pollution from gold mining and oil production, warning that exploitation threatens the balance of creation.
  • Thirty-five Indigenous leaders have been killed in Peru over the past 15 years, most of them this decade, and most killings go unpunished.
  • The speech came on the penultimate day of his 12-day South America tour, hours after Mass in Cusco at 3,400 metres above sea level.
