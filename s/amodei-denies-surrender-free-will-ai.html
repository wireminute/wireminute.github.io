Anthropic Chief Amodei Rejects Claim He Wants Humanity to Hand Free Will to AI

Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei has rejected suggestions that he believes humanity should hand its free will to artificial intelligence, calling the idea absurd in a long public post.

Amodei published the post on Wednesday, 25 November, in response to a report about a growing divide among the leaders of top artificial intelligence companies over how much authority superintelligent systems should be given. The report said some people inside Anthropic believed humanity would safely hand authority to such systems, and often should.

"No, I do not believe that we should 'surrender human free will' to AI systems, and it's absurd to suggest that I do," Amodei wrote. He said he had written hundreds of pages of essays with a constant theme: artificial intelligence must be governed by legitimate democratic processes, and humans must keep ultimate decision-making power.

He drew a distinction between automating specific tasks and handing over governance. Artificial intelligence taking charge of driving, developing cancer drugs or writing software could save lives and was desirable in many cases, he argued, but that was different from giving up free will in politics. "We should never sacrifice legitimacy or self-determination at the altar of efficiency or security," he wrote, adding that turning over governance to artificial intelligence was a bad idea.

He also set out a concrete limit: artificial intelligence systems improving themselves without human help, a process known as recursive self-improvement. "We don't expect it to be safe for Claude to build successive frontier models fully autonomously, without meaningful human supervision or understanding," he wrote, adding that limits on that process may be needed. He accused rival executives of "vibes-based" positioning around slogans such as "human control and agency" while approaching the AI race no differently, and pointed to his 2024 essay "Machines of Loving Grace" and his January 2026 essay "The Adolescence of Technology" as evidence of a consistent position.

Key points

  • Amodei called it absurd to suggest he believes humanity should surrender free will to AI systems.
  • He said artificial intelligence must be governed by legitimate democratic processes, with humans keeping ultimate decision-making power.
  • He drew a line between automating specific tasks, such as driving or developing cancer drugs, and handing over governance.
  • He said it would not be safe for Claude to build successive frontier models fully autonomously, and limits on self-improvement may be needed.
