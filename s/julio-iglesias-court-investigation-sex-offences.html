Spanish Court Opens Criminal Investigation Into Julio Iglesias Over Sex Offence Allegations

Singer Julio Iglesias faces a criminal investigation in Spain after two former employees accused him of sexual offences, assault and degrading treatment.

The ruling was handed down on Wednesday by Judge Antonio Pina of the Audiencia Nacional, Spain's High Court. The court said the complaint contained enough preliminary evidence to justify opening proceedings. It will examine claims of offences against sexual freedom, including sexual assault, along with assault and degrading treatment, said to have taken place after 5 October 2021.

The complaint came from the rights group Women's Link Worldwide, acting for two women, a domestic worker and a physiotherapist, who said they worked at the singer's homes in the Bahamas and the Dominican Republic for ten months in 2021. It followed reporting by the American broadcaster Univision and the Spanish outlet elDiario.es. An earlier complaint had been set aside after a court found Spain lacked jurisdiction over crimes said to have happened abroad, and the women then took the case directly to the High Court.

The judge turned down parts of the complaint covering human trafficking and workers' rights, and the court noted that some of the alleged conduct may now fall outside the time limit for prosecution. The two women have been called to give evidence in person on 3 February.

Iglesias, 83, has rejected the claims outright, calling them completely false in messages posted online. He is one of the best-selling recording artists in history, with record sales past 300 million across a career spanning six decades. Born in Madrid in 1943, Iglesias became one of Spain's most famous singers, with a career that has lasted more than five decades. No charges have been brought, and the investigation is at an early stage.

Key points

  • Spain's High Court has opened a criminal investigation into Julio Iglesias over alleged sexual offences, assault and degrading treatment.
  • Two former employees say the alleged abuse took place at his Caribbean homes in 2021; the complaint was filed through Women's Link Worldwide.
  • The judge rejected claims of human trafficking and workers' rights offences, and some alleged crimes may be time-barred.
  • Iglesias, 83, denies the accusations and calls them completely false; no charges have been brought.
