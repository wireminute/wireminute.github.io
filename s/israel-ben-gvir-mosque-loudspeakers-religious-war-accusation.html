Mosque Loudspeaker Seizures in Israel Draw 'Religious War' Accusation Against Ben-Gvir

Israel's national security minister has been accused of trying to start a religious war after police seized loudspeakers from two mosques in the south of the country.

On Sunday, officers took the speakers down at a mosque in Hura, a Bedouin town in the Negev, and carried away its sound equipment. Days earlier, officers had taken the sound system from the al-Dawa Mosque in the neighbouring town of Tel al-Saba. Both mosques use the speakers to broadcast the call to prayer.

The seizures were ordered by Itamar Ben-Gvir, the far-right minister who oversees the police. Ahmad Tibi, a parliamentarian from the Ta'al Party, said the minister was trying to 'ignite a religious war' and was 'exploiting the atmosphere of war'. Tibi also said the caretaker prime minister, Benjamin Netanyahu, was driving hostility towards Palestinian citizens of Israel.

Ben-Gvir stood by the seizures, arguing the volume had been excessive, and police said neighbours had complained repeatedly about the sound from both mosques. A resident of Tel al-Saba, Ibrahim al-Asam, disputed that account, saying the speaker there had not been loud at all and that officers damaged the mosque and took the recorder linked to its security cameras. Police said warnings and fines had come first at Tel al-Saba, and that officers in Hura had measured the volume and found it above the legal limit.

The two seizures fit a wider pattern. Earlier removals hit a mosque in Bir al-Hammam in April and one in Majd al-Krum, in the Galilee, in June, and several muezzins have been called in for questioning. The Palestinian monitoring group Mo'ta has logged at least 38 cases this year in which noise rules were used against mosques, imams and muezzins across Arab and mixed communities. The group also counted 50 mosques attacked in the occupied West Bank this year, 13 of them set on fire, and said the call to prayer at Hebron's Ibrahimi Mosque had been blocked 1,009 times since the start of the year. Israel's parliament has also given preliminary approval to a bill restricting mosque loudspeakers at certain times.

Key points

  • Police seized loudspeakers from mosques in Hura and Tel al-Saba in southern Israel, saying the call to prayer was too loud.
  • Parliamentarian Ahmad Tibi accused National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir of trying to 'ignite a religious war' and of exploiting the wartime atmosphere.
  • Ben-Gvir defended the seizures on noise grounds, while a Tel al-Saba resident said the speaker was not loud and officers damaged the mosque.
  • The seizures follow earlier loudspeaker removals and at least 38 noise-regulation cases against mosques this year, according to the monitoring group Mo'ta.
