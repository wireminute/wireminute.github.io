Turnout Falls as France Protests Enter Sixth Weekend, With Clashes in Marseille and Lyon

Tens of thousands of people marched across France on Saturday for a sixth straight weekend of protests over the cost of living and public services, but turnout fell sharply from a week earlier as about 70,000 police officers were deployed and sporadic clashes broke out in several cities.

The interior ministry said 82,500 people took part in demonstrations around the country, including 21,500 in Paris. That was down from 106,000 nationwide and 24,000 in the capital a week earlier. Organisers gave a higher count, about 150,000 across France and 45,000 in Paris, but that too was lower than the 200,000 they reported last Saturday and far below the 800,000 they estimated for the first mass protest on October 17.

The ministry said 144 people had been arrested by 6pm. About 70,000 police officers and gendarmes were deployed across the country, after a week in which the government faced criticism both for losing control of some cities and for heavy-handed tactics that left several teenagers seriously injured. The marches were largely peaceful, but clashes broke out on the sidelines of some protests. In Marseille, demonstrators set fire to vehicles and rubbish bins. In Lyon, where demonstrations in the city centre were banned, police used tear gas to disperse about 150 protesters who tried to reach the centre. Groups also tried to rally in banned areas in other cities, throwing projectiles as police fired tear gas.

The movement began in mid-September with high-school students protesting about deteriorating school conditions and teacher shortages. It has since grown into a wider campaign against the rising cost of living, underfunded public services and police violence. Saturday's marches also carried anger at a new law adopted on Friday that gives greater legal protection to police officers who use their weapons, a measure critics have called a licence to kill.

At least 31 people have been seriously injured by security forces since the protests began, according to a count based on victims' accounts, lawyers and non-governmental groups. Unions have called on private-sector workers to join a public-sector mobilisation planned for December 1, suggesting the campaign will continue. Saturday's protests also coincided with the eighth anniversary of the Yellow Vest movement, whose supporters joined some of the marches.

Key points

  • Turnout fell for a sixth straight Saturday of protests, with the interior ministry counting 82,500 people nationwide against 106,000 a week earlier.
  • Police arrested 144 people by 6pm as about 70,000 officers were deployed across France.
  • Marches were largely peaceful, but clashes flared in Marseille and Lyon, where police used tear gas.
  • The movement, begun by students in September, now also targets a new police-protection law adopted on Friday.
