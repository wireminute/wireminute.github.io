South Korea's Top Court Orders Retrial for Ex-PM Han Over Martial Law Role

South Korea's Supreme Court has overturned former prime minister Han Duck-soo's 15-year prison sentence over his role in the December 2024 martial law crisis and ordered a retrial, in the latest twist in the fallout from the six-hour decree.

South Korea's highest court has cancelled the 15-year jail term imposed on former prime minister Han Duck-soo for his part in the short-lived martial law decree of December 2024, ordering the case to be heard again by a lower court.

Han, 77, was first handed a 23-year term in January after a court found he had helped former president Yoon Suk Yeol impose martial law. An appeals court cut that sentence to 15 years in May. Both Han and the prosecution then took the case to the Supreme Court.

A majority of the justices found there was not enough evidence to sustain one of the insurrection findings against him: that he arranged a cabinet meeting before the decree to make it look as though Yoon's decision had gone through proper cabinet discussion. The court said that to hold a person liable for taking part in an insurrection, prosecutors must prove the person intended to overturn the constitutional order, and that bar had not been met for that part of the case.

But the court left standing the finding that Han's conduct after martial law was declared did show such intent, pointing to his efforts to collect ministers' signatures on related documents and discussions of plans to seal off parliament and cut power and water to some news outlets. The Seoul High Court will now retry the case under the Supreme Court's reading of the law.

Yoon declared martial law on 3 December 2024, saying it was needed to deal with what he described as anti-state forces and political deadlock. The decree lasted about six hours before lawmakers voted it down. Yoon was removed from office, convicted of insurrection in February and given a life sentence; he has appealed, with a ruling expected in January.

The special prosecution team said it respected Thursday's ruling. Han has denied wrongdoing on almost all counts, admitting only a single perjury charge, and has been in prison since his original sentence in January. In a separate decision on Thursday, the court upheld a four-year jail term for Kim Keon Hee, Yoon's wife.

Key points

  • The country's highest court threw out the 15-year jail term handed to former prime minister Han Duck-soo and sent the case back for a retrial.
  • Judges ruled there was too little evidence that arranging a cabinet meeting before the decree showed an intent to overturn the constitutional order.
  • The court kept the finding that Han's actions after martial law began, including seeking ministers' signatures, did show such intent.
  • Han, 77, has been in prison since January; Yoon was given a life sentence in February and his appeal ruling is due in January.
