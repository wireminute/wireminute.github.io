Tens of Thousands March Across Portugal in Biggest Housing Protest Yet

Portugal saw its largest housing demonstrations yet on Saturday, with crowds marching in more than 30 cities and towns to demand action on rents and home prices that many say have moved beyond their reach.

The marches were called by Casa Para Viver, a coalition of around 100 housing associations and community groups, marching under the banner "We won't let them steal our right to housing." Lisbon drew the biggest crowd, its column moving from Alameda Dom Afonso Henriques towards Martim Moniz to the sound of drums and chanting, with marchers of all ages. Porto, Faro, Portimao, Coimbra and Braga were among the other cities where people took to the streets.

The crowds want limits on how much rents can rise, stronger legal safeguards for tenants threatened with eviction, and a bigger supply of affordable homes. They are also pressing parliament to abandon a tenancy reform that would loosen the rules on rental contracts and make evictions quicker. The government's changes had already cleared parliament before Saturday's marches.

The marches cap weeks of street action over a housing squeeze that has pushed ownership and renting out of reach for many. Home prices climbed by almost a fifth between the first quarter of last year and the first quarter of this year, the national statistics office said last month, while rents have risen 68 percent since 2020. Portugal recorded the European Union's steepest rise in the ratio of house prices to household incomes between 2015 and 2025, up 61 percent, ahead of the Netherlands at 34 percent, according to the EU statistics office.

Some campaigners have been camping in main squares in Lisbon and Porto for weeks, and the movement has taken encouragement from similar demonstrations that began in neighbouring Spain last month. The president, Antonio Jose Seguro, called a meeting of his advisory Council of State to discuss the unrest earlier this month and sat down with some of the protesters on Friday.

Key points

  • Tens of thousands marched in more than 30 Portuguese cities and towns on Saturday in the largest housing protest so far.
  • Organised by the Casa Para Viver coalition, the marches demanded rent caps, stronger tenant protection and more affordable homes.
  • Home prices rose almost a fifth in the year to early 2026, and rents are up 68 percent since 2020.
  • Protesters also want parliament to drop a tenancy reform that would make evictions faster; the president met demonstrators on Friday.
