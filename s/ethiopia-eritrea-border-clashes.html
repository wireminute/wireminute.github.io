Ethiopian and Eritrean Forces Clash at Border as Fears of Full-Scale War Grow

Ethiopian and Eritrean forces have clashed along their disputed border, raising fears of a full-scale war between the Horn of Africa neighbours, conflict monitors and analysts say.

The conflict monitor ACLED says the two armies fought directly near the town of Badme on 13 November, warning the risk of a full-scale confrontation there is now high. Eritrean forces retook Badme around 15 November, ACLED said, after Ethiopian drones struck suspected Eritrean positions and Eritrean artillery fired back.

Both armies have massed troops along the frontier, within striking distance of each other, monitors say. Reports of the clashes have not been independently confirmed, and one tracker recorded no fighting at Badme since 15 November.

Badme was the epicentre of the two countries' 1998-2000 border war, which killed tens of thousands. Tensions have risen as Eritrea fears its landlocked neighbour plans to seize access to the sea, and fighting in Tigray since September has pushed the two sides closer to open conflict. Eritrea denies its troops crossed into Tigray, saying Ethiopia is looking for an excuse to invade.

Key points

  • ACLED says Ethiopian and Eritrean armies clashed directly near Badme on 13 November, and the risk of a full-scale confrontation on that front is now high.
  • Both armies have massed troops along the border within striking distance, but reports of the clashes have not been independently confirmed.
  • Badme was the epicentre of the 1998-2000 border war that killed tens of thousands, and tensions have risen as Eritrea fears Ethiopia plans to seize access to the sea.
