Two Soldiers Martyred in Drone Attack as Eight Militants Killed in Balochistan Raids

An army major and a soldier were martyred in Balochistan on Saturday when militants attacked a patrol with armed quadcopter drones, hours after security forces killed eight militants in overnight operations in the province, the military said.

The military's media wing said troops had been carrying out several intelligence-based operations since Friday around Kolpur, Kardagap and Nushki, including patrols and area domination along the Quetta-Taftan highway to keep the road secure for traffic. The statement said the patrols were meant to keep the highway secure for local traffic while the operations continued. In the night between Friday and Saturday, troops killed eight militants in the Kolpur area, among them a suicide bomber. Troops also seized a large stockpile of weapons and ammunition.

The military described the dead as members of a group it calls Fitna al Hindustan, which it says is backed by India. Later on Saturday, militants struck military vehicles on patrol with weaponised quadcopter drones, killing Major Omer Minhas Nasir, from Chakwal district, and Havildar Muhammad Adnan Khan, from Kotli Sattian.

The prime minister praised the troops behind the raids and honoured the two soldiers who died. The military said clearance operations were continuing in the area and that the campaign against militant networks in the province would go on.

The fighting came on the same day the military said two soldiers were martyred and 16 militants killed in operations in North Waziristan, and a day after it said 74 militants had been killed in operations across Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Balochistan. The military has said 31 militants have been killed in Balochistan since an operation there began on 3 November. Attacks have risen in both provinces since the banned Tehreek-i-Taliban Pakistan ended a ceasefire in November 2022, and monitoring groups say the number of attacks has grown every year since.

Key points
