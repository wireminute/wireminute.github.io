Iraq Denies Troop Move Into Kurdistan as Court Backs Baghdad's Control of Borders

Iraq's government has denied reports that federal troops are moving into the semi-autonomous Kurdistan region, after a court ruled Baghdad can deploy forces to secure the country's international borders, including in Kurdish-administered territory.

Government spokesman Haider al-Aboudi said on Wednesday night that there was no movement of army units towards the Kurdistan region, and that Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi had stressed protecting the people of Kurdistan rather than threatening them. The denial followed days of tension after al-Zaidi ordered federal authorities on Tuesday to take control of border crossings run by the Kurdistan Regional Government and to stop the region approving crude oil exports except through the federal Oil Ministry.

The Federal Supreme Court ruled on Wednesday that protecting Iraq's international borders falls within the federal government's exclusive constitutional powers, including in areas administered by the Kurdistan region, and that Baghdad can deploy federal troops to secure them. The ruling also affirmed federal authority over border security, customs, fiscal policy and oversight of oil and gas operations.

At the same time, the defence ministry said army units had been ordered to deploy across all provinces, including Kirkuk, to secure areas and close routes used for oil smuggling. The ministry's spokesman said the armed forces were fully prepared to carry out the prime minister's orders. The ministry separately denied reports of an army withdrawal from posts between Tuz Khurmatu and Kirkuk, describing the movement as a redeployment and rotation of units.

The four presidencies, the president, prime minister, parliament speaker and head of the Supreme Judicial Council, met at Baghdad Palace on Wednesday and approved a national dialogue on 1 December aimed at resolving the dispute through constitutional means. The Kurdistan Regional Government's prime minister, Masrour Barzani, called Baghdad's decisions unacceptable but said the door remained open to dialogue, while demonstrations were planned across the Kurdistan region. The dispute centres on oil smuggling routes from Kirkuk and Sulaimaniyah, which Baghdad says move crude to Iran and onward to Afghanistan.

Key points

  • Iraq's government denied reports that army units are moving toward the Kurdistan region, saying the prime minister wants to protect Kurdish people, not threaten them.
  • The Federal Supreme Court ruled Baghdad can deploy federal troops to secure international borders, including in Kurdish-administered areas, and affirmed federal control over border crossings, customs and oil oversight.
  • The defence ministry said troops are deploying across all provinces, including Kirkuk, to close oil-smuggling routes, and denied any withdrawal from posts between Tuz Khurmatu and Kirkuk.
  • Iraq's four presidencies agreed a national dialogue on 1 December to settle the dispute; the Kurdish regional prime minister called Baghdad's orders unacceptable but left the door open to talks.
