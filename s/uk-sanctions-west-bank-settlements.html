Britain Sanctions Eight Israeli Settlement Firms and Gaza Aid Blocking Group

Britain has imposed sanctions on eight Israeli construction and property companies involved in building settlements in the occupied West Bank, along with the activist group Tzav 9, which blocks humanitarian aid from reaching Gaza.

The British government announced the measures on Tuesday, saying the eight firms had helped plan, build, supply or market settlement housing and public infrastructure in the occupied West Bank. The sanctions freeze any assets the firms hold in Britain and bar their directors from running companies. Tzav 9 faces the same asset freeze for organising blockades of crossings, ports and roads used by aid convoys heading into Gaza, including as recently as October.

The eight companies named are Har Hevron Development Company, Hemdat Shomron, S.A.G. (Velvel) Construction and Development, Lev HaHar Yazamut, Harey Zahav, Ariel Residential Judea and Samaria, Ami Gvirtzman Architects 2021 and Z.F. Building Company. The government said the action would disrupt their access to finance and their ability to support further building.

The announcement was timed just before final bidding deadlines for the E1 settlement project, a plan to build on land between East Jerusalem and the settlement of Maale Adumim. Britain warned the project would cut East Jerusalem off from the rest of the West Bank and undermine the viability of a future Palestinian state. Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband said protecting the two-state solution was the guiding principle of the government's approach, and told firms: "do not bid, do not build, and do not profit from the destruction of the two-state solution."

The measures were imposed under Britain's expanded Global Human Rights sanctions regime, after legislation introduced in October allowed sanctions against those involved in serious violations of international humanitarian law anywhere in the world. The move follows a pledge Miliband made in September to respond to settlement expansion, and comes as settler violence against Palestinians in the West Bank has risen sharply.

Key points

  • Britain sanctioned eight Israeli construction and property firms over West Bank settlement building, plus the activist group Tzav 9.
  • The measures freeze assets and disqualify company directors, aiming to cut off finance for further construction.
  • Tzav 9 was sanctioned for blocking crossings, ports and roads used by aid convoys bound for Gaza, as recently as October.
  • The package was announced just before final bidding deadlines for the E1 settlement project, which Britain says would separate East Jerusalem from the West Bank.
