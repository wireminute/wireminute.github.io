Russian Bombers Land in Vietnam, Then Fly Back Past Taiwan and Japan

Two Russian strategic bombers spent two days on the ground in Vietnam this week before flying back north past Taiwan and Japan, in a rare long-range deployment that set off fighter scrambles in three countries.

The bombers, identified as Tu-95MS aircraft, landed at Da Nang in central Vietnam on Tuesday alongside two military transport planes, and stayed on the tarmac there for two days. Defence analysts described it as the first time Russian long-range bombers had deployed to Vietnam since 1993, and the most significant such movement across East Asia in more than a decade.

The aircraft left Da Nang early on Thursday and flew back north. Taiwan's defence ministry said two of the bombers passed the island's east coast and entered its air defence identification zone, the second such sighting in a week, and published a photograph taken from one of its own fighter jets. The registration number on the bomber's tail matched an aircraft photographed in Da Nang earlier in the week.

Japan's defence ministry said the same two bombers flew between its westernmost island, Yonaguni, and Taiwan, then met two patrol aircraft over the East China Sea before crossing into the Sea of Japan. Japan scrambled fighters in response, two days after it protested a brief entry by suspected Russian aircraft into its airspace near Okinoshima.

The original southbound flight on Tuesday had already alarmed the region. Japan said two aircraft spent about three minutes each inside its airspace near Okinoshima and lodged a formal protest, calling the entry completely unacceptable. South Korea said Russian aircraft entered its air defence zone over the East Sea and sent up fighters, while Taiwan said it detected two Russian military aircraft and responded. It was the first confirmed Russian entry into Japanese airspace in more than two years.

The flights unfolded as Russia's president attended an Asia-Pacific summit in China, where he met the American and Chinese leaders. Neither Moscow nor Hanoi has publicly explained the deployment, and analysts say the route showed the bombers could approach both Japan and Taiwan in a single long-distance mission.

Key points

  • Two Tu-95MS bombers and two transport planes spent two days at Da Nang in Vietnam, the first Russian long-range bomber deployment there since 1993, analysts say.
  • The bombers flew back north on Thursday past Taiwan, which scrambled a fighter jet and photographed one of them; the tail number matched an aircraft seen in Da Nang.
  • Japan scrambled fighters as the bombers passed between Yonaguni and Taiwan and met patrol aircraft over the East China Sea, two days after protesting a brief airspace entry near Okinoshima.
  • Neither Moscow nor Hanoi has publicly explained the deployment, which came as Russia's president attended the Asia-Pacific summit in China.
