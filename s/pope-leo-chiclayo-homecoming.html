A Million People Line the Streets as Pope Leo Comes Home to Chiclayo

About 1.5 million people turned out in Chiclayo on Friday as Pope Leo XIV returned to the Peruvian city where he once served as bishop, exactly one month after surgery for lung cancer.

The pope flew in from Lima on Friday morning and was driven through streets packed with well-wishers to an open field outside the city, the Pampas de Pimentel, where he celebrated Mass. The Vatican estimated that about a million people lined his route and that 500,000 attended the service itself, waving yellow and white flags and chanting that the pope was one of their own. He toured the crowd in an open popemobile under a fierce sun, with temperatures around 31C.

Opening his homily, Leo said what a joy it was to be back in Chiclayo, and told the congregation he thanked God that he was alive to see them again. The visit came exactly a month after an operation on October 13 in which surgeons removed the lower lobe of his right lung to treat lung cancer. For a time, there were fears the illness would force him to call off the journey altogether.

Few popes have ever arrived somewhere as a returning local. Born in Chicago as Robert Prevost, Leo first came to Peru as a missionary in 1985 and spent about two decades in the country before leading the Chiclayo diocese from 2015 to 2023. People there still call him Padre Roberto, and on the night of his election in May 2025 he greeted the city in Spanish from the balcony of St Peter's Basilica. He holds Peruvian citizenship and is to receive a new national identity card giving Chiclayo as his address.

Peru is the last stop on a 12-day tour of three South American countries, after Argentina and Uruguay, and the turnouts have outstripped every other stop of his papacy. The Vatican puts total attendance at public events on the trip at around 12 million people, including about three million who turned out in Lima on Wednesday and 60,000 young Peruvians who sang for him at a stadium in the capital on Thursday night.

He remains in the region through the weekend, with a programme of eight engagements that includes dining privately with clergy who served under him when he was their bishop. After the Mass he was due to make a private visit to the Chapel of St Oscar Romero, before the tour comes to an end.

Key points

  • About 1.5 million people welcomed Leo to Chiclayo — a million along his motorcade route and 500,000 at the outdoor Mass.
  • The visit came exactly a month after surgery for lung cancer, which had put the trip in doubt.
  • Leo led the Chiclayo diocese for eight years, until 2023, and is still known there as Padre Roberto.
  • Peru closes a 12-day South America tour whose public events have drawn roughly 12 million people in total.
