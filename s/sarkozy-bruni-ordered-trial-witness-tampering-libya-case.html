Sarkozy and Wife Carla Bruni Ordered to Stand Trial Over Witness Tampering

France's former president Nicolas Sarkozy and his wife, Carla Bruni, have been ordered to stand trial over alleged witness tampering in the long-running case over funding for his 2007 election campaign.

Investigating judges ordered 11 people to stand trial on Tuesday after five years of investigation, the French financial prosecutor's office said. A pre-trial hearing is set for 1 April 2027, with the trial itself due to run from November to December 2027. The couple face charges including criminal conspiracy to commit organised fraud and receiving stolen goods linked to witness tampering. They deny any wrongdoing.

The case turns on Ziad Takieddine, a French-Lebanese businessman who said he held written proof that Sarkozy's 2007 campaign was financed with money from Libya, including payments he put at 50 million euros. Takieddine later retracted the claim in an interview with a French magazine. Prosecutors allege the couple enlisted the help of Mimi Marchand, a celebrity publicist known as the paparazzi queen, to pay Takieddine to change his account.

Bruni is suspected of having asked for a secret phone line in late 2019 to communicate with Marchand about the operation, according to the investigating judges. Marchand faces charges including witness tampering and criminal conspiracy. The defendants face up to 10 years in prison and a fine of 150,000 euros if convicted.

The order is a fresh blow for Sarkozy, 71, who led France from 2007 to 2012. In September he was given a five-year sentence in the Libya funding case itself, becoming the first former French president to go to jail, and was freed after 20 days while he appeals. The appeal court's ruling is expected on Monday, 30 November.

Sarkozy has said he knew nothing of the effort to secure the retraction and would never have had anything to do with those involved. Bruni told judges she was a poor organiser and could not have taken part, according to a transcript of her evidence.

Key points

  • Nicolas Sarkozy and Carla Bruni will stand trial in 2027 over alleged witness tampering in the Libya campaign-funding case; 11 defendants were ordered to trial.
  • The case centres on Ziad Takieddine, who claimed Sarkozy's 2007 campaign was funded by Libya, then retracted the claim.
  • Prosecutors allege the couple enlisted publicist Mimi Marchand to pay Takieddine to change his account; Bruni is suspected of using a secret phone line to contact her.
  • The defendants face up to 10 years in prison and a 150,000 euro fine; Sarkozy was jailed for 20 days in September in the Libya funding case and is appealing.
