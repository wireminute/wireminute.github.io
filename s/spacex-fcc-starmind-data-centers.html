SpaceX Wins FCC Approval to Deploy One Million Data-Center Satellites in Orbit

The United States has authorised SpaceX to deploy up to one million satellites that will work as data centres in orbit, the first approval of its kind in the country's history, as demand for artificial intelligence computing keeps surging.

The Federal Communications Commission announced the decision on Wednesday, clearing the way for the company's planned Starmind constellation of orbital compute modules. The satellites would process vast amounts of data in space and send the results back to Earth, rather than running the work in ground-based data centres.

Commission chairman Brendan Carr said the decision would help the United States stay ahead in artificial intelligence, with the satellites drawing on solar power in orbit. The commission described the move as the first time it had ever authorised a constellation of data centres in space.

SpaceX applied for permission earlier this year. Its chief financial officer, Bret Johnsen, said last week that the first orbital data centres could launch in 2027, with the network expanding through 2029. The authorisation sets deadlines: the first satellite must be operating by 2033, and the full constellation must be in place by 2040. The order also sets interim targets, with a tenth of the network due by 2035 and half by 2038.

The scale is without precedent. Only about 17,000 satellites are in orbit today, and it is far from certain that SpaceX will ever launch the full million. The approval came despite objections from Amazon, satellite operator Viasat and the American Astronomical Society, which has warned that vast numbers of bright satellites could spoil the night sky. The commission also authorised radio links for backup and emergency control of the satellites, and said it was still reviewing other companies' proposals for orbital data centres.

The satellites are designed to run on solar power and connect through high-speed laser links. Moving computing into orbit would let the systems draw on constant sunlight and radiate heat into space, avoiding the land, water and power demands of giant data centres on the ground, though the technology to build and operate them at that scale does not yet exist.

Key points

  • The FCC has cleared SpaceX to build its Starmind network of as many as one million computing satellites in orbit, the first American approval for data centres in space.
  • The satellites would process data for artificial intelligence in orbit and send results back to Earth, running on solar power and laser links.
  • SpaceX aims to launch its first orbital data centres in 2027; the FCC requires the first satellite operating by 2033 and the full constellation by 2040.
  • Approval came despite objections from Amazon, Viasat and astronomers, and only about 17,000 satellites are in orbit today, so the full million is far from certain.
