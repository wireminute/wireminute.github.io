India Finds Bisleri Water Sample Unsafe, Widens Checks to All Bottled Water Brands

India's food safety regulator has found a sample of Bisleri bottled water unsafe and will now test samples from every bottled water company in the country, in a setback for India's best-known water brand.

The Food Safety and Standards Authority of India said a sample collected from Bisleri's premises failed prescribed limits on bromate, a chemical that can form during water disinfection. The sample, taken from the company's Goa facility last year, showed bromate at 12 micrograms per litre against a limit of 10. After Bisleri appealed, the regulator tested the sample again and still found it unsafe, saying it had also detected polynuclear aromatic hydrocarbons, known as PAHs.

The regulator said it would start legal proceedings against Bisleri and test more of the company's samples from across the country, as well as samples from other brands. A second official said the batches linked to the unsafe sample had been recalled and later testing found no problems, though the company told the regulator in February that no stock from those batches remained in the market to recall, suggesting it had already been sold or consumed.

Bisleri disputes the finding. The company said independent testing it arranged showed bromate within the prescribed limits and that its products are safe to drink. It said the scrutiny dated back to September 2025.

Bisleri, introduced in India in 1965, is the country's biggest bottled water brand, a household name in a country where poor tap water quality makes packaged water a daily necessity. It competes with PepsiCo's Aquafina and Coca-Cola's Kinley in a packaged drinking water market valued at $3.6 billion in 2025, and sells hundreds of millions of bottles and cans a year. The action comes as the regulator steps up enforcement against big brands, including legal action against Nestle in September over a baby food sample it found substandard.

Key points

  • India's food safety regulator found a Bisleri sample unsafe: bromate at 12 micrograms per litre against a limit of 10, and later tests also detected PAHs.
  • The regulator will start legal proceedings against Bisleri and test samples from all bottled water companies.
  • Bisleri disputes the finding, saying its own independent testing showed bromate within limits and its water is safe to drink.
  • Bisleri is India's biggest bottled water brand in a $3.6 billion market; the regulator has stepped up enforcement against major brands this year.
