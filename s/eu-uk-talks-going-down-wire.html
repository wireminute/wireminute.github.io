Youth Mobility Scheme for 200,000 a Year Expected as UK-EU Summit Nears

Britain and the European Union are expected to approve a youth mobility scheme for up to 200,000 young people a year at Friday's summit in Brussels, after negotiators largely settled a reset package that also covers food trade and carbon emissions.

The scheme is expected to be approved at the summit, with up to 100,000 young Europeans allowed to live, work and study in Britain each year and the same number of young Britons able to do the same in Europe. A leaked draft shows agreement has largely been reached on the package, after talks led by Prime Minister Andy Burnham.

The cap on numbers was the hardest point. Earlier reporting put the figure at 80,000 to 100,000 in each direction, with young Britons limited to one named country and the EU dividing its places among member states by population. Britain is reported to have made concessions on mobility, while the EU dropped last-minute demands for home tuition fees for its students at British universities.

The summit is also expected to produce deals on food and farming, on trading emissions permits, and on pet passports. Downing Street said the talks had gone down to the wire, and that if a deal is secured the government will bring legislation to Parliament next week. Burnham has defended the reset in Parliament, telling Brexit-backing MPs that the current deal had done more harm than good.

Beyond Friday, Brussels has signalled it will not open talks on a longer-term relationship until London decides whether it wants to reverse Brexit, warning that one-off reset summits have run their course. Burnham is expected to set out his next steps on Monday, but the full range of options, from the status quo to customs union, single market or full rejoin, will not appear until his ten-year plan in December.

Key points

  • The youth mobility scheme is expected to be approved at Friday's Brussels summit, for up to 100,000 young people a year in each direction.
  • A leaked draft shows agreement has largely been reached; Britain conceded on mobility and the EU dropped demands on home tuition fees for its students.
  • Deals on food and farming, emissions trading and pet passports are also expected at the summit.
  • Downing Street says legislation will follow next week if a deal is reached; long-term talks depend on London deciding on reversing Brexit.
