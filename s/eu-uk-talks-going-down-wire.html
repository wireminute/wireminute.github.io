UK-EU Talks Go Down to the Wire Before Friday Summit

Talks between Britain and the European Union are going down to the wire ahead of a summit in Brussels on Friday, with negotiators still racing to close a deal on food, energy and a youth mobility scheme.

Downing Street said the talks were going down to the wire, and that if a deal is secured at Friday's summit the government will bring legislation to Parliament next week. The prime minister's press secretary said the current Brexit deal had done more harm than good and that there was broad agreement in the country that change was needed.

The summit is expected to produce agreements on food and farming, on trading emissions permits, and on a scheme letting people under 30 live, work and study on either side of the Channel, with reports suggesting it could start as early as 2028.

The hardest point is the youth mobility scheme. The two sides have yet to agree a cap on numbers, and control over how many people each country admits sits with individual member states rather than the Commission in Brussels. London is wary of signing a deal with the Commission on an issue that belongs to member states.

A second sticking point is manufacturing. The EU wants Britain to commit to aligning with its trade defence measures, including tariffs on Chinese electric vehicles, in exchange for including British-made goods in its Made in Europe legislation. A Cabinet Office source called that language the highest jeopardy in the talks, while an EU official said it was the part most likely to run to the last minute.

Beyond Friday, Brussels has signalled it will not open talks on a longer-term relationship until London decides whether it wants to reverse Brexit, warning that one-off reset summits have run their course.

Burnham is expected to set out his next steps on Monday, but the full range of options, from the status quo to customs union, single market or full rejoin, will not appear until his ten-year plan in December.

Key points

  • Talks are going down to the wire before Friday's Brussels summit; Downing Street says legislation will follow next week if a deal is reached.
  • Expected deals cover food and farming, emissions trading and a youth mobility scheme for under-30s.
  • The youth mobility cap is the main deadlock, with member states controlling admission numbers.
  • The EU wants UK alignment on trade defence measures, including tariffs on Chinese electric vehicles, for Made in Europe access; long-term talks depend on London deciding on reversing Brexit.
