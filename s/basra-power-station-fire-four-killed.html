Iraq PM Orders Probe Into Basra Power Station Blast as Death Toll Disputed

Iraq's prime minister has ordered an urgent investigation into the gas pipeline explosion that set fire to the Hartha power station near Basra on Saturday, as officials and local reports gave conflicting accounts of how many people were killed.

The blast went off in the early hours of Saturday in a gas pipeline feeding the station, about 20 kilometres north of Basra, and the fire that followed spread to houses and cars nearby. Firefighters brought the blaze under control, and the plant, which had been running at about 400 of its 800 megawatts, was forced to stop operating. The station is one of the main sources of power in southern Iraq.

Officials put the death toll at four, with 35 people injured, and said the toll could still rise. But a local source said eight people had died, including children, and more than 30 were hurt. The provincial health directorate first reported two dead and 28 injured, and the differing figures could not immediately be reconciled.

Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi ordered an urgent investigation into what caused the blast and who was to blame, his office said, and told the health ministry and local authorities to arrange care for the injured and support for the families of those killed and hurt. The plant's director said later on Saturday that the station was ready to resume operations, and authorities have also ordered a survey of homes near oil pipelines in the area.

Iraq's interior ministry said the fire started in the gas system feeding the plant, but the exact cause of the explosion itself was not immediately clear. Claims shared on social media that the plant had been attacked by a drone were not confirmed by any official. Iraq's power network has long struggled with underinvestment and poor maintenance, and fires and faults at power facilities have disrupted supplies before.
