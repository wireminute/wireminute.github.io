Bay of Bengal Depression Set to Become Cyclone Akvan as Andhra Coast Goes on Alert

A depression over the Bay of Bengal is strengthening into a cyclone likely to be named Akvan, with India's east coast on alert for heavy rain and winds of up to 145 kilometres per hour as the system heads towards Andhra Pradesh.

The system was centred about 800 kilometres east-southeast of Chennai on Sunday morning, moving north-northwest at around 15 kilometres per hour with winds of 45 to 55 kilometres per hour, gusting to 65. Forecasters expect it to become a cyclonic storm by Monday morning, taking the name Akvan, then a severe cyclonic storm on Tuesday and a very severe one by Thursday morning, with sustained winds of 120 to 130 kilometres per hour.

A cyclone watch is in effect along the Andhra Pradesh and south Odisha coasts, with heavy to very heavy rain expected along coastal Andhra Pradesh on November 18 and 19, and heavy rain also forecast in Tamil Nadu, Odisha and West Bengal. The exact landfall location has not yet been fixed, and fishermen have been told to stay ashore. It would be the region's third cyclone of the season, after two earlier storms.

Key points

  • The Bay of Bengal depression is expected to become Cyclone Akvan on Monday and a severe storm by Tuesday.
  • Winds could reach 120-130 km/h, gusting to 145 km/h, by Thursday morning, with the system still at sea.
  • A cyclone watch covers the Andhra Pradesh and south Odisha coasts; heavy rain is forecast from November 18, and landfall is not yet fixed.
