Anwar Under Pressure as Malaysian Coalition Suffers Fourth Straight State Poll Defeat

Malaysian Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim is facing growing pressure over his political future after his coalition lost a fourth straight state election, this time in Melaka, where its rivals won 24 of the 28 seats.

In Saturday's vote in Melaka, a small state south of Kuala Lumpur, Anwar's Pakatan Harapan coalition won just four seats, one fewer than in 2021. The Barisan Nasional coalition, its partner in the federal government, won 21 seats, and its ally Perikatan Nasional took the remaining three, giving the two a two-thirds majority in the 28-seat assembly. Barisan Nasional declared victory on Saturday night, and the Election Commission confirmed it had won enough seats to form the state government. The incumbent chief minister, Ab Rauf Yusoh, was put forward for a second term.

It was the coalition's third defeat in a state election this year, after losses in Johor in July and Negeri Sembilan in August, and its fourth in a row counting Sabah late last year. In Negeri Sembilan, the coalition lost control of the state; in Johor, it fell to eight seats from 12. In Melaka, its candidate for chief minister, Adly Zahari, lost his own seat to a candidate from Perikatan Nasional. More than 70 percent of the state's nearly 688,000 registered voters cast ballots.

Analysts say the result sharpens the questions around Anwar, who has led a unity government since November 2022 and must call a national election by February 2028. One Malaysia analyst called the Melaka outcome a heavy defeat for the prime minister's party and warned that moves to remove him would gain strength. The deputy prime minister, Ahmad Zahid Hamidi, who leads Barisan Nasional, had already demanded in September that Anwar call an early national vote, and the Melaka result is expected to strengthen that call. The coalition conceded the result, with its Melaka chairman saying it accepted the voters' decision and would work to do better.

The Melaka vote was also the first time in decades that Barisan Nasional's linchpin party, Umno, fought an election in a formal pact with Perikatan Nasional's main party, the Pan-Malaysian Islamic Party. The two sides cooperated in Negeri Sembilan as well, and the alliance's strength among Malay voters has become the central challenge for Anwar's multiracial coalition as the national election approaches. His coalition's remaining seats in Melaka all came through its largest party, the Democratic Action Party.

Key points

  • Anwar Ibrahim's Pakatan Harapan coalition won just four of 28 seats in Melaka, its third state election defeat this year and fourth in a row.
  • Barisan Nasional won 21 seats and its ally Perikatan Nasional three, giving them a two-thirds majority; the incumbent chief minister was put forward for a second term.
  • Analysts say the result raises fresh questions about Anwar's future, and his deputy prime minister has already demanded an early national election, due by February 2028.
  • The vote marked the first formal electoral pact in decades between Umno and the Pan-Malaysian Islamic Party, consolidating support among Malay voters.
