Eisenkot Invites Netanyahu's Likud to Marathon Coalition Talks in Bid to Form Broad Government

Gadi Eisenkot, the former military chief tasked with forming Israel's next government, has invited Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud party to marathon coalition talks starting on Sunday, in the clearest sign yet that he is trying to build a broad government that leaves Netanyahu out of the top job.

Eisenkot's centrist Yashar party said its negotiating team had been told to invite its Likud counterparts to a series of talks, with the first meeting set for Sunday. The invitation matters because Eisenkot has repeatedly ruled out serving in a government headed by Netanyahu, so any deal would bring Likud into government without its leader staying on as prime minister.

The October 27 election left no bloc with the 61 seats needed for a majority in the 120-seat Knesset. Netanyahu's right-wing bloc won 53 seats, the opposition bloc led by Eisenkot won 48, and Arab-majority parties won 19. Likud finished as the largest single party with 27 seats, ahead of Yashar on 21. President Isaac Herzog gave Eisenkot the first mandate to form a government on November 8, after 67 lawmakers recommended him against 46 for Netanyahu. Under the law he has 28 days, with a possible 14-day extension, putting the first deadline in early December.

Eisenkot has said he will not let the negotiations drag on, and his party is preparing to put a minority government to a vote in the Knesset before December 6 if the coalition talks fail. Naftali Bennett, whose B'Yachad party holds 12 seats, confirmed on Saturday that talks with Likud were under way and said Netanyahu would not return as prime minister, while pressing for a broad unity government.

Netanyahu, who remains caretaker prime minister, has urged the rival camps to form a unity government and warned that Israel stands at a historic crossroads. Any government Eisenkot proposes must still win the confidence of the Knesset before it can take office, and his own camp remains divided over how to reach the 61 votes, including whether to rely on support from Arab parties from outside the coalition.

Key points

  • Eisenkot has invited Netanyahu's Likud party to marathon coalition talks starting on Sunday, seeking a broad government.
  • The October 27 election left no bloc with a majority: Netanyahu's bloc holds 53 seats, Eisenkot's 48, and Arab parties 19.
  • Eisenkot holds the mandate to form a government, backed by 67 lawmakers against 46 for Netanyahu, with a first deadline in early December.
  • If coalition talks fail, his Yashar party plans a Knesset vote on a minority government before December 6.
