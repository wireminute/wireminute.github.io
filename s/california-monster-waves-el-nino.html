Record Surf Pounds California Coast as El Nino Swell Floods Beaches and Closes Piers

Giant waves fuelled by a strengthening El Nino have battered California's coast for a fourth straight day, flooding streets, damaging piers and closing beaches, with forecasters warning another round of heavy surf is on the way.

Waves above 50 feet were recorded in San Mateo County on Saturday, drawing surfers to the famous Mavericks break near Half Moon Bay even as the surf closed beaches and piers up and down the coast. A buoy off Monterey recorded a wave of 34.1 feet on Friday night, the highest reading there in 30 years, beating the previous record of 32.8 feet set in 2008, according to AccuWeather. A buoy near Point Reyes measured 31.2 feet, its highest since 2015. The waves were so powerful that seismographs in the Bay Area registered the vibrations in the earth.

The damage spread the length of the state. Ventura's historic pier took significant damage and is closed until further notice, the city said, after waves of 10 to 14 feet reached the top of the structure. In Pacifica, surf lifted, buckled and cracked concrete panels on the Beach Boulevard seawall near Salada Avenue, pushing water and sand into streets in West Sharp Park. Water surged across West Cliff Drive in Santa Cruz, flooded beachfront homes in Seal Beach, and piers in Hermosa, Manhattan, Huntington and Pacific Beach were closed. Officials issued evacuation warnings for low-lying areas around Capitola and Aptos in Santa Cruz County.

The National Weather Service had a high surf warning in effect from Friday evening to early Sunday for breaking waves of 25 to 35 feet, with occasional sets of 40 to 50 feet at favoured breaks, and a high surf advisory followed for the San Francisco, Marin, San Mateo and Monterey Bay coasts until early Tuesday. Forecasters said a series of storms would bring strong winds, rain and hazardous seas to the West Coast this week, with another round of larger surf expected late in the week. The weather service also reported at least two rare waterspouts over San Francisco Bay, the first recorded inside the bay in 28 years of records, with no injuries reported.

Meteorologists blamed a succession of powerful north-west swells arriving during a strengthening El Nino, which has warmed the Pacific and raised sea levels along the coast, letting storm surf push farther inland at high tide. The National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration says El Nino continues to strengthen. Forecasters also warned of unusually high king tides from November 22 to 27, which could add to the flooding risk along low-lying stretches of coast.

Key points

  • Waves above 50 feet hit San Mateo County on Saturday; the Monterey buoy recorded 34.1 feet, its highest in 30 years, and the swell registered on Bay Area seismographs.
  • Ventura Pier was badly damaged and closed, Pacifica's seawall cracked and buckled, and piers and beaches closed along the coast as streets flooded.
  • Evacuation warnings were issued for low-lying areas around Capitola and Aptos, and the weather service warned of dangerous rip currents and sneaker waves.
  • Forecasters expect another round of heavy surf late in the week, with more storms and king tides from November 22 to 27.
