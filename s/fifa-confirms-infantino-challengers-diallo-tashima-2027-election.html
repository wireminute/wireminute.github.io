Infantino to Face First Contested FIFA Election as Diallo and Tashima Enter 2027 Race

World football's governing body has confirmed that Gianni Infantino will face two challengers in next year's presidential election, the first contested vote of his decade in charge, after Philippe Diallo of France and Kozo Tashima of Japan filed their nominations ahead of the deadline.

The governing body said on Thursday that it had received three candidatures for the election on 18 March 2027 at its congress in Rabat, Morocco: Infantino, Diallo, the president of the French Football Federation, and Tashima, the honorary president of the Japan Football Association. The nominations were submitted before the midnight deadline on Wednesday in Zurich, with each candidate backed by letters of support from at least five member associations.

The candidatures have been passed to the electoral committee, which will examine them and decide who is admitted to the ballot. Member associations will be told which candidates have been approved no later than 18 February 2027, and the vote itself will be cast by the body's 211 member associations.

Infantino, 56, has led the organisation since 2016 and is seeking a fourth term. He was returned unopposed in 2019 and again in 2023, so the vote in March will be the first time he has had to fight for the post. Diallo, 63, announced his candidacy on Monday with the unanimous backing of the European associations on UEFA's executive committee and the FIFA Council, and the French federation ratified his bid two days later. The French federation had withdrawn its support for Infantino in September.

Tashima, a former Japan international who has sat on the FIFA Council since 2015 and led the Japanese federation from 2016, declared his bid earlier this month and formally submitted his nomination on Wednesday. He is the first Japanese candidate for the presidency and has put his chances of winning at between 20 and 30 percent. A fourth hopeful, former Switzerland defender Ramon Vega, did not make the confirmed list.

The contest follows a year in which Infantino's leadership has come under unusual strain, including opposition over a plan, since dropped, to sell off shares in future World Cups to private backers. Diallo's emergence as a single European-backed candidate gives that opposition a focal point, while Tashima offers an alternative from Asia.

Key points

  • FIFA confirmed three candidates for the presidential election on 18 March 2027 in Rabat: Infantino, Diallo and Tashima.
  • Each candidate filed before the 18 November deadline with backing from at least five member associations; the electoral committee will confirm the final ballot by 18 February 2027.
  • Infantino, in office since 2016, was re-elected unopposed in 2019 and 2023, so this is his first contested election.
  • Diallo, 63, has the unanimous backing of Europe's associations; Tashima is the first Japanese candidate for the presidency.
