Alcaraz Beats Zverev to Win First ATP Finals Title in Turin

Carlos Alcaraz beat world number one Alexander Zverev 6-2, 7-6(7) in Turin on Sunday to win the ATP Finals for the first time, ending a season in which he also became the youngest man to complete the career Grand Slam.

Alcaraz, 23, took the first set in commanding fashion and then held his nerve in a tense second set, saving three set points in the tie-break before closing it out 9-7. The match lasted two hours and one minute in front of a crowd of more than 12,000 at the Inalpi Arena.

The Spaniard struck 28 winners to Zverev's 12 and made only eight unforced errors to his opponent's 18. Zverev, who had not lost his serve in four matches in Turin before the final, threw his racket in frustration after being beaten by a tweener in the second set and argued with the umpire over a time violation.

The title is Alcaraz's 30th at tour level and his sixth of 2026. He finished the season unbeaten indoors, winning in Vienna and Paris before Turin, and he went one better than last year, when he lost the final in Turin to Jannik Sinner. He is the third Spaniard to win the season-ending event.

The win also puts Alcaraz alongside Andre Agassi, Roger Federer and Novak Djokovic as the only men to have won all four Grand Slam titles and the ATP Finals. He completed the career Grand Slam by winning the Australian Open in January, the youngest man ever to do so. Zverev, meanwhile, stays as the year-end world number one for the first time despite the defeat, but was denied a third Finals title and a 70th win of the season.

Zverev was generous in defeat, calling Alcaraz an absolute legend at only 23 years old. Alcaraz said the title meant the world to him after a season that began with his Australian Open triumph and ended with the biggest win of his indoor career.

Key points

  • Alcaraz beat world number one Zverev 6-2, 7-6(7) in Turin to win his first ATP Finals title.
  • He saved three set points in the second-set tie-break, which he took 9-7, in a match lasting two hours and one minute.
  • The win is his 30th career title and sixth of 2026, and makes him the fourth man to complete the career Grand Slam and win the Finals.
  • Zverev stays year-end world number one for the first time despite the defeat, and was denied a third Finals title and a 70th win of the season.
