Houthis Claim Strike on Saudi Arabia’s Largest Refinery as Smoke Rises Over Ras Tanura

Yemen’s Houthis said on Wednesday they had attacked Ras Tanura, Saudi Arabia’s largest oil refinery, claiming direct hits that caused fires, as satellite images showed thick black smoke over the site and oil prices jumped.

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said the strikes on the refinery in the kingdom’s Eastern Province were accurate and direct and had caused fires at the facility. Satellite imagery from NASA and Planet Labs showed thick black smoke around the refinery, covering an area of about 600 square kilometres. A source with knowledge of the matter confirmed an attack on the refinery. Saudi Aramco and the Saudi government did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

The Saudi-led coalition said its air defences had intercepted and destroyed four drones over Ras Tanura on Wednesday morning, and that fragments fell from the interceptions with no injuries in an initial assessment. The coalition did not say the refinery itself had been struck. It said later on Wednesday that a projectile fired by the Houthis fell at Saudi Arabia’s Jazan port on the Red Sea, causing minor damage but no civilian injuries.

Oil prices rose sharply on the news. Brent crude traded at 93.60 dollars a barrel by mid-morning in New York on Wednesday, up more than 2 percent, while the American benchmark rose close to 2 percent. Ras Tanura has a refining capacity of 550,000 barrels a day, about 16 percent of Saudi Arabia’s total, and sits beside one of the world’s largest crude export terminals, which can load about 6.5 million barrels a day. Loadings there resumed in late June after a halt of about four months that followed a drone attack in March.

Saree said the Houthis had also carried out a second operation against the financial district in Riyadh, claiming significant damage. A strike on a tower in the district on Tuesday injured 11 people, two Saudis and nine foreign residents. The coalition has answered the attacks with air raids on Houthi targets inside Yemen, including the port of Hodeidah and targets in Saada and Amran provinces. The Houthis resumed attacks on Saudi targets in July, after Yemen’s frozen civil war reignited, and have claimed repeated strikes on Saudi energy sites this autumn, including a ballistic missile attack on Ras Tanura in October and a fire on the East-West pipeline in November.

Key points

  • The Houthis say they hit Saudi Arabia’s largest refinery at Ras Tanura with direct strikes that caused fires.
  • Satellite images showed thick black smoke over the refinery, and a source confirmed an attack on the site.
  • Saudi air defences said they shot down four drones over Ras Tanura, reporting no injuries from the interceptions.
  • Oil prices rose more than 2 percent as the attack raised fears of wider disruption to Gulf energy exports.
