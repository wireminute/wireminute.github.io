Houthis Strike Second Saudi Refinery in Two Days, Killing a Worker at Jazan Plant

A strike on the Aramco refinery at Jazan in southwestern Saudi Arabia killed a Nepali worker and injured several others on Thursday, the second Saudi refinery hit in two days as the Houthis widen their attacks on the kingdom's energy sites.

A worker from Nepal was killed and several workers of other nationalities were injured when the refinery at Jazan was hit on Thursday, Saudi Arabia's energy ministry said. The strike started a fire that was brought under control, and some of the refinery's operations were halted while the damage was assessed. The injured were receiving medical treatment, the ministry said.

The Saudi-led coalition said the Houthis had attacked the refinery and warned that strikes on civilians and civilian sites would be met with a firm military response. The coalition said it had destroyed a ballistic missile launch platform in the Yemeni port of Hodeidah that it said was used in the attack on the refinery.

The Jazan refinery sits on the kingdom's Red Sea coast and is designed to process 400,000 barrels of crude oil a day into fuels including petrol and ultra-low-sulphur diesel. It has been attacked before. The Houthis also struck Jazan port on Wednesday, causing minor damage and no casualties, the coalition said.

The attack came a day after the Houthis said they had struck Ras Tanura, Saudi Arabia's largest refinery, and caused fires there. Satellite imagery showed thick black smoke around the Ras Tanura plant, covering an area of about 600 square kilometres. The coalition said its air defences had intercepted and destroyed four drones over Ras Tanura on Wednesday morning, and that fragments fell from the interceptions with no injuries in an initial assessment. Oil prices rose sharply on the news, with Brent crude trading above 93 dollars a barrel by mid-morning in New York on Wednesday.

The Houthis said they had also carried out a second operation against the financial district in Riyadh, claiming significant damage. A strike on a tower in the district on Tuesday injured 11 people, two Saudis and nine foreign residents. The coalition has answered the attacks with air raids on Houthi targets inside Yemen, including the port of Hodeidah and targets in Saada and Amran provinces. The Houthis resumed attacks on Saudi targets in July, after Yemen's frozen civil war reignited, and have claimed repeated strikes on Saudi energy sites this autumn, including a ballistic missile attack on Ras Tanura in October and a fire on the East-West pipeline in November.

  • The Saudi-led coalition blamed the Houthis for the attack and said it destroyed the missile launch platform in Hodeidah used to carry it out.
