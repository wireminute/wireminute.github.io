Hasina Says She Will Return to Bangladesh in December Despite Death Sentence

Bangladesh's ousted prime minister Sheikh Hasina says she will return home in December, even though a court has sentenced her to death and the government in Dhaka has said she would face execution on arrival.

Hasina, who has been in hiding in India since fleeing in August 2024, said in an email interview on Monday that she was already preparing for the journey. "I am fully aware of the risks I face if I return," she said. "But I have made my decision, and no threat of death will keep me away from my own country." She added that she did not wish to die and wanted to keep serving her country, but was prepared to pay that price if returning home demanded it.

A student-led uprising ended her 15-year rule in August 2024, and she has remained out of sight in India ever since. In November 2025, a tribunal in Dhaka sentenced her to death in her absence, convicting her of crimes against humanity over the crackdown on protesters during the uprising that forced her from office.

Days before her announcement, the government in Dhaka said she would face execution if she came back, adding that she had no legal route left after missing the deadline to appeal against the conviction. Hasina called that position contradictory. Dhaka has also asked India to hand her over, and New Delhi says the extradition request is under legal review.

Her announcement comes as tensions in Bangladesh remain high. Supporters of her Awami League party clashed with security forces in the town of Gopalganj in recent days, prompting the government to deploy paramilitary troops and warn of tough action against further violence. The interview gave no details of how or when exactly she would travel, and it is not clear whether India would allow her to leave.

Key points

  • Sheikh Hasina told an interviewer on Monday that she will return to Bangladesh in December despite facing a death sentence.
  • She said she was already making preparations and that no threat of death would keep her from her own country.
  • She fled to India in August 2024 after a student-led uprising ended her 15-year rule, and a Dhaka tribunal sentenced her to death in absentia in November 2025 over the crackdown on protesters.
  • Dhaka says she would face execution if she returns and has sought her extradition; India says the request is under legal review.
