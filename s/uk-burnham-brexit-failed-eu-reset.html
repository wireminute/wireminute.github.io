Burnham Says Brexit Has Failed as Britain Seeks Closer EU Ties

British Prime Minister Andy Burnham has said Brexit has not worked for the United Kingdom and that the country must rebuild a closer relationship with the European Union, opening a national debate a decade after the vote to leave.

Speaking at the Lord Mayor's Banquet in London on Monday, Burnham said the Brexit deal Britain was given had not worked and that the country had lost more than it had gained. He compared the deal to the fairy tale of the emperor's new clothes, in which no one is willing to say the obvious truth. "The Brexit we were given has not worked. We have lost more than we gained," he said.

The remarks come ahead of a UK-EU summit in Brussels on Friday, where Britain is expected to reach agreements on food and drink trade, energy and emissions, and a scheme letting young people live and work across Britain and the EU for limited periods. Burnham said the summit could deliver real improvements in people's everyday lives, including in parts of the country that have felt neglected for too long.

The prime minister said he would open a discussion with EU partners on Britain's long-term relationship with the bloc soon after the summit, and that a wider national conversation would follow. He remains bound by his party's manifesto commitment not to return to the single market or the customs union, or to restore freedom of movement, but said he wanted to explore options for the longer term, with voters ultimately deciding.

Britain voted to leave the EU in a 2016 referendum and formally left in 2020. The debate over the relationship had largely faded from British politics until Burnham reopened it after taking office in July, even as polling suggested voters increasingly saw the departure as a mistake. British food and drink exports to the bloc were down by almost a third in 2025 compared with 2019. Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband backed the prime minister, saying leaving had worsened the economy, while former prime minister Boris Johnson said he believed Britain would back Brexit again if a vote were forced.

Key points

  • Prime Minister Andy Burnham told a London banquet that the Brexit deal Britain was given has not worked and the country has lost more than it gained.
  • He compared the deal to the emperor's new clothes and said it was time to say the obvious truth about its costs.
  • A UK-EU summit in Brussels on Friday is expected to agree deals on food trade, energy, emissions and youth mobility, with a wider debate on the long-term relationship to follow.
  • Burnham remains bound by a manifesto pledge against rejoining the single market or customs union, while saying voters should ultimately decide the longer-term relationship.
