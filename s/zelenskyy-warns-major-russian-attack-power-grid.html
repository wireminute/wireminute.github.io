Russia Hits Nine Ukrainian Regions Overnight After Zelenskyy Warns of Major Grid Attack

Overnight Russian attacks struck railways, power substations and other infrastructure across nine Ukrainian regions, killing one person and injuring six, hours after President Volodymyr Zelenskyy warned that Moscow is preparing a major attack on the country’s power grid.

Russian forces struck railway and energy infrastructure in nine Ukrainian regions overnight, launching 155 attack drones since Thursday evening, Ukraine's air force and prime minister said. Prime Minister Serhii Koretskyi said one person was killed and six injured in the attacks, which hit railways, the energy sector, civilian businesses and residential buildings.

In the Dnipropetrovsk region, Russian forces struck railway stations in Kryvyi Rih and Kamianske, as well as two coal mines, damaging diesel locomotives. In the Vinnytsia and Kirovohrad regions, they attacked electrical substations, including ones supplying power to railway operations. Production facilities and warehouses were hit in Zaporizhzhia, the Kyiv region and the Mykolaiv region. Ukraine's air force said 110 of the 155 drones were destroyed, though hits were recorded. By Friday morning, consumers in six regions, Dnipropetrovsk, Zaporizhzhia, Kirovohrad, Sumy, Kharkiv and Kherson, were without electricity, with the most extensive outages in Dnipropetrovsk and Kherson.

The overnight barrage came hours after President Volodymyr Zelenskyy warned in his Thursday nightly address that Russia is preparing a new massive strike on Ukraine's energy infrastructure. He said intelligence shows Moscow is completing preparations for an assault on energy facilities, logistics and critical services, and that Russian forces keep hitting substations, overhead power lines and the whole power grid. Kyiv says the campaign is designed to deprive civilians of light, heating and running water as winter approaches.

US President Donald Trump weighed in on Thursday, saying he wanted President Vladimir Putin to pull back military aggression across Europe so a deal between Russia and Ukraine could be made quickly. Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner, who is mediating the peace talks alongside special envoy Steve Witkoff, said his long conversations with Putin had left him believing the Russian leader wants peace, and that Putin had assured the envoys he does not intend to use nuclear weapons. Kushner acknowledged, however, that the negotiations have reached a deadlock over the fate of Ukrainian territory. The war is now in its fifth year since Russia launched its full-scale invasion in February 2022.

Key points

  • Ukraine's air force says 155 attack drones were launched since Thursday evening, with 110 shot down.

  • Zelenskyy warned on Thursday that Russia is preparing a new massive strike on energy, logistics and critical services; Trump urged Putin to pull back.
