Ghana's $60m Presidential Jet Sparks Outrage as Critics Question Mahama's U-Turn

Ghana has taken delivery of a new presidential jet worth about $61 million, setting off a fierce public row over cost and timing as many citizens struggle with the high cost of living.

The Dassault Falcon 6X, built by French manufacturer Dassault Aviation, was handed to the Ghana Air Force on Monday and greeted with a water salute. Parliament approved the purchase last December as part of a wider programme to modernise the military's aviation fleet, which also includes four helicopters from Airbus.

Criticism followed quickly. Opposition MP Samuel Abu Jinapor questioned the government's priorities, pointing to household budgets under pressure and youth unemployment he described as alarmingly high. Online, some users argued the money could have paid for public services, with one widely shared post saying it could employ more than 100,000 nurses and 200,000 teachers.

The charge carries extra weight because President John Mahama himself opposed buying a new presidential jet while in opposition, when his party argued Ghana's economic troubles made such spending unjustifiable. Jinapor said the minority caucus would demand full disclosure of the deal, including the total cost to taxpayers, the financing and the procurement process.

The government has rejected the criticism. It says Ghana's economic position has changed considerably since the party opposed a similar purchase, and that the jet is needed to replace the 16-year-old Falcon 900EX, which has suffered repeated maintenance problems including corrosion and leaks in its fuel tank. Peter Lanchene Toobu, chairman of parliament's defence committee, said the party had never opposed acquiring a presidential jet in principle.

The row comes as Mahama's administration tries to show an economic recovery after years of financial difficulty that forced Ghana to seek help from the International Monetary Fund. The government says the improving economy makes the aircraft affordable; critics say the timing shows the wrong priorities.

Key points

  • Ghana Air Force took delivery of a Dassault Falcon 6X on Monday at a reported cost of about $61 million, welcomed with a water salute.
  • Opposition MP Samuel Abu Jinapor called the purchase misplaced priorities, citing the cost of living and high youth unemployment.
  • President Mahama opposed buying a new presidential jet while in opposition, and critics say the purchase contradicts that stance.
  • The government says the economy has improved and the jet replaces the ageing Falcon 900EX, which has suffered corrosion and fuel tank leaks.
