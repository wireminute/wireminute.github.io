Twelve Charged in Canada Over $4.5m Luxury Car Theft Ring Bound for West Africa

Canadian police have charged 12 people and recovered 96 stolen luxury vehicles worth about C$4.5 million, after breaking up a network they say was shipping the cars overseas in containers.

Peel Regional Police in Ontario said the arrests followed an investigation, codenamed Project Cadetta, that began in March 2026 alongside the Canada Border Services Agency. Officers found the vehicles, mostly luxury SUVs and pickup trucks, hidden in shipping containers at a warehouse in Brampton that police described as the operation's main staging area.

The 12 accused face 231 charges between them, including handling stolen property, trafficking in stolen goods and forgery. Police said five men sat at the centre of the network, coordinating the theft, transport and export of the vehicles, while the others played supporting roles. Warrants have been issued for two more suspects.

Investigators believe the cars were stolen across the Greater Toronto Area and moved through Canadian ports for buyers in West Africa. Police said some of the accused could be deported after their cases are heard. Nine of the accused were released with future court dates, and three were held for bail hearings before being released.

Nigerian news outlets, citing the charge sheet, reported that eight of the accused are Nigerians, though the police announcement itself did not give the nationalities of those charged. Police said the investigation is continuing and further charges are expected. The case fits a wider pattern: Interpol reported in 2025 that it had traced more than 100 luxury vehicles stolen in Canada and Europe to buyers in West Africa.

Key points
