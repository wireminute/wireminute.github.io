EU Approves Indonesia Trade Deal to Blunt Impact of Trump Tariffs

European Union governments have approved the signing of a trade deal with Indonesia aimed at offsetting the impact of United States tariffs, with duties to be scrapped on more than 90 percent of goods traded between the two sides.

European Union governments on Wednesday gave the go-ahead for the signing of a trade agreement with Indonesia that both sides hope will soften the blow of tariffs imposed by United States President Donald Trump. The Council of the European Union approved the signature and provisional application of the deal, which was first agreed in September 2025. Once it takes effect, the two sides will scrap tariffs on more than 90 percent of the goods they trade with each other, most of them immediately.

Indonesia will phase out its 50 percent tariff on cars imported from the EU over five years. The agreement is expected to save companies on both sides about 600 million euros a year in customs duties, according to estimates cited in the announcement. For Brussels, the deal is part of the effort to cushion European exporters from the cost of Washington's tariffs; for Jakarta, it locks in access to one of the world's largest markets.

Timing is pressing on both sides. Indonesia is due to graduate from the EU's generalised scheme of preferences on 1 January 2027, after which its exports will no longer benefit from preferential tariffs. The Council said the two sides wanted the agreements applied as swiftly as possible so that Indonesian exporters do not face a sudden rise in duties at the start of next year.

The agreements will now be formally signed by the EU and Indonesia and then submitted to the European Parliament for approval. The Council's decision allows the deal to be applied provisionally while that process runs its course, meaning much of the tariff relief can begin before the Parliament has finished its vote.

Key points

  • EU governments approved the signing and provisional application of the EU-Indonesia trade deal on Wednesday
  • The two sides will scrap tariffs on more than 90 percent of goods traded between them, and Indonesia will phase out its 50 percent duty on EU cars within five years
  • The deal is expected to save businesses about 600 million euros a year in customs duties
  • Indonesia leaves the EU's preferential tariff scheme on 1 January 2027, so both sides want the deal applied swiftly; it now goes to the European Parliament for approval
