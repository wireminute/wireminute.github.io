Trump Threatens 'Tremendous Tax' on US Firms Building AI Data Centres Abroad

President Donald Trump has said the United States will not allow American companies to build data centres in other countries, threatening them with a heavy tax if they do, in a move aimed at keeping the artificial intelligence race on American soil.

Speaking at a White House news conference on Friday, shortly after returning from a summit in Shenzhen with the leaders of China and Russia, Trump said American firms that build data centres abroad would face a "tremendous tax". "We're not going to allow our companies to build data centers outside of our country," he said. "If they do that, they can. But we're going to put a tremendous tax on them." He argued that computing capacity built overseas would end up in Chinese hands. "China will take all of them, and we're not allowing that to happen."

Trump singled out a project in Finland, where Google announced in September that it would invest about 13 billion euros, roughly 15 billion dollars, in data centres and artificial intelligence infrastructure. He gave no tax rate, no timetable and no details of how a ban or a tax would be enforced, and he told reporters the plan was news to his own staff. "I haven't even said that to my people," he said. "But I'm telling them now."

The threat follows a move by the Commerce Department this week to tighten controls on advanced artificial intelligence chips, requiring licences for data centres anywhere in the world that rent large volumes of computing power from American chips to companies based in China, Macau or other territories under American embargoes. The aim is to stop Chinese firms obtaining advanced computing power through third countries, including in Southeast Asia.

Trump framed the issue as a contest with China over artificial intelligence, which he calls superintelligence. "Whoever wins SI, wins," he said. He also urged Americans to welcome data centres in their own towns rather than block them, saying communities should make the companies pay for the privilege. "Make them pay you. Make them pay your community, but don't shut them off." American stocks fell sharply on Friday as his remarks landed, with the S&P 500 down 2.3 percent and the Nasdaq 100 down 4 percent in a wider sell-off in technology shares. Major American developers including Microsoft, Google, Amazon and Meta all have data centre projects outside the United States, and Microsoft alone plans to invest more than 10 billion dollars in Kuwait, Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates through 2030.

Key points

  • Trump said on Friday that American companies will not be allowed to build data centres abroad and will face a 'tremendous tax' if they do
  • He named Google's roughly 13 billion euro data centre investment in Finland and said China would take over computing capacity built overseas
  • He gave no tax rate, timetable or enforcement mechanism, and said he had not yet told his own staff about the plan
  • The threat follows new American export controls on advanced AI chips, and came as US stocks fell sharply, with the Nasdaq 100 down 4 percent
