Ecuadorian Fishermen Take Trump Administration to Court Over Drone Strike on Their Boat

Sixteen Ecuadorian fishermen have sued the Trump administration in a United States federal court, saying a drone strike wrongly targeted their fishing boat in the Pacific in March, sank it and left two of the crew with serious burns.

The lawsuit was filed on Monday in the US District Court for the Western District of Pennsylvania, in Pittsburgh. The crew of the fishing boat La Negra Francisca Duarte II is seeking financial compensation, a court order ending the lethal strikes on boats in the Caribbean and the Pacific, and any further relief the court deems appropriate. The fishermen are represented by Pittsburgh lawyer Daniel M. Kovalik, and the case is listed as Flores Franco v. United States.

According to the complaint, two armed drones launched from a vessel operated by the United States struck the boat on March 17, roughly 385 nautical miles off Ecuador's port of Manta. The explosions set the vessel on fire and it sank. Two of the 16 people on board suffered serious burns. The fishermen say they were sailing home after a two-week fishing trip, had no connection to drug trafficking, and their boat had already been inspected by Ecuadorian officials.

The fishermen also say they were detained and mistreated by US forces after surviving the attack, before being released. Their complaint describes the strike as premeditated and intentional, and says it had no plausible legal basis. The US government has said it has no information about the attack on their boat, or about two other Ecuadorian vessels sunk this year. It has claimed responsibility for strikes on boats it describes as carrying drug traffickers, as part of its Operation Southern Spear campaign. A report in August said the Ecuadorian vessels had been targeted under a covert programme run by the CIA. The Pentagon declined to comment on pending litigation.

The filing also sets out the scale of the wider campaign. The lawsuit says 71 strikes have been carried out since September 2025, killing at least 235 people. US Southern Command has said it sank at least eight Ecuadorian vessels between late August and mid-September, describing them as supply boats for drug traffickers linked to the Ecuadorian gang Los Choneros, with about 160 crew members on board between them.

Key points

  • Sixteen Ecuadorian fishermen have sued the Trump administration in a Pittsburgh federal court over a drone strike that sank their fishing boat in March.
  • Two armed drones hit the La Negra Francisca Duarte II roughly 385 nautical miles off Manta, setting it ablaze; two crew members suffered serious burns.
  • The fishermen say they were returning from a two-week fishing trip with no drug-trafficking links, and were detained and mistreated after surviving.
  • The US says it has no information about the attack on their boat; the lawsuit counts 71 boat strikes since September 2025, with at least 235 people killed.
