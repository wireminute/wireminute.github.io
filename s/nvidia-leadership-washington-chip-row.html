Huang Agrees to Face Congress as Lawmakers Push Tighter Chip Export Curbs

Former Nvidia chief Jensen Huang has agreed to appear before Congress over the scandal that ended his tenure, as lawmakers from both parties press to tighten controls on advanced chip exports through the annual defence bill.

House Foreign Affairs chairman Brian Mast said Huang had agreed to come before lawmakers, with the date still to be set. Three committee chairmen, John Moolenaar of the China select committee, Mast and Rick Crawford of the intelligence committee, wrote to Huang on Thursday asking him to appear for a closed-door interview on 1 December, warning the Foreign Affairs Committee would issue a subpoena if he did not accept by Friday evening. Senate Banking chairman Tim Scott has also asked Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to testify in the first week of December.

Huang resigned on Thursday after revelations about extramarital affairs, including one with a businesswoman officials suspect of links to Chinese intelligence. Beijing has dismissed the claim as groundless, and Alice Huang has not commented publicly. Megaspeed, the Singapore cloud firm at the centre of the row, said she left the company on 30 August 2025. Singapore said its investigation into the firm and six others, opened in February 2025, remains open, and she has been barred from re-entering the country. Attorney General Todd Blanche said on 12 November the Justice Department had reached no conclusion and the FBI was investigating.

The scandal has become the lever for a bipartisan push to tighten export rules. Lawmakers want to attach four measures to the must-pass defence bill: the AI Overwatch Act, giving Congress more say over chip exports; the Chip Security Act, requiring firms to track chips to combat smuggling; the Match Act, pressing allies to restrict chipmaking equipment bound for China; and the Remote Access Security Act, letting the government restrict remote access to American AI chips via cloud computing. The Senate version already includes the first three, and House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries said on Monday he would work to keep them in.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent pushed back against new laws, saying the existing export rules were sufficient and needed enforcing, while accusing the Commerce Department of delaying a Megaspeed blacklisting. He gave Nvidia's board and its outside lawyers ten days to answer questions about its compliance culture, and said the company should hire its next chief from outside. The board has sounded out Rene Haas, chief executive of British chip designer Arm, while President Donald Trump named former Apple chief Tim Cook. Finance chief Colette Kress is interim chief, and the company reports quarterly results on 17 November, its first earnings call without Huang.

Key points

  • Huang has agreed to appear before Congress, Mast said, after three committee chairmen asked for a closed-door interview on 1 December, with a subpoena threatened if he does not accept by Friday evening.
  • Senate Banking chairman Tim Scott has also asked Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to testify in the first week of December.
  • Lawmakers from both parties want AI chip export curbs attached to the defence bill, including measures to track chips and limit allies' equipment sales to China.
  • Nvidia reports quarterly results on 17 November, its first earnings call without Huang, while the FBI investigation continues with no conclusion reached.
