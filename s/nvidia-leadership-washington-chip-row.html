Bessent Gives Nvidia Ten Days to Answer Export Questions and Calls for an Outside Chief

The United States Treasury Secretary has told Nvidia its board and outside lawyers must answer questions about export-control compliance within ten days, and said the chipmaker should hire its next chief executive from outside the company.

The Treasury Secretary, Scott Bessent, said on a Sunday morning television programme that the board should look beyond its own ranks for a successor to Jensen Huang, who stepped down as chief executive on Thursday. He said the company needed a dramatic shake-up after what had gone on inside it, and gave the board and its outside lawyers ten days to answer questions about its culture of compliance with export controls. He asked whether the firm had knowingly overlooked suspect chip sales that may have lifted its revenue and earnings.

Huang resigned after revelations about extramarital affairs, including one involving a businesswoman whom United States officials suspect of links to Chinese intelligence. Beijing has dismissed the claim as groundless; Alice Huang has not commented publicly. Megaspeed, the Singapore cloud firm at the centre of the row, said on Sunday that she left the company on 30 August 2025 and holds no role there now. Singapore said its investigation into the firm and six other individuals, opened in February 2025, remains open, and that she had been barred from re-entering the country.

The search for a permanent successor is already crowded. The board has sounded out Rene Haas, the chief executive of British chip designer Arm, as a possible replacement, according to the Financial Times. President Donald Trump named former Apple chief Tim Cook as a potential candidate in a social media post on Thursday, while the same newspaper reported on Sunday that Huang's allies were rallying Silicon Valley executives behind his return. Finance chief Colette Kress is interim chief executive, and Congress has called on Huang to testify behind closed doors.

Bessent also pushed back against calls for new laws, saying the existing export rules were sufficient and simply needed enforcing. Lawmakers from both parties disagree and are pressing to tighten the controls through the annual defence bill, including closing a gap that lets Chinese companies hire computing capacity from data centres abroad. Bessent had earlier accused the Commerce Department of sitting for months on blacklisting Megaspeed, saying his department and the defence, state and trade offices had all wanted the move. The company reports quarterly results on 17 November.

Key points

  • Bessent said Nvidia's board and outside lawyers must answer export-compliance questions within ten days.
  • He said the company should hire its next chief executive from outside, calling for a dramatic shake-up.
  • The board has sounded out Arm chief Rene Haas, while Trump named Tim Cook and Huang's allies are reported to be backing his return.
  • Bessent said existing export rules are enough but need enforcing, while lawmakers in both parties want tighter controls.
