Attorney General Sidesteps Questions on Nvidia Inquiry as Huang Heads to Congress

The United States attorney general has refused to say whether federal investigators examined Jensen Huang before the Nvidia chief executive was restored to his job, deepening the political row over President Donald Trump's role in his return.

Todd Blanche, the attorney general, was pressed repeatedly at a news conference on Tuesday about whether the Justice Department or the FBI had investigated Huang. He declined to answer directly, at one point saying he did not understand what was being asked, and he would not say whether Huang still held a security clearance. He described Nvidia as a good corporate citizen and said he had no reason to call it anything else.

The evasive answers sit awkwardly with the account given by Trump, who said on Monday that his administration had carried out a thorough review and cleared Huang of wrongdoing, and that he had personally persuaded Nvidia's board to bring him back. Trump wrote the same day that the attorney general had confirmed the government held no evidence that Huang had broken the law.

Huang was reinstated on Monday by a unanimous board vote, 11 days after he resigned on 12 November following reports of a relationship with a businesswoman officials suspect of links to Chinese intelligence. The board had told investors days earlier that it did not intend to bring him back. Shares jumped about 30 percent in premarket trading on the news, then fell about 6 percent on Tuesday as questions mounted in Washington.

Huang is due to appear before Congress next week, where lawmakers from both parties are expected to question him about national security concerns, including whether advanced Nvidia chips reached China. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer said Democrats would investigate the arrangements, saying senior officials had assured Nvidia that Huang posed no risk without showing the public any evidence. The Commerce Department has also moved to blacklist Megaspeed, the Singapore firm at the centre of the affair, over suspicions it helped divert advanced chips to China. The story is still developing.

Key points

  • Attorney General Todd Blanche repeatedly declined to say whether the Justice Department or the FBI investigated Huang, and said he had no information about his security clearance.
  • His answers contrast with Trump's statement that the administration had cleared Huang and that he personally persuaded the board to reinstate him.
  • Huang was reinstated on Monday, 11 days after resigning; shares jumped about 30 percent, then fell about 6 percent on Tuesday.
  • Huang is due before Congress next week; Democrats say they will investigate, and the Commerce Department is moving to blacklist Megaspeed over chip-diversion suspicions.
