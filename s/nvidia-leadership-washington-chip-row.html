Huang Returns as Nvidia CEO as Board Reverses Course and Shares Jump 30%

Nvidia has brought Jensen Huang back as chief executive, president and board director with immediate effect, reversing its decision to part with the founder 11 days ago and sending the company’s shares sharply higher before the market opened.

The board voted unanimously to reinstate Huang, the company said in an announcement on Monday, after a decision taken on Sunday according to a filing. The move ends an 11-day absence in which interim chief executive Colette Kress ran the company. Huang, who co-founded Nvidia and led it for more than three decades, had resigned on 12 November after reports about extramarital affairs, including one with a businesswoman officials suspect of links to Chinese intelligence. The board had told investors days later that it did not intend to bring him back.

Shares jumped about 30 percent in premarket trading, putting the stock at roughly $165 a share. That is still well below the all-time closing high of about $239 reached on 6 October, after the shares shed roughly a third of their value over the previous two weeks, including a 14 percent fall on Friday. The wider market rose with the news: futures tied to the Nasdaq 100 climbed about 7 percent, while S&P 500 futures gained about 4 percent and Dow futures rose about 2 percent.

President Donald Trump, who had pressed publicly for Huang’s return and said he should be given a second chance, welcomed the decision on Monday, calling it the correct and proper decision. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent had also called over the weekend for Huang to be reinstated. Huang had said last week that he would be honoured to return if the board asked, and an open letter urging his reinstatement had gathered more than 190,000 signatures, including Elon Musk.

The return does not settle the questions around the company. The board has hired a law firm to lead an independent investigation, the securities regulator is conducting a civil investigation and the FBI is investigating, while the Justice Department has reached no conclusion. Huang has agreed to appear before three House committees on 1 December for questioning over concerns that China gained access to advanced Nvidia chips. The board has also set up a new committee to oversee national security and export-control compliance. The story is still developing.
