Nvidia Rules Out Huang Return as Record Results Beat Forecasts

Nvidia has ruled out bringing back Jensen Huang as chief executive, hours after the chipmaker reported record quarterly results that beat Wall Street forecasts.

Board chair Harvey Jones told investors on Tuesday evening that the board unanimously accepted Huang's resignation and does not intend to reinstate him, closing the door on the former chief's public offer earlier in the day to return if asked. Huang had said he would be honored to come back if the board decided his return was in the best interest of shareholders, employees, the company and America.

The results gave the board a strong hand. Revenue more than doubled to $115.8 billion in the quarter ended 25 October, up 103 percent on a year earlier and ahead of the roughly $109 billion analysts had expected. Profit rose 105 percent to $65.3 billion, and adjusted earnings of $2.68 a share beat the $2.48 forecast. Data centre sales, the engine of the artificial intelligence boom, reached $107.6 billion, up 110 percent. The company forecast about $130 billion of revenue for the current quarter and raised its growth outlook for fiscal 2028 to about 75 percent, from 70 percent.

Interim chief executive Colette Kress told the call that the company's momentum was real and its product roadmap was on track. It was the first earnings call in nearly 28 years without Huang at the helm, and shares rose about 3.6 percent in after-hours trading. Huang resigned on 12 November after revelations about extramarital affairs, including one with a businesswoman officials suspect of links to Chinese intelligence. The board has hired the firm Wachtell Lipton to lead an independent investigation, and the Securities and Exchange Commission is conducting a civil investigation of the company. The Justice Department has reached no conclusion and the FBI is investigating.

The board is now searching for a permanent successor, considering internal and external candidates, and has hired an executive search firm. It has sounded out Rene Haas, chief executive of chip designer Arm. An open letter urging Huang's reinstatement has gathered more than 190,000 signatures, including Elon Musk, and more than 8,400 signatories identify themselves as Nvidia employees. President Donald Trump said he would not mind Huang coming back, while Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the next chief should be hired from outside and gave the board ten days to answer questions about its compliance culture. Huang has agreed to appear before three House committees on 1 December for questioning over concerns that China gained access to advanced Nvidia chips.

Key points

  • Nvidia's board has formally ruled out reinstating Jensen Huang as chief executive, chair Harvey Jones told investors, hours after Huang said he would be honoured to return.
  • Quarterly revenue more than doubled to $115.8 billion, up 103 percent, beating forecasts; profit rose 105 percent to $65.3 billion, with data centre sales of $107.6 billion.
  • Interim chief Colette Kress said momentum was real and the roadmap on track; the company forecast about $130 billion for the current quarter and raised its fiscal 2028 growth outlook to about 75 percent.
  • The board is searching for a permanent successor, considering internal and external candidates; Huang faces three House committees on 1 December, and the Securities and Exchange Commission is conducting a civil investigation.
