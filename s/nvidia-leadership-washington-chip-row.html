Musk Joins 158,000-Strong Call to Bring Huang Back as Bessent Demands Outside Successor

Elon Musk has joined more than 158,000 people urging Nvidia's board to reinstate Jensen Huang while an inquiry runs its course, as the Treasury secretary demands the company hire its next chief from outside and Huang himself weighs a comeback.

An open letter signed by more than 158,000 people, including Elon Musk, calls on Nvidia's board to bring Jensen Huang back while an independent investigation establishes the facts. The signatories include Uber co-founder Travis Kalanick, Epic Games founder Tim Sweeney and Y Combinator president Garry Tan. More than 7,500 of those who signed identify themselves as Nvidia employees, about 18 percent of the company's workforce.

Huang has discussed a possible return with people close to him, according to one report, and his allies are lobbying the board and seeking White House support. The board has neither confirmed nor ruled out a return. Huang holds no controlling stake in the company, so he cannot overrule the board, which named finance chief Colette Kress as interim chief after his resignation on Thursday.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, speaking on television on Sunday, said the board should hire its next chief from outside, calling for a dramatic shake-up. He gave the board and its outside lawyers ten days to answer questions about its compliance culture, and questioned whether it had pressed illicit chip sales to China to lift its earnings. The board has sounded out Rene Haas, chief executive of British chip designer Arm, as a possible successor.

Huang has agreed to appear before Congress, House Foreign Affairs chairman Brian Mast said, after three committee chairmen sought a closed-door interview on 1 December, warning of a subpoena if he did not accept by Friday evening. Senate Banking chairman Tim Scott has also asked Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to testify in early December. Lawmakers want four export measures attached to the must-pass defence bill: the AI Overwatch Act, the Chip Security Act, the Match Act and the Remote Access Security Act.

Huang resigned after revelations about extramarital affairs, including one with a businesswoman officials suspect of links to Chinese intelligence. Beijing has dismissed the claim as groundless, and Alice Huang has not commented publicly. Megaspeed, the Singapore cloud firm at the centre of the row, said she left the company on 30 August 2025. Singapore said its investigation into the firm, opened in February 2025, remains open, and she has been barred from re-entering the country. Attorney General Todd Blanche said the Justice Department had reached no conclusion and the FBI was investigating. Nvidia reports quarterly results on 17 November, its first earnings call without Huang.

Key points

  • More than 158,000 people, including Elon Musk, have signed an open letter urging Nvidia to reinstate Huang while an independent investigation establishes the facts.
  • Huang has discussed a possible return and his allies are lobbying the board, according to a report, but the board has neither confirmed nor ruled out a comeback.
  • Bessent wants the next chief hired from outside the company and has given the board ten days to answer questions about its export-compliance culture.
  • Huang has agreed to appear before Congress, with three chairmen seeking a closed-door interview on 1 December and a subpoena threatened if he does not accept by Friday evening.
