Nvidia Board Not Considering Jensen Huang Return Despite Trump Backing

Nvidia's board is not considering bringing Jensen Huang back as chief executive, despite President Donald Trump saying he would not mind a return and a petition urging his reinstatement passing 180,000 signatures.

The board has not held any discussions about bringing Huang back, according to a person familiar with its thinking, and is focused instead on completing an independent investigation into his conduct. The firm Wachtell Lipton has been hired to lead the inquiry, examining whether his actions compromised national security or exposed company secrets. The board had previously neither confirmed nor ruled out a return.

An open letter urging the board to reinstate Huang has gathered more than 180,000 signatures, including Elon Musk, Uber co-founder Travis Kalanick, Epic Games founder Tim Sweeney and Y Combinator president Garry Tan. More than 8,400 of those who signed identify themselves as Nvidia employees. Huang has discussed a possible return with people close to him, according to one report, while his allies lobby the board and seek White House support, though one investor said Huang had not discussed a comeback with him.

Trump said on Monday he would not mind Huang coming back. "I think he learned a big lesson," he said, adding that a lot of people were asking for Huang to return. He said he did not know whether Alice Huang was a spy, and that national security had not been put at risk. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, by contrast, said on Sunday the board should hire its next chief from outside, calling for a dramatic shake-up. He gave the board ten days to answer questions about its compliance culture and whether it pressed illicit chip sales to China to lift earnings.

Huang holds no controlling stake, so he cannot overrule the board. Finance chief Colette Kress is interim chief. The board has sounded out Rene Haas, chief executive of chip designer Arm, as a possible successor, and has hired an executive search firm. Huang has agreed to appear before Congress, House Foreign Affairs chairman Brian Mast said, after three committee chairmen sought an interview on 1 December and threatened a subpoena if he refused.

Huang resigned after revelations about extramarital affairs, including one with a businesswoman officials suspect of links to Chinese intelligence. Beijing has dismissed the claim as groundless; Alice Huang has not commented publicly. Megaspeed, the Singapore firm at the centre of the row, said she left in August 2025. The Justice Department has reached no conclusion and the FBI is investigating, Attorney General Todd Blanche said. Nvidia reports quarterly results on 17 November, its first earnings call without Huang.

Key points

  • Nvidia's board is not considering bringing Jensen Huang back as chief executive and has held no discussions about a return, with its focus on an independent investigation led by Wachtell Lipton.
  • A petition urging his reinstatement has passed 180,000 signatures, backed by Elon Musk and other technology leaders.
  • Trump said on Monday he would not mind Huang returning, while Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent wants the next chief hired from outside the company.
  • Finance chief Colette Kress remains interim chief; the board has sounded out Arm's Rene Haas, and Huang has agreed to appear before Congress on 1 December.
