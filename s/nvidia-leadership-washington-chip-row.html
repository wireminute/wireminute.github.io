Megaspeed Breaks Silence in Nvidia Chip Row, Says Alice Huang Left in August 2025

Megaspeed, the Singapore cloud firm at the centre of the row over Nvidia chips reaching China, broke its silence on Sunday, saying Alice Huang left the company on 30 August 2025 and holds no role there now.

In its first public statement on the affair, the company said it had hired outside lawyers to examine recent reporting about its business, supply chain and global operations, which it described as assertions not supported by verifiable evidence. It said it conducted legitimate and lawful business, and declined to comment on Huang’s private life.

The statement comes three days after Jensen Huang stepped down as Nvidia’s chief executive, following revelations about extramarital affairs, including one involving a businesswoman whom United States officials suspect of links to Chinese intelligence. Finance chief Colette Kress has taken over as interim chief executive, and Congress has called on Huang to testify behind closed doors. The board is understood to have approached Rene Haas, the chief executive of British chip designer Arm, about succeeding him, though neither company has commented publicly on the approach.

Singapore’s home affairs ministry said the police and customs began an independent investigation in February 2025 into several companies and their key officers, including Megaspeed and its controllers, over possible false declarations connected to purchases of servers. That investigation, covering Megaspeed and six other individuals, remains open. The ministry also said Huang had been barred from re-entering Singapore after giving inaccurate information about her role at the company.

In Washington, the fallout has reached the cabinet table. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent publicly accused the Commerce Department of sitting on a decision to blacklist Megaspeed, saying his own department and the defence, state and trade offices had wanted the company blacklisted for months, while the authority to act rested with Commerce. Lawmakers from both parties are pressing to tighten the rules further, including closing a gap that lets Chinese companies hire computing capacity from data centres abroad and run the restricted chips from afar. Nvidia shares fell more than 3 percent on Friday, while Arm’s American-listed shares were down more than 5 percent.

Key points

  • Megaspeed said on Sunday that Alice Huang left the company on 30 August 2025 and holds no role there now.
  • The firm hired outside lawyers to examine recent reporting on its business and supply chain, which it called unsupported by verifiable evidence.
  • Singapore said its investigation into Megaspeed and six other individuals, opened in February 2025, remains open.
  • Jensen Huang stepped down on Thursday; Colette Kress is interim chief executive and Congress has called on him to testify.
