Israeli Strike Kills Family of Four in Gaza City, a Day After Hamas Security Chief's Death

An Israeli strike killed a family of four in a Gaza City refugee camp early on Wednesday, including a senior Hamas security official, a day after Israeli forces killed the head of the group's internal security apparatus.

A helicopter fired at least one missile at a home in the al-Shati refugee camp in western Gaza City, local sources said. The strike killed Colonel Mohammed Abu al-Ouf, the director of internal security for northern Gaza, his wife Noura and their daughters Yusra, 15, and Jana, 13. Several other people were wounded, some of them critically, and were taken to al-Shifa Hospital.

Gaza's interior ministry, which operates under Hamas, confirmed the deaths. The Israeli military said it had targeted a Hamas member, without giving further details. At least 14 Palestinians were killed across the Gaza Strip over the past 24 hours, according to correspondents in the territory.

The strike came a day after an Israeli airstrike killed Naeem Abu Nahel, the director of Hamas's internal security apparatus in Gaza, in the Ansar area of western Gaza City on Tuesday. Gaza's interior ministry confirmed his death, and Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu later confirmed that Abu Nahel was among those killed. Israeli media render his name as Naim Abu Nahal.

At least seven Palestinians were killed in strikes across Gaza on Tuesday, medics said, including three near a police post in Khan Younis, in the south, and three near the Netzarim corridor, further north. The territory's interior ministry said two of those killed in Khan Younis were police officers.

An October 2025 ceasefire backed by the United States halted major fighting in Gaza, but it has not stopped Israeli attacks on the territory. At least 1,600 Palestinians and five Israeli soldiers have been killed since the ceasefire took effect, according to respective Palestinian and Israeli tallies. More than two years of war have killed tens of thousands of Palestinians, caused a hunger crisis and displaced almost the entire population, according to health officials and aid agencies.

Key points

  • A strike on a home in the al-Shati refugee camp early on Wednesday killed Colonel Mohammed Abu al-Ouf, his wife and their two daughters, aged 15 and 13.
  • Gaza's interior ministry confirmed the deaths; the Israeli military said it had targeted a Hamas member.
  • The strike came a day after Naeem Abu Nahel, head of Hamas's internal security apparatus, was killed in western Gaza City.
  • At least 14 Palestinians were killed across Gaza in the past 24 hours; at least seven were killed on Tuesday.
