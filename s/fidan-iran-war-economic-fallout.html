Turkey Warns Iran War's Economic Burden Is Turning Destructive and Urges a US-Iran Deal

Turkey's foreign minister has warned that the economic fallout from the war on Iran is growing heavier for countries around the world, saying a deal between Washington and Tehran is needed to ease the strain and reopen the Strait of Hormuz.

Hakan Fidan, speaking to Turkish broadcaster TGRT Haber on Saturday, said the economic burden brought by the war and the failure to resolve it was getting harder every day for many countries. He warned it was heading toward having very destructive effects, and said a ceasefire was needed to start reversing the damage.

Fidan said a potential agreement between the United States and Iran mattered for far more than reopening the Strait of Hormuz, the waterway through which a large share of the world's oil moves. Such a deal, he said, could also help roll back global financial crises somewhat. He added that Ankara was doing everything it could to help the effort succeed.

Diplomatic efforts to reopen the strait remain active even though the process appears deadlocked, Fidan said. He noted that he had recently discussed the crisis with Qatar's prime minister and foreign minister, Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani, and said mediation was continuing.

Fidan also outlined a two-track approach to the disputes around Iran, arguing that questions over Tehran's nuclear programme could be handled within American and United Nations frameworks. He added that Ankara was keeping the humanitarian crisis in Gaza on the global agenda despite the other crises competing for attention.

The war began in February, when the United States and Israel attacked Iran, and fighting has since spread across the region, with Iranian strikes on American positions in Bahrain and continued attacks by the Houthis. Fidan's warning adds to growing concern among governments in the region that the conflict's economic damage is spreading well beyond the battlefield.

Key points

  • Turkey's foreign minister warned the Iran war's economic burden is growing heavier every day and heading toward very destructive effects.
  • Fidan said a US-Iran deal could ease global financial strain, not just reopen the Strait of Hormuz.
  • Mediation to reopen the strait continues despite an apparent deadlock; he recently discussed the crisis with Qatar's prime minister.
  • He proposed a two-track approach, with Iran's nuclear questions handled through American and United Nations frameworks.
