Albania Denies Role in Libya Ship Attack as Kremlin Vows Strikes on Ukraine Will Continue

Albania has denied any part in the sea drone attack on Russian ships off Libya, as the Kremlin said Russia would keep up large scale strikes on Ukraine and a senior Libyan official called the attack tantamount to a declaration of war.

Ukraine said on Monday that its forces sank the Russian navy corvette Merkury at Benghazi port in Libya with seven sea drones, and destroyed a Russian Ka-27 combat helicopter with it. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said a second target was also hit, the Russian dry cargo ship MV Angara, which he said Moscow used to transport weapons. His security service published video of the operation, saying the drone boats had been launched from a mother ship. Satellite images taken on Sunday showed most of the Merkury capsized and sunk, with only its bow still above water.

Albania rejected suggestions that the attack had been launched from its territory, saying it played no part and its land was not used to launch or help the attack, and that the claims came with no verifiable evidence. Libyan military officials had said an initial investigation pointed to a base run by Ukrainian forces in western Libya, with at least four drones intercepted and two striking the warship. The eastern based government in Libya said the explosion happened aboard the vessel while it was docked at Benghazi to refuel and take on supplies before sailing back to Russia.

The Kremlin responded on Monday by saying Russia would continue large scale strikes on Ukraine, in its first reaction after Kyiv claimed the attacks off Libya and in the Gulf of Finland over the weekend. Spokesman Dmitry Peskov declined direct comment on Zelenskyy’s claim, saying the defence ministry was handling it, but said Moscow had taken note. On the Baltic attack, in which the Russian cargo ship Sparta II was set on fire by drones on Saturday while sailing to Kaliningrad, Peskov called it a highly dangerous spread of terrorist activity.

In Libya, Musa al-Koni, deputy head of the Tripoli based Presidential Council, held Ukraine fully responsible for the strikes and described them as effectively a declaration of war on Libya, according to Russia’s state news agency. The United Nations mission in Libya on Monday called for a swift, independent and open investigation into the attacks, warning against steps that could pull the country into broader regional conflicts. Medical sources said ten people, including Russians and Libyans, were slightly injured, and no deaths had been reported. The story is still developing.

Key points

  • Albania denied any involvement in the attack on Russian ships off Libya, saying its territory was neither used nor made available to launch or facilitate it.
  • Ukraine said seven sea drones sank the corvette Merkury at Benghazi and hit the cargo ship MV Angara, and published video of the operation; satellite images show the Merkury capsized.
  • The Kremlin said Russia would keep up large scale strikes on Ukraine and called the Baltic drone attack a very dangerous trend, while declining direct comment on the Libya claim.
  • A senior Libyan official held Ukraine fully responsible and called the strikes tantamount to a declaration of war; the UN mission in Libya called for an independent investigation.
