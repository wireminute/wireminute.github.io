Zelenskyy Says Ukraine Sank Russian Warship in Libya With Seven Sea Drones

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has said Ukrainian forces sank the Russian navy corvette Merkury at Benghazi port in Libya with seven sea drones, in Kyiv’s first formal claim of responsibility for the strike.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said on Monday that Ukrainian forces sank the Russian navy corvette Merkury off the coast of Libya, using seven Ukrainian-made naval drones. He said the corvette, one of Russia’s newest warships, commissioned in 2023, was sunk in the port of Benghazi, and a Russian Ka-27 combat helicopter was destroyed along with it. Zelenskyy described the operation as a joint effort by Ukraine’s security service and its armed forces, calling it an example of Ukraine’s response to Russia’s war.

Zelenskyy said a second target was also hit: the Russian dry cargo ship MV Angara, which he said Russia used to transport weapons. Satellite imagery provider Vantor said images taken on Sunday showed most of the Merkury had capsized and sunk, with only its bow still visible above water, smoke rising from part of the superstructure and debris scattered along the pier. Libyan military officials said several sea drones were launched at the ship from a base run by Ukrainian forces in western Libya; at least four were intercepted and destroyed, while two hit the warship.

The eastern-based government said the explosion happened aboard the vessel while it was stopped at Benghazi port to take on fuel and supplies on its way back to Russia. Medical sources said ten people, including Russians and Libyans, were slightly injured and were receiving treatment, and no deaths had been reported. In a second attack on Sunday evening, another sea drone hit a Malta-flagged commercial vessel off the port of Benghazi, and the Benghazi health ministry said the injured crew had been rescued and were receiving treatment. Libyan naval and air forces destroyed at least 12 other sea drones off the coast that evening.

The United Nations mission in Libya on Monday called for an immediate, independent and transparent investigation into the attacks, warning against actions that could drag the country into wider regional conflicts. The eastern-based government called the attack an affront to Libya’s sovereignty and a danger to shipping safety, vowing legal and diplomatic steps against those responsible, and ordered a joint investigation with Russia. A day earlier, a Russian cargo ship was hit by a drone in the eastern Baltic Sea, in an attack Finland’s border guard said was Ukrainian. Libya’s eastern administration stressed that Libya was not a party to the war between Russia and Ukraine. The story is still developing.

Key points

  • He said a second target, the Russian dry cargo ship MV Angara, which he said was used to transport weapons, was also hit.
