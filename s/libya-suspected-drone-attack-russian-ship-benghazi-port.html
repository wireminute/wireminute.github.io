Satellite Images Show Russian Corvette Mostly Sunk After Benghazi Drone Strike

Satellite images show a Russian navy corvette has mostly capsized and sunk at Benghazi port in eastern Libya after a sea-drone strike on Sunday, as a second attack hit a Malta-flagged merchant vessel off the coast.

Satellite imagery provider Vantor said images taken on Sunday showed most of the vessel, identified as the Russian navy corvette Merkury, had capsized and sunk, with only its bow still visible above water, smoke rising from part of the superstructure and debris scattered along the pier. Libyan military officials said several sea drones were launched at the ship from a base run by Ukrainian forces in western Libya; at least four were intercepted and destroyed, while two hit the warship. The eastern-based government said the explosion happened aboard the vessel while it was stopped at Benghazi port to take on fuel and supplies on its way back to Russia, under a maritime cooperation arrangement between the two countries.

Medical sources said ten people, including Russians and Libyans, were slightly injured and were receiving treatment in Benghazi, and no deaths had been reported. There was no immediate reaction from Russian authorities. Reports citing sources said Ukraine's security service conducted the strike, and a source familiar with the matter said two Russian cargo ships carrying weapons docked in Benghazi were also hit. Local sources told Al Jazeera the warship was carrying weapons, ammunition and drones. Officials have not confirmed the identity of the ship, and no one has formally claimed responsibility for the attack.

In a second attack on Sunday evening, another sea drone hit a Malta-flagged commercial vessel off the port of Benghazi, and the Benghazi health ministry said the injured crew had been rescued and were receiving treatment. Libyan naval and air forces destroyed at least 12 other sea drones off the coast on Sunday evening, according to local television reports. Earlier reports had spoken of a fire in an ammunition store at the naval base, but the government said the explosion happened on board the ship, and civil defence teams urged people to stay away until the fire was brought under control.

The eastern-based government called the attack an affront to Libya's sovereignty and a danger to shipping safety, vowing legal and diplomatic steps against those responsible, and ordered a joint investigation with Russia. A day earlier, a Russian cargo ship was hit by a drone in the eastern Baltic Sea, in an attack Finland's border guard said was Ukrainian. Libya's eastern administration stressed that Libya was not a party to the war between Russia and Ukraine. The story is still developing.
