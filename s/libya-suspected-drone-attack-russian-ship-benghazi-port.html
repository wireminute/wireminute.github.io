Ukraine Defends Benghazi Ship Strikes as Self-Defence as Two Libyan Sailors Die

Ukraine has defended its sea drone attack on Russian ships off Libya as a lawful act of self-defence, as Libya said two of its sailors had died of wounds from the strike and France backed Kyiv's position.

Ukraine's Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha said on Tuesday that both Russian naval vessels and cargo ships carrying military equipment were valid targets, and that the right to self-defence in Article 51 of the United Nations Charter applied in international waters as well as on land. He said Ukraine was not dragging anyone into the war or transferring it anywhere, and that its actions were aimed at countering Russian aggression and were not intended to harm Libya or its people.

The eastern based Libyan National Army said on Tuesday that two of its naval personnel had died from injuries sustained in the attack on Benghazi port. The deaths were the first reported fatalities from the operation, in which Ukraine said seven sea drones sank the Russian corvette Merkury before dawn on Sunday and hit the cargo ship MV Angara. Satellite images taken on Sunday showed most of the Merkury capsized, with only its bow still above water. Earlier reports had said ten people, including Russians and Libyans, were slightly injured.

France's Foreign Minister Jean-Noel Barrot said on Wednesday that Ukraine had been exercising its absolute right to self-defense by attacking the two Russian ships. He told broadcaster TF1 that any country bombarded day and night by an invader would defend itself. The backing from Paris came a day after Tripoli's Government of National Unity condemned the operation, saying Libya would not accept being drawn into the Russia-Ukraine conflict, and asked whether Libyan territory had been used to plan it.

Albania has denied any part in the attack, saying it played no role and its land was not used to launch or help it. The Kremlin has not commented directly on the Libya claim, but said Russia would keep up large scale strikes on Ukraine. In Libya, Musa al-Koni, deputy head of the Tripoli based Presidential Council, held Ukraine fully responsible and described the strikes as effectively a declaration of war. The United Nations mission in Libya has called for a swift, independent and open investigation into the attacks, warning against steps that could pull the country into broader regional conflicts. The story is still developing.

Key points

  • Ukraine's foreign minister said the Benghazi sea drone attack was lawful self-defence under Article 51 of the UN Charter, and that Russian warships and military cargo ships were valid targets.
  • Two Libyan naval personnel died of wounds from the attack, the first deaths reported; Ukraine said seven sea drones sank the corvette Merkury and hit the cargo ship MV Angara.
  • France's foreign minister said on Wednesday that Ukraine was exercising its absolute right to self-defense, backing Kyiv a day after Tripoli condemned the operation.
  • The UN mission in Libya has called for an independent investigation; the Kremlin has not commented directly on the Libya claim but says its strikes on Ukraine will continue.
