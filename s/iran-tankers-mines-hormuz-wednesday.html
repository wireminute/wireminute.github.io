Iran Says Tankers and LNG Carrier Caught Fire After Mine Strikes in Strait of Hormuz

Iran says two oil tankers and a ship carrying liquefied natural gas struck naval mines and caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday, in the latest disruption to shipping through the world's most important oil waterway.

The Revolutionary Guards' navy said two oil tankers exploded and caught fire early on Wednesday after hitting mines on what it called an unauthorised route through the strait, and a liquefied natural gas carrier hit a mine and caught fire later in the day. Iran's army separately said an oil tanker struck a mine and caught fire in a restricted zone east of the strait. The statements said the vessels had switched off their positioning systems.

There is no independent confirmation of the reports, and no vessel was named. A container ship also stopped on Tuesday after hitting a mine near the strait, according to the Guards. Iran has warned ships to coordinate passage with its forces, while the International Maritime Organization's head said transiting the strait remains unsafe and ships should avoid it. Oil prices rose sharply, with Brent crude finishing Wednesday 3.5 percent higher.

Key points

  • Iran says two oil tankers were left burning in the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday after running over naval mines, and a liquefied natural gas carrier also caught fire after hitting a mine later in the day.
  • Iran's statements said the vessels were on unauthorised routes with their positioning systems switched off; the reports could not be independently verified and no vessel was named.
  • The shipping disruption pushed oil prices sharply higher, with Brent crude finishing Wednesday 3.5 percent higher, as maritime authorities warned ships to avoid the strait.
