India Says It Killed Kashmir's Last Local Militant, but Experts Say the Rebels Have Adapted

India's home minister has declared that the last local armed fighter in Indian-administered Kashmir is dead, after security forces killed a commander in a mosque siege last week, but analysts and former police officers say the armed campaign has not ended, only changed shape.

Security forces spent a week hunting Mohammad Lateef Bhat before cornering him in a mosque in Zangalpora village in Kulgam district, where a gunfight broke out and he was killed on November 18. Officials describe him as a senior figure in Lashkar-e-Taiba, the Pakistan-based armed group, and accuse him of opening fire on paramilitary troops a week earlier alongside another fighter, leaving three soldiers wounded. That second fighter was killed in the days after the shooting.

Home Minister Amit Shah said at a public event that Bhat had been the final local fighter still active in the valley. “This development marks a significant change in the security situation in the [Kashmir] valley. It is a matter of pride for us,” Shah said. “No Kashmiri youth is currently involved in terrorist activities in the valley.” Police in Jammu and Kashmir said Lashkar-e-Taiba was left with no local fighters for the first time since the armed uprising began in 1989.

Analysts and retired security officers, however, say the conflict has not gone away, only changed form. They point to new front groups with different names, fighters who never appear on official lists, more foreign fighters, heavier use of encrypted messaging, and attacks moving out of the Kashmir valley and into the forests of Jammu further south.

Israr Khan, who once served as a deputy inspector general in Jammu and Kashmir's police force, called Bhat's death a big win for security forces and a serious blow to Lashkar-e-Taiba. But he said fighters could stay active without appearing in police records. “Through my experience, I can say he certainly is not the last militant because there are militants who are active but are not in police records,” he said. Police use the label “hybrid militants” for people they accuse of carrying out attacks while remaining outside official lists and living ordinary lives, a category that appeared around 2020. New groups such as The Resistance Front and the People's Anti-Fascist Front have also emerged, presenting themselves as locally rooted while, analysts say, allowing militancy to continue even as open local recruitment falls.

Key points

  • Security forces killed Mohammad Lateef Bhat, described as a Lashkar-e-Taiba commander, in a mosque siege in Zangalpora village, Kulgam, on November 18 after a seven-day search.
  • Home Minister Amit Shah said Bhat was the last local armed fighter, and police said Lashkar-e-Taiba had no local fighters left for the first time since the uprising began in 1989.
  • Analysts say the conflict has changed form rather than ended, with groups using new names, fighters staying off official lists, and violence shifting into the forests of Jammu.
  • A former senior police officer said Bhat was “certainly” not the last militant, because fighters can stay active without appearing in police records.
