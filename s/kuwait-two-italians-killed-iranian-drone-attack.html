Iran Denies Role in Kuwait Drone Attack That Killed Two Italians

Iran's armed forces have denied any role in the drone attack on Kuwait that killed two Italian technicians, as Kuwait condemned what it called a flagrant violation of its sovereignty and Italy mourned the two men.

The two men, civilian technicians working for Rheinmetall Italia, the Italian arm of German defence group Rheinmetall, were killed on Thursday after drones targeted an air-defence detection station in Kuwait where they were working on antennas for the Skyguard air-defence system. Kuwait's Defence Ministry said its forces intercepted several drones and that falling shrapnel injured four people: the two Italians, a Kuwaiti army captain and a Pakistani national. The two Italians later died of their injuries.

Antonio Tajani, Italy's foreign minister, told the Senate in Rome that the attack happened at around 2pm, and Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni expressed her sorrow for what she called the tragic deaths of the two men. Kuwait's foreign minister telephoned Tajani to offer condolences, and Kuwait's Foreign Ministry denounced the strike as a grave breach of Kuwait's sovereignty and international law.

Tehran rejected the accusation. Iran's armed forces spokesman, Sardar Shekarchi, said Iran had carried out no military operations in the region and called the claims a cover for hostile actions, pointing to Israel and the United States. The denial came as the Gulf Cooperation Council condemned what it called a brutal attack and said Iran bore full responsibility for its consequences.

The strike is the latest in a series of Iranian attacks on Kuwait and Bahrain in the past week, as Tehran targets Gulf states that host American military assets, and it adds strain to mediation efforts by Qatar and Pakistan aimed at easing the confrontation over the Strait of Hormuz. The two Italians' names have not yet been made public.

Key points

  • Two Italian technicians working for Rheinmetall Italia were killed in Kuwait on Thursday after Iranian drones targeted an air-defence station and were intercepted.
  • Kuwait said falling shrapnel injured four people, two Italians, a Kuwaiti army captain and a Pakistani national; the two Italians later died of their injuries.
  • Iran's armed forces denied any role in the attack, calling the accusation a cover for hostile actions; Kuwait and the Gulf Cooperation Council condemned the strike.
  • The deaths add strain to mediation efforts by Qatar and Pakistan over the Strait of Hormuz confrontation; the victims' names have not been made public.
