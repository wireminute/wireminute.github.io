Iran Points at Ukraine and Europe's War Role After Kuwait Strike Kills Two Italians

Tehran's denial of any role in the drone strike on a Kuwaiti military site that killed two Italian technicians has taken a new turn, with voices aligned with the Iranian government now pointing at Ukraine and at Europe's part in the war, as Kuwait counts 925 Iranian missiles and drones fired at it since the fighting began.

Tehran's denial of any role in Thursday's drone strike on a Kuwaiti military site has taken a new turn, with voices aligned with Tehran now pointing at Ukraine and Europe's part in the war. The two Italian technicians killed in the attack, Antonio Saccoccio, 65, and Emanuele Lo Russo, 36, were maintaining antennas for Kuwait's Skyguard air-defence system when shrapnel from intercepted drones killed them. A Kuwaiti captain and a Pakistani national were also hurt.

Iran's armed forces spokesman said it had carried out no military operations in the region, and Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi called the incident suspicious, like a precooked recipe for war, and offered a joint investigation. The denial stands out: Tehran has openly claimed its Gulf strikes through the war, five in November alone, and did not deny bombing Kuwait on November 18. Analysts say the difference is who was killed: Italian civilians at a Kuwaiti site, not a US base, undercutting Tehran's claim it hits only American military targets.

Some in Tehran now suspect Ukraine. A professor at the University of Tehran said parts of the establishment believed Kyiv was trying to raise its value to Washington, and a conservative commentator said Ukraine staged the attack as a false flag. Media aligned with Tehran cited an unnamed source claiming Ukrainian operatives had spread drones across the region. No evidence has been presented for the claim. Other analysts say an Iraqi armed faction is the leading alternative suspect, since the drone was Iranian-built, of a type also used by Iran-backed militias. The Houthis are a distant second, and the US, Israel and Ukraine claims come with no evidence.

Kuwait has summoned Iran's ambassador with a formal protest, and the Gulf Cooperation Council condemned the attack and blamed Iran. A professor at Kuwait University said Iran had fired 925 missiles and drones at Kuwait since the war began, and Kuwait was documenting every attack and would likely seek compensation. The European Union's chief called for more pressure on Tehran. Analysts say Europe is already in the war as a supplier, since the two men were maintaining a Gulf air-defence system, and expect sanctions and defensive support, not European strikes. The deaths come as mediators have only weeks to find terms Washington will accept, with Washington ruling out a return to military action until early next year, while Qatar warns the attacks put diplomacy at risk.

Key points

  • Tehran now points at Ukraine and Europe's role, calling the strike a false flag, while Kuwait and Italy blame Iran for the attack.
  • The denial is unusual: Iran openly claimed five strikes on Gulf neighbours in November alone and did not deny bombing Kuwait on November 18.
  • Analysts say an Iraqi armed faction is the leading alternative suspect; the claim against Ukraine comes with no evidence.
  • Kuwait is documenting every attack and will likely seek compensation; Europe is expected to answer with sanctions and more defensive support, not strikes.
