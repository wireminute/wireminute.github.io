Italians Killed in Kuwait Drone Attack Named as Strike Hit Several Bases

The two Italian technicians killed in a drone attack in Kuwait have been named as Antonio Saccoccio and Emanuele Lo Russo, as new details emerged that the strike hit several military sites and destroyed an air-defence unit.

The two men have been named by Italian media as Antonio Saccoccio, 65, from Formia, and Emanuele Lo Russo, 36, from Rome. Both were civilian technicians for Rheinmetall Italia and were carrying out maintenance on detection antennas for the Skyguard air-defence system used by Kuwait's armed forces. They were killed on Thursday after drones targeted an air-defence detection station in Kuwait, and falling shrapnel left them fatally wounded. A Kuwaiti army captain and a Pakistani national were also hurt.

Italy's foreign minister, Antonio Tajani, told the Senate in Rome that several drones of Iranian origin targeted Kuwaiti military installations at around 2pm on Thursday. Two people familiar with the assault said multiple bases came under attack and that one Skyguard unit was destroyed. Tajani added that the drone used in the attack was manufactured in Iran. Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni expressed her sorrow over the deaths.

Tehran rejected the accusation. Iran's armed forces spokesman, Sardar Shekarchi, said Iran had carried out no military operations in the region and dismissed the claims as a cover for hostile actions by Israel and the United States. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi then telephoned his Kuwaiti and Italian counterparts on Thursday to repeat the denial himself. He called the incident suspicious and said it looked like a precooked recipe for war, while insisting Iran had no role in it and offering to work with both countries to establish what happened.

Kuwait summoned Iran's ambassador on Friday and handed him a formal protest note over what it called continued Iranian attacks, the foreign ministry said. Kuwait's Foreign Ministry called the strike a grave breach of its sovereignty and international law, and the Gulf Cooperation Council condemned the attack and said Iran bore full responsibility. Italy said it would still lodge a formal complaint with Iran's embassy, and Britain and Saudi Arabia condemned the strike.

The European Union's chief, Ursula von der Leyen, called for more pressure on Tehran after the two Italians were killed. They are the first European civilians killed since the United States-Israel war on Iran began in late February. The strike is the latest in a series of Iranian attacks on Kuwait and Bahrain in the past week, as Tehran targets Gulf states hosting American military assets, and it adds strain to mediation efforts by Qatar and Pakistan aimed at easing the confrontation over the Strait of Hormuz.

Key points

  • The two Italians killed on Thursday have been named as Antonio Saccoccio, 65, and Emanuele Lo Russo, 36, civilian technicians for Rheinmetall Italia.
  • The attack hit several military sites in Kuwait, and one Skyguard air-defence unit was destroyed, according to reports.
  • Iran denies any role and has offered a joint investigation with Kuwait and Italy; Kuwait and Italy both blame Tehran.
  • Kuwait summoned Iran's ambassador on Friday and handed him a protest note; Britain, Saudi Arabia and the EU condemned the strike.
