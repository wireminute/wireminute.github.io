Teenager's Death in Colombia Raid Fuels Questions Over President's Security Crackdown

The family of an 18-year-old killed in a military operation in northern Colombia says he was a civilian farmworker, not a fighter, in a case that has become the sharpest test yet of the new president's hardline campaign against armed groups.

Ricardo Andres Martinez Rodriguez died on September 21 during an operation in the rural Quebrada del Sol area near Santa Marta, aimed at Autodefensas Conquistadoras de la Sierra Nevada, a criminal group active in the region. The government presented the raid as a success, saying seven people were killed and three captured, among them a commander known as Cholo. His family says the teenager had travelled to the area for cattle work and had no connection to the group.

His mother identified his body after it was taken to the city of Barranquilla, and forensic officials confirmed his identity through fingerprints, saying he had died from a gunshot wound to the chest. Relatives cite a witness who says a uniformed officer seized the teenager by the arm and shot him, though the family acknowledges it has no independent proof of that account. Unverified claims circulating online have linked him to a figure known as Pinocho, which the family rejects.

The death has raised wider questions about a security strategy that relies heavily on airstrikes and ground operations against armed groups. Since the president took office in August, at least 46 suspected rebels have been killed in such strikes, seven of them minors, according to figures given by the defence minister in October. Two further allegations of extrajudicial killings have emerged since, and the case has revived comparisons with the false positives scandal, in which civilians were killed and presented as rebel fighters.

President Abelardo de la Espriella has kept public backing for the approach, with his approval rating above 48 percent in November, and his government says the campaign answers pressure from Washington to dismantle groups the United States calls narcoterrorists. But the teenager's mother has called for an end to bombardments in rural areas and for better intelligence before operations, and analysts warn that armed groups have become harder to tell apart from civilians. She says she wants to know why her son was killed by the same authorities meant to protect people like him.

Key points

  • Ricardo Andres Martinez Rodriguez, 18, was killed in a military operation near Santa Marta on September 21; his family says he was a civilian farmworker hired for cattle work.
  • The government says seven people were killed in the raid on an armed group; forensic officials confirmed the teenager died of a gunshot wound to the chest.
