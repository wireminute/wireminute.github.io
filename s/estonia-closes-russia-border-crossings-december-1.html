Russia Warns Estonia Its Actions Are on the Verge of Triggering Open War

Russia has accused Estonia of hostile actions that are on the verge of triggering open war, summoning Tallinn's envoy in Moscow to deliver a formal protest over the Baltic state's decision to shut its border with Russia.

Russia's Foreign Ministry issued the warning on Monday after calling in Estonia's charge d'affaires, Marek Uhtegi, to protest against the planned closure of the land border. In a statement, the ministry said the Estonian diplomat had been told that Tallinn's provocative and extremely dangerous actions were on the verge of triggering open war with Russia. Moscow said it reserved the right to take comprehensive political and diplomatic measures to protect its interests and its citizens.

The ministry set out a list of grievances against Estonia, a member of NATO and the European Union. It accused Tallinn of quietly allowing Ukraine to use Estonian airspace to launch drone attacks on targets in northwestern Russia, of trying to obstruct Russian cargo shipping in the Baltic Sea, of banning the transit of Russian and Belarusian grain, of seeking to ban the Russian language and of threatening to close the Russian Orthodox Church. It said the border closure would backfire on Tallinn and its allies in the EU and NATO.

Estonia's Foreign Ministry did not answer every accusation but firmly denied the claim about its airspace. It said Estonia had never allowed, and would never allow, its territory to be used for military operations by another country, and it declined to engage with what it called Russian propaganda narratives. Russia has long accused Estonia and other Baltic states of letting Ukrainian drones fly through their airspace.

The confrontation follows Estonia's announcement that it will close all of its road border crossings with Russia for at least six months from 1 December, after an attempted arson attack on Threod Systems, a drone maker near Tallinn, that Estonian security services blame on Russia's GRU military intelligence agency. Eight people were detained over the attempt, which was stopped by the firm's own security staff and caused only minor damage. Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna said the closure was the next step after Russia's aggression against Ukraine and a rising pattern of sabotage and hybrid operations in Europe. Moscow has denied any role in the arson attempt, and its spokeswoman Maria Zakharova has called the border closure a new Iron Curtain.

It is the second suspected Russian attack on an Estonian defence manufacturer this year, after an arson attack in August on Milrem Robotics, a maker of unmanned ground vehicles, which Tallinn also blamed on Moscow.

Key points

  • Russia summoned Estonia's envoy in Moscow and warned that Tallinn's actions are on the verge of triggering open war.
  • Moscow accused Estonia of letting Ukraine use its airspace for drone attacks, a claim Tallinn firmly denied.
  • Estonia will close its road crossings with Russia from 1 December for at least six months after an arson attempt on a drone maker it blames on Russia's GRU.
  • Moscow said it reserved the right to political and diplomatic measures; it denies any role in the arson attempt.
