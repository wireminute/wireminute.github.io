Estonia to Shut All Russia Border Crossings From December 1 After Drone Maker Attack

Estonia will close all three of its border crossings with Russia from 1 December, Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna announced on Sunday, after an attempted arson at a drone maker that Tallinn blames on Russian military intelligence.

Tsahkna announced the decision in a post on social media, saying Estonia had clear evidence that Russia's GRU military intelligence service ordered a sabotage attack against a defence industry company. He said hostile actions against Estonia would carry consequences and that closing the crossings would limit Russia's ability to operate inside the country and to evade sanctions. He gave no end date for the closure.

Estonian authorities detained eight people overnight in connection with the attempted arson at the Threod Systems drone plant in Viimsi, near the capital Tallinn. The attempt took place in the early hours of Saturday and was stopped by the firm's own security staff. Prosecutors said the attack caused only minor damage and no injuries, and the company said production was unaffected. Prosecutors have asked for five of the eight detained people, who live in Latvia, to be held in custody while the investigation continues.

The head of Estonia's Internal Security Service, Margo Palloson, said his agency's findings pointed to the GRU. Moscow has not commented on the accusations or on the border decision. Russia has regularly denied accusations from European governments that it is behind sabotage operations.

Estonia currently has three open crossings with Russia, at Narva, Luhamaa and Koidula, which take pedestrians and freight traffic. The closure will end cross-border passage at all three from 1 December, with the interior minister due to put a formal proposal to the government next week and the prime minister instructing officials to prepare the measures. It is the second suspected Russian attack on an Estonian defence manufacturer this year: in August, an arson attack on Milrem Robotics, a maker of unmanned ground vehicles, was also blamed by Tallinn on Moscow.

Key points

  • Estonia will close its Narva, Luhamaa and Koidula crossings with Russia from 1 December, with no end date given.
  • Eight people were detained over an attempted arson at drone maker Threod Systems, which was stopped by the firm's security staff.
  • Estonian security services say Russia's GRU military intelligence ordered the attack; Moscow has not commented.
  • It is the second suspected Russian attack on an Estonian defence firm this year, after an August arson at Milrem Robotics.
