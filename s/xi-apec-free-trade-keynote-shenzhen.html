Xi Pitches Free Trade at APEC Ahead of First-Ever Meeting With Trump

China's Xi Jinping used the opening of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen on Wednesday to champion free trade and open markets, hours before Donald Trump was due to arrive for the first three-way meeting of the leaders of China, Russia and the United States.

Speaking at the APEC chief executives' summit in the southern Chinese tech hub, Xi said the region's economies should defend a trading system built on the rules of the World Trade Organization, keep trade and investment free and open, and keep global supply chains running smoothly. He said openness brought progress while shutting others out led only to falling behind, and he urged leaders to press ahead with a free trade area spanning the Asia-Pacific, alongside deeper cooperation on digital trade, cross-border online commerce and smarter customs.

Xi promised China would cut tariffs further and open its markets wider, and said Beijing would push ahead with difficult reforms to its rules, regulations and standards. He also called for closer work on artificial intelligence and the shift to greener energy. He did not name the United States, its tariffs or its new curbs on advanced chips, but his remarks landed as a clear answer to Washington's trade policy, with Trump having raised tariffs on China and other partners and tightened access to the advanced semiconductors that power artificial intelligence.

Putin followed Xi on stage and told the same business audience that Western sanctions had failed to halt Russia's growth, arguing that tariffs and export controls could deter but not crush a rival economy. The two men had opened talks of their own earlier in the day, their fifth meeting this year, ahead of Thursday's three-way session with Trump.

Trump was travelling from Manila, where he had attended a summit of South-East Asian nations, and was expected in Shenzhen later on Wednesday, his first time attending the APEC leaders' meeting in person since 2017. Thursday brings a bilateral meeting with Xi, their third this year, and then the first-ever three-way talks with Putin, where nuclear arms and the war against Iran are expected to be raised. The United States and China extended their tariff truce through January 2027 at a summit in September, but disputes over advanced chips and export controls remain unresolved.

Key points

  • Xi told the APEC business summit in Shenzhen that the region must defend free and open trade, a rules-based system centred on the World Trade Organization, and stable global supply chains.
  • China will cut tariffs further, widen market access and press ahead with deep reform of its rules, regulations and standards, Xi said.
  • Xi did not name the United States directly, but his remarks were widely read as an answer to Trump's tariffs and new curbs on advanced chip exports to China.
  • Trump arrives in Shenzhen later on Wednesday; he meets Xi bilaterally and then joins the first-ever three-way talks with Xi and Putin on Thursday.
