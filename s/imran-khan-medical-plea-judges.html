Imran Khan Tells Judges He Fears He May Not Survive Prison Without Urgent Care

Pakistan's jailed former prime minister Imran Khan has written to the country's most senior judges asking for urgent medical treatment for himself and his wife, warning that his health has deteriorated so badly in prison that he fears he may not survive.

The open letter, dated November 16 and addressed to the chief justices of the Supreme Court, the Federal Constitutional Court and the Islamabad High Court, was released on Monday by Khan's lawyer, who said Khan gave him the instructions during a three-hour meeting at Adiala jail on November 11. It is the first such appeal Khan has made since he fell ill earlier this year.

Khan, 74, says he has been held in solitary confinement for up to 22 hours a day since December 20, 2025, when he and his wife Bushra Bibi were sentenced to 17 years in prison in one of several cases against them. He says he has lost 85 percent of the vision in his right eye, that his blood pressure climbed to 192 and 193 on November 10, and that doctors have warned him he is highly vulnerable to a brain haemorrhage and cardiac arrest, with damage already done to his heart. He wrote that his condition was taking him to a place "from which I may not return alive".

He says the Supreme Court ordered on August 18 that he be moved to Shifa International Hospital in Islamabad within two days and examined by a specialist medical board, but that the order was never carried out. Instead, he was taken to the government-run Pakistan Institute of Medical Sciences for an eye examination and returned to jail within hours. He was taken to hospital again late on November 10 for an eye injection, and the hospital said he was clinically stable.

Khan also appealed for his wife, saying she could lose her sight permanently without another operation. Her lawyers applied to the Islamabad High Court on Monday for her complete medical record and her immediate transfer to a specialist hospital.

The government rejects the claims. Ministers say Khan is receiving all the facilities and medical care he needs, and one minister said he was examined in August by doctors including specialists from Shifa and declared fit. Khan has been in prison since August 2023. His party says the convictions are politically motivated; all but one have been suspended or overturned, with appeals pending.

The courts have not yet responded to the letter. Khan wrote that he had never before sought relief on medical grounds, and that writing from his cell had become the only way his voice could leave the prison.
