Kyiv Reopens South Bridge to One-Way Traffic After Russian Strikes, North Bridge Still Closed

Kyiv has partly reopened its South Bridge to one-way traffic after Russian ballistic missiles damaged two of the capital's main river crossings overnight, while the North Bridge remains closed and hundreds of buildings are still without heating.

The South Bridge reopened to limited traffic in one direction, from the capital's left bank to its right bank, after specialists from the National Institute for Infrastructure Development inspected the structure. Vehicles weighing up to 10 metric tons and public transport vehicles of up to 18 metric tons are allowed to cross at regulated intervals. Traffic in the opposite direction remains closed because that section of the bridge was damaged in the overnight attack.

The North Bridge remains fully closed, with the authorities yet to say when it will reopen. Mayor Vitali Klitschko said experts were still analysing inspection data and carrying out calculations to assess the technical condition and load-bearing capacity of the damaged structures. He shared video showing damage to the North Bridge and said municipal workers were clearing the aftermath of the attack and carrying out repairs.

The strikes cut heating to 317 buildings in the Darnytskyi district after damage to the district heating network, the district administration said. One 34-year-old man was injured in the attack and taken to hospital. Metro trains remain suspended on the green line between Osokorky and Chervonyi Khutir stations, and trolleybus routes that crossed the bridges have been diverted.

Russia launched a second ballistic missile attack on the capital during the morning, and strikes were also reported in the Zaporizhzhia, Kharkiv and Cherkasy regions. Three people were injured in Cherkasy, local officials said. Moscow's defence ministry said its forces had struck sites across the Kyiv, Zaporizhzhia, Cherkasy and Dnipropetrovsk regions, among them energy plants, port facilities and ships.

The attack came hours before allies were due to meet in Brussels for the 37th session of the Ukraine Defence Contact Group, and a day after President Volodymyr Zelenskyy warned that Russia was preparing fresh strikes through Tuesday. Ukraine also launched drones toward the Moscow region overnight, with the city's mayor saying 20 were repelled, and struck the bridge linking Russia with Crimea for a second time in less than a day. Both of Kyiv's crossings have been hit repeatedly in recent weeks, putting the capital's river links under sustained strain.

Key points

  • Kyiv has partly reopened its South Bridge to one-way traffic after inspection; the opposite direction stays closed
  • The North Bridge remains closed with no reopening date as experts assess its load-bearing capacity
  • 317 buildings in one district are without heating and one man was injured; metro trains remain suspended on part of the green line
  • A second Russian missile attack hit the capital in the morning, and strikes were also reported in Zaporizhzhia, Kharkiv and Cherkasy
