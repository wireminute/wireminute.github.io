Riyadh's Tallest Tower Struck Again as 11 Injured in Financial District Attack

A projectile hit a tower in Riyadh's financial district on Tuesday, injuring 11 people in the second strike on the area in under a week, Saudi authorities said.

Saudi civil defence said 11 people were hurt when a projectile hit one of the towers in the King Abdullah Financial District on Tuesday. The injured were two Saudi citizens and nine foreign residents, two from India, three from Nepal and four from Egypt. The agency did not describe how serious the injuries were, and said emergency teams had reached the site and put approved response measures into place.

Major General Turki al-Malki, spokesman for the Saudi-led coalition, described the strike as a cowardly terrorist attack and said officials were still examining the scene. The coalition did not name the building that was hit, and it did not say who was behind the attack. Three witnesses at the scene said the 80-storey Public Investment Fund Tower had been struck. The tower, which houses the headquarters of the kingdom's sovereign wealth fund, is the tallest building in the capital.

The same tower was hit last Thursday, in a strike that damaged several floors and two neighbouring buildings without causing casualties. The Houthis said they carried out that attack, but they have said nothing about Tuesday's strike. Earlier in November a tower used by the state oil company was hit in the same district, and Riyadh's airport was struck repeatedly in October, including one attack that killed 14 people.

The Saudi-led coalition, which supports Yemen's government against the Houthis, has answered the strikes with air raids on Houthi targets inside Yemen. Local officials said a coalition strike on buses and trucks in the southwestern province of Lahj on Tuesday killed 26 people. The Houthis resumed attacks on Saudi targets in July, after the coalition first sent forces into Yemen's war in 2015.

Key points

  • A projectile struck a tower in Riyadh's King Abdullah Financial District on Tuesday, injuring 11 people.
  • The injured are two Saudi citizens and nine foreign residents: two from India, three from Nepal and four from Egypt.
  • Witnesses identified the building as the 80-storey Public Investment Fund Tower, the capital's tallest skyscraper and headquarters of the sovereign wealth fund.
  • It is the second strike on the district in under a week; the Houthis claimed the earlier attack but have not claimed this one.
