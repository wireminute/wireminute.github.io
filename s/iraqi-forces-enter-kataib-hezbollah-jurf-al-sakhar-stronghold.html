Iraqi Forces Enter Kataib Hezbollah Stronghold in Deal After Oil-Smuggling Standoff

Iraqi security forces have entered Jurf al-Sakhar, the stronghold of the Iran-aligned Kataib Hezbollah armed group, under a compromise deal that ends a standoff over alleged oil smuggling and restores state control to an area closed to government forces for about 12 years.

Iraqi security forces moved into Jurf al-Sakhar on Friday, taking up positions in the town that has served as the stronghold of Kataib Hezbollah, the Iran-aligned armed group, for more than a decade. Soldiers flying Iraqi flags drove into the centre of the town, in Babil province southwest of Baghdad, and set up a joint command headquarters. The deployment went ahead without any confrontation, and officials described it as smooth.

The move followed a compromise reached on Thursday after a week of rising tension. The government had accused Kataib Hezbollah of running an oil-smuggling network that siphoned hundreds of millions of dollars of state-owned crude through illicit refineries in the area. Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi had ordered the group to leave the town by Friday, warning that the state would reassert its authority. Under the deal, the group's fighters are expected to pull out to other parts of the country within a month, although a source in the group said it would not leave.

The force now in the town brings together an army brigade, a Federal Police battalion, an Energy Police regiment and two brigades from the Popular Mobilisation Forces, the state umbrella for armed groups. The Federal Police will run the new headquarters, and the Energy Police have begun securing an oil pipeline running through the area. Al-Zaidi told troops the deployment marked the start of asserting state authority across all of Iraq, adding that no weapons would be allowed outside state control.

Reports said the arrangement was brokered by senior Shi'ite politicians, with Iran's Quds Force commander, Esmail Qaani, helping to press the group to avoid a fight. The group keeps its weapons under the deal. Kataib Hezbollah, which the United States has branded a terrorist organisation, denies the smuggling claims. The deployment is understood to have followed pressure from the United States and its Gulf allies. Jurf al-Sakhar, also known as Jurf al-Nasr, has been closed to state forces for about 12 years, since the group helped recapture it from Islamic State.

Key points

  • Iraqi security forces deployed in Jurf al-Sakhar on Friday, entering the Kataib Hezbollah stronghold for the first time in about 12 years without a confrontation.
  • The deployment followed a compromise after the government accused the group of smuggling hundreds of millions of dollars of state oil; the fighters are expected to withdraw within a month, though the group says it will not leave.
  • An army brigade, Federal Police, Energy Police and two Popular Mobilisation Forces brigades now hold the town, with the Federal Police running a new joint headquarters.
