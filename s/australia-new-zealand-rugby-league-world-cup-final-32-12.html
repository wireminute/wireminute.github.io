Australia Beat New Zealand 32-12 to Win Rugby League World Cup for 13th Time

Australia beat New Zealand 32-12 in the Rugby League World Cup final in Brisbane on Sunday, winning the title for a 13th time and a fourth tournament in a row.

Liam Martin crossed after just 84 seconds, the fastest try ever scored in a World Cup final, after New Zealand kicked out on the full from the opening kick-off. Martin scored again before half-time, and Jack Bostock also crossed twice, as the hosts raced to a 24-6 lead at the break in front of 52,461 fans at Suncorp Stadium.

Bradman Best and Tom Trbojevic added further tries after the break, with Cameron Munster kicking four goals, as Australia pulled clear of a New Zealand side that had beaten them in the opening match of the tournament. Casey McLean and Isaiah Papali'i scored the Kiwis' tries, with Jamayne Isaako kicking both goals, but a disallowed try and a string of errors kept the visitors chasing the game.

The win extends Australia's unbeaten run in World Cup matches to 23 and gives coach Kevin Walters a first title in charge. It is the Kangaroos' fourth straight crown, after victories over New Zealand in 2013, England in 2017 and Samoa in 2022, and their first title on home soil since 2017. New Zealand's only World Cup triumph remains their famous upset of Australia at the same ground in 2008.

Full-back Reece Walsh was again outstanding for the hosts, and winger Bostock was named player of the match. Veteran James Tedesco, who captained Australia to the 2022 title, came off the bench late in his final Test match. Australia also won the women's final on the same day, completing a double for the hosts in Brisbane.

Key points

  • Australia beat New Zealand 32-12 at Suncorp Stadium in Brisbane to win the Rugby League World Cup for a 13th time.
  • Liam Martin scored after 84 seconds, the fastest try in a World Cup final; Martin and Jack Bostock both scored twice in a 24-6 half-time lead.
  • It is Australia's fourth straight title and their 23rd consecutive World Cup win; New Zealand's only title remains their 2008 upset at the same ground.
  • James Tedesco played his final Test match off the bench, and Australia also won the women's final on the same day.
