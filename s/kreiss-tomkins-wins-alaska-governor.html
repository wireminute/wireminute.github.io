Democrat Kreiss-Tomkins Wins Alaska Governor's Race, First Since 1998

Alaska voters have elected a Democrat as governor for the first time since 1998, handing former state lawmaker Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins a comeback victory that completes his party's sweep of statehouses in the midterm elections.

The race was called overnight, with Kreiss-Tomkins, 37, holding just over half of first-choice votes counted as ballots continued to be tallied. He defeated three Republicans: Bernadette Wilson, a businesswoman backed by President Donald Trump; Dave Bronson, a former mayor of Anchorage; and Treg Taylor, a former state attorney general. He passed 50 percent late on Thursday with most ballots counted, and held 50.8 percent of first-choice votes as of Friday, with the count still to be finalised.

The result gives Democrats the governor's office in 30 states, against 24 before the November 3 elections, after the party also flipped the governor's offices in Iowa, Georgia, Ohio and Texas. Alaska had spent eight years under Republican Governor Mike Dunleavy, barred by term limits from seeking another term.

Kreiss-Tomkins, from the fishing town of Sitka, is back in politics three years after he left the state legislature. He first won his seat by 32 votes after a recount, and he campaigned on working across party lines, saying he wanted a cabinet drawn from Republicans, Democrats and independents. He finished first in the open primary in August, ahead of a second Democrat who later withdrew from the race.

He will be sworn in on December 7, with Zac Johnson, an independent, as his lieutenant governor. The last Democrat to win the office, Tony Knowles, was elected in 1998 and left the post in 2002.

The count is not yet final, and the margin will shift as the remaining ballots are tallied. But with Kreiss-Tomkins past 50 percent, the outcome is settled: Alaska, long one of the hardest states for Democrats to win, will have a Democratic governor for the first time in 28 years.

Key points

  • Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins, 37, has won Alaska's governor's race — no Democrat has won the office since 1998.
  • He defeated three Republicans, including Bernadette Wilson, who was backed by President Donald Trump.
  • Democrats now hold 30 governorships, up from 24 before the November 3 midterm elections.
  • He will be sworn in on December 7, succeeding term-limited Republican Governor Mike Dunleavy, with independent Zac Johnson as lieutenant governor.
