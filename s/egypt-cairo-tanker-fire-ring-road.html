At Least 31 Killed as Fuel Tanker Overturns and Catches Fire on Cairo Ring Road

A fuel tanker overturned and caught fire on Cairo's ring road on Wednesday, killing at least 31 people and injuring 21, Egypt's health ministry said.

A tanker truck carrying petroleum products overturned and caught fire on a major ring road near Cairo on Wednesday, killing at least 31 people and injuring 21, according to the Egyptian Ministry of Health and Population. The ministry first reported 21 dead and later raised the toll to 30, then to 31, and said the toll was still preliminary and could change as rescue work continued.

The crash happened above Tunnel 10 in the Al-Warraq district of Giza, on the ring road that links Cairo, Giza and Qalyubia. The petroleum ministry said the privately operated tanker veered off the road and crashed before exploding. The fire spread to 27 cars and motorcycles, which were destroyed, according to a preliminary estimate from the public prosecutor's office.

The tanker driver was among those killed, local media reported. Civil protection teams have since extinguished the fire, and the road was being cleared. Nearly 40 ambulances were sent to the scene, and the injured were taken to three hospitals. The health ministry said it was continuing to monitor the incident and provide medical care to those hurt.

The ring road is the main bypass around greater Cairo, connecting the capital's highways with Giza and Qalyubia and carrying heavy goods traffic around the city. The bypass connects key highways across the greater Cairo area. Al-Warraq is a densely populated, working-class district on the west bank of the Nile, in the Imbaba area of Giza.

The ministry gave the updated toll in a statement on Wednesday. Officials have not yet said what caused the tanker to leave the road, and the incident was still developing.
