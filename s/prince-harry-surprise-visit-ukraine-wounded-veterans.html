Prince Harry Tells Wounded Ukrainian Veterans: 'You're Going to Win the War'

Prince Harry has made a surprise two-day visit to Ukraine, meeting wounded veterans and medics in the western city of Lviv and telling them they will win the war against Russia.

The Duke of Sussex, 42, spent Saturday and Sunday at events in Lviv, his fourth visit to Ukraine since Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022 and his second this year. The trip was kept secret until he had left the country for security reasons. He attended Ukraine's national Invictus Games heats and a conference for medics organised by Superhumans, a clinic that specialises in treating and rehabilitating soldiers wounded in the war.

At the Superhumans Centre he met veterans being fitted with prosthetic limbs, including Kostiantyn Vlasenko, 49, who cut off his own feet after being wounded and unable to reach help, and Artem, a drone pilot who lost both arms in an artillery attack and is learning to use his feet for everyday tasks while being fitted with hi-tech prosthetic arms. He also met Illia Pylypenko, 33, from Odesa, who suffered severe burns and lost a lung when the tank he was driving struck a mine.

"You're going to win the war and then you're going to be set up for success," he told veterans, adding that he had taken a risk to come. "Every single time I come back to Ukraine, I feel welcome, I feel warmth," he said. He called the veterans in the room "role models for society" with "so much to give", and promised that when the war is over and Ukraine wins, organisers will be ready to support holding an Invictus Games in the country.

Harry founded the Invictus Games in 2014 while still serving in the British Army, where he spent a decade as an officer including two tours in Afghanistan. In Lviv he watched wheelchair rugby and pickleball as around 500 Ukrainians competed for places in the 60-strong team, the country's largest ever, for next year's Games in Birmingham in July 2027. Ukraine has about 1.4 million combat veterans, more than one in ten of whom have a disability.

Key points

  • Prince Harry made an unannounced two-day visit to Lviv, his fourth trip to Ukraine since Russia's invasion in 2022.
  • He met wounded veterans at the Superhumans rehabilitation centre and attended national Invictus Games heats.
  • He told veterans "you're going to win the war" and promised support for hosting the Games in Ukraine after the war.
  • Ukraine plans its largest ever team, 60 athletes, for the 2027 Invictus Games in Birmingham.
