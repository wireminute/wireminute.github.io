Trump, Xi and Putin Issue Joint Peace Declaration After First-Ever Trilateral Summit

Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin have issued a joint declaration on peace after the first-ever trilateral meeting of the leaders of the United States, China and Russia, pledging that a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought.

The three leaders met at Kylin Villa, a lakeside state guesthouse in Shenzhen, in their third substantial meeting this year. After a late lunch, Trump and Xi moved into the three-way session with Putin, which began in the late afternoon local time and ran for roughly 90 minutes.

The joint Declaration toward Peace, released by the Kremlin and Chinese state media after the talks, said the three heads of state reaffirmed that a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought. The three countries said they would hold talks on reducing nuclear risks and preventing the spread of nuclear weapons.

As permanent members of the United Nations Security Council, the three also committed themselves to reforming the UN so it can respond more effectively to global challenges. The declaration pledged to increase supplies of refined fuel, but it set no specific nuclear disarmament targets and carried no binding commitments.

The declaration also took aim at the International Criminal Court, saying state officials should enjoy immunity from its prosecutions and warning the court against abusing its jurisdiction. The language was one of the declaration's four points, published by China's state news agency and the Kremlin, though the United States has not itself released the document.

The declaration noted that the leaders had discussed Ukraine, the Middle East and the Korean Peninsula, but it took no joint position on any of them, and it made no mention of the wars in Ukraine or Iran, two issues Trump said he had raised with Xi and Putin during the day. It also made no mention of North Korea's nuclear programme. The omission underlined how far apart the three remain on the conflicts, even as they presented a united front on nuclear risks.

On trade, Trump and Xi agreed in principle to extend the truce that is due to expire in January, and they plan a further bilateral meeting at the Group of Twenty summit in Florida in December. On security, both leaders said they shared the goal of keeping crude oil moving through the Strait of Hormuz and preventing Iran from obtaining nuclear weapons.

Xi called the meeting positive, constructive and fruitful, and Trump left Shenzhen shortly after the talks ended. Whether the declaration leads to real arms talks, or remains a statement of intent, will be tested in the weeks ahead.

Key points

  • The three leaders issued a joint Declaration toward Peace after about 90 minutes of talks in Shenzhen.
  • They declared that a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought, and promised dialogue on reducing nuclear risks.
  • The declaration backed UN reform and more refined fuel supplies, warned the International Criminal Court against overreaching, and set no disarmament targets.
  • It made no mention of the wars in Ukraine or Iran, despite Trump saying he raised both.
