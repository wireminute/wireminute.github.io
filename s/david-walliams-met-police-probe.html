London Police Open Inquiry Into David Walliams After New Sex-Offence Claims

London's police have opened an investigation into claims of serious sexual offences against the comedian and children's author David Walliams, after new allegations reached them this week — separate from the claims published by two British news organisations on Tuesday.

London's police have opened an investigation into allegations of non-recent serious sexual offences involving a man now in his 50s, after a number of people came forward this week. The force did not name the man, in line with its policy of not identifying suspects before charge, but the announcement came days after reports named the comedian and children's author David Walliams, 55, in connection with claims of sexual misconduct.

The allegations now under investigation are separate from those published on Tuesday by two British news organisations. In reports that followed a three-year investigation, in which reporters spoke to more than 100 people, several women accused Walliams of using his fame to pursue teenage girls and young women for sex, with claims going back to 2003. One woman said she was sexually assaulted at 15 after meeting Walliams on a social networking site in 2007. The reports were published after he lost a court bid to stop the news organisations from naming him.

Walliams denies the claims. “I am not a sexual predator … I have not broken the law,” he said in a statement this week. He has not spoken publicly since the reports appeared on Tuesday.

The detective leading the inquiry, Detective Superintendent Mike Cagney, urged anyone who had been the victim of a sexual offence to come forward, “no matter when it happened”. Police said detectives were also reviewing the published reporting to establish whether it contained evidence of any offences. Anyone with information was asked to call 101, quoting reference 4900/10NOV26.

The reports came a day after the comedian Russell Brand was jailed for more than 10 years for rape and sexual assault. Walliams, a former television talent-show judge, has sold more than 60 million children's books around the world. No arrest has been made and no charges have been brought.

Key points

  • London police have opened an investigation into allegations of non-recent serious sexual offences involving a man in his 50s; the force did not name him, in line with its policy.
  • The allegations are separate from claims published on Tuesday by two British news organisations, in which several women accused Walliams of sexual misconduct going back to 2003.
  • Walliams, 55, denies the allegations, saying he is not a sexual predator and has not broken the law.
  • Detectives are also reviewing the published reporting for evidence of offences, and police have urged anyone affected to come forward.
