India Pledges $1.62 Billion to Rebuild Flood-Hit Nepal After Modi-Shah Talks

India will give Nepal an economic package worth $1.62 billion to rebuild after the devastating Himalayan floods of August, Prime Minister Narendra Modi announced on Monday after talks in New Delhi with his Nepali counterpart Balendra Shah.

The package, worth 240 billion Nepalese rupees, is meant to give fresh impetus to Nepal's reconstruction and long-term development, Modi said after the meeting. It is the headline outcome of Shah's two-day visit to the Indian capital, his first bilateral trip abroad since he came to power in March. The two sides also signed eight agreements and agreed to push ahead with six joint projects, including a rail link joining Ayodhya with Janakpur and the 669-megawatt Lower Arun hydropower scheme.

The floods struck on 26 August, when a glacier collapse high on the Nepal-China border sent a surge of water, mud and rock down the Lhende Khola into the Bhotekoshi river, sweeping away roads, bridges and settlements near the Rasuwagadhi border crossing. The latest counts put the toll at about 1,500 dead, with a further 5,200 unaccounted for and presumed dead. The Red Cross estimates at least 93,000 people have been affected, and three districts were declared disaster crisis areas for three months.

How the money will be divided between grants, concessional loans and lines of credit has not yet been made public. Nepal has been pressing at the COP31 climate summit in Antalya for climate finance to reach mountain nations as grants rather than loans, and its foreign minister has asked for a dedicated agenda for countries like Nepal.

The visit also produced a ten-point vision document for closer ties, spanning trade, energy, water, transport, digital links, education, health and sport. For Shah, the package is an early result from a relationship with New Delhi that his government is trying to deepen, in a year when the floods have dominated its first months in office.
