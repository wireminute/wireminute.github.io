Seven Killed as Strike Hits Gaza's Nasser Hospital, Knocking Out Its Surgery Wing

The death toll from an overnight strike on Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis has risen to seven after a young man died of his wounds on Friday, in an attack that knocked out the surgery department of the largest hospital in southern Gaza.

The strike came just after midnight on Thursday, when two missiles crashed through the roof of the hospital's five-storey Al-Yasin building, which houses the operating rooms, radiology department, laboratories and surgical wards. The surgery department, which serves about half of Gaza's two million people, was knocked out of service, and several essential departments had ceased operating until further notice.

Among the dead was Dr Rezik Abushira, a general surgeon who had just finished an operation and was resting in a break room with colleagues when the building was hit. He had been featured in a 2026 documentary filmed with visiting American doctors. A 13-year-old boy was also killed, and at least two of the dead were patients. The toll rose to seven on Friday when a 20-year-old man died of wounds from the strike. The hospital put the wounded at 28, while an international medical charity put the figure at 52.

Israel's military confirmed the attack and said it had targeted Muhammad Abu Ismail, commander of Hamas's Khan Younis brigade, and other fighters it accused of operating from inside the hospital. Hamas denied the claim. The commander named by the military was not among the dead. Medics said 11 people were killed across Gaza on Thursday, including a nine-month-old baby who died of cold in a displacement tent. Two more people were killed in strikes elsewhere in Gaza after Thursday, and the toll for Thursday across the territory was put at 12.

The strike landed a year into a ceasefire that took effect in October 2025, with the wider peace plan stalled. Turkey and Spain denounced the attack on Friday, and the head of the UN agency for Palestinian refugees questioned whether the ceasefire could still be called meaningful. The US-led Board of Peace, which oversees the ceasefire, called on both sides to protect civilians. The World Health Organization confirmed the casualties and the extensive damage to the surgery department. Hospital officials say Nasser has been struck at least seven times since the war began, including a double strike in August 2025 that killed 22 people, among them five journalists.

The hospital's director called the attack a crime and said the damage to the intensive care and surgery units was extensive, with one missile failing to explode. With the surgical wing out of action, patients across the south face longer journeys for care that was already scarce.

Key points

  • Seven people have now been killed, including surgeon Dr Rezik Abushira and a 13-year-old boy, after a 20-year-old man died of his wounds on Friday; dozens more were wounded.
  • Two missiles hit the Al-Yasin building, knocking out the surgery department that serves about half of Gaza's two million people.
  • Israel's military said it targeted a Hamas commander it accused of operating from the hospital; Hamas denied the claim and the commander was not among the dead.
  • The US-led Board of Peace urged both sides to protect civilians, as Turkey and Spain condemned the attack a year into a ceasefire whose wider peace plan has stalled.
