Six Killed as Strike Hits Gaza's Nasser Hospital, Knocking Out Its Surgery Wing

Six people, including a surgeon and a 13-year-old boy, were killed when a strike hit the surgery department of Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis, the largest hospital in southern Gaza, the territory's health authorities and the World Health Organization said.

The strike came just after midnight on Thursday, when two missiles hit the five-storey Al-Yasin building, which houses the operating rooms, radiology department, laboratories and surgical wards. The hospital's director said one of the missiles failed to explode. The surgery department, which serves about half of Gaza's two million people, was knocked out of service, and several essential departments had ceased operating until further notice.

Among the dead was Dr Rizq Abu Shaira, a general surgeon who had just finished an operation and was resting in a break room with colleagues when the building was hit. A 13-year-old boy, Jihad Abu Teilkh, was also killed, along with patients and people accompanying them. At least 27 people were wounded, according to the World Health Organization, while the hospital's director put the figure at 28. Earlier reports had put the death toll higher, but the hospital and the World Health Organization later confirmed six deaths.

Israel's military confirmed the attack and said it had targeted Muhammad Abu Ismail, commander of Hamas's Khan Younis brigade, and other fighters it accused of operating from inside the hospital. Hamas denied the claim. The commander named by the military was not among the dead.

The United Nations human rights office urged an end to strikes on hospitals in Gaza after the attack, describing it as one of a series of attacks on the territory's health facilities. The head of the World Health Organization confirmed the casualties and the extensive damage to the surgery department. Hospital officials say Nasser has been struck seven times since the war began, including a double strike in August 2025 that killed 22 people, among them five journalists.

The strike landed a year into a ceasefire that took effect in October 2025, with the wider peace plan stalled. Gaza's health ministry says 1,622 people have been killed and 5,878 injured since the truce took effect. Turkey and Spain denounced the attack, and the US-led Board of Peace, which oversees the ceasefire, called on both sides to protect civilians. With the surgical wing out of action, patients across the south face longer journeys for care that was already scarce.

Key points

  • Six people were killed when a strike hit Nasser Hospital's surgery wing; at least 27 more were wounded. The dead included a 13-year-old boy and surgeon Dr Rizq Abu Shaira.
  • Two missiles hit the Al-Yasin building, knocking out the surgery department that serves about half of Gaza's two million people; one missile failed to explode.
  • Israel's military said it targeted a Hamas commander it accused of operating from the hospital; Hamas denied the claim and the commander was not among the dead.
  • The UN human rights office urged a halt to strikes on Gaza hospitals, as the WHO confirmed the casualties; it was the seventh strike on Nasser since 2023.
