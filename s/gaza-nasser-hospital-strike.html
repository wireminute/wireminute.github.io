Hamas Commander Killed in Gaza Strike as Ceasefire Strain Deepens

A senior Hamas commander has been killed in an Israeli drone strike in southern Gaza, days after 18 Arab and Muslim countries condemned an attack on a hospital in the same city and warned that the ceasefire is being worn down.

An Israeli drone strike in southern Gaza on Saturday killed Samir Abu Younis, commander of Hamas's Rafah brigade, in the latest attack to test a ceasefire that has held for more than a year while the killing has not stopped. Hamas confirmed his death hours later and said his funeral would be held in Khan Younis on Sunday afternoon.

The strike hit in al-Mawasi, a coastal strip west of Khan Younis crowded with tents sheltering displaced families. Abu Younis, 53, was killed and two other people were wounded, medics and Gaza's civil defence said. Israel's military and its Shin Bet agency said they had targeted a senior Hamas commander in a drone strike in Khan Younis, and the defence minister indicated that the target had been killed.

Abu Younis had taken command of the Rafah brigade after his predecessor was killed in a strike in September. His death comes as the truce's first phase remains unfinished, with Hamas not yet disarmed and Israeli forces still striking across the territory.

Gaza's health ministry said on Saturday that Israeli attacks had killed 16 people and injured 68 over the past 48 hours. At least 1,643 people have been killed and 5,964 injured since the ceasefire took effect on 11 October 2025, it said. The ministry also said deaths linked to the cold had risen to 11, after a nine-month-old baby died of hypothermia in a tent earlier in the week.

The killing came two days after the foreign ministers of 18 Arab and Muslim countries, Pakistan among them, condemned the strike on Gaza's Nasser Hospital, warning that continued attacks on civilians and medical sites are putting the ceasefire at risk. The European Union separately warned on Saturday that flooding and winter weather were worsening already dire living conditions, and urged Israel to allow immediate, large-scale aid deliveries.

The hospital strike came just after midnight on Thursday, when two missiles hit the Al-Yasin building, home to the operating rooms and surgical wards; one failed to explode. The surgery department, which serves about half of Gaza's two million people, was knocked out of service. Six people were killed, among them surgeon Dr Rizq Abu Shaira and 13-year-old Jihad Abu Teilkh, and at least 27 were wounded. Israel's military said it had targeted Muhammad Abu Ismail, commander of Hamas's Khan Younis brigade; Hamas denied the claim, and the commander named was not among the dead.
