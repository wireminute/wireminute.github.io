Israel Vows Gaza Offensive Will Continue as Hamas Appeals for Winter Aid and Ceasefire Frays

Israel's defence minister has said the offensive in Gaza will go on until the territory is demilitarised and Hamas is gone, as Hamas's political chief appealed to Arab and Muslim countries to act and aid agencies warned that winter floods are endangering hundreds of thousands of displaced people.

Israel's defence minister said on Sunday that the offensive in Gaza will continue until the territory is demilitarised and Hamas is gone. The pledge came days after Arab and Muslim foreign ministers demanded Israel honour the ceasefire signed last October and lift aid restrictions, though the government that signed the deal has been voted out.

Hamas's political chief, Khalil Al-Hayya, wrote to Arab and Muslim governments on Saturday, saying the humanitarian situation had gone beyond an ordinary emergency. He called for an emergency winter relief campaign, a draft resolution requiring Israel to meet its humanitarian obligations, and an emergency open session of the United Nations Security Council, plus full implementation of the Sharm el-Sheikh humanitarian protocol and an Israeli withdrawal.

The World Health Organization said about 950,000 displaced people are living in flood-prone areas, including 400,000 at particular risk, as winter storms hit the territory. The European Union warned that flooding and cold were worsening already dire conditions and urged Israel to allow immediate, large-scale aid. Gaza's health ministry said deaths linked to the cold had risen to 11, after a nine-month-old baby died of hypothermia in a tent.

Britain, Australia, Canada and Italy have now added their voices, with their foreign ministers jointly urging Israel to open the crossings and let aid move freely across Gaza, where they said conditions were dire. They said 150,000 tents and other vital supplies were being held at the border after floods brought further suffering, and warned there was still time to act before winter fully set in. The Israeli military authority that controls the crossings said it was working with the American-led Board of Peace and the United Nations on a winter plan, and that 93,000 tents and 8,600 aid trucks carrying winter supplies had already entered.

The pressure follows an Israeli drone strike on Saturday that killed Samir Abu Younis, commander of Hamas's Rafah brigade, in al-Mawasi, a tent-crowded coastal strip west of Khan Younis. Abu Younis, 53, was killed and two other people were wounded, medics and Gaza's civil defence said. Hamas confirmed his death hours later and said his funeral would be held in Khan Younis on Sunday afternoon. Israel's military said it had targeted a senior Hamas commander in Khan Younis.

Gaza's health ministry said Israeli attacks had killed 16 people and injured 68 in the past 48 hours, and at least 1,643 have been killed since the ceasefire took effect on 11 October 2025. The killing came two days after a strike on Nasser Hospital that Arab and Muslim foreign ministers condemned, warning it put the ceasefire at risk. The strike, just after midnight on Thursday, hit the Al-Yasin building, home to the operating rooms and surgical wards, knocking the surgery department out of service. Six people were killed, among them surgeon Dr Rizq Abu Shaira and 13-year-old Jihad Abu Teilkh, and at least 27 were wounded. Israel's military said it had targeted a Hamas commander in Khan Younis; Hamas denied the claim, and the man named was not among the dead.
