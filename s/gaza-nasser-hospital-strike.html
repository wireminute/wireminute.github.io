Seventeen Nations Condemn Strike on Gaza's Nasser Hospital, Warn Ceasefire at Risk

The foreign ministers of 17 Arab and Muslim countries, Pakistan among them, have condemned in the strongest terms the strike on Gaza's Nasser Hospital, warning that continued attacks on civilians and medical sites are putting the ceasefire at risk.

The joint statement, issued on Saturday, said the strike on the Nasser hospital in Khan Younis had killed and wounded patients and medical staff, in breach of the laws of war that give hospitals special protection. The ministers — from Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, Egypt, Qatar, Turkiye, the United Arab Emirates, Jordan and Indonesia, among others — also condemned continuing strikes on civilians, including attacks on tents and other places sheltering displaced families.

The statement said the attacks were wearing down the ceasefire and the work to bring back security and stability in Gaza, and did not sit with the peace plan put forward by US President Donald Trump. It called on Israel to keep civilians and medical facilities safe and to let humanitarian aid flow in without interruption, and urged the United States and the Board of Peace, which oversees the truce, to press harder for the deal's first-phase commitments to be carried out in full.

It also raised alarm over the limits on aid, shelter material and medical supplies entering Gaza, saying they were deepening the humanitarian crisis as winter sets in. With shelter, water, sanitation and health services deteriorating, the ministers said, the danger to civilians was growing, above all for young children facing another winter in tents.

The strike itself came just after midnight on Thursday, when two missiles hit the Al-Yasin building, home to the operating rooms, radiology department, laboratories and surgical wards; one of the missiles failed to explode. The surgery department, which serves about half of Gaza's two million people, was knocked out of service. Among the dead was Dr Rizq Abu Shaira, a general surgeon who had just finished an operation, and 13-year-old Jihad Abu Teilkh. At least 27 people were wounded, according to the World Health Organization.

Israel's military confirmed the attack and said it had targeted Muhammad Abu Ismail, commander of Hamas's Khan Younis brigade, and other fighters it accused of operating from inside the hospital. Hamas denied the claim, and the commander named by the military was not among the dead. It was the seventh strike on Nasser since the war began, including a double strike in August 2025 that killed 22 people, among them five journalists. Gaza's health ministry says 1,622 people have been killed and 5,878 injured since the truce took effect in October 2025.

Key points

  • Foreign ministers from 17 Arab and Muslim countries, including Pakistan, condemned the strike on Nasser Hospital in the strongest terms, calling it a breach of the laws protecting medical sites.
  • The joint statement warned the attacks are undermining the ceasefire and urged Israel to protect civilians and let aid in without interruption.
  • Six people were killed when the strike hit Nasser Hospital's surgery wing; at least 27 more were wounded, including a surgeon and a 13-year-old boy.
  • Israel said it targeted a Hamas commander it accused of operating from the hospital; Hamas denied the claim and the commander was not among the dead.
