Russia Rejects Black Sea Grain-Ship Ceasefire as Ukraine Warns Food Exports Are at Risk

Russia has rejected Ukraine's proposal for a ceasefire covering grain ships in the Black Sea, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said, as Moscow linked any deal to a halt in Ukrainian strikes on its oil refineries and pipelines.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Ukraine is ready for a ceasefire covering attacks on grain ships in the Black Sea, but Russia is not. Moscow rejected the idea and wants it linked to a halt in Ukrainian strikes on Russian oil refineries and pipelines, he said. Zelenskyy argued that attacks on grain ships and strikes on energy infrastructure are not comparable, and that Russia's priority is protecting its energy sector rather than the safety of food shipments.

At the United Nations Security Council on Monday, Russia ruled out any new Black Sea grain deal, even a limited one. The European Union's envoy said Moscow had shown no interest in diplomatic options and had ruled out even a limited Black Sea ceasefire or arrangement. Russia's ambassador said a new grain deal would be, in practice, "a scheme of Ukrainian food for Western weapons", and accused Ukraine of attacking more than 200 commercial vessels in 2026. Ukraine's envoy said Moscow was trying to cut the country off from the sea and turn hunger into a weapon.

The proposal itself was announced on Friday by Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha at the Food from Ukraine conference in Kyiv. It covers the safety of ships and the transport of agricultural produce only. Sybiha said Russia wanted oil shipments included in any deal, but Kyiv would discuss oil only as part of a broader energy ceasefire. He thanked India, Turkiye and Egypt for their mediation efforts and said the proposal had been sent to Ukraine's international partners.

The offer comes as attacks on shipping have escalated sharply. Russia attacked two foreign grain carriers carrying more than 100,000 tonnes of grain near the Black Sea port of Odesa this week, and grain shipments from the Odesa ports have all but stopped since late July as strikes on terminals and merchant vessels intensified. A previous agreement that let Ukrainian grain leave by sea collapsed when Russia withdrew from it in July 2023. Ukraine and Russia together account for about 27 percent of global wheat exports, so a prolonged halt threatens food supplies far beyond the region. President Vladimir Putin said last week that Moscow would no longer consider maritime or energy moratoriums after Ukrainian strikes on the Kerch Strait Bridge.

Key points

  • Zelenskyy said Russia rejected a ceasefire covering attacks on grain ships and wants it linked to a halt in Ukrainian strikes on Russian oil refineries and pipelines.
  • At the UN, Russia ruled out any new Black Sea grain deal, even a limited ceasefire, calling it "a scheme of Ukrainian food for Western weapons".
  • Ukraine's proposal, announced on Friday, covers safe navigation and farm exports only; Kyiv says oil belongs in a broader energy ceasefire.
  • Grain shipments from Odesa have nearly stopped since late July after repeated strikes on ships and terminals, threatening food supplies far beyond Ukraine.
