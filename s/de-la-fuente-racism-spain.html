Spain Coach Insists 'There Is No Racism' in Spain as Yamal and Vinicius Say Otherwise

Spain head coach Luis de la Fuente insisted on Saturday that there is no racism in Spain, a day before his side face England in Madrid, despite his own star players saying they have repeatedly suffered racist abuse in the country.

De la Fuente, 65, was asked about England manager Thomas Tuchel, who said he would run his players through UEFA's anti-racism protocol before Sunday's Nations League match. "You can tell this to Thomas," De la Fuente told reporters. "As clear as that. There is no racism. Exactly the same as in England, in Spain, there is no racism. We're not racists, none of us." He later added that he had only answered a question politely and had never set out to talk about racism in Spain.

The claim sits uneasily with the record. Lamine Yamal, the Barcelona forward, said in September that he had experienced racism "a thousand times" in Spain, often from people who did not recognise their behaviour as racist. He also denounced supporters who aimed Islamophobic chants at Muslims during a friendly against Egypt in Barcelona in March, calling them ignorant and racist. Football's world governing body fined Spain's federation 20,000 euros over the chants and ordered the national team to play behind a partly closed stadium.

Real Madrid forward Vinicius Junior has been the most prominent target of racist abuse in Spanish football. In May 2023 he said the Spanish league "now belongs to racists" after being abused at Valencia, and a year later three Valencia supporters were jailed for eight months for racially abusing him at the same ground. England's players were also subjected to racist abuse on a visit to Madrid in 2004.

UEFA's anti-racism protocol lets a referee halt a match, take the players off temporarily, and ultimately abandon the game if discriminatory behaviour continues. Tuchel said he would take five minutes to remind his players of the procedure before Sunday's game. De la Fuente also gave a fitness update on Yamal, who missed Thursday's win over the Czech Republic with a knee problem, saying he was unsure whether the forward would start or last a full 90 minutes, but that he was in perfect shape.

Key points

  • Spain coach Luis de la Fuente said on Saturday that "there is no racism" in Spain, ahead of Sunday's Nations League match against England in Madrid.
  • Lamine Yamal said in September he had experienced racism "a thousand times"; Vinicius Junior has been repeatedly targeted, and three Valencia fans were jailed in 2024 for abusing him.
  • Spain's federation was fined 20,000 euros and ordered to play behind a partly closed stadium after Islamophobic chants by supporters during a friendly against Egypt in March.
  • England coach Thomas Tuchel said he would brief his players on UEFA's anti-racism protocol, which allows a referee to stop or abandon a match over discriminatory behaviour.
