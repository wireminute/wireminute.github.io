Hegseth Restricts US Military Cooperation With Canada in Secret Memo

US Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth has imposed new restrictions on military cooperation with Canada through a classified memo circulated last week, according to officials familiar with the directive.

US Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth has imposed new restrictions on military cooperation with Canada in a classified memo circulated last week, according to a report citing officials familiar with the directive. The memo itself has not been made public, so its exact scope is unknown.

A Pentagon spokesman said the department is narrowing its engagements with Canada to those that advance clear American defence objectives, while still supporting Canada's ability to meet its NATO commitments. The directive does not affect defence intelligence sharing, or cooperation through NATO and the joint North American air defence command, NORAD.

The move comes as strains between the two allies deepen over trade, defence spending and the US president's remarks about Canada. One official described the restrictions as a 'slap in the face' for Ottawa; which programmes are affected, from joint exercises to training exchanges, is not yet clear.

Key points

  • Hegseth issued the restrictions in a classified memo circulated last week; the memo has not been made public, so its exact scope is unknown.
  • The Pentagon says engagements with Canada will be narrowed to those advancing clear US defence objectives, while still supporting Canada's NATO commitments.
  • Defence intelligence sharing and cooperation through NATO and NORAD are unaffected; one official called the move a 'slap in the face' for Ottawa.
