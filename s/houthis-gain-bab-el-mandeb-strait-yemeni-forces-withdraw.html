Saudi Arabia Replaces Yemen Coalition Commander as Houthis Close In on Aden

Saudi Arabia has replaced the commander of its coalition forces fighting the Houthis in Yemen, a day after the fighters advanced to within about 60 kilometres of Aden, the seat of Yemen's internationally recognised government.

King Salman issued a decree on Monday naming General Fayyad al-Ruwaili, the chief of staff of the Saudi armed forces, to command the coalition. Lieutenant General Fahd bin Hamad al-Salman, who had led coalition operations since August 2024, was moved to a post as military adviser to the defence minister. The change came as coalition forces gave up a push to retake ground along the Red Sea coast so they could reinforce Aden.

Houthi fighters have seized the Al-Maqatirah and Al-Madaribah districts in Lahj and are positioned at the Al-Kharaz camp, about 60 kilometres from Aden. The Southern Armed Forces, the main force fighting for the government in the south, pulled back from Dhubab airport and the strait coastline to defend the city, ending a six-week government counteroffensive. The withdrawal leaves the Houthis holding the ground above the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, the narrow passage joining the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden.

Saudi warplanes hit targets across Yemen in overnight raids, striking Hodeidah's international airport, a market and a school in Lahj, and a fuel station in Amran, killing two people and wounding eight. Houthi-affiliated media also reported coalition air strikes on the capital Sanaa and said one person was killed in an attack on a school in Taiz. Taiz is now completely besieged, and its authorities have called for humanitarian corridors so civilians can leave, as medical officials warned hospitals could be forced to stop operating within two days for lack of fuel and oxygen.

Saudi Arabia said on Monday it had shot down two Houthi drones aimed at the Mecca and Riyadh regions. Government forces say they killed or wounded 173 Houthi fighters in their Operation Dawn of Yemen, and the Houthis have been firing ballistic missiles at Saudi airports and oil facilities since Yemen's civil war restarted in July. A Houthi offensive in September took the Red Sea coast, including the key port city of Mocha, which government forces have been unable to retake.

Key points

  • Saudi Arabia replaced its Yemen coalition commander on Monday, naming General Fayyad al-Ruwaili after Lieutenant General Fahd bin Hamad al-Salman was moved to an advisory post.
  • The change follows the Houthi advance to about 60 kilometres from Aden, forcing government and coalition forces to fall back to defend the interim capital.
  • Taiz is now completely besieged, with hospitals warning they could stop operating within two days without fuel and oxygen, and calls for humanitarian corridors.
  • Saudi Arabia said it shot down two Houthi drones aimed at the Mecca and Riyadh regions on Monday, as fighting continued across several provinces.
