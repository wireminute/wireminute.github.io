Saudi Jets Strike Hodeidah Airport, School and Market Overnight as Houthis Hold Strait Ground

Saudi warplanes hit targets across Yemen in overnight raids, striking Hodeidah's international airport, a market and a school in Lahj, and a fuel station in Amran, killing two people and wounding eight, as Houthi fighters held the ground they seized around the Bab el-Mandeb Strait.

The raids began in the Ash Shamayatayn district of Taiz, then hit the international airport at Hodeidah, the Red Sea port city. In Lahj, a school and a market were hit, along with a road between Taiz and Lahj, and a privately owned fuel station in the Harf Sufyan district of Amran was struck as well. Reports from the capital said two people were killed and eight wounded in the attacks. The airport had been hit before, on 9 November.

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said Saudi jets and missiles had struck civilian areas in five provinces, Saada, Taiz, Lahj, al-Jawf and Marib, 75 times in the previous day, flying F-15s and Typhoons out of Khamis Mushait and Taif and firing missiles from Najran. He put the total since the fighting escalated at 5,095. The figures could not be independently verified. The coalition said its defences had stopped a ballistic missile headed for the kingdom's southern region, and civil defence alerts in four Saudi cities were issued and then lifted.

The strikes came as Houthi fighters held the ground they took at the weekend. Four military sources and two government sources said the Houthis had seized Al-Maqatirah and Al-Madaribah districts in Lahj and were positioned at the Al-Kharaz camp, about 60 kilometres from Aden. The Southern Armed Forces, the main force fighting for the government in the south, announced a redeployment from Dhubab airport and the strait coastline to defend Aden. Troops regrouped at al-Suqyah, about 150 kilometres west of the city. The pullback appears to end a six-week government counteroffensive.

Near Aden on Sunday, a Houthi missile hit a checkpoint at Khor Omeira in Lahj, killing eight people and wounding 12, and explosions were heard in the city that evening. Fighting spread across several provinces: a senior commander was killed in Al-Jawf, the army said it intercepted Houthi drones over Marib, and six civilians were hurt in Taiz. The coalition said it had shot down two ballistic missiles fired toward Riyadh. The withdrawal hands the Houthis the ground above the narrow passage joining the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden. The United Nations refugee agency said the clashes were disrupting help for about 9,600 refugees.

Key points

  • Saudi warplanes struck Hodeidah airport, a school and a market in Lahj and a fuel station in Amran overnight; two people were killed and eight wounded.
  • Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree says 75 Saudi air and missile attacks hit five provinces in 24 hours, taking the total since the escalation began to 5,095; the figures are unverified.
  • Houthi fighters hold ground about 60 kilometres from Aden after seizing two districts, as government forces fall back to defend the city.
  • The coalition says it intercepted a missile aimed at southern Saudi Arabia; the withdrawal leaves the Houthis overlooking the Bab el-Mandeb Strait.
