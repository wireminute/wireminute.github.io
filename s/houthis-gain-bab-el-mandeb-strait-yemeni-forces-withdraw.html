Houthis Take Control of Bab el-Mandeb Strait as Yemeni Forces Pull Back

Forces loyal to Yemen's internationally recognised government said on Sunday they were pulling out of positions overlooking the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, leaving the Iran-aligned Houthis in control of one of the world's most important shipping chokepoints.

The Southern Armed Forces said their units were leaving the airport at Dhubab and the coastline around the strait, west of Taiz governorate, and falling back toward Aden, where the government is based. They said the move was aimed at securing Aden and the governorates around it, stiffening the front lines and breaking the Houthi siege of the city of Taiz, with backing from the Saudi-led coalition. Military sources said the troops had regrouped at al-Suqyah, just south of Dhubab and about 150 kilometres west of Aden, and were still trying to push Houthi fighters from coastal positions nearby.

The pullback hands the Houthis the ground above the narrow passage that joins the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden and carries shipping between Asia and Europe through the Suez Canal. At its tightest point the channel is about 26 kilometres across. The Houthis already hold much of northern Yemen, including the capital Sanaa and the country's most heavily populated areas. The retreat appears to end a six-week government counteroffensive to retake the coastal belt, and Houthi fighters have been reported just outside Tur al-Bahah district in Lahj governorate, about 80 kilometres from Aden, with Taiz now the fiercest front in the war.

It reverses gains from last month, when government troops said they had won back Dhubab and the port city of Mokha. The government opened a campaign in October to retake the coastal belt after the Houthis drove south in September in a bid to take Yemen's whole Red Sea shore, an advance that brought them within reach of the strait. There was no immediate response from the Houthis or from Saudi Arabia, the government's main international ally.

The shift puts a second key waterway under strain alongside the Strait of Hormuz, where Iran has disrupted tanker traffic in recent days. The Bab el-Mandeb passage is among the busiest lanes in the world for oil and goods moving by sea, and any threat to safe passage there raises costs and delays for trade between Asia and Europe. The International Committee of the Red Cross said on Sunday that the Houthis had released 600 prisoners.

Key points

  • Government-backed forces in Yemen said on Sunday they were withdrawing from the Bab el-Mandeb area and redeploying toward Aden.
  • The pullback leaves the Iran-aligned Houthis in control of land overlooking the strait, a vital link between the Red Sea and the Gulf of Aden.
  • Troops have regrouped at al-Suqyah, 150km west of Aden, while Houthi fighters were reported about 80km from the city; the Houthis also released 600 prisoners.
  • The move reverses government gains from last month and appears to end a six-week counteroffensive to retake the coast.
