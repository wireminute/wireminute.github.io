Houthi Missile Kills Eight Near Aden as Government Forces Dig In to Defend the City

A Houthi missile strike killed eight people at a checkpoint near Aden on Sunday, as government forces dug in around the interim capital after pulling back from the Bab el-Mandeb Strait.

The checkpoint at Khor Omeira in Lahj province, close to Aden, was hit on Sunday, killing eight people and wounding 12, according to military and government sources. An earlier count had put the toll at five dead and eight wounded. Explosions were heard in Aden itself on Sunday evening, and local reports said two ballistic missiles were fired at the Al-Buraiqa district on the city's western coast, while fighting flared in Lahj around supply routes near the strait.

The strike came as government troops strengthened their positions around Aden, building sandbag defences and digging tunnels on the city's outskirts after a week of setbacks. On Friday the Houthis seized the Kharaz military base and nearby mountain positions in Lahj, a key line guarding the western approaches to the city, and on Sunday the Southern Armed Forces said they were pulling units out of Dhubab airport and the coastline around the strait, falling back toward Aden to secure the city and nearby governorates. Troops regrouped at al-Suqyah, roughly 150 kilometres west of Aden, while Houthi fighters were reported about 80 kilometres from the city.

The pullback hands the Houthis the ground above the narrow passage joining the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden, through which shipping moves between Asia and Europe via the Suez Canal. At its tightest point the channel is about 26 kilometres across. The shift caps a swift reversal: government forces had retaken Dhubab and the port city of Mokha last month, but the Houthi advance, which began on Friday, appears to have ended a six-week government counteroffensive launched in October. The Houthis said on Sunday that the strait area was now fully secure and that they had no plans to advance on Aden, a claim made by a member of their political bureau.

The fighting is rippling outward. The Saudi-led coalition said on Sunday that it had shot down two ballistic missiles fired toward Riyadh, the Saudi capital, while the Houthis have intensified drone and missile attacks on Saudi Arabia in recent days. The United Nations refugee agency warned that the clashes were disrupting protection and assistance for about 9,600 refugees. The Houthis already hold much of northern Yemen, including the capital Sanaa, and the International Committee of the Red Cross said on Sunday that they had released 600 prisoners.

Key points

  • A Houthi missile strike on a checkpoint at Khor Omeira in Lahj killed eight people and wounded 12 on Sunday; explosions were also heard in Aden.
  • Government forces have pulled back from Dhubab and the strait coastline toward Aden and are building defences around the city.
  • The withdrawal hands the Houthis control of land overlooking the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, a vital shipping chokepoint between the Red Sea and the Gulf of Aden.
  • The Houthis say the strait area is now secure and they have no plans to advance on Aden, while the Saudi-led coalition says it intercepted two missiles fired toward Riyadh.
