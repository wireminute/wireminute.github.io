Houthis Seize Kharaz Base in Lahj as Advance Closes In on Aden

Houthi fighters seized the strategic Kharaz military base in the southern province of Lahj early on Sunday, pushing closer to the port city of Aden after government troops were forced to retreat.

The base and several mountain positions around it, in Lahj's Al-Madaribah wa Al-Arah district near the Bab el-Mandeb Strait and the Gulf of Aden, fell after fierce fighting, two military sources said. The loss was described on the ground as a major battlefield setback: Kharaz had served as a key defensive line guarding the western approaches to Aden, the temporary seat of Yemen's internationally recognised government, and the routes leading to the strait. Retreating forces were reported to be building new fortifications along Aden's western edge to slow any further advance.

The fall of Kharaz caps a swift reversal along Yemen's Red Sea coast. Government-backed forces said on Sunday they were pulling out of the airport at Dhubab and the coastline around the strait, west of Taiz governorate, and falling back toward Aden to secure the city and its surrounding governorates, stiffen the front lines and break the siege of Taiz, with backing from the Saudi-led coalition. Military sources said the troops had regrouped at al-Suqyah, just south of Dhubab and about 150 kilometres west of Aden, while Houthi fighters were reported just outside Tur al-Bahah district in Lahj, about 80 kilometres from the city.

The advance began on Friday, when Houthi fighters took Yemen's entire western coast and pushed beyond the strait into Lahj, firing ballistic missiles at retreating government forces, according to government and military officials. It reverses gains from last month, when government troops said they had won back Dhubab and the port city of Mokha, and appears to end a six-week government counteroffensive to retake the coastal belt, launched in October after the Houthis drove south in September to take Yemen's whole Red Sea shore.

The pullback hands the Houthis the ground above the narrow passage that joins the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden and carries shipping between Asia and Europe through the Suez Canal. At its tightest point the channel is about 26 kilometres across. The Houthis already hold much of northern Yemen, including the capital Sanaa and the country's most heavily populated areas. The shift puts a second key waterway under strain alongside the Strait of Hormuz, where tanker traffic has been disrupted in recent days, and any threat to safe passage there raises costs and delays for trade between Asia and Europe. The International Committee of the Red Cross said on Sunday that the Houthis had released 600 prisoners.

Key points

  • Houthi fighters seized the Kharaz military base and surrounding mountain positions in Lahj early on Sunday after fierce clashes forced government troops to retreat.
  • The base guarded the western approaches to Aden and the routes to the Bab el-Mandeb Strait; its loss was described as a major battlefield setback.
  • Government-backed forces have pulled back from Dhubab and the strait coastline toward Aden, regrouping about 150km west of the city, with Houthi fighters reported about 80km away.
  • The advance reverses last month's government gains and appears to end a six-week counteroffensive, leaving the Houthis in control of land overlooking a vital shipping chokepoint.
