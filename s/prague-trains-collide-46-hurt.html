46 Hurt as Two Trains Collide Near Prague's Main Station

Two passenger trains collided on a railway bridge near Prague's main station during the morning rush hour on Tuesday, injuring 46 people, four of them critically, and halting rail services across the Czech capital.

The collision happened at about 7am local time on the bridge above Seifertova Street in the Prague 3 district, close to Praha hlavní nádraží, the city's main station. One train was a CityElefant electric service heading towards Milovice, and the other was a RegioFox diesel train coming from Mladá Boleslav. Emergency services said both trains were travelling at low speed when they came together, and no deaths have been reported.

Prague's emergency medical service said 46 people were hurt, including four whose injuries were described as critical or life threatening, among them both train drivers. Twelve more were moderately injured and 30 suffered minor injuries. Ukraine's foreign ministry said 15 Ukrainian citizens were among the injured. Firefighters and medical teams treated passengers at the scene, and all of the injured were taken to hospitals across the city. Rescue operations had ended by late morning, with the situation under control by 10am.

The crash disrupted rail traffic through the main station, with services to Praha-Vysočany and Praha-Libeň suspended and at least six lines affected. The national operator, České dráhy, said disruption was expected to last until midday, diverted some trains through Praha-Holešovice and allowed passengers to use selected city public transport instead.

No official cause has been established. Transport Minister Ivan Bednárik described the collision as a very serious incident and said the immediate priority was care for the injured. He said early signs pointed to one train having gone past a red signal, but he warned that no conclusion should be drawn until the inquiry is complete. The Rail Safety Inspection Office is examining how the two trains came to be on the same track, and suggestions of a signalling fault remain unconfirmed. The crash happened on a national holiday in the Czech Republic, marking the anniversary of the Velvet Revolution. The Rail Safety Inspection Office put early material losses at more than 16 million Czech koruna.

Key points

  • Two passenger trains collided at about 7am on a bridge above Seifertova Street near Prague's main station, one heading to Milovice and the other coming from Mladá Boleslav.
  • 46 people were injured, four of them critically, including both drivers; 12 were moderately hurt and 30 had minor injuries, and 15 of the injured were Ukrainian citizens. No deaths have been reported.
  • Rail services between the main station and Praha-Vysočany and Praha-Libeň were suspended, with at least six lines disrupted until midday.
  • The cause is under investigation. The transport minister said early signs pointed to one train having gone past a red signal, but warned against conclusions before the inquiry ends.
