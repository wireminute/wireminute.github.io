Iran Unveils New Twin-Missile Launcher at Underground Base as Guard Chief Warns Enemies

Iran's Revolutionary Guard unveiled a new missile launcher on Saturday that it says can fire two liquid-fuelled missiles at the same time, during a tour of an underground missile base by its top commanders.

State television broadcast footage of the visit, showing the Guard's commander-in-chief, Major General Ahmad Vahidi, walking through the underground complex alongside the Aerospace Force chief, Brigadier General Majid Mousavi, as the new launcher was presented. The footage also showed rows of other launchers inside the facility, which Iranian media describe as a missile city. The location of the site was not disclosed.

Vahidi used the visit to warn Iran's adversaries, saying what had happened to them so far was only the beginning and that they faced humiliation. The language underlined how the Guard is presenting its missile programme as central to the war with the United States and Israel and as a deterrent against further strikes on Iran.

The new system is designed for liquid-fuelled missiles, unlike a twin launcher for solid-fuelled missiles the force has said it used in strikes on American and Israeli targets in April. Liquid-fuelled missiles can carry larger warheads but take longer to prepare for launch, and the Guard presented the new launcher as a step forward in its arsenal, with Mousavi saying its capabilities had been strengthened between the two wars.

The unveiling came on a day of duelling claims in the war. Iran said its air defences had hit an American F-16 over the Gulf, a claim Washington denied, and said it had brought down American drones over the Strait of Hormuz. The Guard also said it had shot down another American drone over the southern port city of Sirik, the nineteenth such claim of its kind.

Key points
