Norse Atlantic Suspends All Flights and Warns of Bankruptcy Without Urgent Funding

Norwegian long-haul carrier Norse Atlantic has suspended all of its flights and warned it will file for bankruptcy unless it secures emergency financing urgently, grounding its fleet of Boeing 787 Dreamliners and stranding passengers across its network.

The airline said on Thursday it had suspended all flights scheduled for 26 November while it seeks clarity on whether a viable financing solution can be found. Its board said it intends to file for bankruptcy if short-term funding is not secured urgently, adding that there is no assurance a solution will be found. The carrier is in talks with investors about a possible takeover that would include financing, and it held a meeting with the holders of its 52 million dollar senior secured bonds on Wednesday.

The suspension came a day after the company warned its financial situation was critical and that it needed new financing to meet immediate liquidity needs and keep operating. Its shares fell 85 percent after that warning. Norse, which launched in June 2022, operates 12 Boeing 787-9 aircraft on long-haul routes from London, Oslo, Berlin, Athens and Manchester to destinations including New York, Los Angeles, Miami, Bangkok and Cape Town. It had already been seeking replacement business after a six-aircraft wet-lease arrangement with IndiGo ended on 1 November, and it launched a formal process in July to explore a sale or merger.

The disruption also hit Pakistan International Airlines, which had wet-leased two of the Dreamliners for its UK routes. The Pakistani carrier cancelled two departures on Wednesday, from London Heathrow to Islamabad and from Manchester to Lahore, saying Norse had notified it of the suspension without warning just as passengers were preparing to board. The airline said it would operate its own aircraft on the routes instead.

Key points

  • Norse Atlantic suspended all flights on Thursday and warned it will file for bankruptcy unless emergency financing is secured urgently.
  • The airline's shares fell 85 percent a day earlier after it warned its financial situation was critical.
  • The carrier is in talks with investors about a possible takeover that would include a financing solution.
  • Pakistan International Airlines cancelled two UK flights after the suspension of its wet-lease deal with Norse.
