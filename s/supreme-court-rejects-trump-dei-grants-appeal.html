Supreme Court Lets Stand Ruling Against Trump Cuts to Diversity Research Grants

The United States Supreme Court has declined to hear the Trump administration's appeal against a ruling that blocked its cancellation of diversity-related research grants, leaving about $59 million in funding for University of California researchers in place.

The justices turned away the appeal on Monday without giving reasons, as is usual when the court declines a case. No dissent was recorded. The decision leaves in force a ruling by the San Francisco-based appeals court for the western states, which found the grant cancellations likely breached the researchers' right to free speech. The case now returns to the trial court, where the researchers' lawsuit will continue.

The dispute began in January 2025, when the president signed orders aimed at diversity, equity and inclusion programmes. The National Science Foundation and the National Endowment for the Humanities then ended grants held by researchers across the University of California system. The researchers sued, arguing the funding was cut because of the views expressed in their work. A federal judge in California, Rita Lin, halted the cancellations, finding the researchers were likely to show the cuts amounted to discrimination based on viewpoint. The appeals court upheld that part of her ruling in May 2026, and the administration asked the Supreme Court to intervene in August.

The grants at issue supported work including discussion of racial equity in science teaching and efforts to widen science education for Indigenous young people. The administration had argued the Constitution does not require the government to fund research that promotes diversity programmes. The researchers argued the opposite: that withdrawing money already awarded because of the content of the work punished protected speech.

The outcome stands out because the court, with a six-to-three conservative majority, has largely allowed the administration's spending cuts to go ahead. Last year it permitted deep cuts to health research grants to proceed, though that decision turned on procedure rather than the free-speech question at the heart of this case. The administration's wider campaign to reshape federal spending and end support for diversity initiatives continues, and the ruling does not settle the underlying lawsuit.

Key points

  • The Supreme Court declined on Monday to hear the administration's appeal, leaving in place a ruling that about $59 million in grants to University of California researchers must keep flowing.
  • Lower courts found the cancellations likely violated the researchers' free-speech rights, ruling the funding was cut because of the views in their work.
  • The grants, from the National Science Foundation and the National Endowment for the Humanities, supported work on racial equity in science teaching and science education for Indigenous young people.
  • The case returns to the trial court, where the lawsuit continues; the court's refusal to take the case does not set a national precedent.
