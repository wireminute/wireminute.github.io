JPMorgan Chase Says Customer Names and Emails May Have Been Exposed in Vendor Cyberattack

JPMorgan Chase has said some customer names and email addresses may have been exposed after hackers attacked outside companies that provide services to the bank.

The bank said on Thursday that the attackers reached marketing information held by those outside firms. It said it believed the material could include some customer names and email addresses, and that its own banking services were not disrupted.

The bank said it would contact customers directly where needed and offer support, including free credit monitoring. It did not name the firms that were attacked, say when the attack happened, or say how many customers might be affected.

Cybersecurity researchers reported that a ransomware group had published email addresses said to belong to about 16,000 customers, in some cases with phone numbers and account numbers, and linked the leak to the bank's invitation-only Reserve card programme. The bank has not confirmed those details. It also said the attack was not connected to a banking outage earlier in the week, which it blamed on a software fault.

Key points

  • JPMorgan Chase says hackers attacked outside firms that provide it with services and reached marketing data that could hold some customer names and email addresses.
  • The bank says its own systems were not disrupted, and it will contact affected customers and offer free credit monitoring.
  • Researchers reported a ransomware group published details said to belong to about 16,000 customers; the bank has not confirmed that figure, and says the attack is unrelated to this week's outage.
