Nearly 1,500 Migrants Reach Canary Islands in a Single Day, the Most Since 2020

Almost 1,500 migrants arrived in Spain’s Canary Islands on Saturday, the highest number in a single day since 2020, after the coastguard rescued them from 14 boats, the EFE news agency reported.

A total of 1,488 people were rescued by the Spanish coastguard and brought ashore on Saturday, EFE reported on Sunday, citing the maritime rescue service. The previous one-day record was 1,461 people, rescued in waters off the islands on 7 November 2020.

Arrivals from West African countries have surged since October, putting additional pressure on reception centres and emergency services across parts of the archipelago, particularly on its smaller and less populated islands. One boat that arrived on Saturday was carrying 240 people, including 12 women and 36 children, and most of those rescued were nationals of Senegal, Gambia and Guinea-Conakry.

Spain’s Territorial Policy Minister, Angel Victor Torres, will visit El Hierro on Sunday to discuss the surge with local officials. The Atlantic route from West Africa to the Canary Islands is one of the world’s deadliest migration crossings, with small wooden boats spending days at sea before reaching the islands.

The Canary Islands, a Spanish archipelago off the north-west coast of Africa, have become the main sea gateway into Europe for people leaving West Africa. The crossing is far longer and more exposed than the short hop across the Strait of Gibraltar, and the small boats that make it spend days at sea before reaching the islands.

The record day comes even though overall arrivals are down on last year. Government figures show arrivals in the Canary Islands between 1 January and 31 October were 5.5 percent lower than in the same period last year, making Saturday’s surge a sharp break from the recent trend.

Key points

  • A total of 1,488 migrants in 14 boats were rescued by the Spanish coastguard on Saturday, the most in a single day since 2020.
  • The previous one-day record was 1,461 people, rescued off the islands on 7 November 2020.
  • Arrivals from West African countries have surged since October; most of those rescued were from Senegal, Gambia and Guinea-Conakry.
  • Spain’s Territorial Policy Minister will visit El Hierro on Sunday to discuss the surge.
