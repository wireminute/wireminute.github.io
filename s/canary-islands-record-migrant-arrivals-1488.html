More Than 3,600 Migrants Reach Canary Islands in Three Days as El Hierro Is Overwhelmed

More than 3,600 migrants have reached Spain’s Canary Islands by boat since Friday, with the tiny island of El Hierro taking over 1,000 arrivals on a second straight day as Spain’s government announced reinforcements for its maritime rescue service.

Seven more boats carrying 1,261 people reached El Hierro on Sunday, the second day in a row that more than 1,000 people landed on the island, according to figures compiled by the Spanish news agency EFE from health and emergency services. Five of the boats, carrying 937 people, came ashore at the port of La Restinga, while two more, carrying 324 people, arrived at La Estaca, the island’s main port. At least three people were taken to hospital.

The Sunday arrivals bring the total since Friday to 3,684 people on 29 boats, EFE’s tally shows. On Friday 843 people arrived on eight boats, followed by 1,580 on Saturday, the highest single-day total since 2020. Spain’s maritime rescue service put Saturday’s figure at 1,488 people on 14 boats, with the difference reflecting different cut-off times and provisional counts.

Spain’s Territorial Policy Minister, Angel Victor Torres, visited La Restinga on Sunday as the boats kept coming. He announced that maritime rescue services would be reinforced and said the government would discuss new measures at a meeting of the interministerial commission on migration on Tuesday. The island’s president, Fernando Clavijo, has said this month’s boats have completely overwhelmed the island’s resources and has written to the European Union’s migration commissioner asking for urgent help.

El Hierro, home to about 11,000 people, has recorded 96 boats so far this month, compared with 66 in the previous ten months of the year. Around 17,000 migrants have reached the island this year, Torres said. Its reception centre at La Restinga already held 317 people on Saturday before the new boats arrived, and some of those brought ashore have been moved to Tenerife by ferry.

Spain declared a migration emergency in the Canary Islands on 11 November after more than 9,000 people reached the archipelago by sea since the start of the month. The Atlantic route from West Africa is one of the world’s deadliest migration crossings, and the International Organization for Migration recorded at least 450 deaths on the route in the first ten months of 2026. No deaths at sea were reported from the weekend’s crossings.

Key points

  • A total of 3,684 migrants have reached the Canary Islands on 29 boats since Friday, including 1,261 on El Hierro on Sunday alone.
  • Saturday’s 1,488 arrivals were the most in a single day since November 2020, beating the previous record of 1,461.
  • El Hierro, home to about 11,000 people, has taken 96 boats this month, against 66 in the previous ten months.
  • Spain’s government will discuss new measures at a migration commission meeting on Tuesday, with maritime rescue services to be reinforced.
