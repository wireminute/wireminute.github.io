Injury Toll Rises to 19 After Car Bomb Outside Meta Governor's Office in Villavicencio

The number of people hurt in the car bomb outside the Meta governor's office in Villavicencio has risen to 19, Colombian police said, with some of the injuries serious, in the second such attack in the department in eight days.

The blast went off late on Monday near Plaza Los Libertadores, wrecking the front of the governor's office and damaging shops and buildings nearby. The National Police said on Monday night that at least 19 people had been wounded, up from an earlier count of 12, and that some of the injuries were serious. Two members of the governor's office staff were among the hurt, along with five security guards working at the headquarters. No deaths had been reported by Tuesday morning, and rescue crews were still searching the rubble for anyone trapped. Images from the scene showed the front of the multi-storey government building badly damaged, with residents seeking shelter amid rubble and wrecked facades.

The explosion damaged buildings within about 500 metres, destroyed two shops including a two-storey cafe that collapsed, wrecked at least three vehicles and cut power in the area. Colombian media described the device as a car bomb, though officials have not confirmed what kind of explosive was used. President Abelardo De La Espriella said the governor's office had been hit in an explosive attack and promised a security-force response, blaming what he called narcoterrorism. No group has said it carried out the attack, and no specific organisation has been named.

Meta Governor Rafaela Cortes said she was coordinating emergency and security measures with the army and police. It follows a car bomb at the Pipiral toll on the Bogota-Villavicencio road on November 15, which damaged buildings there but left no one dead or hurt. The story is still developing.
