Nine Injured, Three Critically, in Blast and Fire at Bangkok Oil Refinery

Nine people were injured, three of them critically, when a powerful explosion tore through an oil facility in eastern Bangkok on Thursday, shaking doors kilometres away and sending a column of flame and black smoke over the Thai capital.

The blast struck the Bangchak site in the Phra Khanong district at about 3pm local time. The city's Erawan Emergency Medical Centre first put the injured at seven, then revised the figure to nine, with three in a critical condition and most of the injured suffering burns. No deaths have been reported.

Residents nearby were told to leave at once after a mobile phone alert warned them to stay clear of the area, and students were moved quickly out of nearby schools. Reporters some distance from the plant said the explosion rattled doors, and a column of flames rose above the site followed by thick black smoke that could be seen across Bangkok.

Bangchak, which runs the site, said firefighters had the blaze under control inside half an hour and that all production had been halted. Energy Minister Akanat Promphan, who visited the scene, said the fire appeared to have started at a distillation tower roughly 100 metres from the oil storage tanks, which limited the damage. The cause is still under investigation, and the government will keep production suspended until the energy ministry is confident the site can run safely.

Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul said he was monitoring the situation and had ordered urgent checks on the possible effects on the health and safety of people living near the refinery, adding that it was too soon to apportion blame. Officials tested the air for carbon monoxide, sulphur dioxide and other contaminants within a kilometre of the site and found no irregularities, and Bangchak said its own air quality monitoring around the refinery showed normal levels.

The Phra Khanong site is Thailand's fifth-largest refinery and the only one in an urban area, with a capacity of 120,000 barrels a day. It sits in a residential district, which is why the blast caused such alarm. Streets around the plant were kept closed while emergency crews worked, and the situation was returning to normal by the late afternoon.

Key points

  • Nine people were injured, three critically, in an explosion and fire at the Bangchak facility in eastern Bangkok on Thursday afternoon; no deaths have been reported.
  • The Erawan medical centre revised its first assessment of seven injured; most of the injured suffered burns.
