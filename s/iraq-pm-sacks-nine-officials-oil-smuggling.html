Iraq Orders Army Into Iran-Backed Militia Stronghold as Oil Smuggling Standoff Escalates

Iraq's prime minister has ordered the army to move into a stronghold of the Iran-aligned Kataib Hezbollah militia and given the group until Friday to leave, sharply escalating a confrontation over alleged oil smuggling.

Iraq's prime minister has ordered the army to move into a stronghold of the Iran-aligned Kataib Hezbollah militia and given the group until Friday to leave, sharply escalating a confrontation over alleged oil smuggling. Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi ordered the armed forces to enter Jurf al-Nasr, also known as Jurf al-Sakhar, about 60 kilometres south-west of Baghdad, his spokesman told the state news agency on Monday. A security source said the deadline for the faction to withdraw was Friday.

Military units are already moving towards the area, a senior security official said, and the air force is ready to support ground troops once the deadline expires. The order follows a security meeting on Sunday chaired by the director of the prime minister's military office, attended by senior commanders. The federal police commander also inspected a brigade stationed in the district that day.

The confrontation began after a government investigation committee said on Saturday it had confirmed oil smuggling in the area. Al-Zaidi chaired an emergency meeting that night, ordered everyone accused of involvement to be referred to the judiciary, and dismissed nine officials, including the police chief of Babil province, senior intelligence and security officers there, and the director of the state-run Oil Pipelines Company.

The findings follow a leaked intelligence report published by Iraqi media on 9 November, which used satellite images from 2021 to 2026 to document illegal extraction near pipelines passing through the area, along with illegal refineries. A committee set up by the prime minister visited Jurf al-Nasr on Friday and confirmed the findings, including evidence that a pipeline there had been tapped.

Kataib Hezbollah denied the accusations on Sunday, calling them fabricated and accusing the prime minister's office of lying. The group said its own technical committee had found no interference with oil pipelines. The district was retaken from the Islamic State group in 2014 and has since been controlled by the faction, which has brigades in the Popular Mobilisation Forces and is designated a terrorist organisation by Washington. Most of the area's predominantly Sunni residents remain displaced and unable to return. The standoff comes amid pressure from the United States and its Gulf allies to curb Tehran's influence in Iraq, while a deadline for armed groups to hand over their weapons has been pushed back to June 2027.

Key points

  • Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi ordered the army to enter Jurf al-Nasr on Monday and set a deadline of Friday for Kataib Hezbollah to leave the area.
  • Military units are moving towards the district and the air force is ready to support ground troops once the deadline expires.
  • Kataib Hezbollah denies the oil smuggling accusations, calling them fabricated and saying its own checks found no pipeline interference.
  • Nine senior officials were dismissed on Saturday after investigators confirmed smuggling, and all suspects have been referred to the judiciary.
