Baghdad and Kataib Hezbollah Reach Deal as Federal Troops Prepare to Enter Militia Stronghold

Iraq's government has reached a compromise with the Iran-aligned Kataib Hezbollah militia over control of its stronghold south of Baghdad, with federal troops due to move into the area on Friday morning under a joint command.

The agreement, reached late on Thursday, defuses a confrontation that had threatened to turn into open fighting between government forces and one of Iraq's most powerful armed factions. A joint operations centre will open in Jurf al-Sakhar, also known as Jurf al-Nasr, at 6am local time on Friday, the armed forces commander-in-chief said, and federal units will move in at the same time.

The units moving into the town will come from the Iraqi Army, the Federal Police and the Energy Police, whose regiment will guard the oil pipelines, alongside two brigades from the Popular Mobilisation Forces, the state umbrella that includes some Kataib Hezbollah fighters. The militia's own fighters will move to other areas, the head of the Security Media Cell said, and the prime minister has approved a redeployment plan assigning different parts of the town to the army, the federal police and the mobilisation forces.

The deal followed days of mediation by senior Shiite political figures. The commander of Iran's Revolutionary Guard Quds Force, Esmail Qaani, arrived in Baghdad late on Monday and met the militia's secretary-general, Abu Hussein al-Hamidawi, the Badr Organisation leader Hadi al-Amiri and the mobilisation forces chairman Falih al-Fayyadh. A formal order has now been issued to set up a command headquarters in the town.

On Thursday, Hamidawi told his fighters to go back to their routine work while keeping combat readiness high, after days on heightened alert. He did not say explicitly that the group would hand over control of the town, leaving the exact terms of the handover unclear. The group has held the district, about 60 kilometres south-west of Baghdad, since it was retaken from the Islamic State group in 2014, and most of its displaced residents have still not been able to return.

The standoff began when a government inquiry said it had confirmed large-scale oil smuggling in the area, and the prime minister dismissed nine officials, including the police chief of Babil province and the head of the state oil pipelines company, and sent the accused for prosecution. Kataib Hezbollah denies the smuggling claims and says its own checks found no interference with pipelines. The confrontation unfolded as the United States and its Gulf allies press Baghdad to curb Tehran's influence in Iraq, and as a deadline for armed groups to hand over their weapons has been pushed back to June 2027.

Key points

  • Baghdad and Kataib Hezbollah agreed a compromise late on Thursday, and federal forces will enter Jurf al-Sakhar on Friday, with a joint operations centre opening at 6am.
  • The entering force combines army, federal police and energy police units with two Popular Mobilisation Forces brigades, while militia fighters redeploy to other areas.
  • The deal was brokered after Iran's Quds Force commander flew to Baghdad and met the militia chief and senior Shiite leaders.
  • The militia chief told fighters to resume normal duties but did not explicitly confirm the group would hand over control of the town.
