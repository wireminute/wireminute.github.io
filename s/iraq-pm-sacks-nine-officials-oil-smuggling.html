Iraqi Forces Enter Kataib Hezbollah Stronghold as Militia Vows to Stay

Iraqi security forces have entered Jurf al-Sakhar, the stronghold of the Iran-aligned Kataib Hezbollah militia south of Baghdad, under a compromise deal, though the group says it has no intention of leaving the town.

Iraqi security forces moved into Jurf al-Sakhar on Friday, taking up positions in the centre of the stronghold held by the Iran-aligned Kataib Hezbollah militia south of Baghdad. A convoy carrying Iraqi flags drove towards the town, and troops set up positions in its centre after officials announced at a briefing that state forces would take charge of security there. A correspondent on the ground saw police pass through the first militia checkpoint, while the group's fighters stayed deployed nearby.

The deployment follows a compromise reached late on Thursday after about two months of difficult negotiations that nearly collapsed. Under the arrangement, the entering force combines units from the Iraqi Army, the Federal Police and the Energy Police, whose regiment will guard the oil pipelines, with two brigades from the Popular Mobilisation Forces, the state umbrella that includes some Kataib Hezbollah fighters. A joint security headquarters will open in the town on Saturday to oversee the handover, and the government spokesman said the new command had been given sweeping authority to keep order and apply the law in the town.

But the exact terms of the deal remain unclear. A source from the militia said the group had allowed the government a way out of the difficulty it had created and insisted it will not leave Jurf al-Sakhar. The group's military spokesman had earlier said the dispute would be settled out of public view, and its chief told fighters on Thursday to return to routine duties while keeping combat readiness high, without saying explicitly that the group would hand over control of the town.

The standoff began when a government inquiry said it had confirmed large-scale oil smuggling in the area, and the prime minister dismissed nine officials, including the police chief of Babil province and the head of the state oil pipelines company, and sent the accused for prosecution. Kataib Hezbollah denies the smuggling claims and says its own checks found no interference with pipelines. The dispute was defused in part by the commander of Iran's Revolutionary Guard Quds Force, Esmail Qaani, who arrived in Baghdad on Monday and met the militia's secretary-general, the Badr Organisation leader and the mobilisation forces chairman.

The group has held the district, about 60 kilometres south-west of Baghdad, since it was retaken from the Islamic State group in 2014, and most of its displaced residents have still not been able to return. The confrontation unfolded as the United States and its Gulf allies press Baghdad to curb Tehran's influence in Iraq, and as a deadline for armed groups to hand over their weapons has been pushed back to June 2027.

Key points

  • Iraqi forces entered Jurf al-Sakhar on Friday, with armoured vehicles taking up positions in the centre.
  • The move follows a late-Thursday compromise after about two months of talks that nearly collapsed.
  • A joint security headquarters opens on Saturday to oversee the handover of the town.
  • The militia insists it will not leave the town, leaving the exact terms of the deal unclear.
