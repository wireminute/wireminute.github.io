Iraq's Prime Minister Sacks Nine Officials in Oil Smuggling Crackdown on Militia Stronghold

Iraq's prime minister has dismissed nine senior security and oil officials and ordered suspects to face the courts, after an investigation found organised oil smuggling in a district controlled by one of the country's most powerful Iran-aligned armed groups.

Ali al-Zaidi chaired an emergency security meeting late on Saturday to review the findings of a committee investigating smuggling in Jurf al-Nasr, in Babil province about 60 kilometres south-west of Baghdad. His military spokesman, Sabah al-Numan, said the prime minister ordered that everyone accused of involvement be referred to the judiciary, and dismissed nine officials, including the police chief of Babil, senior intelligence and security officers in the province, and the director of the state-run Oil Pipelines Company.

The crackdown follows a leaked intelligence report published by Iraqi media on Monday, which used satellite images from 2021 to 2026 to document illegal extraction near pipelines passing through the area, along with illegal refineries. A committee set up by the prime minister visited Jurf al-Nasr on Friday and confirmed the findings, including evidence that a pipeline there had been tapped. The government's statement did not name the armed group involved or say how many people face prosecution.

Jurf al-Nasr, formerly known as Jurf al-Sakhar, was retaken from the Islamic State group in 2014 and has since been controlled by Kataib Hezbollah, an Iran-aligned faction with brigades in the Popular Mobilisation Forces that Washington designates a terrorist organisation. Most of the area's predominantly Sunni residents remain displaced and unable to return. The group has in recent days issued sharp statements threatening the government and accusing the prime minister of corruption.

The dismissals are the sharpest step so far in al-Zaidi's confrontation with the Iran-backed militias and his push to disarm them. Analysts quoted in regional coverage said the prime minister wants to cut off the financial lifeline of a group that has resisted disarmament, and the move comes after he took office with American backing. It was not clear when the dismissed officials would be replaced, or when the court cases would begin.

Key points

  • Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi dismissed nine senior officials — Babil's police chief, intelligence and security chiefs, and the head of the state Oil Pipelines Company — after an emergency meeting on Saturday.
  • All suspects in the smuggling operation have been referred to the judiciary; no names have been made public.
  • Investigators found illegal refineries and pipeline tapping in Jurf al-Nasr, backed by satellite images covering 2021 to 2026, with the smuggling alleged to date back to 2021.
  • The district is a stronghold of Kataib Hezbollah, an Iran-aligned faction that has threatened the government and resisted disarmament — making this the sharpest move yet in the prime minister's confrontation with the militias.
