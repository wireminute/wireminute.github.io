Under-17 World Cup Kicks Off in Qatar Without the USA

The Under-17 World Cup has kicked off in Qatar with 48 teams, after the United States withdrew its side over security concerns and was replaced by the Dominican Republic.

The tournament opened on Thursday with two matches played at the same time, and will run until December 13 at the Aspire Zone sporting complex in Doha. The final is scheduled for December 13 at the Ahmad bin Ali Stadium, one of the venues from the 2022 World Cup.

US Soccer pulled its team out last week, pointing to the American government's travel advice for Qatar, which urges citizens to reconsider travel because of the risk of armed conflict in the region. The governing body said the safety of its players and staff came first.

FIFA said its own security assessments for the tournament had not changed and that it had seen no new development affecting safety in the country. Its president, Gianni Infantino, said in Doha last week that everyone in the Qatari capital was safe.

The Dominican Republic, which finished second in the qualifying group won by the United States, takes the American team's place. The United States had been due to open its campaign against Montenegro on November 21.

Qatar has not been hit by missiles since July, despite the war between the United States and Israel on one side and Iran on the other, which has kept the Gulf on high alert since February. A Formula One race is also scheduled in Doha this month.

Under tournament rules, a team that withdraws within 30 days of the opening match can be fined at least 20,000 Swiss francs, about 24,000 dollars. Qatar is set to host every edition of the annual tournament through 2029, with 104 matches in this year's expanded 48-team event.

Key points

  • The 48-team Under-17 World Cup opened in Qatar on Thursday, with the final set for December 13 at the Ahmad bin Ali Stadium.
  • The United States withdrew last week over security concerns, citing American travel advice for Qatar; the Dominican Republic takes its place.
  • FIFA said its security assessments were unchanged and that it had seen no new development affecting safety.
  • A team withdrawing within 30 days of kickoff can be fined at least 20,000 Swiss francs; Qatar will host the tournament every year through 2029.
