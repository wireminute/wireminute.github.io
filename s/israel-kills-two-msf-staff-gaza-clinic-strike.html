Israel Says Two MSF Staff Killed in Gaza Clinic Strike Were Hamas Members

The Israeli military has said two Doctors Without Borders staff killed in a strike on the charity's Gaza City clinic were members of Hamas, as the World Health Organization condemned the attack and the charity demanded accountability.

Doctors Without Borders, known by its French initials MSF, said its two watchmen were killed late on Friday when a strike hit one of its clinics in Gaza City, calling it a direct hit on a facility clearly marked with its logos and whose location had been shared with the Israeli military. The deaths brought the number of MSF staff killed in Gaza to 17, the group said, adding that it was devastated and outraged and calling for those responsible to be held accountable.

The Israeli military named the two men as Ahmed Sufyan Muhammad Ali Abu Hasira and Ma'ad Yousef Ali Sabeh, and said the second was an artillery commander in Hamas's military wing. It said the strike had not targeted a hospital and no medical infrastructure was damaged, and claimed the men had been planning attacks in violation of the ceasefire. It provided no evidence for the claim.

The head of the World Health Organization, Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, called the attack outrageous and unacceptable, saying those risking their lives to save others must be protected, not attacked. A European human rights monitor also condemned the strike on the clearly marked clinic and accused Israel of systematically targeting humanitarian personnel and facilities.

More than 1,700 health workers have been killed in Gaza since the war began, and 15 MSF staff had been killed as of January. Israel revoked the licences of MSF and other aid groups in January after they failed to complete a registration process, and the group has said the requirements threatened its ability to provide medical services. Strikes have continued across Gaza despite a ceasefire in force since October 2025.

Key points

  • Two MSF watchmen were killed late on Friday in a strike on the charity's clinic in Gaza City; MSF called it a direct hit and said 17 of its staff have now been killed in Gaza.
  • The Israeli military named the two men and said they were Hamas members, including an artillery commander, and denied targeting a hospital, but provided no evidence.
  • The WHO chief called the attack outrageous and unacceptable, and a rights monitor condemned the strike on a clearly marked clinic.
  • More than 1,700 health workers have been killed in Gaza; MSF's licence was revoked in January and strikes have continued despite the October 2025 ceasefire.
