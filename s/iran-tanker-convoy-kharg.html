Iran Readies Tanker Convoy at Kharg Island to Test US Blockade and Warns Gulf Oil Sites Face Strikes

Iran has massed more than 14 loaded oil tankers at Kharg Island and warned that any attack on them would bring strikes on oil facilities across the Gulf, in the first direct challenge to the United States naval blockade of its oil exports since July.

Iran has positioned more than 14 oil tankers at Kharg Island, with crews aboard, Iranian flags raised and many of their tracking systems switched off, as it prepares to send a loaded convoy past the United States naval blockade of its oil exports, according to satellite imagery and ship-tracking data. Iranian state-affiliated media said the tankers would sail toward the Gulf of Oman in the coming days, aiming for Asian markets, in the first direct attempt to break the blockade since it was reimposed in July.

Ship trackers also reported a tanker taking on Iranian crude from another vessel in the Gulf, in a ship-to-ship transfer carried out with tracking switched off. If that cargo clears the blockade line, it would be the first successful seaborne export of Iranian crude since mid-July. Kharg Island handles about 85 to 90 percent of Iran's crude oil exports, and loadings have fallen from about two million barrels a day in March to zero in September, according to shipping data, costing Tehran tens of billions of dollars in lost revenue.

Tehran warned that any attack on the tankers would be answered with strikes on oil and gas infrastructure across the Gulf, naming the United Arab Emirates, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait and Oman. The warning was carried by Iranian state-affiliated media and amplified by commentators close to the Revolutionary Guard. The United States tightened the blockade on Wednesday, warning that vessels receiving Iranian cargo through ship-to-ship transfers could be stopped, boarded, diverted or seized, and that ships refusing orders could face force.

The blockade was first imposed in April, lifted briefly in June, and reinstated on 14 July after talks collapsed. The standoff has already spilled into attacks on shipping, with American strikes on Guard facilities on Qeshm Island, a tanker hit off Oman, and Iranian drone and missile attacks aimed at American facilities in Bahrain and Kuwait. Analysts warn the tanker convoy is one of the most dangerous escalation risks of recent weeks, since either side firing on the ships could widen the confrontation across the world's busiest oil waterway.

Key points

  • More than 14 crewed tankers are gathered at Kharg Island, many with tracking switched off, preparing to sail toward the Gulf of Oman in the coming days.
  • A ship-to-ship transfer of Iranian crude is already under way; if it clears the blockade it would be the first seaborne crude export since mid-July.
  • Tehran warned that any attack on the tankers would be answered with strikes on oil and gas sites in six Gulf states.
  • The United States warned this week that vessels taking Iranian cargo in ship-to-ship transfers may be stopped, boarded or seized, and face force if they refuse orders.
