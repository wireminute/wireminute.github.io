SpaceX Catches Returning Starship in Tower Arms in Historic First

SpaceX has caught its Starship upper stage in the launch tower's giant arms after a full day in orbit, completing the first recovery of a rocket stage returning from space.

The spacecraft, known as Ship 42, came back to its Texas launch site on Saturday afternoon after circling the planet about 14 times over more than 22 hours. It fired its engines for the final descent and settled into the grip of the tower's mechanical arms at 3:20 p.m. local time, ending a flight that began with Friday afternoon's liftoff. No upper stage returning from orbit had ever been caught this way before.

The return capped a mission that had already delivered its payload. Shortly after reaching orbit on Friday, the ship released 26 of the company's newest internet satellites, three of them carrying cameras to photograph the heat shield once they had separated. The flight lasted far longer than any earlier Starship test, beating the roughly three-hour mark set by the previous flight in September.

The catch came a day after the mission's first recovery attempt went wrong. The Super Heavy booster, the rocket's lower half, turned back toward the tower minutes after liftoff but called off its own catch after detecting a guidance and navigation problem, company spokespeople said. It diverted to a splashdown point in the Gulf of Mexico and broke apart just above the water, leaving a section of the wreckage sticking out of the sea. The company has not yet said what caused the fault.

The double recovery was the boldest part of the flight plan: catching both halves of the rocket on a single mission, a first for the upgraded version of the vehicle. The company has caught the booster three times before, most recently in March 2025, but never the ship itself after an orbital flight. A fully reusable rocket is the core of the company's plans for Moon and Mars missions, and Saturday's catch is the closest it has come to that goal. The company said the ship and its heat shield were healthy through the flight, and that the tower catch would only be attempted if the vehicle passed a series of checks on the way home.

Key points

  • Ship 42 was caught by the launch tower's mechanical arms at Starbase on Saturday afternoon, the first time an upper stage returning from orbit has been recovered this way.
  • The ship spent more than 22 hours in space, circling Earth about 14 times, after deploying 26 new satellites on Friday.
  • The Super Heavy booster aborted its own catch on Friday after a guidance and navigation fault and broke apart over the Gulf of Mexico.
  • The company has now caught a booster three times and a ship once, moving closer to a fully reusable rocket for Moon and Mars missions.
