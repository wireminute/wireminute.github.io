Iran War Has Cost US Military $48.3 Billion, Pentagon Tells Senators

The war against Iran has cost the United States military $48.3 billion, a senior Pentagon budget official told senators on Thursday, in the first official update of the war's price in more than a month.

Arthur Miller, the Pentagon's deputy comptroller, gave the figure during a Senate Armed Services Committee hearing on his nomination to a senior Navy financial post, saying the total was current as of November 13. He said it covers the cost of military operations, the munitions used and the replacement of equipment lost or worn out in the fighting.

The total is about 26 percent higher than the 38 billion dollars estimated by the Congressional Budget Office as of August 1, and 1.2 billion dollars more than the previous Pentagon estimate of 47.1 billion dollars, which covered the war through October 16.

The figure does not include the cost of repairing damage to American military bases in the Middle East, several of which have been struck by Iranian drones and missiles during the war, nor does it count the wider harm to the world economy. Lawmakers at the hearing pressed Miller for those missing figures. Senator Elizabeth Warren said the Navy's blockade of Iranian shipping costs about 32.5 million dollars a day, and pointed to an Iranian attack on the Navy's regional headquarters in Bahrain that caused at least 400 million dollars of damage.

The war began in late February, when the United States and Israel launched their campaign against Iran, and it has since spread across the region, drawing in Gulf states and disrupting shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. The Pentagon has been asked repeatedly to account for the full cost of the conflict, including the damage to its bases, and lawmakers said they would keep pressing until those figures were produced.
