Poland Confirms Russia Tried to Kill Ukrainian Rapper Twice as Denmark Presses EU for Action

Poland has confirmed that Russian intelligence services tried to kill the Ukrainian-American rapper Albert Vasiliev, known as Kyivstoner, twice, once in Poland in August and again at a concert in Denmark in September, as Denmark prepares to ask the European Union for tougher measures against Moscow.

Poland's foreign ministry said on Sunday that the August attempt on Polish soil was thwarted and that a Russian citizen allegedly recruited by Moscow was detained on 7 August. A ministry spokesman confirmed to a news agency that the target was the same man Denmark later said was marked for killing at a concert. Poland's prime minister had disclosed on 13 August that a plot to kill a Ukrainian-American in Warsaw had been foiled, saying it was the first time someone acting on Russia's orders had sought to attack a United States citizen on the territory of another NATO country. He did not name the target at the time.

Denmark's security and intelligence service said the second plot was planned for 25 September, during a concert north of Copenhagen, and was planned and initiated by Russian intelligence services. Two Danish men appeared in court near Copenhagen on Friday charged with assisting a foreign intelligence service and attempted murder. The intelligence service linked the men to Loyal to Familia, a banned gang known for extensive and serious violent crime. Denmark expelled Russia's defence attache on Friday in response. Russia dismissed the allegations through its embassy in Copenhagen, saying they were unsubstantiated.

Denmark's foreign minister announced the expulsion on Friday after summoning Russia's ambassador to the foreign ministry. He said Denmark was certain Russian intelligence services were behind the attempt, and added that Copenhagen was considering suspending Schengen visas for Russian nationals. The two Danish men were arrested on 25 September, minutes before the rapper was due to perform at a venue in Charlottenlund, near Copenhagen. The men were stopped in a car nearby carrying two pistols and two knives, and the concert was cancelled. Both men deny the allegations and have been held in custody since September.

Denmark is expected to ask the European Union this week for tougher measures in response to the case, possibly restricting Russian citizens from entering and travelling freely within the EU. Poland said it would support such steps. The head of Denmark's intelligence service said it was the service's clear assessment that the assassination attempt was planned by Russian intelligence, and Denmark's prime minister called the case a clear illustration of the serious security situation Denmark and Europe face. The investigations are continuing. The story is still developing.

Key points

  • Poland has confirmed that the same Ukrainian-American rapper was targeted twice: an attempt in Poland in August that was thwarted, and a planned attack at a concert in Denmark in September.
  • A Russian citizen allegedly recruited by Moscow was detained in Poland on 7 August; two Danish men were arrested on 25 September minutes before the rapper's concert and have been charged with attempted murder and assisting a foreign intelligence service.
  • Denmark expelled Russia's defence attache on Friday and is considering suspending Schengen visas for Russian nationals; Moscow dismissed the allegations as unsubstantiated.
  • Denmark is expected to ask the EU this week for tougher measures, possibly restricting Russian citizens' travel in the EU, and Poland says it will support them.
