UK to Send Ukraine More Than 1,000 Long-Range Strike Drones in £260m Package

Britain will supply Ukraine with more than 1,000 long-range strike drones under a new £260 million military aid package, Prime Minister Andy Burnham announced on Thursday in Berlin, saying the weapons will let Kyiv strike military targets far behind the front line.

The package, worth about $342 million, was announced as Burnham arrived in Berlin to co-chair a meeting of the Coalition of the Willing, the group of countries backing Ukraine. The drones can fly more than 1,000 kilometres, roughly the distance from London to Berlin, and carry heavy warheads designed to destroy a target in a single strike.

London said the drones would let Ukraine hit military production sites and other lawful military targets far behind the front line, deepening Kyiv's ability to strike at the facilities that sustain the attacks on its cities. The government has not named the drone systems involved or given a breakdown of how the £260 million is divided between the different parts of the package.

The package also includes maritime, reconnaissance and logistics drones, including systems built to carry blood and medical supplies to troops at the front. More than 80 percent of the money will be spent in Britain, the government said, with small and medium-sized firms across Wales, Northern Ireland and Scotland among those expected to benefit.

Twelve British companies have also been chosen from 92 applicants to work with Ukraine's Avengers AI Labs, a programme pairing British firms with Ukrainian developers. The announcement follows a major air-defence package agreed at Tuesday's meeting of the Ukraine Defense Contact Group and a broad round of sanctions announced last week.

Defence Secretary Wes Streeting said the support was being turned into real military capability to help Ukraine defend itself against attacks on civilians. Britain says its total support for Ukraine since Russia's full-scale invasion in February 2022 now stands at £35.5 billion.
