China Warns of Countermeasures After US Bars Its Telecom Firms From American Phone Numbers

China's commerce ministry said on Sunday it firmly opposes the US regulator's vote to bar Chinese telecom firms from direct access to American phone numbers, warning Beijing will take necessary countermeasures if the ban goes ahead.

China's commerce ministry said on Sunday it strongly objects to the US Federal Communications Commission's decision to bar Chinese telecom companies from direct access to American phone numbers, warning that Beijing will take necessary countermeasures if Washington goes ahead with the ban. The ministry called on the United States to withdraw the measure at once.

The FCC decision, announced on Thursday, cited national security concerns. It will prevent China Mobile, China Telecom and China Unicom, along with other providers of internet-based telephone services, from obtaining US phone numbers directly. The regulator has in recent months pushed to separate the two countries in the telecoms, communications and testing and certification sectors, the ministry said in a statement.

Beijing said the move runs counter to the consensus reached by the leaders of the two countries and undermines their stable economic and trade relationship. The ministry accused the FCC of abusing its regulatory powers and of widening national security claims without a factual basis, and said the action amounted to the suppression of Chinese telecom companies operating in the United States.

The row comes days after the leaders of the United States, China and Russia met in Shenzhen, where they adopted a joint declaration after their first three-way summit. The ministry said the FCC's action contradicted the understanding reached between the American and Chinese leaders and urged Washington to return to resolving differences through consultation. It did not say what countermeasures Beijing might take, or when.

The FCC has been tightening its stance on Chinese telecom companies for years. In 2019 it denied China Mobile permission to provide services in the United States, citing the risk that Beijing could use the company for espionage, and in 2020 it moved to revoke the operating authorisations of China Telecom Americas, China Unicom Americas and other state-controlled carriers.

Key points

  • China's commerce ministry said on Sunday it firmly opposes the FCC vote and warned Beijing will take necessary countermeasures.
  • The FCC decision, announced on Thursday on national security grounds, bars China Mobile, China Telecom and China Unicom from direct access to US phone numbers.
  • Beijing said the move runs counter to the consensus reached by the two countries' leaders and urged Washington to retract it.
  • The FCC has tightened restrictions on Chinese carriers for years, including denying China Mobile US operating rights in 2019.
