Madonna Painting Peeled Back in Naples Chapel to Reveal a Mother and Child from Gaza

An Italian artist has turned a painting of the Madonna inside a chapel in Naples into a window onto Gaza, peeling back the sacred image to reveal a photograph of a Palestinian mother holding her child beneath it.

Eduardo Castaldo unveiled the work, titled Holy Mothers of Gaza, inside the Pappacoda Chapel in Naples. The outer layer is a painting of the Virgin Mary; where it has been torn away, a photograph taken in Gaza by Palestinian photojournalist Omar El Qattaa shows through, of a mother cradling her child.

El Qattaa is a Palestinian photojournalist whose work has documented the mourning of families in Gaza. His photograph is the heart of the piece: the painted Madonna above, a real mother holding her child below, the torn edge of the painting framing the image beneath.

The piece was revealed on Wednesday and forms part of a wider installation under the same name. Castaldo builds his works by layering one image over another, so that the picture hidden underneath carries the meaning. The installation is meant to keep the suffering of mothers and children in Gaza in front of the public.

Castaldo spent four years working as a photojournalist in the Palestinian territories, from 2007 to 2011, before leaving the profession. He has since reworked his own photographs into street art in Naples, including a mural showing Palestinian workers queuing at a checkpoint near Bethlehem, and an earlier composite of his photographs that took a prize at the Sony World Photography Awards.

The chapel, also known as San Giovanni dei Pappacoda, was raised in the early 1400s, and its carved Gothic portal is one of the few medieval survivals in the city. It is now used as a hall by the nearby university, and the installation places images of Gaza mothers and children inside a building raised for worship more than 600 years ago.
