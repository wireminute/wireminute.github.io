Democrats Choose Jeffries as Speaker Nominee, Clearing Path to Historic First

House Democrats have chosen Hakeem Jeffries as their nominee for speaker, putting the New York congressman on course to become the first Black person to hold the post when the new Congress meets in January.

Jeffries won a closed-door ballot of the incoming Democratic caucus on Wednesday, taking 251 votes to 11. He faced no declared challenger. The vote followed the party's sweeping midterm gains about two weeks ago, which will give Democrats a majority of at least 257 seats in the next House, with a handful of races still uncalled.

The nomination does not settle the job. The full House must still elect a speaker when it convenes on January 3, and 218 votes are needed on the floor. With Democrats holding the majority, Jeffries is widely expected to win, and even lawmakers who opposed him in the caucus ballot are expected to support him in January. The speaker is second in line to the presidency, after the vice president.

Eleven Democrats voted against Jeffries in the secret ballot. A small group of newly elected progressives had held back their support in the run-up, pressing him for commitments including a vote on Medicare for All and a halt to arms sales to Israel. Jeffries had asked for a recorded vote rather than being chosen by acclamation, as some allies had proposed. In the room, veteran congressman Jim Clyburn, one of the most senior members of the Congressional Black Caucus, spoke in his favour.

Jeffries, 56, has represented a New York district in the House since 2013 and has led the Democratic caucus since 2023, when he succeeded Nancy Pelosi. When he took that job he became the first Black lawmaker to lead a party in either chamber of Congress. On the Republican side, the party's conference last week chose Jim Jordan, chairman of the Judiciary Committee, as its candidate to lead the minority.

Key points

  • House Democrats nominated Hakeem Jeffries for speaker on Wednesday in a closed-door secret ballot, 251 votes to 11.
  • If the full House elects him on January 3, Jeffries will become the first Black speaker in US history.
  • Eleven Democrats voted against him after a small group of newly elected progressives pressed for policy concessions.
  • Democrats will hold at least 257 seats in the next House after sweeping midterm wins, and 218 votes are needed to elect a speaker.
