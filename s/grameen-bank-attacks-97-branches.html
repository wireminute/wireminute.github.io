Grameen Bank Records 97 Attacks in Two Weeks as Bangladesh Unrest Targets Yunus's Lender

Grameen Bank, the Nobel Prize-winning microcredit lender founded by Muhammad Yunus, has recorded 97 attacks on its branches and offices across Bangladesh in less than two weeks, as arson and vandalism turn the bank into a flashpoint of the country's political tensions.

The bank's latest internal memorandum, dated November 23, puts the total at 97 attacks between November 10 and 23. In a letter to the home minister on November 19, the bank had counted 72 branches and offices hit in the first ten days, five of them completely gutted and 67 damaged. The attacks span 40 districts. At Kashiani in Gopalganj on November 18, about a dozen men carrying machetes and petrol broke the locks of a branch, tried to force open the vault with a grinding tool, dragged out around 40 sacks of vouchers and records and set them on fire, while shouting slogans associated with the Awami League.

The government blames supporters of the Awami League, the party led by former Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina. Hasina was ousted in August 2024, later convicted by a tribunal over the deadly crackdown on protesters that year and faces a death sentence. She now lives in exile in India and has said she intends to return. Her party is banned from electoral politics. Yunus, who led the interim government after her removal, has since stepped down as chief adviser. Analysts say the bank has become the easiest symbol of Yunus for the party's supporters to strike, and a way to show the party still has a street presence despite the ban.

The stakes go beyond damaged buildings. The bank counts roughly 10.6 million members, the large majority of them women in rural areas, and its managing director puts outstanding loans at more than 19,000 crore taka, about $1.53 billion, with members' deposits of around 25,000 crore taka, about $2 billion. A burning branch destroys loan files, savings records and repayment schedules, and the bank has suspended interest on loans at branches unable to operate. Its 21,000 employees have been left fearful, and the bank has moved staff from the riskiest locations and raised security.

The United Nations called on November 16 for thorough investigations into all the violence, and the bank has asked the home minister for an impartial inquiry, legal action against those responsible and protection for its branches. Police say eight cases have been filed and eight people arrested, but a home ministry official conceded the force is short of manpower. The government says it is making every effort to maintain order, while the bank says it has mostly had to stay alert and protect itself.

Key points

  • Grameen Bank counts 97 attacks on its branches and offices from November 10 to November 23, according to a memorandum dated November 23.
  • Five branches were completely gutted and 67 damaged in the first ten days, the bank told the home minister on November 19.
  • The bank serves roughly 10.6 million members, the large majority rural women, holding $1.53 billion in outstanding loans and $2 billion in deposits.
  • The UN has called for thorough investigations; police say eight cases have been filed and eight people arrested.
