Yunus Appeals for Global Pressure as Attacks on Grameen Bank Reach 97

Muhammad Yunus has appealed for international pressure to stop the attacks on Grameen Bank, after his own home was set on fire and the Nobel Prize-winning lender recorded 97 attacks on its branches and offices in less than two weeks.

The bank's latest internal memorandum, dated November 23, puts the total at 97 attacks between November 10 and 23, across 40 districts. In a letter to the home minister on November 19, the bank had counted 72 hit in the first ten days, five completely gutted and 67 damaged. At Kashiani in Gopalganj on November 18, about a dozen men carrying machetes and petrol broke the locks of a branch, tried to cut open the vault, dragged out some 40 sacks of vouchers and records and set them alight.

In a statement on Monday, Yunus called the systematic assault on the bank "unbelievably shocking" and said bank officials and borrower-members had "narrowly escaped fatal injury" in several incidents. He called the attacks a "calculated campaign of arson and terror" and appealed to the international community to press those behind them, warning it threatened the security and livelihoods of millions of poor rural women who own the institution. His own home in Chattogram was among the targets, where two of his younger brothers live with their families.

The government blames supporters of the Awami League, the party of former Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina, ousted in August 2024 and later convicted over the deadly crackdown on protesters that year. She now lives in exile in India and says she intends to return. Her party is banned from electoral politics. Yunus, who led the interim government after her removal, has since stepped down as chief adviser. Analysts say the bank is the easiest symbol of Yunus for the party's supporters to strike. The unrest has spread beyond the bank, with police stations and the homes of local MPs set on fire and three members of the BNP allegedly killed in Faridpur and Narail.

The stakes go beyond damaged buildings. The bank counts roughly 10.6 million members, mostly rural women, with about $1.53 billion in outstanding loans and around $2 billion in deposits. A burning branch destroys loan files, savings records and repayment schedules, and the bank has suspended interest on loans at branches unable to operate. Its 21,000 employees are fearful.

The United Nations called on November 16 for thorough investigations, and the bank has asked the home minister for an impartial inquiry and branch protection. Police say eight cases have been filed and eight people arrested, but a home ministry official conceded the force is short of manpower.

  • Muhammad Yunus has appealed for international pressure to stop the attacks, calling them a calculated campaign of arson and terror; his own home in Chattogram was among the targets.
  • Grameen Bank counts 97 attacks on its branches and offices from November 10 to November 23, according to a memorandum dated November 23.
  • The bank serves roughly 10.6 million members, the large majority rural women, holding $1.53 billion in outstanding loans and $2 billion in deposits.
