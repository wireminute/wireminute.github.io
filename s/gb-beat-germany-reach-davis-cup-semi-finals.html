Great Britain Beat Germany to Reach Davis Cup Semi-Finals for First Time Since 2019

Great Britain are through to the Davis Cup semi-finals for the first time since 2019 after Henry Patten and Neal Skupski won the deciding doubles against Germany in Bologna, sealing a 2-1 quarter-final victory.

Patten and Skupski beat Kevin Krawietz and Tim Puetz in the deciding rubber on Thursday, winning 6-2, 7-6(6) after the two singles matches had been shared. Britain will play Austria or Spain in Saturday's semi-final, with the winners of Thursday evening's other quarter-final deciding the opponent.

Cameron Norrie had put Britain ahead, beating Jan-Lennard Struff in three sets (6-3, 3-6, 6-2) after fighting back from 0-40 down on his serve at 2-2 in the deciding set. Alexander Zverev, in his first Davis Cup outing since becoming world No. 1, levelled the tie for Germany with a 6-3, 6-2 win over Arthur Fery in one hour and 10 minutes.

The doubles was the sternest test. Krawietz and Puetz arrived with 18 wins from 20 Davis Cup matches as a pair, the most successful German doubles team in the competition's history. But Patten and Skupski, playing together for the first time since announcing they will team up for the 2027 season, held off four break points in the opening set and edged a tense second-set tie-break, with Skupski setting up match point with a sharp half-volley and finishing the job with a return winner.

Patten's decision to end his long-running partnership with Finland's Harri Heliovaara had drawn public criticism this week, including an angry blog post from the Finn, but the new pairing delivered when it mattered. The result moves Britain a step closer to ending an 11-year wait for the title, last won in 2015, and extends captain Leon Smith's 17-year tenure into one last week in charge. Defending champions Italy face the Czech Republic in Friday's first semi-final, with the final on Sunday.

Key points

  • Patten and Skupski beat Krawietz and Puetz in the deciding doubles (6-2, 7-6(6)) to complete a 2-1 quarter-final win over Germany in Bologna.
  • Norrie beat Struff 6-3, 3-6, 6-2 to put Britain ahead, before Zverev levelled the tie with a 6-3, 6-2 win over Fery in his first Davis Cup outing since becoming world No. 1.
  • Britain reach the semi-finals for the first time since 2019 and will face Austria or Spain on Saturday, chasing a first title since 2015.
  • The win extends captain Leon Smith's 17-year tenure into a final week in charge; defending champions Italy play the Czech Republic in Friday's first semi-final.
