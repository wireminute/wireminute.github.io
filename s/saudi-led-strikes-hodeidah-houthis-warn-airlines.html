Houthis Threaten Saudi Red Sea Ports as Coalition Orders Hodeidah Evacuation

Yemen's Houthis have threatened to strike every Saudi port on the Red Sea, including Jeddah, after the Saudi-led coalition ordered civilians to evacuate Hodeidah port, raising fears of a sharp escalation in the Red Sea.

The coalition's spokesman, Major General Turki al-Maliki, told civilians to leave the port area for their safety, marking out the zone in a map posted online. Minutes later, the Houthis warned workers and shipping companies to pull out of Saudi Arabia's Red Sea ports at once. "We warn all employees, workers and anyone near these ports to evacuate immediately and stay away from them," the group said in a statement.

Houthi-run media later reported that the coalition had attacked Hodeidah port, shortly after the evacuation order. That report has not been independently confirmed. The coalition said it had intercepted and destroyed two drones in Saudi Arabia's Eastern Province. The United States State Department urged its citizens across the Middle East to exercise heightened vigilance, citing the increased threat of attacks by the Houthis and Iranian efforts to target American officials and facilities.

Saudi Arabia's air defences shot down five drones and a ballistic missile fired by the Houthi militia in Yemen on Friday, the Saudi-led coalition said, in the heaviest day of cross-border attacks since the fighting reignited in July. Four drones and the missile were intercepted over the kingdom's southern region, and a fifth drone was destroyed over the Riyadh region, the coalition said. No damage or casualties from the intercepted attacks were reported.

Shortly after the strikes on Hodeidah port, the coalition said it had also intercepted two ballistic missiles launched by the Houthis towards the Jazan region, with debris falling in the Al Shuqaiq area. Saudi civil defence issued early-warning alerts in the southern regions of Asir and Jazan, and in the cities of Abha and Khamis Mushait, before announcing that the danger had passed. The attempted strikes came a day after a Houthi attack damaged a tower in Riyadh's King Abdullah Financial District, home to the kingdom's sovereign wealth fund.

Earlier on Friday the coalition said it had hit facilities at Hodeidah port used to smuggle weapons and move ballistic missiles, with operations also under way in Sanaa, Saada and Taiz. It had told civilians to leave the port area first. The coalition said it destroyed seven military targets in Sanaa, including missile and drone facilities, six workshops used to build and assemble ballistic missile and drone launch platforms, and a major supply depot. Houthi-run media also reported a coalition air raid on a power station in the port town of Mocha. Yemen's information minister said the port kept working.

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree answered by warning that Jeddah and every Saudi port on the Red Sea would become targets if Hodeidah was hit, telling workers and shipping firms to leave at once. He warned airlines that Saudi airspace would be the scene of Houthi operations in the coming hours, except over Mecca and Medina, and named a power station at Shuqaiq and Riyadh's financial district as targets. The group later said the King Abdullah Financial District was a military target and told people to stay away from it immediately.

The Houthis also accused the coalition of striking a civilian boat early on Friday, killing 19 people. That claim could not be independently verified. Hodeidah is the main entry point for food and supplies into Houthi-held northern Yemen.

On Saturday, the fighting spread further. Houthi-affiliated media reported coalition air strikes on telecommunications networks in Saada and Taiz, saying infrastructure was destroyed and homes damaged in Taiz. Yemeni government forces said a senior Houthi commander was killed as they pushed back attacks in Taiz, and coalition air strikes hit missile launch sites west of the city, according to reports from the ground.
