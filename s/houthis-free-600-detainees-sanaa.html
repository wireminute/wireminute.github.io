Houthis Free 600 Detainees in Sanaa in Largest Unilateral Release of Yemen War

Yemen's Houthi authorities have freed 600 people detained in connection with the country's war, in a unilateral release in the capital Sanaa confirmed by the Red Cross, as fighting intensified in the south of the country.

The release in Sanaa on Sunday was organised by the Houthis' National Committee for Prisoners' Affairs, with support from the International Committee of the Red Cross. The freed men were fighters from Saudi-backed government forces who had been captured on several fronts. Red Cross delegates interviewed each detainee privately before the release, checked their health and helped arrange their return home. The organisation said it played no part in deciding who was freed or how many.

The head of the Houthi prisoners committee, Abdulqader al-Murtada, described the move as the biggest one-sided release of prisoners since the conflict began, and said it brought the total freed under the group's amnesty initiatives to 2,612, all without an exchange. Those figures could not be independently verified, and there was no immediate comment from the government.

The release was not part of the Stockholm Agreement, the United Nations-brokered framework for prisoner exchanges between the two sides, and no reciprocal release by the government was announced. The Houthis called the move a humanitarian initiative while accusing their adversaries of failing to make matching moves, and renewed their readiness to carry out a broader exchange deal.

Fighting between the Houthis and government forces resumed in July, when a 2022 United Nations-brokered truce collapsed. The release came on a day of heavy fighting in the south: a Houthi missile strike killed eight people and wounded 12 at a checkpoint in Lahj province, near Aden, according to military and government sources. Government forces said they were withdrawing from the Bab el-Mandeb area to focus on defending Aden, leaving the Houthis in control of ground overlooking the strategic strait.

Key points

  • Houthi authorities in Sanaa freed 600 people detained in connection with Yemen's war, in a unilateral move confirmed by the Red Cross.
  • The Red Cross interviewed each detainee privately and checked their health, but said it did not choose who was released.
  • The Houthis call it the biggest one-sided release of the conflict and say 2,612 detainees have now been freed without exchange.
