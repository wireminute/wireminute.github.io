Iran Sets Seven Conditions for US Talks as Hormuz Tanker Attacks Continue

A third tanker struck a mine in the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday, Iran's army said, hours after the Revolutionary Guard reported two other tankers had hit mines and caught fire, as Tehran offered to reopen the waterway within 48 hours if Washington meets its conditions.

Iran's army and the Revolutionary Guard's navy said on Tuesday that six tankers had struck mines in the strait over the past day, in the latest in a run of attacks on shipping in the world's most important oil waterway.

Parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, who leads Tehran's negotiating team, told lawmakers there would be no practical steps and no talks with Washington until the conditions are met. Iranian state media said the demands include an end to hostilities, the lifting of US sanctions and the naval blockade of Iranian ports, the release of frozen Iranian assets, compensation for war damage and guarantees against renewed attacks. He warned Tehran would widen the conflict if its adversaries made a miscalculation. Qatar said on Tuesday that diplomatic contacts were continuing to bridge the differences and restore freedom of navigation through the strait.

Ship-tracking data shows the scale of the disruption. Between November 1 and 7, a total of 226 vessels entered or left the Gulf through the strait. Between November 17 and 23, only 125 did, a fall of more than 40%, according to figures from the tracking platform MarineTraffic. On Sunday, only one vessel left the Gulf and two entered, the lowest daily figures of the month. Before the war, 125 to 140 ships passed through the strait each day.

The same reporting put the toll for the past week at over 25 tankers struck. The army commander, Amir Hatami, called on Tuesday for tighter control of the waterway, saying conditions should be made more difficult for the enemy. The head of the Revolutionary Guard navy warned ships not to use routes Tehran had not approved and not to disregard its maritime warnings.

The disruption has pushed up shipping costs, but crude prices dropped to their lowest in three months on Tuesday as traders pointed to growing supply and a possible surplus, even as the danger to shipping through the strait remained. Brent crude had traded above $120 a barrel at one point during the war.

Three tankers caught fire on Monday night, the Guard said, one off the strait's southern coast and two to its east. The Guard said the vessels were sailing under extensive American air cover when they hit the mines, and that the escort had failed to stop the attacks. It warned ship owners, captains and shipping firms that American military cover would not excuse them from following its own naval traffic rules, and said the strait was closed to vessels that ignored its warnings. The tankers were not named and no casualty figures were given.

Britain's maritime trade body gave the first independent backing for an attack in the strait this week, verifying that a tanker had been hit on Sunday evening, November 22, at 20:32 GMT, by a projectile whose origin was unknown. The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations organisation said it could not assess the damage, and advised vessels to transit the area cautiously.

Iran also widened its enforcement, with the Persian Gulf Strait Authority adding 26 tugboats to a list of vessels barred from working for ships it says broke its rules south of the strait. Iran's armed forces chief of staff, Major-General Ali Abdollahi, said moving American bases elsewhere in the region would not put them beyond the reach of Iranian missiles.

There is still no independent confirmation of the full toll, and the American military command in the region has not responded to the claims. The Guard said it would release footage of the explosions and fires in the coming hours, but none had appeared by Tuesday evening.

The incident follows a claim on Sunday that two tankers hit mines in the strait and that one supertanker carrying more than two million barrels of crude sank, a claim that also remains unverified. Since November 10 the Guard has reported a string of similar incidents, and maritime data shows reported attacks on shipping in the strait, the Gulf and the Gulf of Oman reached more than 30 in October, the highest monthly figure since the war began in February, with at least 22 more reported in November.

The United States is also investigating Iran as the main suspect behind cyberattacks on as many as 50 ships carrying oil, gas and chemicals, according to a news report. Iran tightened its control of the strait in late February, barring passage for vessels linked to Israel and the United States after the two countries carried out joint strikes on Iranian territory.

Two more tankers were hit early on Wednesday, the Guard's navy said, with explosions and engine-room fires after the vessels struck mines on the southern and eastern sides of the strait. There was no independent confirmation of the latest incidents, and it was not clear whether anyone aboard was hurt.

A third tanker was later reported hit on Wednesday, according to Iran's semi-official Fars news agency, citing a statement from the Iranian army. The vessel struck a mine and caught fire in a restricted zone of the strait. It was not named and no casualty figures were given, and there was no independent confirmation of the latest incident.

Tehran has told Washington it will not restore normal shipping through the strait until its demands are met, including the lifting of a US naval blockade of Iranian ports and an end to the war on all fronts. Under a proposal conveyed through Qatari mediators, Tehran offered to reopen the waterway within 48 hours of Washington meeting its conditions.

Iran's army said it would watch the strait more closely, warning ships that tried to pass without its approval faced greater danger, and an Iranian diplomat said no ships would be allowed through while the American blockade stayed in place. In the past ten days alone there have been at least 40 such incidents in the strait, with more than 160 since the start of the year, according to Iranian figures reported from Tehran.

Key points

  • Iran's parliament speaker said there will be no talks with Washington until seven conditions are met, including lifting sanctions and the US naval blockade.
  • Three tankers were hit by mines on Wednesday, Iran's forces said, two reported by the Guard early in the day and a third later reported by the army.
  • Tehran offered to reopen the strait within 48 hours if Washington meets its conditions, under a proposal conveyed through Qatari mediators.
  • Vessel crossings through the strait have fallen by more than 40% this month, from 226 in the first week of November to 125 in the third week, ship-tracking data shows.
