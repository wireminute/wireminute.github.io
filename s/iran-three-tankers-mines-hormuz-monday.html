Trump Says Iran War Funded Through January as Tehran Tightens Its Grip on Hormuz

President Donald Trump has said the war against Iran is funded through the end of January, a disclosure that puts a time limit on any new American offensive, as Tehran ordered its navy to tighten restrictions on shipping through the Strait of Hormuz and three more vessels were reported hit.

Trump made the disclosure in an interview with the New York Post on Tuesday, saying the campaign was funded through January. Democrats take control of Congress on January 3, and Trump warned they could turn what he called a military victory into a defeat. He said regime change in Iran was probably achievable but not his aim, saying his only concern was stopping Tehran obtaining a nuclear weapon. He announced no decision on resuming strikes.

Pressure to act before the window closes is building. The New York Times reported that Israel’s prime minister has personally urged Trump to restart operations before January. NBC News reported that the administration is weighing strikes on senior Iranian leaders, and Israel’s defence minister has accused Iran of developing chemical weapons.

Tehran has hardened its own position. Army chief Major General Amir Hatami ordered the navy to progressively tighten restrictions on the strait, while parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said there would be no talks until seven conditions are met. State media said the demands include an end to hostilities, the lifting of sanctions and the naval blockade, the release of frozen assets, compensation and guarantees against renewed attacks. Tehran has offered to reopen the waterway within 48 hours of Washington meeting its conditions, under a proposal conveyed through Qatari mediators.

The confrontation at sea is escalating. Britain’s maritime trade body said three commercial vessels were struck by unidentified projectiles while crossing the strait. Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said a ship carrying liquefied natural gas struck a naval mine and caught fire, and Iran’s army said an oil tanker had hit a mine and caught fire east of the strait. Ship-tracking data shows the disruption: 226 vessels entered or left the Gulf through the strait between November 1 and 7, but only 125 between November 17 and 23, a fall of more than 40 percent, according to MarineTraffic.

Key points

  • Trump says the war against Iran is funded through the end of January, narrowing the window for a new offensive before Democrats take Congress.
  • Iran’s army chief ordered the navy to tighten restrictions on the Strait of Hormuz, and Tehran ruled out talks until seven conditions are met.
  • Three more commercial vessels were reported struck by projectiles in the strait, and Iran said a gas carrier and an oil tanker hit mines and caught fire.
  • Shipping through the strait has fallen by more than 40 percent this month as attacks on tankers continue.
