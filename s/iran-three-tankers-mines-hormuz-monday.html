Six Tankers Hit in Strait of Hormuz in 24 Hours as Iran Widens Shipping Crackdown

Iran's army and Revolutionary Guard navy said on Tuesday that mines had struck six oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz during the past day, as Tehran widened its crackdown on shipping in the world's most important oil waterway.

Iran's army and the Revolutionary Guard's navy said on Tuesday that six tankers had struck mines in the strait over the past day, in the latest in a run of attacks on shipping in the world's most important oil waterway.

Three tankers caught fire on Monday night, the Guard said, one off the strait's southern coast and two to its east. The Guard said the vessels were sailing under extensive American air cover when they hit the mines, and that the escort had failed to stop the attacks. It warned ship owners, captains and shipping firms that American military cover would not excuse them from following its own naval traffic rules, and said the strait was closed to vessels that ignored its warnings. The tankers were not named and no casualty figures were given.

Britain's maritime trade body gave the first independent backing for an attack in the strait this week, verifying that a tanker had been hit on Sunday evening, November 22, at 20:32 GMT, by a projectile whose origin was unknown. The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations organisation said it could not assess the damage, and advised vessels to transit the area cautiously.

Iran also widened its enforcement, with the Persian Gulf Strait Authority adding 26 tugboats to a list of vessels barred from working for ships it says broke its rules south of the strait. Iran's armed forces chief of staff, Major-General Ali Abdollahi, said moving American bases elsewhere in the region would not put them beyond the reach of Iranian missiles.

There is still no independent confirmation of the full toll, and the American military command in the region has not responded to the claims. The Guard said it would release footage of the explosions and fires in the coming hours, but none had appeared by Tuesday evening.

The incident follows a claim on Sunday that two tankers hit mines in the strait and that one supertanker carrying more than two million barrels of crude sank, a claim that also remains unverified. Since November 10 the Guard has reported a string of similar incidents, and maritime data shows reported attacks on shipping in the strait, the Gulf and the Gulf of Oman reached more than 30 in October, the highest monthly figure since the war began in February, with at least 22 more reported in November.

The United States is also investigating Iran as the main suspect behind cyberattacks on as many as 50 ships carrying oil, gas and chemicals, according to a news report. Iran tightened its control of the strait in late February, barring passage for vessels linked to Israel and the United States after the two countries carried out joint strikes on Iranian territory.

Key points

  • Six tankers were hit by mines in the Strait of Hormuz in 24 hours, Iran's army and Revolutionary Guard navy said; three caught fire on Monday night, one off the southern coast and two to the east.
  • Britain's maritime trade body verified that a tanker was struck by an unknown projectile on Sunday, and advised vessels to transit cautiously; it could not assess the damage.
  • Iran added 26 tugboats to a non-compliance list and warned shipping firms that American air cover does not excuse vessels from its naval rules.
  • No vessels have been named, no casualties were reported and there is no independent confirmation of the full toll; the US military command has not responded and promised footage has not appeared.
