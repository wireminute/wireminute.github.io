Iran Claims LNG Carrier Hit a Mine and Caught Fire in Hormuz as Tanker Attacks Continue

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said on Wednesday evening that a ship carrying liquefied natural gas struck a naval mine and caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz, the fourth vessel Tehran has said was hit in the strait that day, as it held to its conditions for talks with Washington.

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said on Wednesday evening that a ship carrying liquefied natural gas struck a naval mine and exploded in the Strait of Hormuz. The Guards' navy said the carrier had switched off its positioning systems and was using a route Tehran considers unauthorised, one it described as suggested by the United States. The vessel was not named, no casualty figures were given, and the claim could not be independently verified.

Earlier on Wednesday, Iran's army said an oil tanker had struck a mine and caught fire in a restricted zone east of the strait, hours after the Guards said two oil tankers had struck mines, exploded and caught fire on the southern and eastern sides of the waterway. None of the three vessels was named and no casualty figures were given.

A maritime security firm separately reported that a Hong Kong-flagged tanker was hit by an unidentified projectile about nine and a half nautical miles off Lima, Oman, on Wednesday. The firm said the vessel suffered minimal damage, was able to continue its transit and reported no casualties, and that it had been sailing with its tracking transponder switched off.

Iran's army said it had deployed new systems and upgraded existing ones to strengthen surveillance and control of the strait. A military spokesperson warned that vessels passing through without coordinating with Iranian forces faced greater risks and had struck mines in recent weeks.

Parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, who leads Tehran's negotiating team, told lawmakers there would be no practical steps and no talks with Washington until seven conditions are met. State media said the demands include an end to hostilities, the lifting of US sanctions and the naval blockade of Iranian ports, the release of frozen Iranian assets, compensation for war damage and guarantees against renewed attacks. Tehran has offered to reopen the waterway within 48 hours of Washington meeting its conditions, under a proposal conveyed through Qatari mediators.

Ship-tracking data shows the scale of the disruption. Between November 1 and 7, a total of 226 vessels entered or left the Gulf through the strait. Between November 17 and 23, only 125 did, a fall of more than 40 percent, according to figures from the tracking platform MarineTraffic. Before the war, 125 to 140 ships passed through the strait each day.

Britain's maritime trade body gave the first independent backing for an attack in the strait this week, verifying that a tanker had been hit on Sunday evening, November 22, at 20:32 GMT, by a projectile whose origin was unknown. The organisation said it could not assess the damage and advised vessels to transit the area cautiously.

There is still no independent confirmation of the full toll, and the American military command in the region has not responded to the claims. The Guard said it would release footage of the explosions and fires in the coming hours, but none had appeared by Tuesday evening.

The incidents are the latest in a run of attacks on shipping in the waterway since the war between the United States, Israel and Iran began in February. Iran tightened its control of the strait in late February, barring passage for vessels linked to Israel and the United States, and the disruption has pushed up shipping costs even as crude prices dropped to their lowest in three months on Tuesday.

Key points

  • The Guards said an LNG carrier struck a mine and caught fire in the strait on Wednesday evening, the fourth vessel Tehran said was hit that day; the ship was not named and the claim is unverified.
  • A Hong Kong-flagged tanker was separately hit by an unidentified projectile off Oman on Wednesday and continued its transit with minimal damage, a maritime security firm said.
  • Iran's army said it has deployed new surveillance systems in the strait and warned ships passing without its coordination face greater risks.
  • Tehran still refuses talks until Washington meets seven conditions, including lifting sanctions and the naval blockade, but has offered to reopen the waterway within 48 hours if they are met.
