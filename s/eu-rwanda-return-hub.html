Five EU Countries Set to Announce Rwanda Migrant Return Hub in First Outside the Bloc

Five European Union countries plan to unveil on Monday a deal that would move rejected asylum seekers to a migrant return hub in Rwanda, the first facility of its kind outside the bloc, according to people familiar with the plans.

Germany, Denmark, Austria, Greece and the Netherlands are expected to unveil the agreement at a migration conference in Vienna, where Rwanda is sending a delegation. In September the five said they were pursuing an agreement with a state outside the EU to host so-called return hubs, as part of a wider push to raise deportations and curb irregular migration.

The hub would start as a pilot holding about 100 people, and the five hope to have it running in 2027. It would apply only to people who have exhausted every legal route to stay in the EU, a narrower scope than Britain's abandoned Rwanda plan, struck down as unlawful in 2023. Rwanda confirmed in August that early talks were under way, and the deal follows the EU's new returns rules, which let member states set up return hubs beyond the bloc. Rights groups have raised concerns.

Key points

  • Germany, Denmark, Austria, Greece and the Netherlands plan to unveil a deal on Monday under which rejected asylum seekers would be moved to a return hub in Rwanda, a first for any such facility outside the EU.
  • The hub would start as a pilot for about 100 people, possibly 20 from each country, and could be running in 2027; it would apply only to people who have exhausted all legal routes to remain.
  • Rwanda confirmed in August that early talks were under way; the deal follows the EU's new returns rules, and rights groups have raised concerns about the plan.
