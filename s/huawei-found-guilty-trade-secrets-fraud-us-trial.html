Huawei Found Guilty of Stealing Trade Secrets and Bank Fraud in US Trial

A federal jury in Brooklyn has convicted Chinese telecoms giant Huawei of conspiracy to steal trade secrets and bank fraud, after a trial lasting almost three months.

The jury found Huawei and three of its subsidiaries guilty on 10 of the 12 counts they faced on Tuesday, including conspiracy to steal trade secrets, bank fraud, wire fraud, violating United States sanctions on Iran and conspiracy to launder money. It acquitted the company of the most serious charge, racketeering conspiracy, and of conspiracy to obstruct justice.

Prosecutors told the court that Huawei misled banks, including HSBC, Standard Chartered and BNP Paribas, about its business in Iran, where it helped build internet and mobile networks, while running a decades-long scheme to take technology from American rivals. The examples put before the jury included the source code for Cisco routers, antenna technology from the firm Quintel, a circuit board design from chip company CNEX, and the secrets of a T-Mobile testing robot, known as Tappy, used to check how phone screens respond to touch.

The case began in 2018, when Huawei chief financial officer Meng Wanzhou was detained in Canada on a United States warrant. Criminal charges were filed in early 2019 and expanded the following year, and in 2019 Washington placed Huawei on a trade blacklist that cut its access to American technology. The trial heard from more than 60 witnesses for the prosecution and 15 for the defence.

Huawei lawyer Brian Heberlig said the company would appeal the convictions, calling the acquittal on racketeering a vindication and a rejection of the prosecution's central account of the company. Huawei said no court had ever found it liable for wilful theft of intellectual property. Sentencing has been set for September 14, 2027, and the company could face penalties running into billions of dollars.
