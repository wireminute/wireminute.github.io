German Boat Dealer Suspected of Supplying Smugglers Behind a Third of Channel Crossings

A small boat dealer in western Germany is suspected of supplying the smuggling gangs behind nearly a third of the small-boat crossings to Britain this year, according to a joint investigation by European broadcasters and newspapers.

Marvin Reuter, 35, runs MaReBoote, a boat dealership in Werne, near Dortmund. Files reviewed by German broadcasters WDR and NDR, the newspaper Suddeutsche Zeitung and Le Monde, show that boats or engines from his firm were involved in 29 percent of detected crossings in the first four months of 2026, according to a British security assessment. French investigators say at least 82 of the firm's boats reached the English coast in just over two years to May.

Reuter is the subject of a European Arrest Warrant issued by France in May, and he is contesting extradition. If he is tried in France, he could face charges of smuggling, criminal conspiracy and involuntary manslaughter. His equipment is suspected of being linked to two fatal crossing attempts in which four men died. He has not been formally charged and is presumed innocent until proven guilty.

The investigation draws on hundreds of pages of police, court and tax files. In early May, French police stopped two German men near the northern French coast with an inflatable boat and an engine in a car and trailer carrying the MaRe Boote logo. The men, who have since been convicted of smuggling offences, said they had already delivered equipment to France four to six times for Reuter and knew what the boats would be used for. Recordings from a bugged prison cell captured a suspected gang leader talking about nautical equipment from a German firm run by a man called Marvin, and the firm's address was found on an alleged smuggler's phone.

Reuter was arrested in Germany in May and later released pending extradition. Police seized more than 30 dinghies and 9,000 lifejackets on the day of his arrest, and the National Crime Agency's account of raids on 21 May lists more than 30 boats, more than 20 engines, 360 pumps and over 9,000 lifejackets. Five other men have been charged in the wider investigation. When confronted at his business, Reuter declined to answer questions and accused reporters of harassment. He has previously said he was horrified to learn his boats were being used by smugglers. Germany outlawed aiding smuggling to Britain at the end of last year, after an earlier investigation exposed the country's role as a storage hub for the gangs. Crossings have fallen sharply in 2026, which British authorities put down to law enforcement.

Key points

  • Boats or engines from MaReBoote were allegedly involved in 29 percent of detected Channel crossings in the first four months of 2026.
  • French investigators say at least 82 of the firm's boats reached the English coast in just over two years to May.
  • The firm's managing director, Marvin Reuter, 35, is contesting extradition to France, where he could face smuggling, conspiracy and manslaughter charges.
  • His equipment is suspected of being linked to two fatal crossing attempts in which four men died; he has not been charged and is presumed innocent.
