Russian Charged With Spying on Palantir Staff in US Over Ukraine Support

A Russian man has been charged in the United States with acting as a foreign agent after prosecutors said he stalked employees of the defence technology company Palantir because of its support for Ukraine.

Igor D'iu was indicted by a federal grand jury in California on charges of acting as an agent of a foreign government, interstate stalking and fraud linked to identity documents, the Justice Department said on Wednesday. He faces up to 21 years in prison if convicted. D'iu has been in federal custody since his first court appearance on 2 November, and is due back in court on 24 November.

Prosecutors say D'iu was recruited through the Telegram messaging app by Russian handlers who told him his work was for "the Motherland" and directed him to gather information on the company because of the help it gives Ukraine. The indictment does not name the company, describing it only as a publicly traded technology and defence company whose software is used by Ukraine in the war with Russia. People familiar with the case identified it as Palantir Technologies, and the company's chief executive, Alex Karp, has publicly backed Ukraine.

According to the indictment, D'iu flew from Istanbul to Mexico City in June and crossed into the United States from Mexico the following month with the help of smugglers arranged by his co-conspirators. Prosecutors say he then looked up the home addresses of employees, followed them as they left the company's offices in Palo Alto, California, and photographed their laptop screens from a coffee shop nearby. On 3 September, they say, he followed a senior female executive's car for about 30 miles (48 kilometres) and drove off only when she went to a police station. He is also accused of using tracking devices and false identity documents, and of buying laptops, phones and remote-access software for the operation.

Prosecutors described the case as a state-sponsored operation targeting the staff of an American technology and defence company because of its support for Ukraine. Palantir has said it deployed its technology in Ukraine, and Karp said in 2023 that the company's software was behind most of the targeting in the country. A person familiar with the matter said no Palantir data was breached. The company did not respond to a request for comment.

Key points

  • Igor D'iu is charged with acting as a foreign agent, stalking and identity-document fraud, and faces up to 21 years in prison
  • Prosecutors say he was recruited on Telegram and told to gather information on the company because of its help for Ukraine
  • He allegedly followed staff in Palo Alto, photographed laptop screens and tailed a senior executive for 30 miles
  • The company is not named in the indictment, but people familiar with the case identified it as Palantir Technologies
