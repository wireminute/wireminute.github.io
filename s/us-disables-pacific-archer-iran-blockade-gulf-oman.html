US Disables Another Ship Trying to Run Iran Blockade in Gulf of Oman

The United States said it disabled a commercial ship in the Gulf of Oman on Thursday after the vessel tried to run the naval blockade of Iranian ports, the latest ship to be stopped as Tehran vowed to break the embargo.

A military aircraft hit the stern of the Palau-flagged bulk carrier Pacific Archer with precision-guided weapons, the military's regional command said in a statement on Friday. The strike knocked out the ship's propulsion, and the civilian crew was not hurt.

The command said the vessel had sailed from the Iranian port of Bandar Abbas and pressed on despite repeated warnings to turn back. Tracking sites listed its destination as Singapore. The ship is Chinese-owned, a 52,400-tonne bulk carrier built in 2001, according to shipping records.

The military said it was at least the 15th ship it had disabled since the blockade began in the spring, and the sixth since the blockade was put back in place in July. It said 158 other ships had complied with its orders and been redirected. The last ship to be disabled before this one was stopped on November 7.

The blockade was first imposed in April, lifted during the summer while talks were under way, and then reimposed in July. Earlier this month the United States announced a new task force to restrict the movement of weapons and sanctioned goods across Iran's land borders and the Caspian Sea.

Iran answered with defiance. Mohammad Mokhber, an adviser to Iran's supreme leader, said the Strait of Hormuz would stay closed and the blockade would not be allowed to stand. A maritime monitoring firm said at least one sanctioned tanker appeared to have slipped through the blockade in recent days, while a second had stopped short of the line.

Iran's Revolutionary Guard said on Friday that two tankers had tried to cross the strait through what it called an unauthorised route, and claimed one of them had struck a naval mine while the other turned back. The claim could not be independently verified. A senior Guard commander warned that the United States should expect military action from the air and the sea, and the head of Iran's navy said operations to break the blockade were already under way. The Guard also claimed its air defence force had fired warning shots at an American F-16 jet near Hengam Island in the Gulf, forcing it to turn back. The United States had not responded to that claim.

Fighting between the United States and Iran started in late February and has since eased into a quiet standoff, with efforts to reach a diplomatic settlement stalled.

  • The vessel sailed out of Bandar Abbas, Iran, and kept going after repeated warnings; tracking sites listed Singapore as its destination.
