One Worker Killed as Projectile Hits Saudi Arabia's Jazan Oil Refinery

A projectile struck Saudi Aramco's Jazan oil refinery in southwestern Saudi Arabia on Thursday, killing a Nepali worker and injuring several others, the Saudi Energy Ministry said.

The ministry said a hostile aerial projectile hit the refinery in the Jazan region, near the Yemeni border, and started a fire that was brought under control. Some operations at the plant, which can process up to 400,000 barrels of crude oil a day, were temporarily halted. The worker who died was a Nepali national, and the injured were of several nationalities.

The ministry's statement did not name who carried out the attack, but the Saudi-led coalition fighting Yemen's Houthis said earlier on Thursday that the group had struck the refinery. The coalition said it had destroyed a ballistic missile launch platform in Yemen's Hodeidah port that was used in the attack. The strike came a day after the Houthis claimed attacks on Ras Tanura, the kingdom's largest refinery, and the Red Sea ports of Jeddah and Jazan.

Key points

  • The Energy Ministry did not name the attacker; the Saudi-led coalition blamed the Houthis and said it destroyed a missile launch platform in Hodeidah used in the attack.
