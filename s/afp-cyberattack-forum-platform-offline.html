News Agency AFP Takes Content Platform Offline After Cyberattack

One of the world's biggest news agencies has taken its main content platform offline after a cyberattack in the early hours of Thursday, and says it does not yet know who was behind it.

Agence France-Presse said the attack began at 1:30 in the morning, Paris time, and targeted AFP Forum, the platform subscribers use to browse and download its reporting, photographs and video. Once the intrusion was spotted, its teams moved to contain it and took the platform offline as a precaution. The platform remains unavailable until further notice, and the agency is offering backup ways for subscribers to reach its content while the outage lasts.

Investigators have since found evidence that the attackers got in using the login credentials of clients, and the agency has told affected clients to reset their passwords. Its core news production systems were not hit, and its newsrooms kept working normally, the agency said. It has told the French authorities about the attack and is working with them.

An investigation is under way with the help of outside cybersecurity experts. The agency said it had no information yet on who carried out the attack, and has not said whether any information was taken. No group has claimed responsibility. France's cybersecurity chief said on Thursday that the country was facing cyber threats on a scale not seen before, and urged a joint effort to strengthen defences.

The agency's 1,840 journalists come from 135 nationalities and work across 210 bureaus, publishing in six languages. It supplies reporting in text, photographs, video, audio and graphics to subscribers worldwide. Its material reaches media organisations around the world, so a long outage of its distribution platform could disrupt how subscribers receive its reporting, photographs and video.

It is the latest cyber disruption to hit a European institution. Football's world governing body confirmed this week that hackers had gained unauthorised access to its personal and business information. AFP's technical teams are working to assess what happened and bring the platform back. The agency has not said when the platform will return.

Key points

  • AFP took the platform offline as a precaution; it remains unavailable until further notice, with backup access offered to subscribers.
