Australian MP Helen Haines Leaves Hospital Four Weeks After Attack That Cost Her Both Legs

Independent MP Helen Haines has left hospital in Melbourne four weeks after she was struck by a car outside her electorate office in an incident police allege was a deliberate attack, saying she is full of gratitude as she moves into rehabilitation.

Haines, 65, was discharged from The Alfred hospital on Monday, after four weeks of treatment for critical injuries suffered on 19 October outside her office in Wangaratta, in north-east Victoria. In her first public statement since the incident, she confirmed she had moved to the next stage of her recovery, which will continue in Melbourne for the immediate future. "This is a really positive moment, and I am full of gratitude for the expert and compassionate care I received at the Alfred and am receiving in this next stage," she said, thanking the thousands of people across her electorate, Australia and the world who had sent messages of support.

The MP for Indi lost both of her legs as a result of the incident. She had been retrieving items from the boot of her vehicle outside her office on Murphy Street when she was struck by a Toyota LandCruiser. Police allege the driver deliberately targeted her, and witness accounts described the vehicle turning before ramming her against her own car and reversing to strike her again. She was flown to Melbourne in a critical condition, and her husband Phil later confirmed that doctors had been forced to amputate her second leg after efforts to save it failed.

A 43-year-old man from Tarrawingee, Brendan Nugent, has been charged over the incident, including dangerous driving causing serious injury, intentionally causing serious injury, and the federal offence of causing harm to a Commonwealth public official. He remains in custody, has not entered a plea, and is due back before the Wangaratta Magistrates' Court on 4 February for a committal mention. Police say the investigation is continuing and a motive has not yet been established.

Prime Minister Anthony Albanese said all thoughts and prayers continued to be with Haines for her recovery. Haines, a former nurse and midwife who has represented Indi since 2019 and played a leading role in establishing the national anti-corruption commission, is on leave from parliament until at least the end of the parliamentary year. Well-wishers have raised more than one million dollars for Save the Children at her request, in place of flowers.

Key points

  • Independent MP Helen Haines, 65, has been discharged from The Alfred hospital in Melbourne four weeks after the alleged attack outside her Wangaratta office, and has moved into rehabilitation.
  • She lost both legs in the 19 October incident, in which police allege a driver deliberately rammed her with a four-wheel drive outside her electorate office.
  • Brendan Nugent, 43, is charged with dangerous driving causing serious injury, intentionally causing serious injury and harming a Commonwealth public official; he returns to court on 4 February.
  • Haines thanked thousands of well-wishers and said she was full of gratitude for her hospital care; more than one million dollars has been raised for Save the Children at her request.
