Kim Jong Un Calls for Wider Deployment of Tactical Nuclear Weapons

North Korean leader Kim Jong Un has called for tactical nuclear weapons to be deployed more widely across his country's armed forces, during a tour of nuclear production and research facilities.

Kim inspected nuclear material production facilities and a weapons research institute on Wednesday, according to state media, which reported his remarks on Thursday. The facilities and their locations were not identified.

During the visit, Kim said the country should focus on putting tactical nuclear weapons to use across multiple domains and for different missions. He described the task as important and urgent, pointing to the value of the weapons in modern warfare and to the security situation on the Korean Peninsula.

Kim said production capacity for nuclear material was being systematically expanded to strengthen the country's arsenal, and he called recent progress in weapons production a major success. Photographs released with the report showed him inspecting equipment that analysts said resembled gas centrifuges used to enrich uranium, alongside miniaturised tactical warheads.

He declared that North Korea's standing as a nuclear-weapons state was absolutely inviolable and would not change because of outside pressure or talk of denuclearisation. Any attempt to deny that status, he said, would be regarded as an open declaration of war.

Kim blamed what he called growing nuclear threats from the United States and its allies, arguing that Washington was spreading nuclear sharing and joint nuclear arrangements among its partners in Northeast Asia. He said keeping the weapons was an irreversible choice while hostile forces used nuclear arms to pursue dominance.

The visit comes during a worsening border dispute with South Korea, after a landmine blast in the Demilitarized Zone injured three South Korean soldiers in September. It was the fourth time Kim has toured a nuclear facility in public, following visits in September 2024, January 2025 and June this year.

North Korea wrote its status as a nuclear-weapons state into its constitution in 2023, the same year it unveiled the Hwasan-31, a tactical nuclear warhead it said could be fitted to a range of delivery systems, including short-range missiles. Tactical nuclear weapons are lower-yield warheads designed for battlefield use, in contrast to strategic weapons built for long-range strikes.

Key points

  • Kim Jong Un inspected nuclear material production facilities and a weapons research institute on Wednesday, with state media reporting his remarks on Thursday.
  • He said tactical nuclear weapons should be deployed across multiple domains and for different missions, calling the task important and urgent.
  • He said nuclear material production capacity was being systematically expanded and declared the country's nuclear-weapons status absolutely inviolable.
  • He warned any attempt to deny that status would be treated as an open declaration of war, blaming growing nuclear threats from the United States and its allies.
