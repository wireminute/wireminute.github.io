South Korea Education Minister Resigns Over AI Cheating Scandal That Shook College Entrance Exam

South Korea's education minister has resigned after a cheating scandal involving artificial intelligence overshadowed the country's most important exam, with the president accepting his resignation on the same day.

Education Minister Choi Kyo-jin offered to step down on Sunday, saying he took responsibility for the confusion caused by last-minute measures to stop AI-assisted cheating ahead of the College Scholastic Ability Test, known as the Suneung, which was held on Thursday. President Lee Jae Myung accepted the resignation within the hour, and the process of choosing a successor has begun.

The crisis began the previous Sunday, when the highest scorer on the country's certified tax accountant test admitted he had used a tiny camera and a phone to earn an almost perfect mark, only four days before the college entrance exam. The admission raised fears that similar technology could be smuggled into exam halls across the country.

The education ministry rushed out a series of countermeasures. It switched off public Wi-Fi near test centres, covered the buttons on candidates' clothes with stickers, sent emergency alerts to flush out phones that had not been handed in, and drafted in about 8,400 extra staff. On the eve of the exam it also restricted the stationery candidates could carry, allowing only black pencils.

The measures drew heavy criticism for changing almost daily and for arriving too late, and remarks Choi made three days before the exam added to the anger. In his resignation statement he said the exam had ended without major incidents, but that as the minister in charge he could not escape public criticism that preparations had been inadequate in advance.

Officials said examiners caught 111 candidates with electronic devices during Thursday's test, with nearly half of them found through the emergency alerts. The cases included smart glasses in one instance and a miniature camera with wireless earphones in another. A ministry committee is reviewing the cases and will decide on penalties before scores are released on December 11.

President Lee said worries about AI-driven cheating were spreading beyond the college entrance exam to essay-based admission tests and school exams, and that protecting the fairness of exams mattered above everything else.

Key points

  • Choi Kyo-jin resigned on Sunday and President Lee Jae Myung accepted the resignation the same day.
  • The scandal began when the highest scorer on a tax accountant exam admitted cheating with a tiny camera and a phone.
  • The ministry's last-minute measures included switching off public Wi-Fi, covering clothing buttons with stickers, emergency alerts and 8,400 extra staff.
  • Examiners caught 111 candidates with electronic devices during Thursday's exam; penalties will be decided before scores are released on December 11.
