US Officer Accused of Spying for Russia Passed Nothing to Moscow, Officials Say; Charges Expected Within Days

The American army officer arrested in Britain on suspicion of spying for Russia passed no information to Moscow, American officials say — and he is expected to be charged within days, first in a British court and then under the US military justice system.

Lt Col Brandon Carroll Tyner, 41, was arrested near Gloucester on Tuesday after communicating with people he believed were acting for Russia but who were in fact working with the British authorities, in an undercover operation run with American agencies. He joined the army in 2008 as a senior human resources officer, with service in Iraq, Qatar and Afghanistan, and was based at Imjin Barracks, home to the Allied Rapid Reaction Corps, a British-led NATO command.

“No information made it into Russian hands,” an American official said. A second official said investigators believed nothing sensitive had reached Russia. The assurance matters because Tyner worked in a personnel role at NATO's rapid-reaction headquarters, and the head of US Army counterintelligence had described him as a senior soldier entrusted with sensitive information.

He is held on suspicion of offences under sections 1 and 3 of the National Security Act 2023 — obtaining or disclosing protected information, and assisting a foreign intelligence service. The first can carry a life sentence; the second up to 14 years in prison. He has not been charged. British prosecutors are expected to bring charges on Sunday or Monday, after which the intention is to prosecute him under the US military justice system. Police have a warrant to hold him until Tuesday, 17 November.

The investigation is led by Counter Terrorism Policing London, working with the FBI and US military counterintelligence. The FBI director said the arrest involved allegations of espionage of the utmost concern to the United States and its allies. Officers have searched two locations in Gloucestershire. Police said the case is not linked to any other ongoing counter-terrorism investigation.

The Kremlin has denied any connection to the officer. Authorities have not publicly established a motive or said what information investigators believe he tried to obtain. Tyner, who has a Bronze Star, remains in custody at a London police station.

Key points

  • American officials say no information reached Moscow; Tyner was caught in an undercover operation, speaking to people working with the British authorities.
  • He is expected to be charged in Britain on Sunday or Monday, then prosecuted under the US military justice system.
  • The 41-year-old lieutenant colonel is held on suspicion of offences under the National Security Act 2023, which can carry a life sentence.
  • He can be held until Tuesday, 17 November; the Kremlin denies any connection to him.
