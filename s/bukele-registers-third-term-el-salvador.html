Bukele Registers for Third Term in El Salvador After Term Limits Scrapped

El Salvador's President Nayib Bukele has formally registered to seek an unprecedented third term in office, after constitutional changes scrapped presidential term limits and cleared his path to the February 2027 election.

El Salvador's President Nayib Bukele registered his candidacy on Thursday with the country's electoral court, formally entering the race for an unprecedented third term in the election set for February 28, 2027. He will run alongside Vice President Felix Ulloa, his running mate since he first took office, after securing his party's nomination in July.

The bid follows a series of changes that removed every legal barrier to his re-election. In 2021 the country's top court cleared the way for a sitting president to stand again, opening the path to his second-term win in 2024. A year later, allies in Congress passed a constitutional amendment allowing presidents to run for re-election without limit, extended presidential terms from five years to six, abolished second-round run-offs and moved the vote forward to February 2027 so it falls on the same day as legislative and municipal elections.

Bukele, 45, is the clear favourite to win. Polls put his support at between 55 and 70 percent, while opposition parties poll in single digits. He will face four challengers, including Maytee Iraheta of the right-wing ARENA party and Rafael Aguirre of the left-wing FMLN, the two parties that shared power for three decades before he won in 2019. His Nuevas Ideas party holds 54 of the 60 seats in Congress.

Critics and rights groups say the changes leave his authority without effective checks, pointing to a state of emergency in force since 2022 under which tens of thousands have been detained and due process, they say, has suffered. The government rejects that charge, saying it puts the victims of gang violence first. His popularity rests on a crackdown that has driven homicides sharply down. If he wins in February, he will remain in office until 2033, fourteen years after he first took power.

Key points

  • Bukele registered on Thursday for a third term in the February 28, 2027 election, running alongside Vice President Felix Ulloa.
  • Term limits were scrapped in a 2025 constitutional amendment; presidential terms were extended from five years to six.
  • He is the clear favourite, polling 55 to 70 percent, with four opposition candidates in the race.
  • Critics warn his power is unchecked after the 2022 state of emergency; his government rejects that and points to the fall in killings.
