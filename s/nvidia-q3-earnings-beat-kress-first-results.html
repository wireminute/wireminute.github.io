Nvidia's New Boss Says Ambition Has Never Been Greater as Revenue More Than Doubles

Nvidia more than doubled its revenue and profit in its first results since founder Jensen Huang resigned, beating Wall Street forecasts on every measure and lifting its outlook for the rest of the year.

Revenue for the three months to 25 October reached $115.8 billion, up 103 percent on a year earlier and well ahead of the roughly $109.1 billion analysts had expected. The figure also beat the $108 billion the company itself had forecast in August. Adjusted earnings came in at $2.68 a share, against forecasts of about $2.47, while net income more than doubled to $65.3 billion from $31.9 billion a year earlier.

Sales in the data centre business, by far the company's largest, rose 110 percent to $107.6 billion as demand for artificial intelligence infrastructure kept climbing. The company said its newest generation of AI processors, Vera Rubin, began shipping during the quarter. Shares rose about 2 percent in after-hours trading after the results, recovering some ground after a sharp sell-off since Huang's departure.

Interim chief executive Colette Kress, who served as finance chief for 13 years before stepping in, said the company's roadmap was on track and its ambition had never been greater. The quarter itself ended before Huang left, so the figures cover a period when he was still in charge, and the report was the first real test of the company under its new leadership.

Nvidia forecast revenue of $130 billion, plus or minus 3 percent, for the current quarter, above the roughly $125 billion analysts had expected. The forecast covers a quarter with an extra week and assumes no sales of data centre chips to China. Gross margins are expected to narrow to between 71 and 72 percent, from 74.9 percent in the third quarter. The company returned $28.1 billion to shareholders through buybacks and dividends during the quarter.

The leadership question still hangs over the results. The board is searching for a permanent chief executive, and Huang said on Tuesday that he would be honoured to return if the board asked. The board has also set up a new committee to oversee national security and export-control compliance, and Huang is due to testify before lawmakers on 1 December. Until a permanent successor is named, Kress will present the numbers and face investors' questions about the company's direction.

Key points

  • Revenue reached $115.8 billion, up 103 percent on a year earlier and ahead of the roughly $109.1 billion analysts expected; adjusted earnings were $2.68 a share against forecasts of about $2.47.
  • Data centre sales rose 110 percent to $107.6 billion, and net income more than doubled to $65.3 billion.
  • Nvidia forecast $130 billion in revenue for the current quarter, above the roughly $125 billion expected, assuming no data centre chip sales to China.
  • The results are the first under interim chief executive Colette Kress since Jensen Huang resigned; the board is still searching for a permanent successor.
