Evacuations Lifted After Bomb Found at Peoria Ethanol Plant; Hunt for Suspect Goes On

A bomb found at an ethanol plant in Peoria, Illinois, forced more than 5,000 people from their homes and workplaces for a day. Police lifted the evacuation on Saturday afternoon after a full search found no other devices, but no one has been arrested and the person who planted it is still being sought.

Police were called to the BioUrja Renewables plant just after 1 p.m. local time on Friday after the company reported a bomb threat. Officers found a suspicious package about an hour later, and specialists made the device safe at about 9:12 p.m. The plant stands on the Illinois River in an industrial area just southwest of downtown Peoria, a city of about 110,000 people roughly 130 miles southwest of Chicago, on a site police said covers more than 44 football fields.

Because the plant stores ethanol and other highly flammable material, officials set a one-mile blast radius and ordered more than 5,000 residents and workers out of the area, including parts of neighbouring East Peoria. Officers went door to door overnight to move people out, and shelters opened at a high school in Peoria and a sports complex across the river. A country music concert at the downtown civic centre, with about 2,000 people inside, was called off, along with a minor league hockey game and the first day of a comic convention.

The evacuation stayed in place through Saturday morning while bomb teams and plant staff searched the sprawling complex for other devices. Police said the search was complete by Saturday afternoon, with nothing else found, and the order was lifted at about 1 p.m. local time, roughly 24 hours after it began. The Coast Guard also lifted a safety zone on the river at the same time, allowing vessels to move again.

No injuries were reported and no arrests have been made. The FBI and federal explosives investigators are working with local and state police to find whoever planted the device, and officials have not named a suspect or a motive. The state governor directed state police to help coordinate the response and thanked officers who worked through the night to keep the city safe.

Key points

  • A real bomb was found at the BioUrja Renewables ethanol plant in Peoria on Friday; specialists made it safe that evening.
  • More than 5,000 people were evacuated from a one-mile zone around the plant, which stores highly flammable material.
  • Police lifted the evacuation on Saturday afternoon after a full search found no other devices; no injuries were reported.
  • The FBI and federal explosives investigators are hunting for whoever planted the device; no arrests, suspect or motive have been announced.
