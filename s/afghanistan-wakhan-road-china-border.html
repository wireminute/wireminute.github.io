Afghanistan's Road to China Is Finished — but the Border Stays Shut

Afghanistan has finished a road to its border with China, but the gate at the end of it is still shut — and Beijing has given no sign it plans to open it.

The 121-kilometre gravel road was opened on 4 October, running through the remote Wakhan Corridor in Badakhshan province, from Sarhad-e Broghil to the frontier near Wakhjir Pass, nearly 4,900 metres above sea level. It is the country's first direct road link to China, built with Afghan money and Afghan engineers at a reported cost of 365 million afghanis, about $5.5 million. Work on the route began years ago, stalled after the Taliban takeover in 2021, and resumed in 2023.

But the road ends at a closed border. There is no customs post, no transit agreement and no regular movement of goods across the frontier. China sent no representative to the opening ceremony and has not publicly acknowledged the road. Satellite pictures published days after the ceremony showed the new track stopping a few dozen metres short of the Chinese side.

Beijing's caution is about security rather than engineering. China has long worried that militant groups could use Afghan territory to reach Xinjiang, and a United Nations monitoring report earlier this year said members of the East Turkestan Islamic Movement had been gathering in Badakhshan. A Chinese scholar who studies Afghanistan said Beijing was highly unlikely to open the pass in the near future.

For Kabul, the road was meant to open a direct trade route to the Chinese market for pine nuts, dried apricots and gemstones. For now, traders still rely on the long way round through Pakistan. The corridor itself was drawn in the 1890s as a buffer between the British and Russian empires — and more than a century later, it still works as one.
