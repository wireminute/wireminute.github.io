Hackers Stole FIFA Data in Cyber Attack, Governing Body Confirms

FIFA has confirmed hackers broke into its systems in a criminal cyberattack and stole internal personal and business information, the world football governing body said on Thursday.

FIFA said it was cooperating with the authorities, including law enforcement, while it investigates the incident, and condemned the theft and online publication of personal data. The Zurich-based body said the attackers reached a limited set of personal and business files, but it has not said how they got in, when the intrusion was spotted, or whose information was involved.

A group calling itself the Handsome Boy Hacking Club has claimed responsibility, saying it took more than 12 gigabytes of internal files and put them on a cybercrime forum after a failed attempt to extort FIFA. FIFA has not confirmed the group's claim, the size of the theft, or what the files contain. The claim first appeared last week, with the group saying the material included identity and immigration papers, financial and legal records, and files on FIFA's leadership and internal decision-making.

The breach was not caused by a failure of FIFA's systems or technology, according to reports, with the attackers said to have got in by posing as staff and exploiting them. The stolen material did not include documents about the FIFA Forward Enterprise proposal, the controversial plan to sell World Cup rights to investors that was later abandoned, and the incident is not linked to that plan. England's Football Association chair Debbie Hewitt was shown the files after signing a non-disclosure agreement.

FIFA called the theft and publication of personal data a serious and unacceptable breach of privacy that could have far-reaching consequences for those affected. The governing body has faced false hacking claims before, and it has not blamed any group for this attack. The investigation continues, and FIFA has not said when it will share further details.

Key points

  • FIFA confirmed a criminal cyberattack gave hackers access to a limited set of personal and business information, and it is working with law enforcement on the investigation.
  • A group calling itself the Handsome Boy Hacking Club claims it stole more than 12 gigabytes of files, but FIFA has not confirmed the claim, the amount, or the contents.
  • The attackers are reported to have got in by posing as staff rather than through a failure of FIFA's systems, and the stolen files did not include material on the FIFA Forward Enterprise plan.
  • FIFA called the theft and publication of personal data a serious breach of privacy; it has not said whose information was affected or when more details will follow.
