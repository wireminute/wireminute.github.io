US Puts Megaspeed on Trade Blacklist Over Alleged Nvidia Chip Diversion to China

The United States has put Singapore cloud firm Megaspeed on its trade blacklist, cutting the company off from American AI chips, in the sharpest move yet in the investigation into alleged smuggling of Nvidia semiconductors to China.

The Commerce Department's export enforcement arm published a final rule on Monday adding Megaspeed and six affiliated entities to the Entity List, the roster of foreign parties barred from receiving US goods without a special licence. The listing takes effect on 25 November. The rule states the entities were found to work against US national security or foreign policy interests, and licences to supply listed firms are routinely denied.

The seven entities span three countries. In Singapore they are Swiftdata, the parent company of Megaspeed, along with Megaspeed International and Apex Enterprise Solutions. In Malaysia they are MSFusion, PT Megaspeed International and Speedmatrix, and in Thailand, Megaspeed Co. Apex Enterprise Solutions was reported to have been used by Alibaba, the Chinese e-commerce group, to buy computing power in Singapore.

The decision came on the same day Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive, weeks after he resigned amid the scandal around his relationship with Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive whom US officials suspect of ties to Chinese intelligence. No charge has been brought against her over the allegation, Beijing has dismissed the espionage claims as baseless, and President Donald Trump has said no one has proven she is a spy. The listing also followed weeks of friction inside the administration, with Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent blaming Commerce for the delay and saying his department and others had backed the move for months.

The criminal investigation continues. Commerce began examining Megaspeed in 2024, and in June 2025 American agents and Singaporean officers visited the company and questioned Alice Huang, after Nvidia had put it on its no-sell list. The Justice Department now leads the criminal probe, and no charges have been announced. Megaspeed says its business was legitimate and lawful. A separate interim rule issued last week already requires licences for advanced AI chips sent to Singapore, Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand and Brazil, and for data centres renting large computing power to firms based in China and other restricted countries.

Key points

  • Commerce added Megaspeed and six affiliates to the Entity List on Monday; the listing takes effect on 25 November and US suppliers will need a licence that is routinely denied.
  • The seven entities span Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand, including parent company Swiftdata and Apex Enterprise Solutions.
  • The move came the same day Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive, after White House pressure; Treasury had pushed for the listing for months.
  • The Justice Department's criminal investigation continues with no charges announced; Megaspeed denies wrongdoing and Beijing has dismissed the espionage claims as baseless.
