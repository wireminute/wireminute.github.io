US Confirms Criminal Probe Into Singapore Firm Megaspeed Over Suspected Chip Diversion

The United States has confirmed that its Justice Department is leading a criminal investigation into Singapore cloud-computing firm Megaspeed over suspected violations of export controls on advanced semiconductors, after the Commerce Department began looking into the company in 2024.

The Commerce Department and the Justice Department said in a joint statement on Monday that Commerce began investigating Megaspeed in 2024 after monitoring identified suspicious activity suggesting sensitive semiconductors might have been diverted, raising possible export control violations. The statement gave the first official account of an investigation that had drawn growing public attention. It said the two departments decided to speak publicly because of the attention and what they called inaccurate information in public reports.

In May 2025, Commerce investigators asked Nvidia for records about its direct and indirect sales to Megaspeed, the statement said. The following month, Nvidia placed Megaspeed on its list of customers it would not sell to. In June 2025, agents from Commerce's export enforcement arm, working with Singaporean law enforcement, carried out an on-site check at Megaspeed and interviewed Alice Huang, who was then the company's chief executive. After that enforcement action, the Justice Department took the lead in the criminal investigation, the statement said, and the two departments pledged to pursue any criminal conduct that may have occurred.

The statement announced no criminal charges against Megaspeed or Huang. Megaspeed said on Sunday that Huang had officially left the company on August 30, 2025, and that the company had conducted legitimate and lawful business. The case has drawn attention in Washington, where Senator Michael Bennet, a member of the Senate Intelligence Committee, wrote to the administration last week demanding an update on the investigation and stronger action against the suspected diversion of advanced American chips to China. Reports have said US officials suspect Huang had ties to Chinese intelligence efforts, an allegation Beijing has rejected. The departments' statement did not mention Nvidia's former chief executive Jensen Huang, who resigned last week.

The departments also pointed to wider enforcement against semiconductor smuggling, saying 17 defendants had been charged across six separate investigations under the current administration in connection with allegations involving billions of dollars' worth of chips. Singaporean authorities have said they investigated possible false declarations involving servers exported through Singapore to Malaysia that may then have been routed onward, and Malaysia's trade ministry said last week it had never cleared Megaspeed or its local affiliate of any wrongdoing.

Key points

  • The Commerce Department began investigating Megaspeed in 2024 over suspected diversion of sensitive semiconductors, and the Justice Department now leads a criminal probe.
  • In June 2025, US agents and Singaporean officers carried out an on-site check at Megaspeed and interviewed Alice Huang, then its chief executive; no charges have been announced.
  • Nvidia placed Megaspeed on its no-sell list in June 2025 after Commerce requested records of its sales to the firm.
  • Reports say US officials suspect Huang had ties to Chinese intelligence efforts, an allegation Beijing rejects; Megaspeed says its business was legitimate and lawful.
