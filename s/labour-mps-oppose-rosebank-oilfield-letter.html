Nearly 100 Labour MPs Urge Burnham to Block Rosebank Oilfield

Ninety-two Labour MPs, including ministerial aides and frontbenchers, have written to Prime Minister Andy Burnham urging him not to approve drilling at the Rosebank oilfield, in the first big internal revolt of his premiership.

The letter, sent on Thursday, was put together by Monmouthshire MP Catherine Fookes, who said concern about the project was widespread across the parliamentary party and that more MPs shared the worry but had not signed. It warns that approving the field, the largest undeveloped oilfield in British waters, would damage the party's standing as a climate leader and would not bring down household energy bills.

The decision rests with Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh, who is expected to rule within weeks, with some expecting a decision by mid-December. She is said to be minded to refuse approval, while Chancellor John Healey is among those who want it approved, along with many Scottish MPs, and unions have pressed for it to go ahead. The government has said nothing publicly or privately about which way it will go.

The rebellion borrows the tactics of the backbench letter that forced the previous Labour leadership to abandon welfare cuts: a private letter, built quietly over weeks, designed to show the scale of opposition before any vote is taken. More than one ministerial aide is said to be considering resigning if the field is approved.

Rosebank lies about 80 miles north-west of Shetland and is estimated to hold between 300 million and 500 million barrels of oil. Its operator says the field, together with the smaller Jackdaw gas field, would bring in 1.4 billion pounds in tax across this parliament and 3.8 billion across the next. The government approved Jackdaw last month, leaving Rosebank as the defining test of its approach to new North Sea drilling.

Key points

  • A letter signed by 92 Labour MPs, among them ministerial aides and at least four frontbenchers, urges the prime minister to block the Rosebank oilfield.
  • The letter warns approval would damage the party's climate credibility and would not cut energy bills; organisers say more MPs share the concern.
  • Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh is expected to decide within weeks, possibly by mid-December; the chancellor backs the project.
  • Rosebank, 80 miles north-west of Shetland, is the largest undeveloped field in British waters, with estimates of 300 million to 500 million barrels of oil.
