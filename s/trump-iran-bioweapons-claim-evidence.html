Trump Says There Are Signs Iran Is Turning to Chemical and Biological Weapons, but Offers No Evidence

Donald Trump has said there are signs Iran is exploring chemical and biological weapons after its nuclear programme was damaged, while offering no evidence and playing down the threat to Americans.

Trump, speaking to Fox News from Shenzhen on Thursday, said there were indications Iran was examining chemical agents and biological weapons because the nuclear route had been closed off by American and Israeli strikes. He gave no further details and named no specific programme, and said Americans did not need to be concerned.

The remarks followed a report earlier this week that American intelligence had picked up indications Iran was developing plans to target senior administration officials in Washington with a dirty bomb or chemical weapon. Iran has rejected the account. Its mission to the United Nations said it considers the use of chemical weapons wholly illegitimate, and its foreign ministry spokesperson called the report baseless.

At a congressional hearing on Wednesday, the FBI's deputy director, Christopher Raia, cautioned that much of the reporting on such plots was largely aspirational, while saying the bureau took the threat seriously. The director of national intelligence, Jay Clayton, told the same hearing that Iranian threats against Americans had increased substantially. The White House said this week it was monitoring all potential threats but that there was no credible threat to the United States at this time, and the secretary of state said he did not see an imminent threat.

The claim also comes as Israel presses Washington for action. The prime minister sent Mossad chief Roman Gofman to Washington last week with intelligence suggesting Iran was advancing a chemical weapons programme, according to reports in two American outlets. American intelligence officials are said to have evidence of stepped-up Iranian efforts to strengthen chemical and biological weapon capacities, though officials disagree on how strong the Israeli material is, and the prevailing view is that Iran is seeking to deter large-scale attacks rather than preparing a first strike.

Trump suggested an American strike was not imminent, saying they would see what happens after January 1. He added that if Iran did use such a weapon, the United States would respond with overwhelming force.

Key points

  • Trump said there were indications Iran was examining chemical and biological weapons after its nuclear programme was damaged, but he gave no evidence.
  • Iran has denied the account, and US officials say there is no credible threat to the country at this time.
  • The FBI's deputy director told Congress that much of the reporting on such plots was largely aspirational.
  • Israel has shared intelligence with Washington suggesting Iran is advancing a chemical weapons programme, but US officials disagree on how strong it is.
