Six Killed as Small Plane Crashes Into Sea Off Mississippi Coast

Six people, including two boys aged eight and ten, have been found dead after the small plane they were travelling in crashed into the sea off the Mississippi coast, the US Coast Guard said.

All six people on board the aircraft were killed, the Coast Guard said early on Wednesday, after divers located their bodies inside the wrecked fuselage. The single-engine Cessna 210 went down about two miles north-east of Horn Island, a remote barrier island off the Mississippi coast, in the waters of the Gulf of Mexico.

The plane had left New Orleans Lakefront Airport shortly after 2pm local time on Tuesday, bound for Fort Myers in Florida, with four adults and the two boys on board. Air traffic controllers lost contact with it at about 3:45pm. Flight tracking showed the aircraft falling sharply from about 8,800 feet while it was south of Pascagoula, and the Federal Aviation Administration said it had been descending at about 10,000 feet a minute when contact was lost.

Search teams from the Coast Guard, the Mississippi Department of Marine Resources and local police worked the area through the night. The search ended after about four hours when divers found all six victims still inside the fuselage. Horn Island is undeveloped and about 12 nautical miles from Biloxi, reachable only by private boat, which complicated the recovery effort. The island sits within the Gulf Islands National Seashore.

The National Transportation Safety Board has taken charge of the inquiry, and the Federal Aviation Administration is assisting it. Officials have not said what caused the plane to go down, and the identities of the six people on board have not been released. Federal records show the aircraft was a 1972 Cessna 210L, registered to EAB Services, based in Cape Coral, Florida.

Key points

  • A Cessna 210 with six people aboard went down in the water about two miles north-east of Horn Island, Mississippi; four were adults and two were boys, aged eight and ten
  • All six people on board were killed; divers found their bodies inside the fuselage after a search lasting about four hours
  • The plane had left New Orleans for Fort Myers, Florida, and controllers lost contact at about 3:45pm on Tuesday after it fell sharply from 8,800 feet
  • The safety board is examining the crash, and officials have not given a cause for it
