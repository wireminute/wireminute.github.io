Iran Calls Dirty Bomb Report 'Baseless' as Trump Warns of Unprecedented Price

Iran has dismissed a US intelligence report alleging it is drawing up plans to detonate a dirty bomb in Washington, calling the claims baseless, while President Donald Trump warned Tehran it would pay an unprecedented price if any attack on the American capital were carried out.

The report, published on Monday and citing seven US officials, said new American intelligence indicated Iran was developing plans to potentially detonate a dirty bomb or use a chemical weapon in Washington, targeting senior members of the Trump administration. The officials described the planning as an effort to give Tehran options rather than a sign of an imminent attack, though two of the sources said the plotting named specific locations and targets. US agencies also picked up Iranian chatter claiming bomb-making materials had been stashed inside the United States, though officials are divided over how credible that claim is.

Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesman, Esmaeil Baghaei, rejected the report on Tuesday in a post on X, calling the allegations baseless and a fabricated media narrative. He accused the United States of manufacturing propaganda to justify further attacks on Iran, and rejected separate claims linking Iranian dams to missile bases. Iran's mission to the United Nations had earlier rejected the chemical weapons allegations as false and fabricated, saying Tehran considers the use of chemical weapons illegitimate under any circumstances.

Trump responded after landing in Manila on Tuesday for the first leg of an Asian tour. Asked whether Washington was a potential target, he said: "If there is, they will pay a price the likes of which no country has ever paid before." He added: "We take it very seriously because they happen to be crazy. This is why they cannot have a nuclear weapon."

The White House said it is monitoring all potential threats but that there are no credible threats to the homeland at this time. The exchange comes as tensions between the two countries remain high, with the United States sending a third carrier strike group to the Middle East and Iran warning it will not wait to be hit. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi arrived in Oman on Tuesday for talks with his Omani counterpart, a sign that diplomatic channels remain open even as the rhetoric hardens.

Key points

  • Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei has denied the report, calling the dirty bomb allegations baseless and a fabricated media narrative.
  • President Donald Trump warned after landing in Manila that Iran would pay a price "the likes of which no country has ever paid before" if Washington were targeted.
  • The original report, citing seven US officials, said US intelligence indicated Iran was drawing up dirty bomb or chemical weapon plans targeting senior Trump administration officials, with no attack seen as imminent.
  • The White House says there are currently no credible threats to the homeland; Iran's UN mission has rejected the chemical weapons claims as false and fabricated.
