US Rejects Iranian Claims of Seized Tankers, Carrier Strike and Hormuz Toll

The United States has denied claims by Iran's navy chief that American forces seized two Iranian oil tankers, that Iranian forces struck the aircraft carrier USS Abraham Lincoln, and that Washington charges ships $2.5 million to pass through the Strait of Hormuz.

US Central Command rejected the claims as false and fabricated in a statement posted on X, saying Iran's navy chief had described a situation that was 'another fabricated lie'. 'Iran no longer has a navy,' the command wrote. It said the carrier strike group was well defended, was never struck, had completed its deployment and had returned safely to the United States last month.

The claims were made by Rear Admiral Shahram Irani, the commander of Iran's navy, in an interview with Iranian state television on Thursday. He said Iranian forces had boarded the two tankers while their American crews slept and recovered them, and that an Iranian missile had struck the Lincoln on the first day of the US-Israel war on Iran. Central Command also denied charging any toll for crossing the strait, saying thousands of ships had passed freely under US protection.

Key points

  • US Central Command has denied three claims by Iran's navy chief, Rear Admiral Shahram Irani, calling them false and fabricated.
  • Irani claimed Iranian forces recovered two oil tankers seized by the US and struck the carrier USS Abraham Lincoln; Central Command says the carrier was never hit and returned home safely.
  • The US also denied charging ships $2.5 million to cross the Strait of Hormuz, saying there is no toll and navigation has been restored.
