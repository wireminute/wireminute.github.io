Two More Tankers Hit Mines in Strait of Hormuz as US Rejects Iran's Claims

Iran's Revolutionary Guards navy said on Saturday that two more oil tankers exploded and caught fire after striking naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz, hours after the United States rejected a series of Iranian claims about seized tankers, a strike on an aircraft carrier and a toll for crossing the waterway.

Iran's Revolutionary Guards navy said on Saturday that two oil tankers exploded and caught fire after striking naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz the latest in a run of attacks on shipping in the waterway since the war between the United States, Israel and Iran began in late February. One tanker struck a mine off Oman's Khasab port and the other hit mines on what Tehran called an unauthorised route east of the strait, the Guards said. The vessels had switched off their tracking systems and were trying to cross on what the Guards called a fake route, the statement said.

There is no independent confirmation of the claim and no vessel was named, and the condition of the crews is not known. The Guards said the ships were violating rules for the strait and warned tanker operators against using unauthorised routes, saying they must coordinate with Iran's armed forces for safe passage. A similar claim on Thursday said two tankers, including a supertanker carrying more than two million barrels of crude, had hit mines near Khasab. Britain's maritime agency separately reported a tanker struck by an unknown projectile near Khasab earlier in the week.

The claim came a day after US Central Command rejected a series of claims by Iran's navy chief, Rear Admiral Shahram Irani, calling them false and fabricated. Irani had said in an interview with Iranian state television on Thursday that Iranian forces boarded two tankers while their American crews slept and recovered them, and that an Iranian missile struck the carrier USS Abraham Lincoln on the first day of the war. Central Command said the carrier was never struck, had completed its deployment and returned safely to the United States last month, and wrote: "Iran no longer has a navy."

Central Command also denied charging ships $2.5 million to cross the strait, saying thousands of ships and billions of barrels of crude had passed freely and safely under US protection. The US military said separately it disabled the Palau-flagged bulk carrier Pacific Archer in the Gulf of Oman on Thursday after the crew tried to run the naval blockade despite repeated warnings, striking the stern and disabling its propulsion with the crew unharmed. Oil prices rose through the week, with Brent crude closing on Friday at $95.44 a barrel, up about 3 percent for the week.

Key points

  • Iran's Revolutionary Guards navy says two oil tankers exploded after hitting mines in the Strait of Hormuz on Saturday, one off Khasab and one east of the strait.
  • The Guards say the vessels had switched off tracking systems and were using an unauthorised route; there is no independent confirmation and no vessel was named.
  • US Central Command has denied Iranian claims that its forces seized two tankers, that the carrier USS Abraham Lincoln was struck, and that ships are charged $2.5 million to cross the strait.
  • The US says it disabled the bulk carrier Pacific Archer in the Gulf of Oman on Thursday after it tried to run the blockade; Brent crude closed Friday at $95.44 a barrel, up about 3 percent on the week.
