Boehly-Led Group Agrees to Buy Lukoil's Overseas Assets in $10 Billion Share Swap

A consortium led by US billionaire Todd Boehly has agreed to buy the overseas energy assets of sanctioned Russian oil producer Lukoil, in a deal structured as a share swap that would give the US government a stake in the business.

Boehly's investment firm Eldridge announced on Wednesday that the group, which includes the US International Development Finance Corporation, had agreed to acquire Lukoil International from its Russian parent. The other partners are UAE-based Catena Investment Holding and US investment bank Xtellus Partners. No cash will change hands: the buyers will hand over Lukoil securities worth $10 billion in exchange for the international assets.

The portfolio covers oil and gas fields, three refineries in Europe, an international trading business and a network of about 2,000 filling stations in Europe, North America and Central Asia. Its operations in 11 countries pump about 350,000 barrels of oil equivalent a day. People familiar with the terms put the value of the package at around $16 billion.

Under the deal, the US government would receive an equity stake through the development finance corporation, along with a share of future profits once the private investors have recovered their capital and an agreed return, and a seat on the company's governing board. The Lukoil securities being exchanged were bought from US and British funds and will be parked in escrow in the United Arab Emirates instead of going directly to Lukoil. The agreement gives the consortium a 60-day exclusivity period and still needs approval from the US Treasury Department's sanctions office.

The sale ends a process that has dragged on for more than a year, since the Treasury sanctioned Lukoil in October 2025 in an effort to cut off revenue funding Russia's war in Ukraine. An earlier offer from commodities trader Gunvor was blocked by the Treasury, and a provisional deal with private equity firm Carlyle agreed in January later expired without approval. Boehly said completion might take as long as a year and that the business would be renamed. He is a part-owner of the Los Angeles Dodgers and the Los Angeles Lakers, and Eldridge manages more than $75 billion in assets.
