Landmine Toll Reaches Five-Year High as Myanmar and Syria Bear the Brunt

More people were killed or wounded by landmines and leftover explosives in 2025 than in any year since 2020, a global monitoring report said on Tuesday, with Myanmar and Syria accounting for nearly half of all victims.

The Landmine Monitor 2026 report, published by the International Campaign to Ban Landmines, counted at least 6,929 casualties from landmines and explosive remnants of war last year. That is about 10 per cent more than in 2024 and the highest annual figure in five years. Civilians made up 84 per cent of those killed or hurt, and children accounted for half of the civilian toll.

Myanmar recorded the most casualties of any country for the third year in a row, with 1,891 people killed or wounded, a record for the country. Both the military government and the armed groups fighting it lay mines widely, and the report found mines at some level in nearly two out of three townships across the country. On average, five people a day were killed or wounded by mines in Myanmar.

Syria had the second-highest toll, with 1,602 casualties, up by more than half on 2024. The figure has climbed every year since 2022, when 834 were recorded, as families return to land left littered with mines and unexploded ordnance after Assad's government fell in late 2024. The report's casualty editor said people were going back to places they once knew, only to find the hazards now deadly, and called for clearance work to be scaled up sharply.

Southeast Asia supplied 27 per cent of the world's total, even though only a little over 8 per cent of people live there, driven by Myanmar and by contamination in Cambodia, Laos and Vietnam. Russia, which never signed the treaty, has laid mines on a large scale across Ukraine, and the report recorded Ukrainian use of the weapons too. Kyiv moved during the war to put the treaty aside, a step the report calls unlawful.

The report also noted an investigation suggesting the United States had dropped mines from the air in Iran in the early days of the war there. The campaign behind the report called on more states to sign up to the 1997 treaty banning anti-personnel mines and to honour its bans on making, storing and passing on the weapons.

Key points

  • The 2025 toll was at least 6,929 people killed or wounded by landmines and leftover explosives, the highest in five years and about 10 per cent up on 2024.
  • Myanmar recorded 1,891 casualties, the most of any country for the third year running, with five people killed or wounded every day on average.
  • Syria's toll rose by more than half to 1,602 as families returned to mined land after the fall of the Assad government.
  • Civilians made up 84 per cent of all casualties, and half of those were children.
