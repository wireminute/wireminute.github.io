Bangladesh Lockdown Sparks Bomb Blasts and Arson Across 28 Districts

Crude bombs exploded in Dhaka before dawn and attackers burned the family home of former interim leader Muhammad Yunus on Thursday, as a nationwide lockdown called by the banned Awami League brought a wave of violence across Bangladesh.

The lockdown began with a series of small explosions in the capital in the early hours, police and witnesses said, and at least four people were wounded. In Chattogram, attackers burned down the family home of Muhammad Yunus in the Panchlaish neighbourhood. Vehicles were torched on city roads, and police stepped up security overnight, posting extra officers at major flyovers across Dhaka.

The violence has spread far beyond the capital. Over the past 10 days, officers have logged scores of homemade bomb explosions and vehicles set alight in at least 28 of the country's 64 districts. Border Guard Bangladesh troops have been sent to sensitive districts, including Gopalganj, and the government has asked for a nationwide deployment of the force from Friday. In Dhaka alone, officers detained 565 people in a single day of raids before the lockdown began.

The trouble flared in the south-west earlier in the week, when Raju Mollah, a local leader of the ruling party's farmers' wing, was killed in Bhanga, in Faridpur district, during clashes between police and Awami League supporters on Tuesday evening. Two more leaders of party-affiliated groups were killed in Narail. Demonstrators in Tungipara, the sub-district where Hasina was born, surrounded the local police station, freed detained supporters and set the building and parked vehicles on fire. Attackers also torched the home of lawmaker SM Zilany and a local party office, and burned several motorcycles.

Thursday's lockdown falls on the anniversary of a tribunal verdict that sentenced Hasina to death in absentia on charges of crimes against humanity. Hasina, 79, has lived in exile in India since her government fell in August 2024, and has said she plans to return to Bangladesh in December after requesting a new passport and identity card. The Awami League announced a week of protests and rallies building up to the lockdown, and Home Minister Salahuddin Ahmed has warned that those behind the violence will face stern legal action, using audio and video evidence.
