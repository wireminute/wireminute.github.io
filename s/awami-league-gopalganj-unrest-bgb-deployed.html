Local BNP Leader Killed as Violence Flares Again in Hasina's Home District

A local leader of Bangladesh's ruling party has been killed and a police station and a lawmaker's home set on fire in fresh overnight violence in the home district of former prime minister Sheikh Hasina, as unrest spread to the capital, Dhaka.

Fresh violence erupted overnight in Gopalganj, the home district of former prime minister Sheikh Hasina, after a local leader of the ruling Bangladesh Nationalist Party was killed in clashes in the neighbouring district of Faridpur. Police and media reports said Raju Mollah, a leader of the BNP's farmers' wing in Bhanga, died on Tuesday evening during confrontations between police and supporters of Hasina's now-banned Awami League.

The unrest began in Tungipara, the sub-district where Hasina was born, after police arrested several suspected Awami League supporters. Demonstrators surrounded the local police station, and reports said some of those detained were freed before the building and vehicles parked outside were set on fire. Attackers also torched the home of BNP lawmaker SM Zilany in Gopalganj, forcing his family and others to flee, and burned a local BNP office and several motorcycles.

The violence spread beyond the two districts. In the capital, Dhaka, police said unidentified attackers set at least four parked buses on fire between 8.45pm and 11.15pm and detonated crude bombs at several locations. Ten platoons of Border Guard Bangladesh troops have been posted at key points across Gopalganj to keep order, and Home Minister Salahuddin Ahmed said those behind the violence would face stern legal action, using audio and video evidence.

The latest outbreak comes two days after Awami League supporters staged aggressive demonstrations in Gopalganj, attacking police vehicles and installations, and as the party presses ahead with a planned lockdown on 19 November. Hasina, who has been living in India since her government fell last year, has said she plans to return to Bangladesh in December. The government has warned it will take tough action against further disorder, and security forces remain on alert across the district.

Key points

  • Raju Mollah, a local BNP farmers' wing leader, was killed in clashes between police and Awami League supporters in Bhanga, Faridpur, on Tuesday evening.
  • A police station and vehicles were torched in Tungipara, Gopalganj, and the home of BNP lawmaker SM Zilany was set on fire; a BNP office and motorcycles were also burned.
  • Ten Border Guard platoons are posted across Gopalganj, and in Dhaka at least four buses were torched and crude bombs detonated overnight.
  • Home Minister Salahuddin Ahmed pledged stern action using audio and video evidence; the unrest comes ahead of the Awami League's planned 19 November lockdown and Hasina's stated December return.
