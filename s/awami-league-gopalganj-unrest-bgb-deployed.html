Paramilitary Deployed as Bangladesh Lockdown Brings More Killings and Arson

Hundreds of paramilitary troops have been deployed across Bangladesh as a nationwide lockdown called by the banned Awami League takes hold, after a petrol-bomb attack killed an activist in Gopalganj and a bus helper was burned to death in Pirojpur.

The troops were sent out on Wednesday, a day before the lockdown, the culmination of a week of Awami League protests. The dawn-to-dusk shutdown covers public transport, shops, factories, offices, banks, courts and schools, and tests the banned party's strength since its leader was ousted.

Late on Tuesday, a Bangladesh Nationalist Party activist was killed by unidentified attackers during a protest near Gopalganj, the home district of former prime minister Sheikh Hasina, police said. The same night, a crude bomb exploded outside the Dhaka home of Cultural Affairs Minister Nitai Roy Chowdhury, and a BNP lawmaker's house and shops belonging to BNP supporters were set on fire in Gopalganj. On Wednesday morning, a bus helper was burned to death in Pirojpur after two buses were set alight.

At least 37 branches and offices of Grameen Bank have been targeted in arson attacks or attempted fires since 10 November, the bank said, with 14 more attacked since midnight on Tuesday. The bank was founded by Nobel laureate Muhammad Yunus, who led the interim government after Hasina's fall and whose family home in Chattogram was burned down earlier in the unrest.

Police have recorded dozens of crude bomb blasts and torched vehicles in at least 28 of the country's 64 districts over the past 10 days. Officers detained 565 people in Dhaka in a single day of raids before the lockdown, and Dhaka police said at least 71 members of banned organisations, including the Awami League and its affiliated groups, were arrested in the past 24 hours.

The trouble flared in the south-west earlier in the week, when Raju Mollah, a local leader of the ruling party's farmers' wing, was killed in Bhanga, in Faridpur district, during clashes between police and Awami League supporters on Tuesday evening. Two more leaders of party-affiliated groups were killed in Narail. In Tungipara, where Hasina was born, demonstrators surrounded the police station, freed detained supporters and set the building and parked vehicles on fire.

Thursday's lockdown falls on the anniversary of a tribunal verdict that sentenced Hasina to death in absentia for crimes against humanity. Hasina, 79, has lived in exile in India since her government fell in August 2024, and has said she plans to return in December after requesting a new passport and identity card. Home Minister Salahuddin Ahmed has warned that those behind the violence will face stern legal action.

Key points

  • Hundreds of paramilitary troops were deployed nationwide on Wednesday ahead of Thursday's dawn-to-dusk lockdown called by the banned Awami League.
  • A BNP activist was killed in a petrol-bomb attack in Gopalganj late on Tuesday, and a bus helper was burned to death in Pirojpur on Wednesday morning.
  • At least 37 Grameen Bank branches and offices have been targeted in arson attacks since 10 November, the bank said, with 14 more hit since midnight on Tuesday.
  • The lockdown marks a year since a tribunal sentenced Hasina to death in absentia; she plans to return from exile in India in December.
