Gopalganj Under Guard as Government Warns of Tough Action After Arson and Attacks on Police

Bangladesh has put the district of Gopalganj under the guard of paramilitary border troops and warned of tough action against further disorder, after a night of arson and attacks on police linked to supporters of the banned Awami League.

The unrest broke out on Sunday in Gopalganj, the home district of former prime minister Sheikh Hasina, as hundreds of Awami League supporters took to the streets demanding her return from India. Marchers carrying machetes and spears burned an armoured police vehicle, vandalised a police outpost and damaged plaques at the July Memorial and Hadi Square. Two offices of the ruling Bangladesh Nationalist Party were set on fire in Tungipara, Hasina's birthplace, and the home of a local party leader, Imdadul Haque Molla, was torched at Patgati Bazar on Sunday evening.

Ten platoons of Border Guard Bangladesh troops are now posted across key areas of the district, backed by 1,350 police officers. Six platoons were deployed first, three on 10 November and three more on Sunday as the violence spread, and four more were added on Monday morning, the border force said. Armoured personnel carriers have also been sent in, troops are wearing body cameras and drones are monitoring the streets. Between 1,500 and 2,000 people gathered around the district commissioner's office complex during the rallies, which organisers said were aimed at making the party's planned lockdown on 19 November a success.

The government has also replaced the district's top civilian and police officials. GM Sarfaraz, previously posted in Habiganj, takes over as deputy commissioner, and Pratyush Kumar Majumdar, previously in Feni, becomes superintendent of police. The shake-up came as officials moved to reassert control over a district where the banned party still draws large crowds and where its supporters have repeatedly clashed with police since the government fell last year. The government has held two high-level meetings in the past two days to review security in the district and, according to reports, has decided to stay patient while keeping the situation under close watch.

A police sub-inspector who filed an anti-terrorism case over the torching of a police vehicle on 10 November was hacked and seriously injured by unidentified attackers on Sunday evening. The case names 2,224 people. The government has warned that it will take tough action if the party creates further disorder, and security forces remain on alert across the district ahead of the planned lockdown. Hasina, who has been living in India, has said she plans to return in December.

Key points

  • Ten Border Guard platoons are now posted in Gopalganj, backed by 1,350 police officers, after Sunday's marches by Awami League supporters turned violent; four more platoons were added on Monday morning, with armoured carriers, body cameras and drones also in use.
