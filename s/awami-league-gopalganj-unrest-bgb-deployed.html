Border Guards Deployed in Gopalganj as Hasina Supporters' Armed Rallies Turn Violent

Bangladesh has deployed paramilitary border guards across Gopalganj after supporters of the banned Awami League marched through the district with machetes and spears, torched a police vehicle and vandalised a war memorial.

The unrest broke out on Sunday in Gopalganj, the home district of former prime minister Sheikh Hasina, as hundreds of Awami League supporters took to the streets demanding her return from India. Protesters burned an armoured police vehicle, vandalised a police outpost and damaged plaques at the July Memorial and Hadi Square. Two offices of the ruling Bangladesh Nationalist Party were also set on fire in Tungipara, Hasina's birthplace.

The government responded on Monday by transferring the district's top civilian and police officials and deploying Border Guard Bangladesh troops, with ten platoons now posted across key areas. A police sub-inspector who filed an anti-terrorism case over the 10 November torching of a police vehicle was hacked and seriously injured by unidentified attackers on Sunday evening. The case names 2,224 people.

Key points

  • Awami League supporters marched through Gopalganj with machetes and spears on Sunday, torching a police vehicle and vandalising the July Memorial.
  • Bangladesh deployed ten platoons of Border Guard troops and transferred the district's top civilian and police officials.
  • The unrest follows Sheikh Hasina's plan to return from India in December; a police officer who filed a case over the violence was attacked on Sunday evening.
