Firms Tied to Alice Huang Financed $270 Million in AI Servers in China, Records Show

Companies linked to Alice Huang, the businesswoman at the centre of the scandal that briefly cost Nvidia chief Jensen Huang his job, raised financing against hundreds of powerful AI servers in China, financial records show.

The records, drawn from a Chinese central-bank credit registry, list as many as 764 AI servers and modules worth $270 million, together with $31 million of equipment used to link them into large computing networks. Much of the hardware was listed as being at a Shanghai data centre first developed under a partnership with Chinese technology group Tencent.

The central business named in the filings is Century Litong, an Nvidia preferred cloud partner which leases powerful servers to clients. The filings show five leasing deals for as many as 512 servers, declared at 1.42 billion yuan, about $200 million, between May and December 2024. Four of the deals listed equipment at the Yangtze River Delta AI supercomputing centre in Shanghai, a 6.3-billion-dollar complex built by the gaming firm Century Huatong, in which Tencent holds a stake of about 10 percent.

Corporate records connect Century Litong and related companies to Huang's sister, Huang Yu, and to Kang Wei, an investment executive who is Huang's husband. Nothing in the filings proves the servers held chips that Washington bars from export to China, and they give no account of how or when the equipment arrived there. Nor do they show that Tencent bought, rented or operated the specific servers named in the filings.

The findings land as US authorities investigate whether Megaspeed, the Singapore-based cloud company Alice Huang formerly controlled, helped Chinese buyers get hold of Nvidia technology under US export restrictions. Megaspeed has denied wrongdoing and says Huang left the company in August 2025. Washington added Megaspeed and a Singapore subsidiary of Alibaba to its trade blacklist on Monday. Jensen Huang, who resigned on November 12 after revelations about his relationship with Alice Huang, was reinstated as Nvidia chief executive last week. He faces a Senate Banking Committee hearing on export controls on December 3, where he is scheduled to give evidence. China has denied that Alice Huang is a spy.

Key points

  • Financial records list as many as 764 AI servers and modules worth $270 million linked to firms connected to Alice Huang, plus $31 million in networking equipment.
  • The central firm named is Century Litong, an Nvidia preferred cloud partner; five leasing deals covered as many as 512 servers worth about $200 million between May and December 2024.
  • Much of the hardware was listed at a Shanghai AI supercomputing centre built by Century Huatong, in which Tencent holds a stake of about 10 percent; the firms are linked to Huang's sister and her husband.
  • The filings do not prove the servers held chips barred from export to China; Megaspeed denies wrongdoing, and China denies Huang is a spy.
