Depardieu to Face Rape Trial After Paris Court Rejects His Appeal

A Paris appeal court has ordered French film star Gérard Depardieu to stand trial over the alleged rape of actress Charlotte Arnould in 2018, turning down his attempt to have the case dismissed.

Judges in Paris have ordered French film star Gérard Depardieu to stand trial on a charge of raping actress Charlotte Arnould in 2018, turning down his attempt to have the case dismissed. The appeal court ruled that he will be tried over an alleged attack on 7 August 2018, while dismissing a second alleged attack said to have happened six days later.

Arnould, who is now 30, first made the accusation in 2018, saying she was assaulted twice at Depardieu's Paris home when she was in her early twenties. Prosecutors initially dropped the complaint after a nine-month inquiry, but she pursued it again as a civil party. An investigating judge took up the case in 2020, and Depardieu was formally charged the following year.

Depardieu, 77, denies the accusation. His lawyers challenged the decision to send the case to trial, and the appeal court dismissed that challenge on Thursday. His lawyer, David-Olivier Kaminski, said the defence had not yet decided whether to take the matter to the Cour de Cassation, France's highest court, which examines only whether the law was correctly applied.

The trial is the gravest legal threat the actor has faced. Earlier this month he dropped his appeal against a separate conviction for sexual assault handed down in 2025, accepting his guilt and apologising to the two women involved. That case, over incidents on the set of the film The Green Shutters in 2021, brought an 18-month suspended prison sentence. A conviction on the rape charge could mean 15 to 20 years in jail.

Arnould's lawyer, Carine Durrieu Diebolt, called the ruling a relief for her client. The case will now go before a criminal court, which in France hears the most serious cases.
