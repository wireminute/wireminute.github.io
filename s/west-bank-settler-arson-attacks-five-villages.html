Settlers Torch Cars and Spray Racist Graffiti in Overnight Attacks on West Bank Villages

Israeli settlers torched cars and vandalised homes in several Palestinian villages across the occupied West Bank overnight, residents said, in attacks that a leading rival of Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu condemned as 'Jewish terrorism'.

The attacks happened in the early hours of Sunday in villages near Ramallah, Deir as-Sudan, Deir Abu Masha'al and Kafr Malik, and in At-Tuwani in the South Hebron Hills. At least 12 vehicles were set on fire, homes were vandalised and Hebrew graffiti, including the word 'revenge', was sprayed on walls. No injuries were reported from the overnight attacks. Reports put the number of villages hit at five, with an agricultural building also set on fire in Deir Ballut.

In Kafr Malik, residents said settlers torched around eight vehicles and sprayed racist slogans on homes. One resident said seven vehicles belonging to him and his brother were burned, and that the attackers had come from an outpost about four kilometres away. Graffiti reading 'Greetings from Susya' appeared in the village, apparently referring to the recent evacuation of an unauthorised settlement outpost in the Susya area.

Israeli police said they had opened an investigation into arson, graffiti and property damage, and would enter the villages with a military escort to collect evidence and take testimony. The military said soldiers and police were sent to the areas after reports and footage showed vehicles on fire, and condemned the violence.

Gadi Eisenkot, chairman of the Yashar party and the main rival of Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, said the night of arson attacks 'constitutes Jewish terrorism in every sense of the word'. The attacks came nearly a month after an inconclusive election stripped Netanyahu of his majority, and followed a weekend in which a Palestinian man was shot dead on Friday and at least 15 people were wounded in settler attacks and military operations, according to the Palestinian Red Crescent and Palestinian media.

The attacks are the latest in an escalation of settler violence that began after Hamas launched attacks on Israel from Gaza in 2023, and come amid a surge in attacks around the annual olive harvest, when Palestinian farmers have repeatedly been harassed and attacked.

Key points

  • Settlers attacked villages near Ramallah and in the South Hebron Hills overnight, torching at least 12 vehicles and spraying Hebrew graffiti on homes; no injuries were reported.
  • Israeli police opened an investigation into arson, graffiti and property damage, and the military condemned the violence.
  • Gadi Eisenkot, the main rival of Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, called the attacks 'Jewish terrorism'.
  • The attacks followed a weekend in which a Palestinian man was shot dead and at least 15 people were wounded in settler attacks and military operations.
