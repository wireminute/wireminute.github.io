Coalition Orders Civilians Out of Hodeidah Port as Houthis Threaten Saudi Ports

The Saudi-led coalition has told civilians to leave Yemen's main Red Sea port of Hodeidah ahead of expected strikes, and the Houthis have answered by threatening to hit Saudi ports along the same coast.

The Saudi-led coalition has ordered civilians to leave the port of Hodeidah in Yemen, publishing a map that marks out the area to be cleared. The coalition's Joint Forces Command said the evacuation was for the safety of civilians, in a statement carried by its spokesman, Maj. Gen. Turki al-Maliki. Hodeidah is the main Red Sea port for Houthi-held Yemen and the principal entry point for food, fuel and humanitarian aid.

The Houthis answered within hours. Their military spokesman, Yahya Saree, warned that Jeddah and all Saudi harbours on the Red Sea coast would be struck if the coalition attacked Hodeidah. He told port workers and people nearby to leave at once and urged shipping companies to move their vessels away from Saudi ports. Houthi-run media also reported Saudi air strikes across Saada province and the capital, Sanaa.

The warning follows a sharp escalation in the air war. On Wednesday the coalition said its aircraft had hit 60 Houthi military sites in Saada, Amran, Sanaa and Hodeidah, including tunnels dug into mountains to store ballistic missiles and workshops where drones are built and stored. It said the raids were aimed at weakening the group's ability to fire missiles and fly drones and at blunting the threat to shipping. The coalition said it had also intercepted a cruise missile fired towards the Riyadh region, and later a second cruise missile and two drones. Its account could not be independently verified.

A day before that operation, the Houthi-run health ministry said at least 14 people had been killed and 10 wounded in a strike on a sponge factory in Hodeidah, with some hurt while rescue workers were at the scene. The coalition has not commented on that incident. Houthi-run media said 17 people had been killed and 39 wounded in Saudi air strikes across Houthi-held areas in the past day, a claim that could not be verified.

Fighting between Yemen's government and the Houthis resumed in July after a long period of relative calm, and the Houthis have since pushed towards Aden and the Bab el-Mandeb strait, the narrow sea passage at the mouth of the Red Sea. The new strikes come as the wider war between the United States, Israel and Iran, now in its eighth month, has spread fighting across the Gulf and the Red Sea.

Key points

  • The coalition told civilians to evacuate Hodeidah port, publishing a map of the area to be cleared.
  • The Houthis warned they would strike Jeddah and other Saudi Red Sea ports if Hodeidah is hit.
  • The warning follows a coalition operation that it said hit 60 Houthi military sites in four provinces.
  • Hodeidah is the main entry point for food, fuel and aid into Houthi-held parts of Yemen.
