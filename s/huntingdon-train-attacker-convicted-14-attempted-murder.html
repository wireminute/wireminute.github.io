Huntingdon Train Attacker Guilty of 14 Counts of Attempted Murder

A man who stabbed ten people on a London-bound train at the end of a 24-hour knife rampage has been found guilty of 14 counts of attempted murder, after a four-week trial at Cambridge Crown Court.

Anthony Williams, 33, from Peterborough, was convicted on Thursday 26 November. The jury at Cambridge Crown Court deliberated for 16 hours and 52 minutes, returning some verdicts by majority. Williams had admitted carrying out the attacks but denied that he intended to kill anyone, and the prosecution had to prove that he acted with intent to kill rather than only to cause serious harm.

The train attack came on the evening of 1 November 2025. Williams boarded a London-bound LNER service at Peterborough shortly before 7.30pm and began stabbing passengers soon after it left the station. Nine passengers and a member of the on-board staff, Samir Zitouni, 48, were stabbed. The driver brought the train to an unscheduled stop at Huntingdon, where Williams was tasered and arrested in the station car park.

The court heard the train attack was the end of a day of violence. On the evening of 31 October, Williams stabbed a 14-year-old boy in Peterborough and tried to stab two men. In the early hours of 1 November he stabbed a 17-year-old, Isaiah Idris, in the face on a Docklands Light Railway train approaching Pontoon Dock station in east London. Before the trial he had pleaded guilty to wounding with intent, attempting to wound, affray at a barber's shop, theft of knives, possession of bladed articles, common assault and assaulting a police officer.

Psychiatrists instructed by both the prosecution and the defence told the jury that Williams was suffering from acute psychosis caused by schizophrenia and believed the devil meant to kill him. He did not give evidence and followed the trial by video link from Rampton Hospital, a high-security psychiatric hospital. Sentencing is expected in early 2027.

Key points

  • Anthony Williams, 33, was convicted on 26 November of 14 counts of attempted murder after a four-week trial at Cambridge Crown Court.
  • He stabbed ten people on a London-bound train on 1 November 2025, nine passengers and a member of staff, before the train stopped at Huntingdon and he was arrested.
  • The attacks were the end of a 24-hour rampage that also left a 14-year-old boy stabbed in Peterborough and a 17-year-old stabbed on a London DLR train.
  • Psychiatrists for both sides said Williams was suffering acute psychosis caused by schizophrenia; sentencing is expected in early 2027.
