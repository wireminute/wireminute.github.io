Philippines Files Three Child Abuse Material Charges Against Meghan's Father Thomas Markle

Police in the Philippines have filed three criminal complaints against Thomas Markle, the 82-year-old estranged father of Meghan, Duchess of Sussex, over alleged child sexual abuse material, adding a second country's case to the federal charge he already faces in the United States.

The Philippine National Police lodged the complaints before prosecutors in Cebu on Monday, and the move was made public on Wednesday. Two of the counts cover the alleged distribution of the material, and the third covers possession. The complaints were brought under Philippine statutes that criminalise the online sexual abuse and exploitation of children. Prosecutors in Cebu will now examine the evidence and decide whether the case should go before a court, and whether Markle should be called to answer the allegations.

The investigation grew out of the months Markle spent living in Cebu before returning to the United States earlier this year. Police said the case began after his son, Thomas Markle Jr, reported finding suspected illegal images among belongings left at his father's home there and handed over electronic devices. Investigators say they have counted about 7,500 photographs and one video taken from devices linked to the elder Markle, and forensic checks on the remaining devices are still under way. Police have said more charges could follow if the extra material supports them.

The Philippine case is separate from the one Markle faces in America. Federal agents arrested him at his home in Florida on November 6 and charged him with one count of possessing child sexual abuse material. Agents said they seized around 78 objects, among them film negatives, slides and books, from two rented storage units in Los Angeles. He is in custody while doctors assess whether he is fit to stand trial. Markle has denied the allegations in both countries, calling them untrue.

The regional prosecutor in Cebu, Graeme Elmido, said the authorities could ask for Markle to be sent back to the Philippines if prosecutors decide there is enough evidence to take the case to court. His son, who is 60, has been described as a person of interest in the Philippine investigation, but police have stressed that he is not a suspect and that no charges have been brought against him. Meghan, who is estranged from her father, said last month that she was deeply distressed by the allegations.

Key points

  • Philippine police filed three complaints in Cebu on Monday: two counts covering alleged distribution of such material and one count of possession.
  • Prosecutors will review the evidence before deciding whether to take the case to court; Markle could be asked to return to the Philippines if they proceed.
  • Investigators say about 7,500 photographs and one video were recovered from devices linked to Markle, and forensic checks are continuing.
  • Markle, 82, was arrested in Florida on November 6 on a separate US possession charge and is in custody awaiting a fitness assessment; he denies the allegations.
