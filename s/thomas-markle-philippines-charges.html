Thomas Markle Ruled Mentally Fit to Stand Trial in US as Philippine Case Awaits Prosecutors

A federal judge in Florida has found Thomas Markle mentally fit to stand trial on a child sexual abuse material charge in the United States, clearing the main procedural hurdle in the case against the 82-year-old father of Meghan, Duchess of Sussex, while prosecutors in the Philippines weigh their own charges against him.

Markle appeared in a Tampa courtroom on Thursday for a competency hearing ordered after he was diagnosed with dementia earlier this year. A psychiatrist told the court that, with a reasonable degree of medical certainty, Markle had sufficient ability to understand the nature and consequences of the charges against him. Federal Judge Lindsay Griffin accepted the report as evidence, and it was not contested. Markle's lawyer, Mary Wolf, said she would not challenge the findings at this stage but reserved the right to do so once the case moves to California.

Griffin ordered Markle to remain in custody until the end of his trial, after he violated the conditions of his earlier release. He is due for a preliminary hearing in Florida on December 1; if he is indicted before then, the case will be heard in California, where federal prosecutors first filed the charge.

The Philippine National Police lodged three complaints before prosecutors in Cebu on Monday, and the move was made public on Wednesday. Two of the counts cover the alleged distribution of the material, and the third covers possession. The complaints were brought under Philippine statutes that criminalise the online sexual abuse and exploitation of children. Prosecutors in Cebu will now examine the evidence and decide whether the case should go before a court, and whether Markle should be called to answer the allegations.

The investigation grew out of the months Markle spent living in Cebu before returning to the United States earlier this year. Police said the case began after his son, Thomas Markle Jr, reported finding suspected illegal images among belongings left at his father's home there and handed over electronic devices. Investigators say they have counted about 7,500 photographs and one video taken from devices linked to the elder Markle, and forensic checks on the remaining devices are still under way. The regional prosecutor in Cebu, Graeme Elmido, said the authorities could ask for Markle to be sent back to the Philippines if prosecutors decide there is enough evidence to take the case to court. Markle has denied the allegations in both countries, calling them untrue.

Key points

  • A Florida judge found Thomas Markle mentally fit to stand trial after a psychiatrist said he understood the charges; the finding was not contested.
  • Markle stays in custody until trial; a preliminary hearing is set for December 1 in Florida, with the case expected to move to California if he is indicted.
  • Philippine police filed three complaints in Cebu: two counts covering alleged distribution of such material and one count of possession.
  • Investigators say about 7,500 photographs and one video were recovered from devices linked to Markle; he denies the allegations in both countries.
