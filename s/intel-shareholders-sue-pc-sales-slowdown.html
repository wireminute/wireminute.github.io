Intel Sued by Shareholders Over PC Sales Slump After Shares Plunged 29% in a Day

Intel faces a shareholder lawsuit accusing the chipmaker of hiding the depth of a slump in personal computer sales, weeks after its shares fell by almost 29 percent in a single day and wiped about $140 billion from its market value.

The proposed class action was filed on Friday in a federal court in San Jose, California, by a Michigan pension fund, the City of Southfield Fire and Police Retirement System. It names the company, chief executive Lip-Bu Tan and chief financial officer David Zinsner as defendants, and seeks unspecified damages for investors who bought Intel shares between July 23 and October 29. The claims have not been tested in court, and Intel did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

The fund argues Intel was too slow to tell investors how badly weakening demand for personal computers and a glut of unsold stock were hurting its revenue. It says the company's troubles in its PC chip business outweighed the gains it expected from data centre building and the boom in artificial intelligence. The suit also says Intel had reason to stay quiet: in mid-August, halfway through what the filing calls a disastrous third quarter, the company raised $23 billion by selling about 242 million shares at $95 each.

The scale of the downturn became clear on October 29, when Intel reported third-quarter results. Revenue from its PC chip unit came in at $8.1 billion, down 9 percent, or about $800 million, from the previous quarter. Sales of chips for desktop and laptop computers fell by $424 million from a year earlier to $6.9 billion, with volumes down 14 percent. The company also forecast revenue of $15 billion to $16 billion for the fourth quarter, roughly $1.5 billion below what analysts had expected, and Tan warned the PC downturn could last at least into the first half of 2027.

Investors reacted sharply. Intel shares fell from $92.07 on October 29 to $65.76 the next day, a drop of 28.6 percent that the filing describes as the steepest one-day fall in the stock in more than five decades. The shares closed on Friday at $59.66. They remain up 62 percent this year, but far below their peak of $142.35 at the end of June. Investors who bought during the period covered by the suit have until January 12, 2027 to ask the court to appoint them as lead plaintiff.

Key points

  • A Michigan pension fund filed a proposed class action on Friday in San Jose, accusing Intel of concealing the impact of the PC sales slump; CEO Lip-Bu Tan and CFO David Zinsner are also named.
  • Intel shares fell 28.6 percent on October 30, wiping about $140 billion from its market value, after weaker than expected fourth-quarter guidance.
  • PC chip unit revenue fell to $8.1 billion in the third quarter, down 9 percent, while desktop and laptop chip volumes dropped 14 percent from a year earlier.
  • The suit points to a $23 billion share sale in mid-August at $95 a share as a motive; the claims are allegations and have not been ruled on in court.
