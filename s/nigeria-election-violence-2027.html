Nigeria's Election Campaign Turns Violent as Attacks Spread Ahead of 2027 Vote

Nigeria's campaign for elections in January has been hit by a wave of attacks on candidates, convoys and rallies, with monitors counting more than 30 incidents and at least 10 deaths since campaigning began in August.

The violence has spread across the country in the three months since campaigning opened on August 19 for the presidential and National Assembly elections on January 16, 2027. Conflict researchers tracking the campaign say more than 30 attacks have targeted rallies, convoys, candidates and their supporters, leaving at least 10 people dead. A separate count by a Nigerian monitoring group recorded at least 70 physical attacks between January and late October, with 27 deaths. The National Peace Committee, which oversees a peace accord signed by the parties, has logged 109 breaches of the accord across 26 states and the capital, with political violence and thuggery the most common violation.

The most widely reported attack came on November 12 in Oregun, a district of Lagos, where men carrying axes, cutlasses and clubs stormed a town hall meeting organised by an opposition party. Supporters fled as the men chased them through the venue, and several people were hurt, among them a journalist covering the meeting who suffered a broken finger and injuries to his ribs. Vehicles belonging to supporters were damaged. Five days earlier, in Asaba in Delta State, one person was killed and five were injured when violence broke out during a street procession backing the opposition presidential candidate. On November 15, the convoy of the opposition vice-presidential candidate was attacked during a visit to Bichi in Kano State.

President Bola Tinubu, who is seeking re-election, broke his silence on November 13 with a signed statement condemning the attacks and directing security agencies to confront political thuggery with full force. On Tuesday, the Senate warned that candidates whose supporters take part in electoral violence could be disqualified, and urged the electoral commission to enforce the provisions of the 2026 Electoral Act against offenders. Senators also called on the police to investigate reported attacks on the convoys of the president and two leading opposition candidates and to prosecute those behind them. The lower house has opened its own inquiry into the rising violence.

The attacks have raised fears about the January vote in Africa's most populous country, where elections have a long history of violence. Monitors warn the pattern could worsen as polling day approaches, with the worst-affected region so far the north-west, including Kano, Kaduna, Zamfara and Katsina states. Campaign groups say the intimidation risks discouraging voters from taking part, and have called on all parties to rein in their supporters.

Key points

  • More than 30 attacks on campaigns, candidates and supporters have been recorded since campaigning began on August 19, with at least 10 people killed, according to conflict monitors.
  • Men armed with axes and cutlasses broke up an opposition town hall in Lagos on November 12, and one person was killed at a procession in Asaba on November 7.
  • President Tinubu condemned the attacks on November 13, and the Senate warned on Tuesday that candidates linked to electoral violence could face disqualification.
  • Nigeria votes on January 16, 2027; monitors warn the violence could worsen as polling day nears and erode confidence in the election.
