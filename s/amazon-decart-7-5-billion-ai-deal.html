Amazon Strikes Reported $7.5 Billion Deal for Decart AI Technology in Chip Push

Amazon has signed a deal to take over the core technology and engineering team of Israeli artificial intelligence startup Decart in a transaction reported to be worth about $7.5 billion, as the company moves to strengthen the chips that power its cloud business.

The agreement covers the software behind Decart's Optimization Stack, a system designed to make AI models run faster and more efficiently across different kinds of hardware. Amazon will also hire Decart's three founders and its team of researchers and engineers. The deal was signed overnight and announced on Monday, according to Israeli business newspaper Calcalist, which first reported it. The companies have not publicly confirmed the price, and the reported $7.5 billion figure comes from people familiar with the transaction.

The technology will sit inside Amazon's chip division, Annapurna Labs, and will be used to improve the performance of Trainium and Inferentia, the company's own processors for training and running AI models. The move is aimed at cutting Amazon's reliance on Nvidia, whose chips dominate the market for AI computing. Amazon will not take over Decart's AI models, which stay outside the deal.

Decart was founded in 2023 by Dean Leitersdorf, his brother Orian Leitersdorf and Moshe Shalev, and builds tools for real-time AI generation and for making AI training more efficient. The three founders, who hold about 64 per cent of the company between them, have agreed to stay at Amazon for five years. The price will be paid in a mix of cash and shares, in instalments tied to performance targets over five years, and the deal still needs regulatory clearance.

The purchase would rank among Amazon's largest AI investments and underlines how fiercely the biggest cloud providers are competing for the technology that makes AI cheaper to run. Reports say a formal announcement could come at Amazon's AWS re:Invent gathering on November 30.

Key points

  • Amazon has signed a deal to acquire Decart's Optimization Stack technology and hire its founders and engineering team, in a transaction reported at about $7.5 billion.
  • The companies have not disclosed the financial terms, and the deal is subject to regulatory clearance.
  • The technology will be folded into Amazon's Annapurna Labs chip unit to improve its Trainium and Inferentia processors.
  • Decart's three founders have agreed to stay at Amazon for five years, with part of the price tied to performance targets.
