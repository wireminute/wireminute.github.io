Putin Says Retaliation Is Inevitable as Ukraine Keeps Hitting Russian Oil Refineries

Russian President Vladimir Putin has said retaliation is inevitable after Ukraine kept striking Russian oil refineries, despite a direct request from US President Donald Trump to stop.

Putin spoke on Tuesday at a Kremlin meeting with government officials and senior oil executives, saying Kyiv was ignoring Trump's appeal. "We see that the Kyiv regime continues to attack our refining facilities, despite the US president's appeal and direct request to refrain from such attacks," he said. "But the Kyiv authorities are ignoring this advice."

"In this regard, I would like to stress that retaliatory measures on our part are inevitable," Putin said. He also told the meeting he wanted additional security measures for energy facilities, and that systematic work to reinforce protection at fuel and energy sites must continue.

Kyiv has said it will stop striking Russian energy targets only when Moscow does the same. Ukrainian strikes eased after Trump declared an energy ceasefire in mid-October, but they picked up again late that month. In September, Trump said he had asked Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to scale back the strikes on refineries, and that Zelenskyy had agreed.

The campaign has bitten deep into Russia's refining capacity. Industry sources estimate that Ukrainian drone attacks have cut Russian refineries' combined crude processing volumes by about 45 percent since late October.

Ukraine answered Putin's complaint by pointing to the war itself. Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha said Putin could end the strikes on refineries by halting the invasion. "If Putin doesn't like Ukraine's strikes on his oil refineries, he can order an end to the war," he wrote. "Stop complaining. End the war."

The exchange leaves the energy war in a familiar pattern: Ukraine hitting refineries, Russia vowing a response, and Washington's appeal for a halt going unheeded by Kyiv while Moscow keeps striking Ukrainian energy infrastructure. Putin gave no date or detail for the retaliation he promised.

Key points

  • Putin said retaliation is inevitable after Ukraine kept hitting oil refineries despite Trump's direct request to stop
  • He spoke at a Kremlin meeting with officials and oil executives and ordered stronger protection for energy sites
  • Ukrainian drone strikes have cut Russian refineries' crude processing by about 45 percent since late October, industry sources estimate
  • Kyiv says it will stop striking energy targets only when Moscow does the same; Ukraine's foreign minister said Putin could end the strikes by ending the war
