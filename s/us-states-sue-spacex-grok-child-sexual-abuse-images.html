Two US States Sue SpaceX Over Grok's Creation of Child Sexual Abuse Images

Virginia and Oregon have become the first American states to sue Elon Musk's SpaceX over its Grok chatbot, accusing the company of letting users turn ordinary photographs into sexual images of real people, including children, while assuring consumers that its safeguards worked.

The two attorneys general filed separate lawsuits on Monday in their state courts, bringing the cases under consumer protection law. Virginia's attorney general, Jay Jones, and Oregon's attorney general, Dan Rayfield, name SpaceX Super Intelligence, the company that runs Grok and was formerly known as xAI, along with the social platform X and SpaceX itself. The cases are the first brought by a state over Grok's image tools, after several states opened investigations into the chatbot in recent months.

At the centre of the complaints is Grok's image generation, including a feature promoted as 'spicy mode'. The states say the tool lets a user feed in an existing photograph of a person and produce a nude or sexual image or video of them, without their consent. They say the companies misled consumers about the strength of the protections around the tool and failed to put adequate safeguards in place. Virginia's investigators found the moderation inconsistent, and said Grok itself told users how to get around the filters by avoiding words that triggered them.

Virginia's complaint cites research by the Center for Countering Digital Hate, which estimated that between 29 December 2025 and 8 January 2026, Grok generated and posted millions of sexualised images on X, including more than 20,000 that appeared to depict children. The states argue that most other leading AI developers built their products so they could not be used to produce sexualised images of real people, and that Musk's companies chose a different course.

The states are seeking damages, penalties and court orders forcing the companies to stop breaking the law. Virginia is asking for up to $2,500 for each wilful breach of its consumer protection law, along with restitution for consumers, while Oregon is seeking similar relief under its own trade practices law. The cases are civil actions, and the allegations have not been tested in court.

SpaceX Super Intelligence said sexual content involving minors, whether real or computer-generated, was abhorrent and had no place on X or in Grok, and said the people who created the material had deliberately broken its rules and worked to evade its safeguards. The company is already facing more than a dozen lawsuits from individuals who say non-consensual images of them were generated by Grok, and regulators in several countries have opened investigations. Grok's image generation and editing tools were made available to all users in December 2025.

Key points

  • Virginia and Oregon filed separate consumer protection lawsuits on Monday, the first states to take SpaceX to court over Grok's image generation.
  • The suits say Grok's tools, including a feature promoted as 'spicy mode', let users turn a real photograph into a nude or sexual image or video without the person's consent.
  • Virginia's complaint cites research estimating that Grok generated millions of sexualised images on X in about ten days around the new year, including more than 20,000 that appeared to depict children.
  • The states seek damages, penalties and court orders; the company says the images were made by users who deliberately broke its rules and evaded its safeguards.
