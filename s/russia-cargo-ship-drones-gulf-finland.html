Russian Cargo Ship Bound for Kaliningrad Hit by Drones and Left Burning in Gulf of Finland

A Russian cargo vessel bound for Kaliningrad was struck by two drones in the Gulf of Finland on Saturday, catching fire before drifting into Finnish waters, Russian officials and Finnish authorities said.

The Leningrad region governor, Alexander Drozdenko, said the Sparta II was attacked by two drones in neutral waters about three nautical miles from Russia's border, and that the crew was being evacuated. The vessel had left St Petersburg and was sailing to Kaliningrad, Russia's Baltic exclave.

Finland said the damaged vessel was on fire and had drifted from international waters into its territorial waters, with the Finnish Border Guard coordinating the rescue and preparing for possible pollution control. Estonia said it received a report of a fire aboard the vessel at 9.35am, with early reports of explosions on board. No group has claimed responsibility for the attack, and the origin of the drones is unknown. The Sparta II is under US sanctions and belongs to a shipping company linked to Russia's Defence Ministry.

Key points

  • The Russian cargo vessel Sparta II, sailing from St Petersburg to Kaliningrad, was struck by two drones in the Gulf of Finland and caught fire, according to Russian officials.
  • Finland said the burning vessel drifted into its territorial waters, with the Border Guard coordinating rescue efforts; the crew was being evacuated.
  • No one has claimed responsibility and the origin of the drones is unknown; the ship is under US sanctions and belongs to a company linked to Russia's Defence Ministry.
