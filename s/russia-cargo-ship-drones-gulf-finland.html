Russia Begins Towing Fire-Damaged Sparta II Out of Finnish Waters

A Russian rescue vessel began towing the fire-damaged cargo ship Sparta II out of Finnish waters on Sunday, two days after drones struck it in the Gulf of Finland, as coast guard crews said the vessel was stable enough to move even though smoke was still rising and some containers remained warm.

A Russian rescue vessel began towing the Sparta II out of Finnish waters on Sunday after two days of firefighting, the coast guard said. Thermal cameras showed that some containers were still warm, but the vessel was judged stable enough for towing to begin, and responsibility for the rescue will pass to Russia once the ship leaves Finnish waters. Smoke was still coming from the vessel, and there was clearly still heat on board, a coast guard officer said.

The fire burned through the night into Sunday, and vessels kept spraying the ship with water cannon to douse the flames and cool the containers stacked on its deck. Seven vessels helped fight the fire, including two Russian and two Estonian ships alongside Finnish units, and a Russian rescue ship took part in the operation at the coast guard's request. All 16 crew members have been evacuated onto a nearby Russian ship and are on their way back to Russia; one crew member was slightly injured.

The coast guard warned that crews must watch the ship's stability closely, because water collecting inside the hull as they fight the fire risks causing the vessel to lose stability and sink. What the containers hold is still not known, which complicates the assessment of fire and explosion risks. No environmental damage has been detected, and the incident poses no known danger to surrounding shipping.

The ship, bound from St Petersburg to the Russian exclave of Kaliningrad, was hit by two drones in international waters west of Gogland Island on Saturday, according to Russian officials, and later drifted into Finnish waters, where it anchored off the town of Loviisa. Finland's Defence Forces said the strike was likely carried out by Ukraine, though it was not clear why this particular ship had been targeted. Ukraine has not claimed responsibility. The vessel is under US sanctions and belongs to a company linked to Russia's Defence Ministry. Estonia's navy commander said on Sunday that the risk of further attacks in the Gulf of Finland could not be ruled out. Finland's Defence Ministry also reported a suspected violation of Finnish airspace by a Russian helicopter, which flew more than four kilometres into Finnish territory, and the coast guard is investigating that incident. The story is still developing.

Key points

  • A Russian rescue vessel began towing the Sparta II out of Finnish waters on Sunday; thermal cameras showed some containers still warm, but the ship was judged stable enough to move.
  • Smoke was still rising from the vessel and heat remained on board; one crew member was slightly injured and all 16 have been evacuated.
  • Seven vessels from Finland, Russia and Estonia helped fight the fire; what the containers hold is still unknown.
