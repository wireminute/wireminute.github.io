Fire still burning on Russian cargo ship Sparta II as rescue crews warn it could sink

A fire was still burning on the Russian cargo ship Sparta II on Sunday, a day after drones struck it in the Gulf of Finland, as Finnish rescue crews warned that the water being pumped aboard to fight the flames could make the vessel sink.

The fire burned through the night into Sunday, and Finnish coast guard vessels kept spraying the ship with water cannon overnight to douse the flames and cool the containers stacked on its deck. Finland's Border Guard said firefighting and cooling operations had continued through Saturday night and were still under way on Sunday morning.

All 16 crew members have now been evacuated onto a nearby Russian ship and are on their way back to Russia, the Border Guard said. The Sparta II remains anchored in Finnish waters off Loviisa, where it drifted after the strike, and Russian rescue vessels are expected to tow it out once conditions allow.

The Border Guard warned on Sunday that crews must watch the ship's stability closely, because water collecting inside the hull as they fight the fire risks causing the vessel to lose stability and sink. The patrol vessel Uisko is coordinating the effort to contain the blaze and cool the containers on deck, but what the containers hold is still not known, which complicates the assessment of fire and explosion risks. The Border Guard said no environmental damage had been detected and the incident posed no known danger to surrounding shipping.

Finland's Defence Forces said on Saturday that the strike was likely carried out by Ukraine, though it was not clear why this particular ship had been targeted. Ukraine has not claimed responsibility. The Sparta II, bound from St Petersburg to Kaliningrad, was hit by two drones in international waters west of Gogland Island, according to Russian officials, and the vessel is under US sanctions and belongs to a company linked to Russia's Defence Ministry. Estonia's navy commander said on Sunday that the risk of further attacks in the Gulf of Finland could not be ruled out. Finland's Defence Ministry also reported a suspected violation of Finnish airspace by a Russian helicopter, which flew more than four kilometres into Finnish territory, and the Border Guard is investigating that incident.

Key points
