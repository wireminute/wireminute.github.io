Trump Wants Ukraine Peace Deal by January as Zelenskyy Calls Summit a Failure for Putin

The Trump administration has told European and Ukrainian officials it wants Kyiv to reach a peace agreement with Russia by early January, before Democrats take control of Congress, as President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said this week's three-way summit had been a failure for Vladimir Putin.

The timetable was reported on Friday, citing four people briefed on the talks. It said US special envoy Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, the president's son-in-law, have been pressing officials in Moscow, Dublin and Berlin to move quickly after restarting negotiations in Abu Dhabi this month. One European official said the Americans were pushing hard, while another said Putin was in no hurry. A senior Ukrainian official said the deadline had raised fears in Kyiv that Zelenskyy could be pressed into a deal largely on Moscow's terms before the new Congress is seated.

Zelenskyy said on Saturday that the trilateral summit between Trump, Putin and Chinese President Xi Jinping in Shenzhen this week had been very unsuccessful for Putin, who he said had failed to recreate the 1945 Yalta conference that decided Europe's fate. He said he was not convinced by Putin's stated desire to end the war. Trump said on his return to Washington on Friday that Putin had clearly expressed a wish to end the fighting, but Putin has shown no sign of dropping his demand that Ukraine give up all of its eastern Donbas region, which Zelenskyy has rejected.

The push comes after Trump told European leaders this week that they were spending a lot of money on the war in Ukraine and could use it at home once a peace deal was reached, according to Zelenskyy, who attended the meeting in Berlin. Zelenskyy welcomed recent announcements that Europe would be included as a full partner in the peace process, which resumed after a nine-month pause. He also repeated that he was ready to attend next month's Group of 20 summit in Miami, hosted by Trump, and would be happy to hold direct talks with Putin there.

Kyiv's concern is that an agreement struck before January would lock in terms more favourable to Moscow, since Democrats are expected to push for stronger support for Ukraine and more pressure on Russia once they take control of Congress. Trump has broad authority to conduct the negotiations, and his envoys have stressed the urgency of reaching an agreement, though the United States has not pressed Ukraine to make specific concessions.

Key points

  • The Trump administration wants a Ukraine peace deal by early January, before Democrats take control of Congress, according to people briefed on the talks.
  • Zelenskyy said the Trump-Putin-Xi summit in Shenzhen was very unsuccessful for Putin, who failed to recreate the 1945 Yalta conference.
  • Trump told European leaders they were spending heavily on Ukraine and could use the money at home once peace is reached, Zelenskyy said.
  • Kyiv fears a deal struck before January would favour Moscow, as Democrats are expected to back stronger support for Ukraine once seated.
