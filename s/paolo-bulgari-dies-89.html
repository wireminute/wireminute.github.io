Paolo Bulgari Dies at 89, the Jeweller Who Turned a Family Business Into a Global Luxury House

Paolo Bulgari, the grandson of a Greek silversmith who turned a small family jewellery business into one of the world's great luxury houses, has died at the age of 89, his family has announced.

His family announced his death on Saturday, describing him as "the greatest jeweller of our time" and praising his humanity and entrepreneurial talent. He died in Rome, the city where he was born in 1937 and where the business his grandfather founded became a byword for Italian glamour. His funeral will be held on Monday at the Basilica of Santa Croce in Rome.

Paolo Bulgari was the grandson of Sotirios Voulgaris, a silversmith from Paramythia, a mountain village in the Epirus region of northwestern Greece. Voulgaris fled Ottoman rule, settled in Naples and then opened a shop in Rome, adapting both his craft and his family name to his new home. Paolo joined the business shortly after leaving school, working alongside his father, Giorgio, and developing an expertise in precious stones that shaped his work as a designer and businessman.

Turning away from the delicate style of the Parisian jewellery houses, the family embraced bold gemstones, rich yellow gold, generous proportions and unexpected combinations of colour, drawing on ancient Rome and Italy's artistic heritage. Alongside his brother Nicola and his nephew Francesco Trapani, Paolo led the company's expansion into New York, Paris, Geneva and Monte Carlo, and diversified it beyond jewellery into watches, perfumes, leather goods and hotels.

He headed the group from the mid-1980s, guiding it through a period of rapid international growth. Its jewellery was worn by stars from Elizabeth Taylor to Zendaya, and Bulgari himself, tall and silver-haired, occasionally appeared beside them, keeping a characteristically reserved presence. In 2011 the family sold a controlling stake to the French luxury group LVMH in a deal valued at more than five billion dollars, ending decades of direct family control.

Away from the glamour, Bulgari often spoke of his family's humble beginnings. In 2019 he and his wife set up the Paolo Bulgari Foundation to support young people through schooling and job training in disadvantaged neighbourhoods on the outskirts of Rome, work that later funded the redevelopment of a square in Tor Bella Monaca. He was cleared in 2018 after a tax-evasion trial in Rome, a case he always denied, and Forbes estimated his fortune at 1.3 billion dollars in 2015.
