Drone Strike Sets Kyiv Hospital Ablaze, Injuring Three as Air Defences Strain

A Russian drone strike set a hospital in central Kyiv on fire overnight, injuring three patients and forcing dozens to flee, as Ukraine's president warned that limits on a satellite system used by its air defences were making it harder to shoot down incoming drones.

The drone hit the capital's National Institute of Traumatology and Orthopedics shortly after 3am local time on Friday, in the Shevchenkivskyi district near several Western embassies. The blast partly destroyed the second floor, blew out more than 100 windows and damaged a neighbouring 19th-century building, and a fire swept through the upper floors before firefighters brought it under control.

Three patients were injured and treated at the scene. Thirty people were evacuated by hospital staff and seven more were rescued by emergency crews. The hospital's medical director said services had been temporarily suspended while the damage was cleared.

The six-storey hospital stands in a busy part of the capital, close to cafes and a small public park. The blast shattered windows in buildings across the road, including the National Academy of Arts, and the fire spread across two floors and the building's facade before it was put out. The institute, which treats bone and joint injuries, is one of the country's main orthopaedic centres.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said speed restrictions on Starshield, the military satellite service run by SpaceX, had again slowed Ukraine's ability to track and intercept drones. Ukraine's air force said 117 Russian drones were launched overnight and 80 were shot down, including 39 jet-powered ones.

The strike came two days after US-backed talks in Abu Dhabi ended, and a day after President Vladimir Putin met US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner in Moscow. In Odesa, strikes were still under way on Friday morning and at least two people had been killed, the city's military administration said. Russia said it had intercepted 1,198 Ukrainian drones overnight, one of the largest waves of the war, and reported strikes on oil infrastructure. Across Ukraine, nine people were killed and 61 injured over the past day, officials said.
