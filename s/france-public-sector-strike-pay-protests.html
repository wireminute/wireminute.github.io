Tens of Thousands March Across France as Public Workers Strike Over Pay

Tens of thousands of people marched across France on Tuesday as public sector workers staged a nationwide strike to demand pay rises, in the third united day of action since September.

Unions called out the country's 5.8 million public sector employees after Friday's talks with Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu ended without a deal on their call for pay rises across the board. The government had announced a higher minimum wage for private sector workers last week, but public sector unions said it did nothing for their members. Marches were held in dozens of cities, with the main Paris rally moving from Place de la Nation to Place d'Italie from the early afternoon. Police put the expected turnout at 40,000 to 80,000 in Paris and as many as 300,000 nationwide. Students from high schools and universities marched alongside them, as did some private sector workers.

At the centre of the dispute is the pay index used to work out basic salaries for public employees, which the draft budget leaves frozen for a fourth year running. Unions say the freeze has left public servants' spending power down by over 16 percent since 2017, pushing growing numbers onto or close to the minimum wage. They want the index tied to inflation, alongside a review of pay scales and career paths.

The strike follows more than a month of school blockades and protests over classroom conditions and the cost of living. An earlier mobilisation this month drew hundreds of thousands of people, with tear gas used by police and clashes in several cities. The government is racing to pass a deficit-cutting budget for 2027 before presidential elections next year, in which the far right hopes to make gains.

Public transport was disrupted in Paris, with several Metro and suburban rail stations closed for security reasons, and rail services were affected elsewhere. Most tourist sites stayed open. The turnout figures were police estimates issued before the marches, and final numbers were still being counted as the day went on.

Key points

  • Unions called 5.8 million public sector workers out in a nationwide strike over pay, the third united day of action since September.
  • Police expected 40,000 to 80,000 demonstrators in Paris and as many as 300,000 across France, with students joining the marches.
  • Friday's talks with Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu ended without a deal, and the draft budget leaves the public sector pay index frozen for a fourth year.
  • Unions put the loss in public servants' spending power since 2017 at over 16 percent and want the pay index linked to inflation.
