EU Proposes €45 Billion Loan for Ukraine, to Be Repaid From Russian Assets

The European Commission has proposed a new loan of up to €45 billion for Ukraine in 2027, to be repaid from future Russian war reparations, with frozen Russian assets held in reserve as the fallback.

The Commission presented the plan on Wednesday, saying the money would be raised through joint EU borrowing on the capital markets. The loan would cover about two-thirds of Ukraine's estimated €70 billion funding gap for next year, with the rest expected to come from Kyiv's other international partners. Of the new loan, up to €25 billion would go to military assistance and up to €20 billion to general budget support.

The Commission said extra funding was needed because the war with Russia had intensified. EU Defence Commissioner Andrius Kubilius said Russia was expected to double its deployment of drones next year to 20 million units, underlining the scale of the military pressure Ukraine faces.

The loan is structured as limited recourse, meaning Ukraine would repay the principal only after receiving reparations from Russia. Under the proposal, the EU would reserve the right to use Russia's immobilised cash balances to repay the loan, and windfall profits generated by frozen assets, typically €4 billion to €6 billion a year, could also support repayment. About €210 billion in Russian assets is frozen in the EU, most of it held at the Euroclear financial depository in Brussels.

The proposal follows the €90 billion Ukraine Support Loan that EU leaders agreed in December 2025 to fund Kyiv in 2026 and 2027, from which the Czech Republic, Hungary and Slovakia opted out of financial participation. Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said she was confident all 27 member states would back the new loan, although Hungary, Slovakia, the Czech Republic and Bulgaria want to stay outside it. EU leaders will discuss the plan in Brussels at the end of next week, and the Commission hopes partners such as Canada and Japan will cover the remainder of the funding gap. Interest on the loan, first due in 2028, would be covered by an EU borrowing-cost subsidy estimated at €2 billion to €2.5 billion a year.

Key points

  • The European Commission proposes a loan of up to €45 billion for Ukraine in 2027
  • It would cover about two-thirds of Kyiv's €70 billion funding gap, with €25 billion for military aid and €20 billion for budget support
  • Ukraine would repay the principal only after receiving Russian reparations, and the EU reserves the right to use frozen Russian assets
  • Von der Leyen says she is confident all 27 member states will back the plan, which EU leaders discuss in Brussels next week
