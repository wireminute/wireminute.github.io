Iran Says Reopening Hormuz Is 'Not Difficult' if US Accepts Its Terms

Iran's foreign minister says getting the Strait of Hormuz open again would be straightforward if Washington accepts conditions he called reasonable and in line with international law, as Tehran calls the American blockade an act of war and mediation through Qatar continues.

Abbas Araghchi said on Saturday that there was no solution to the standoff over the strait unless the United States acknowledged its mistakes and fulfilled its previous commitments. He said reopening the waterway would be straightforward once Washington accepted reasonable conditions, and he also asked for an emergency meeting of the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation to discuss the situation in Gaza.

His deputy, Kazem Gharibabadi, told reporters that Iran regarded the American economic blockade as an act of war and would take any measure needed to break it. He said negotiations on the nuclear programme had not yet begun and that Iran's enriched material would under no circumstances be sent out of the country.

Iran also said a delegation from Qatar held two hours of talks in Tehran on Friday evening before leaving, and that Tehran remained in contact with mediators, including Qatar, to see whether diplomacy could be given another chance. Messages between Iran and the United States have continued through Qatar and Pakistan.

On the American side, President Donald Trump said in a television interview that he planned to make a deal with Iran that was permanent and prevented Tehran from obtaining a nuclear weapon. He said he had never been very interested in regime change, described Iran's new leadership as more rational, and added that it made agreements and broke them the next day.

The plan under discussion would see Washington act on the first day, ending the fighting on all fronts, lifting oil sanctions, releasing frozen Iranian assets and ending the naval blockade of Iranian ports. On the second day Iran would reopen the strait to commercial shipping and talks on a final agreement, including the nuclear programme, would begin. Araghchi has said the timetable could be shortened to two hours if Iran's demands are met, and that an earlier memorandum reached in Islamabad is no longer on the table after Washington violated a key provision.

Key points

  • Iran's foreign minister says reopening Hormuz is 'not difficult' if the US accepts reasonable conditions under international law.
  • Tehran calls the American blockade an act of war and says it will take any measure needed to break it.
  • A Qatari delegation held two hours of talks in Tehran as mediation through Qatar and Pakistan continues.
  • Trump says he wants a permanent deal that stops Iran obtaining a nuclear weapon, but says Tehran breaks agreements.
