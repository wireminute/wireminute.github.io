Hours-Long Fuel Queues and Taxi Protests Across Iran on Eve of 'Bloody November' Anniversary

Motorists across Iran spent hours in line at petrol stations on Saturday, and taxi drivers in one northern city stopped work in protest, as the country's fuel rationing system buckled on the eve of the seventh anniversary of the deadly 2019 fuel protests.

Videos shared online showed long lines of cars stretching for miles outside stations in southern cities including Kazeroun, Bandar Abbas, Evaz and Lordegan, with drivers in Kazeroun shouting in anger at the shortages. In the northern city of Ardabil, taxi drivers parked their cars and refused to pick up passengers, leaving people stranded, while in Bandar Abbas people waiting to refuel blocked a nearby road.

The government has rationed petrol to 160 litres a month per vehicle since the 2019 unrest, when an overnight price rise set off nationwide protests that became known as 'Bloody November'. Sunday marks seven years since that announcement. Officials have avoided another price rise since, but sanctions, a collapsing economy and the blockade of the Strait of Hormuz have deepened the shortages.

Key points

  • Hours-long queues formed at petrol stations across Iran on Saturday, with lines stretching for miles in several southern cities.
  • Taxi drivers in Ardabil stopped work in protest, and residents blocked a road near a station in Bandar Abbas.
  • The unrest comes on the eve of the seventh anniversary of the 2019 'Bloody November' fuel protests; petrol is rationed to 160 litres a month per vehicle.
