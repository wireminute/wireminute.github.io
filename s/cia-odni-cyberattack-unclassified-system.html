CIA and National Intelligence Office Suffer Apparent Cyberattack on Unclassified System

The CIA and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence have suffered an apparent cyberattack affecting unclassified information, in the latest breach to hit the United States government.

Four people familiar with the matter said the intrusion hit a system the two agencies use for open-source intelligence work, drawing on publicly available information. Officials said no classified information was affected, and classified systems and critical capabilities were not disrupted. The CIA declined to comment.

It was not immediately clear when the breach happened, who was behind it or whether it has been resolved. The incident was first reported by the New York Times. Two of the sources said it did not appear to be connected to the apparent hacking of the FBI's nationwide crime database, reported last week.

The breach adds to a run of cyber incidents and outages across the United States government in recent days, including a multi-day outage of the classified network used by the National Security Agency, which officials said was caused by a failed software update rather than an attack.

Key points

  • The CIA and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence suffered an apparent cyberattack affecting unclassified information.
  • The intrusion hit a system used for open-source intelligence work; officials said no classified information was affected.
  • It is unclear when the breach happened, who was behind it or whether it has been resolved, and it does not appear linked to the FBI database breach.
