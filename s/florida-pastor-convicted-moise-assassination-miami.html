Florida Pastor Convicted in Miami Over Plot to Kill Haiti's President Jovenel Moise

A federal jury in Miami has convicted Florida pastor Christian Emmanuel Sanon on all eight charges arising from the 2021 assassination of Haitian President Jovenel Moise, after a trial that ran for nearly eight weeks.

Sanon, 68, a pastor with dual Haitian and American citizenship, was found guilty on Wednesday, including on a count of conspiring to kill Moise. He showed little reaction as the verdict was read out in the Miami courtroom, and he remains in federal custody. He faces up to life in prison, with sentencing scheduled for 10 February.

Moise, 53, was shot dead by gunmen at his private home on 7 July 2021, in a night raid in which his wife Martine was also wounded. The killing left Haiti without a president, and the country has had none since, as political turmoil deepened in the years that followed.

Prosecutors told the court that Sanon wanted to replace Moise as president, and that the scheme to kill him included paying about two dozen foreign mercenaries with money taken from United States government programmes meant to help businesses during the Covid-19 pandemic. South Florida, they said, was the hub where the plot was planned and financed, and the conspirators wanted to install a president of their own choosing. Sanon was arrested in the Haitian capital, Port-au-Prince, days after the assassination.

Sanon admitted during the proceedings that he had been in contact with people who wanted to replace Moise, but he said the group had moved to another candidate long before the killing, and he denied knowing of any plan to kill anyone. His lawyer, Zeljka Bozanic, said the defence team was disappointed and would appeal at the appropriate time. Prosecutors declined to comment after the verdict. Sanon's case had been separated from those of four other men convicted earlier this year, and his trial had been delayed for health reasons.

United States prosecutors have charged more than 40 people over the assassination, and a dozen have been convicted so far, five of them now serving life terms. The verdict closes one chapter of a case that has stretched across two countries for more than five years, while the question of who ordered the killing, and why, remains only partly answered in court.

  • Christian Emmanuel Sanon, 68, was convicted on all eight charges, including conspiracy to kill President Jovenel Moise; he faces up to life in prison and is due to be sentenced on 10 February.
  • Prosecutors said Sanon sought to replace Moise and helped pay about two dozen mercenaries with money from United States pandemic business relief programmes.
  • Moise, 53, was shot dead at his home on 7 July 2021 and his wife Martine was wounded; Haiti has had no president since.
