Trump, Xi and Putin Begin First-Ever Three-Way Summit in Shenzhen

Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin have begun the first three-way meeting of the sitting leaders of the United States, China and Russia, with Xi chairing a closed-door session in Shenzhen focused on nuclear weapons, Ukraine and Iran.

Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin have begun the first three-way meeting of the sitting leaders of the United States, China and Russia, gathering in Shenzhen on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit. The three leaders posed for a group photograph in front of their national flags, delivered short opening remarks and then moved into a closed-door session chaired by Xi.

Xi opened the meeting by saying it had "groundbreaking significance" and that healthy interaction between major countries was essential. Trump said the world's three largest nuclear powers had to find ways to cut their arsenals, while Putin said the three countries were capable of making a significant contribution to peace and cooperation in the world.

The session has no fixed end time. The Kremlin said the three leaders would decide the length of the meeting themselves, and it could run past the hour set aside in the protocol. The agenda covers nuclear arms, the war in Ukraine, the conflict involving Iran, trade and the military use of artificial intelligence. Officials on all sides have cautioned that no agreement is likely, calling the session a first conversation at leader level rather than a negotiation.

Trump held separate meetings with each leader before the three-way session. He met Putin first, calling him "my friend" as their talks began, in their first meeting since their summit in Alaska in August 2025. He then went to Kylin Villa, a lakeside resort in Shenzhen, where he posed for photographs with Xi before their own bilateral meeting, their third this year. Earlier in the day, the three leaders posed for a group photograph with the other APEC leaders.

Cutting nuclear arsenals is expected to lead the agenda. The last treaty limiting the nuclear weapons of the United States and Russia expired in February, leaving no binding caps in place between the world's two largest nuclear powers. It is not certain whether the three leaders will adopt a joint statement. Putin's top adviser said Moscow was working on the assumption that they would, while officials in Beijing and Washington have been more cautious.

Key points

  • Trump, Xi and Putin have begun the first three-way meeting of the sitting leaders of the United States, China and Russia, chaired by Xi in Shenzhen.
  • Xi called the summit of groundbreaking significance; Trump said the three largest nuclear powers must discuss cutting arsenals; Putin said the three can contribute to peace.
  • The closed-door session has no fixed length and covers nuclear arms, Ukraine, Iran, trade and AI; officials caution no agreement is likely.
  • Trump met Putin and Xi separately beforehand; the last US-Russia nuclear treaty expired in February, leaving no binding caps in place.
