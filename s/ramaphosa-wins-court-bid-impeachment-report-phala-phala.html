Court Throws Out Report That Could Have Impeached South Africa's Ramaphosa

South Africa's President Cyril Ramaphosa has won a court battle to set aside the report that paved the way for possible impeachment proceedings against him, ending parliament's inquiry into his handling of a 2020 burglary at his private game farm.

The Western Cape High Court ruled on Wednesday that the 2022 report of an independent parliamentary panel was unlawful and set it aside in full. Judge President Nolwazi Mabindla-Boqwana, delivering the unanimous judgment of a three-judge bench, said the panel had overstepped its mandate, asked itself the wrong questions and made several material errors of law. "Any steps taken by the National Assembly pursuant to the report are reviewed, declared unlawful and set aside," she ruled.

The panel, chaired by former chief justice Sandile Ngcobo, had found that Ramaphosa may have committed a serious violation of the law in how he handled the theft of about $580,000 in foreign currency hidden in a sofa at his Phala Phala game farm in Limpopo province on 9 February 2020. The panel said he may have failed in his duty to report a theft of more than R100,000 to the police, and that he may have committed serious misconduct. Parliament voted in December 2022 not to adopt the report, but the Constitutional Court ruled in May that the vote was invalid and ordered lawmakers to set up an impeachment committee. In July, the High Court paused the committee's hearings while Ramaphosa's challenge was heard.

The court drew a distinction between information and evidence, finding the panel was required to establish whether sufficient evidence, not merely information, existed to justify an impeachment inquiry. It also found the panel had erred in law in concluding that cabinet members could not hold business interests while in office. The judges found the panel had fundamentally misconstrued its functions and the inquiry it was required to conduct, and that its conclusions were not rationally related to the power it was exercising.

The ruling removes the basis for parliament's impeachment inquiry, and Ramaphosa will no longer face a committee process that could have considered removing him from office. The judgment can still be appealed, and the National Assembly could in principle decide to open a full impeachment inquiry of its own, regardless of the panel report's status. The public prosecutor dropped related criminal charges in 2024.

Key points

  • The Western Cape High Court declared the 2022 Phala Phala panel report unlawful and set it aside in full, ending parliament's impeachment inquiry.
  • The panel had found Ramaphosa may have committed serious misconduct over about $580,000 stolen from a sofa at his Phala Phala farm in February 2020.
  • The judges said the panel overstepped its mandate, asked the wrong questions and made material errors of law, including confusing information with evidence.
  • The ruling can be appealed, and parliament could still decide to open an impeachment inquiry of its own.
