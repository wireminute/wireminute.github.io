Journalist Who Named Alleged Drug Traffickers Shot Dead in Costa Rica, First Such Killing in 25 Years

A Costa Rican journalist who used social media to publish allegations linking suspected drug traffickers with politicians has been shot dead outside the capital, in the first known killing of a journalist in the country in 25 years.

Camilo Rodriguez was driving through Santo Domingo de Heredia, a town on the edge of San Jose, at about 3:20 p.m. local time on Monday when two men on a motorcycle opened fire on his vehicle, police said. The Red Cross said he was found dead inside the car with multiple gunshot wounds.

Rodriguez had built a following by naming people he accused of links to drug trafficking, publishing their full names and claiming he had passed reports to the United States Drug Enforcement Administration. Police said he had reported receiving death threats from drug trafficking groups and had turned down a place in a witness protection programme.

Attorney General Carlo Diaz said investigators found multiple bullet impacts at the scene and that the killing had the marks of a contract hit, likely connected to the allegations Rodriguez had published. He said no motive had been established and no suspects had been named, and an investigation was under way. The Costa Rican Journalists' Association condemned the killing.

The killing comes as Costa Rica, long seen as one of Central America's most peaceful and stable democracies, grapples with a surge in drug violence. The country has shifted in recent years from a transit point for drugs to a logistics hub for trafficking groups. When President Laura Fernandez was running for office last year, she sued Rodriguez for defamation after he posted a video claiming her home had been built with money allegedly provided by a drug trafficker.

Key points

  • Camilo Rodriguez was shot dead on Monday as he drove through Santo Domingo de Heredia, near San Jose; two men on a motorcycle opened fire on his car
  • He had published allegations naming suspected drug traffickers and politicians on social media, and had reported death threats and turned down witness protection
  • The attorney general said the scene resembled a contract killing, likely linked to his published allegations; no suspects have been named
  • It is the first known killing of a journalist in Costa Rica in 25 years, as the country faces a surge in drug-related violence
