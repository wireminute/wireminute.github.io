Trump Says Vance Got It Wrong on the Iran War as Questions Grow Over His Deputy

President Donald Trump has said Vice President JD Vance erred in how he talked about the war with Iran, as questions grow about the vice president's standing after the midterm elections.

President Donald Trump has said Vice President JD Vance made a mistake in how he spoke about the war with Iran, after Vance blamed the conflict for heavy Republican losses in this month's midterm elections. Speaking to Fox News during his visit to China, Trump said Vance was doing a very good job but had left out one point. Asked whether he would go to war again if he had the choice, Trump said the answer was absolutely yes.

The row began the morning after the November 3 elections, when Vance published a long post online arguing that the war was the reason Republicans lost control of both chambers of Congress. Some read the post as an indirect criticism of Trump, his own boss, because Vance did not say in it that launching the war had been the right decision. Trump first said Vance's reading of the result was largely true, but insisted the strikes were the only way to stop Iran from obtaining a nuclear weapon.

Trump has since moved Vance away from the Iran file, telling him to focus instead on fraud in Democratic-run states. Special envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner are now leading the negotiations with Tehran. Trump also described the memorandum of understanding Vance negotiated with Iran in June as a waste of time, though he said signing it was not a mistake. The agreement collapsed in July after Iran attacked commercial ships in a dispute over control of the Strait of Hormuz.

Reports in the American press say Trump has grown frustrated with Vance in private while praising Secretary of State Marco Rubio, who is seen as a possible rival for the Republican presidential nomination in 2028. Vance has rejected any suggestion of a rift, saying his comments were about the political cost of the war rather than the decision to launch it. Trump has declined to say whether he would support Vance if he ran in 2028, and said earlier this month that the war was effectively over, even as the American naval blockade of Iranian ports stays in place.

Key points

  • Trump said Vance left out one point when discussing the Iran war: asked whether he would do it again, the answer is absolutely yes.
  • The row follows Vance's post after the midterm elections blaming the Iran war for Republican losses in both chambers of Congress.
  • Vance has been moved off Iran talks to focus on fraud in Democratic-run states, with Witkoff and Kushner now leading negotiations with Tehran.
  • Trump called the June memorandum Vance negotiated with Iran a waste of time but not a mistake, and has privately praised Rubio as a possible 2028 contender, according to reports.
