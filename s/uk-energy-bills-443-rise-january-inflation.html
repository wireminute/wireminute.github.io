British Households Face £443 Annual Energy Bill Rise From January as Inflation Picks Up Again

Household energy bills in Britain are forecast to rise by £443 a year from January, taking the typical annual bill to £2,166, as the cost of living climbs again and wholesale gas prices stay high.

The forecast rise of about 26 percent comes from energy consultancy Cornwall Insight and supplier EDF, and it would land on the roughly 22 million households on standard variable tariffs when the new price cap takes effect in January. The typical dual-fuel bill would climb to £2,166 a year. Households on fixed-rate deals, around 11 million of them, would not see their unit rates change.

The pressure comes from wholesale gas markets. Prices have roughly tripled since February, when strikes on Iran by the United States and Israel shut shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. Winter demand is expected to push prices higher still, and earlier forecasts from the big suppliers had already pointed to a rise of around £460 a year.

Official figures published on Wednesday put inflation at 3.6 percent for the year to October, against 3.5 percent in September, the highest annual rate in a year. The cost of motor fuel was a major driver, as the conflict in the Middle East pushed up pump prices.

The Bank of England raised rates earlier this month, the first increase in three years, taking them to 4 percent from 3.75 percent. It expects inflation could reach a high of 4.9 percent around the middle of next year, and a return to its 2 percent target may not come until 2028. The regulator Ofgem will confirm the January to March price cap on 25 November. Ministers have pointed to support for households, including a £100 winter payment and a cut in VAT on electricity bills that runs until July 2027, while Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh has said affordability is her overriding objective.

Key points

  • Energy bills are forecast to rise by £443 a year from January, taking the typical annual bill to £2,166, a jump of about 26 percent.
  • About 22 million households on standard variable tariffs would be affected; the regulator Ofgem confirms the new cap on 25 November.
  • Wholesale gas prices have roughly tripled since February, when strikes on Iran shut shipping through the Strait of Hormuz.
  • UK inflation reached 3.6 percent in the year to October, and the Bank of England expects it could reach a high of 4.9 percent around the middle of next year.
