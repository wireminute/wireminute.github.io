Nvidia Halts AI Server Shipments to Southeast Asia as US Curbs Bite

Nvidia has told the companies that build its servers to stop shipping them to four Southeast Asian countries, as new American export controls close routes Chinese firms are said to use to reach advanced artificial intelligence computing power.

Dell and Supermicro, which assemble servers around Nvidia's chips, have informed customers in Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia that the hardware they ordered will not be sent, according to a report that cites three people briefed on the decision. It is not clear whether the halt will last, as the chipmaker and its partners work through compliance with the new American rules.

The move follows controls announced by the US Commerce Department on 16 November and in force since 19 November. Advanced AI chips bound for Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, Indonesia or Brazil now require a permit from Washington. Under the same rules, any data centre must seek permission before letting firms based in China, Macau or embargoed states use large amounts of advanced-chip computing power. Cloud providers have 90 days to win licences by demonstrating that none of their customers are on the restricted list. Chinese developers have got around the controls by renting computing power from graphics processors installed in data centres across Southeast Asia.

The disruption is already hitting finance for data centre projects in the region. A Singapore cloud company, Aolani, had been seeking a loan of about $500 million to buy Nvidia graphics processors for a data centre in Malaysia that was to supply computing power to ByteDance, TikTok's Chinese parent company; bankers have now put that lending on hold. Buyers are also being sought for thousands of the newest B300 processors that were meant for Chinese firms to use from afar. Singapore is a major hub for the company: its subsidiary there recorded revenue of $104 billion in 2025, a rise of 72 percent on the year before.

The controls were published days after chief executive Jensen Huang stepped down following reports about his personal conduct and links to a Singapore cloud company under investigation over alleged chip diversion to China. The board brought Huang back as chief executive on 23 November. The cloud company, Megaspeed, which rents out computing power from its data centres, is under scrutiny over whether advanced chips reached China. California has also demanded documents from Nvidia in an inquiry into whether its advanced chips reached China.

Key points

  • Nvidia has told server makers including Dell and Supermicro to halt shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia.
  • The halt follows US export controls in force since 19 November, requiring permits for advanced AI chips bound for those countries and Brazil.
  • Chinese developers have rented computing power from data centres in the region to get around the chip controls.
  • Data centre finance is stalling, including a loan of about $500 million tied to a Malaysian site planned to serve ByteDance.
