England Retain Wheelchair Rugby League World Cup With 50-38 Win Over France in Wollongong

England are wheelchair rugby league world champions for a third time after beating France 50-38 in a breathless final in Wollongong, Australia, keeping the crown they won on home soil four years ago.

The final at the WIN Entertainment Centre in Wollongong on Friday was the latest chapter in the sport's great rivalry, and it did not disappoint. England led 28-22 at half-time, with Joe Coyd scoring four tries, Jack Brown coming off the bench to complete a hat-trick, and Rob Hawkins crossing twice. France refused to go away, with Denuwelaere scoring three of their tries, but England held their nerve in a final where the lead changed hands repeatedly.

The victory makes England the first country to win the wheelchair world title three times. They also lifted the trophy in 2008, when the tournament was last held in Australia, and in 2021, when they edged France 28-24 in front of a home crowd in Manchester. Of the five wheelchair World Cup finals ever played, England and France have contested every one: France won in 2013 and 2017, and England have now taken the other three.

England arrived in Australia as defending champions and the world's top-ranked side, a position they have held since November 2022, and head coach Tom Coyd's team delivered when it mattered most. The triumph also made up for disappointment elsewhere in the England camp, with the men's and women's teams both going out in the group stage of their own tournaments.

Since that Manchester final four years ago, the two rivals had met four times, winning twice each, and the three finals before this one had been decided by a combined total of only ten points. Friday's twelve-point margin was, by the standards of this fixture, almost comfortable — but only after a final that stayed in the balance until late on.

Key points

  • England beat France 50-38 in the Wheelchair Rugby League World Cup final in Wollongong, Australia, on Friday.
  • Joe Coyd scored four tries and Jack Brown added a hat-trick off the bench as England led 28-22 at half-time.
  • England are the first nation to win the wheelchair world title three times, after victories in 2008 and 2021.
  • England and France have contested all five wheelchair World Cup finals; France won in 2013 and 2017.
