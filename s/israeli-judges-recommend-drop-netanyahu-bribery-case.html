Israeli Judges Recommend Dropping Netanyahu's Bribery Case in Major Trial Setback

Judges in Benjamin Netanyahu's long-running corruption trial have recommended that prosecutors drop the most serious case against the Israeli prime minister, in a major setback for the prosecution.

The three-judge panel at the Jerusalem District Court gave its view at a hearing on Wednesday, telling Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara that a conviction in the case known as Case 4000 looked unlikely and that the case should be dropped. Presiding judge Rivka Friedman-Feldman said the bribery charge presented considerable difficulties and that there was serious doubt over whether the evidence behind it held up. Baharav-Miara, who attended the trial for the first time after being summoned by the court, said she would review the judges' remarks with prosecutors.

Case 4000 is the gravest of the three cases Netanyahu faces. Prosecutors allege that he granted regulatory favours worth hundreds of millions of dollars to the telecoms company Bezeq in return for favourable coverage on the Walla news website, with both businesses then controlled by businessman Shaul Elovitch. Netanyahu faces charges of bribery, fraud and breach of trust in the case. The judges also urged prosecutors to reconsider a second case, Case 2000, in which Netanyahu is accused of seeking a deal with Arnon Mozes, owner of a leading newspaper, trading favourable coverage for legislation that would slow a rival paper. They said they doubted the conduct described in that case amounted to a criminal offence.

On the third case, Case 1000, the judges called for an agreement to bring it to a close. It concerns luxury gifts, including cigars and champagne, that Netanyahu is accused of receiving from film producer Arnon Milchan and Australian businessman James Packer. The court stressed that its remarks were recommendations rather than a verdict, and that they concerned Netanyahu alone, since the panel has yet to hear the other defendants. The judges had twice before urged prosecutors to reconsider the bribery charge, in June 2023 and again this June, but their latest assessment goes further, making clear they do not expect a conviction in the case at all.

Netanyahu was indicted in 2019 and has denied any wrongdoing, saying the cases were built on false testimony to damage him. His own testimony in the trial is complete. If prosecutors follow the recommendation, the bribery charge would fall away and only the lesser fraud and breach of trust counts would remain, a result that would amount to a major political victory for Israel's longest-serving leader. The panel faces a practical deadline of its own: Friedman-Feldman, who leads it, is set to retire in March 2028.

Key points

  • Judges at the Jerusalem District Court recommended dropping Case 4000, the bribery case, saying a conviction looks unlikely.
  • They also urged prosecutors to reconsider Case 2000 and to seek an agreement to close Case 1000, the gifts case.
  • The remarks are recommendations, not a verdict; the attorney general said she would review them with the prosecution team.
  • Netanyahu, indicted in 2019, denies wrongdoing; the judges had twice before urged the bribery charge to be reconsidered.
