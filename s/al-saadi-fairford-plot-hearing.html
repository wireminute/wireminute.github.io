Iran-Linked Plot Suspect Declares Innocence and Seeks New Lawyers in New York Court

A dual Iranian-Iraqi national accused of directing a failed attack on a British airbase used by the United States military told a federal judge in New York on Thursday that he is innocent and no longer wants his court-appointed lawyers, in a hearing that ended with marshals escorting him from the courtroom.

Mohammad Baqer Saad Dawood Al-Saadi appeared before Judge Colleen McMahon in Manhattan for a hearing lasting nearly two hours. Speaking through a translator, he said his family was trying to hire private attorneys, described himself as an important statesman, and said his current lawyers no longer represented him. He told the court he was innocent of the charges against him.

The judge accepted his declaration of innocence as a not-guilty plea. She told his lawyers that his family has an absolute right to hire its own counsel, put all case deadlines on hold until February, and set February 10 for his next court appearance to give the family time to find lawyers.

Prosecutors say Al-Saadi was a commander in Kataib Hizballah, an Iran-aligned armed group, and directed a failed attack in September on RAF Fairford in western England, a base used by the US Air Force. A rewritten indictment filed last week accuses him of passing secret messages to operatives planning the attack, using coded language in calls to his mother and sister in Iraq. He was arrested last spring and has been held without bail since. In June he pleaded not guilty to charges that he plotted at least 18 attacks across Europe in retaliation for the war between the United States, Israel and Iran.

Prosecutors say Al-Saadi was one of three men who conspired through contacts in Britain to carry out attacks for an Iran-aligned network including the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps and Kataib Hizballah. Both groups have been designated by the United States government as foreign terrorist organisations. A group of men was detained on September 27 after three suspicious vans were spotted travelling towards the airbase.

The hearing also covered limits on his communications and access to religious writings. His lawyers said the government was refusing him Arabic texts, including a biography of Imam Husayn ibn Ali, because they describe violence. The judge said he must be allowed to read them, noting violent passages in the Bible would never be banned from jails. Prosecutors said he had kept using coded words with family despite the limits and was directing relatives to open social media accounts to shape the narrative. After repeatedly trying to address the judge directly, he was led out by marshals while shouting in Arabic, and his lawyer said he had chosen to return to the detention center rather than come back into court.

Key points

  • Al-Saadi told a Manhattan federal judge he is innocent and wants his court-appointed lawyers replaced; the judge recorded a not-guilty plea.
  • Judge Colleen McMahon put all deadlines on hold until February and set February 10 for his return to court so his family can hire private attorneys.
  • Prosecutors accuse him of directing a failed September attack on RAF Fairford, a British base used by the US Air Force, and of plotting at least 18 attacks across Europe.
  • The judge also ruled he must be allowed access to religious texts, after his lawyers said Arabic writings about Imam Husayn ibn Ali were being withheld.
