Navy Chief Tells Senators the Iran War Is Straining the Fleet

The acting head of the US Navy has told senators the war with Iran is straining the service, as lawmakers from both parties pressed him on long deployments, dwindling weapons stocks and the cost of the naval blockade.

The acting head of the United States Navy has told senators that the war with Iran is straining the service, as lawmakers from both parties pressed him on the human and material costs of a conflict now in its ninth month. Hung Cao, nominated to lead the Navy permanently, was testifying on Tuesday at his confirmation hearing before the Senate Armed Services Committee. "This war, again, I know it's a strain on our navy," Cao said, while insisting that the joint military campaign had "really set China back on their heels."

Much of the questioning focused on the USS Abraham Lincoln, which completed a record 265-day deployment across the Pacific, the South China Sea, the Indian Ocean and the North Arabian Sea before returning to San Diego in October. Senators raised low crew morale, severe supply shortages and mental health strains aboard the carrier. The Navy has confirmed eight suicide attempts aboard the Lincoln, a figure that drew sharp questions about living conditions and support for sailors on long deployments.

Senator Elizabeth Warren pressed Cao on the price of the campaign, citing independent estimates that the naval blockade of Iranian shipping costs about $32.5 million a day, and reports that Iranian strikes on the Navy's regional headquarters in Bahrain caused at least $400 million in damage. Cao confirmed the blockade figure but said he could not give a full assessment of the Bahrain damage because the site was a "nonpermissive area" for US personnel to visit. He said weapons production had risen sharply, with Tomahawk cruise missile output now running at more than 60 a month, against 125 a year when he was in uniform.

The United States is currently massing three carrier strike groups in the region, with the USS George H.W. Bush and the newly arrived USS Theodore Roosevelt joining the effort to tighten the months-long naval blockade of Iran. President Donald Trump said last week that further military strikes on Iran were "quite unlikely" and described the military phase of the war as "effectively over", though no ceasefire is in force and the blockade continues. Cao, a retired Navy captain who left active duty in 2021, has served as acting secretary since April after the departure of John Phelan. He was nominated for the permanent post in September and needs a waiver from Congress because he left active service less than seven years ago.

Key points

  • Acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao told a Senate confirmation hearing the Iran war is "a strain on our navy", while saying the joint campaign has set China back.
  • Senators raised the USS Abraham Lincoln's record 265-day deployment, crew morale and supply shortages; the Navy has confirmed eight suicide attempts aboard the carrier.
  • Cao confirmed the naval blockade of Iran costs about $32.5 million a day; he could not give a full damage figure for the strike on the Bahrain headquarters.
  • The US is massing three carrier strike groups in the region as Tomahawk missile production rises to more than 60 a month.
