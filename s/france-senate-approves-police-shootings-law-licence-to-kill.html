French Senate Approves Police Shootings Law Critics Call a 'Licence to Kill'

France's Senate has given final approval to a law that presumes police officers who fire their weapons acted within the law, a measure critics describe as a licence to kill and left-wing lawmakers plan to challenge in court.

Senators backed the text by 205 votes to 110 on Friday, completing parliament's approval after the lower house passed it in July. The measure states that when officers use their weapons, they are presumed to have acted within the framework of the law, unless evidence shows otherwise.

The government argues the change protects officers who work in dangerous conditions and stops them from being automatically suspected of using disproportionate force. Interior Minister Laurent Nunez has backed the law, and the party of far-right presidential candidate Marine Le Pen supports it. The idea has been a long-standing demand of far-right politicians.

Opponents say the presumption reverses the burden of proof and will shield officers after fatal shootings. Left-wing parliamentarians said they would ask the Constitutional Council to review the law. Activists have marched against the bill under the slogan 'permis de tuer', and protests against it were held in Paris in September.

The vote comes as claims of police brutality have returned to public debate during student-led protests in recent weeks. The Constitutional Council challenge means the measure's fate is not yet settled, and the law will face scrutiny there before it can take full effect.

The approval comes in the same week a Paris court sentenced three French officers to prison over the beating of music producer Michel Zecler, a case that became a symbol of the debate over police violence in France. Campaigners say the new law will make it harder to hold officers to account; the government insists it simply protects officers who act lawfully in dangerous situations.
