Venezuela to Repeal Law That Banned Opposition Figures From Office for Decades

Venezuela's National Assembly will move next week to repeal a 2024 law that barred opposition politicians from public office for decades if they backed foreign sanctions, the Assembly's president announced on Thursday.

The law, known as the Simón Bolívar Organic Law, was passed in November 2024, months after a disputed presidential election in July of that year in which Nicolás Maduro was declared the winner amid widespread fraud allegations. It threatened jail sentences of between 25 and 30 years, along with fines, seized assets and a ban from office lasting as long as 60 years for anyone found to have promoted or supported sanctions against Venezuela.

Assembly president Jorge Rodríguez said the repeal process would begin on Tuesday, December 1, and he expected the changes to be approved within the week. He said no politician had ever been punished under the law, and described it as a measure tied to a specific moment that had now passed. He added that the separate Law Against Hate would stay in place.

The repeal is one of the outcomes of talks between the interim government of Delcy Rodríguez and parts of the opposition, under way since August as part of what the two sides call a roadmap towards democracy. Delcy Rodríguez took power in January after Maduro was seized by US forces, and has promised free and fair elections but has given no date for them.

Opposition leader María Corina Machado, who won the opposition primary in 2023 but was barred from standing in 2024, returned from exile last month and is widely expected to run if an election is held, although she has been left out of the transition talks. Her ally Edmundo González, who stood in her place in 2024, has said he will return to Venezuela on December 5.

Key points

  • The National Assembly will start repealing the Simón Bolívar Law on Tuesday, December 1, with approval expected within the week.
  • The 2024 law threatened jail terms of 25 to 30 years, fines, seized assets and a ban from office of up to 60 years for backing foreign sanctions.
  • The move follows talks between Delcy Rodríguez's interim government and parts of the opposition on a roadmap towards democracy; she has promised free and fair elections but set no date.
  • Opposition leader María Corina Machado, barred from the 2024 race, returned from exile last month and is expected to run if an election is held, though she is excluded from the talks.
