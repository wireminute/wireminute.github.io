Democrats Clinch 258 House Seats, Their Largest Majority Since 1992

Democrats have secured at least 258 seats in the United States House of Representatives, giving the party its largest majority in the chamber since 1992 and a sweeping mandate from a midterm election fought largely on the cost of living.

The 258th seat was decided on Sunday when Democrat Chuck Hubbard defeated Republican Representative Virginia Foxx in North Carolina. Foxx, a 20-year veteran of the House, conceded the race, taking Democrats past the 218 seats needed for a majority and on to a tally not seen since Bill Clinton was first elected president in 1992.

Democrats have gained 44 seats so far, with 47 seats flipping from Republican to Democratic control, four more than the party managed in the 2018 midterms, during President Donald Trump's first term. Republicans hold at least 173 seats, with a small number of races still to be called. The party also won the nationwide House popular vote by 14 points, the widest margin in a midterm since 1974.

The scale of the result puts Democrats in a commanding position when the new Congress convenes, with control of the House agenda, its committees and the power to set the terms of debate with the White House. Hakeem Jeffries, already chosen by Democrats as their nominee for Speaker, is set to lead the chamber in a historic first for the party. The win also reverses the usual midterm pattern, in which the party holding the White House loses ground, and gives Democrats a platform to challenge the administration over the two years ahead.

The result also brings pressure. The party campaigned heavily on affordability, and voters will now expect action on household costs. The size of the majority gives Democrats room to pass legislation through the House, but any bill would still need to clear the Senate, where Democrats hold a narrower 56-44 majority after their own gains there.

Key points

  • Democrats have won at least 258 House seats, their largest majority since 1992; Republicans hold at least 173.
  • The decisive seat came on Sunday when Chuck Hubbard beat Virginia Foxx in North Carolina; Foxx had served in the House for two decades.
  • Democrats have gained 44 seats, with 47 red-to-blue flips, and won the House popular vote by 14 points, the widest midterm margin since 1974.
  • Hakeem Jeffries, already nominated by Democrats as Speaker, is set to lead the chamber, but the party now faces pressure to deliver on affordability.
