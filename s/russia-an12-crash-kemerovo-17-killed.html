Russian Military Transport Plane Crashes in Siberia, Killing All 19 on Board

A Russian military transport plane crashed in southwestern Siberia on Tuesday, killing all 19 people on board, Russia's Defence Ministry said, after the aircraft caught fire and came down near a village in the Kemerovo region.

The aircraft, an Antonov An-12, came down near the village of Yagunovo, south of the regional capital Kemerovo, on Tuesday evening, the Izvestia newspaper reported, citing sources familiar with the incident. The plane was flying from Novosibirsk to Kansk in Krasnoyarsk Krai when it went down. Witnesses described the aircraft on fire as it descended sharply before exploding on impact, according to Russian media reports.

Kemerovo region governor Ilya Seredyuk confirmed that an aviation incident had occurred in the Yagunovo district and said emergency services were working at the scene. He did not identify the aircraft or give a casualty figure. Early reports put the death toll far lower, with some outlets initially reporting three dead and others 13, before Russian media put the figure at 17. The Defence Ministry later confirmed 19 people were on board, and all of them were killed.

Russia's Defence Ministry later confirmed the crash, saying the An-12 was on a scheduled flight and came down in an uninhabited area. The ministry said a technical malfunction was the preliminary cause, and a commission from the Russian Aerospace Forces is working at the site to establish the circumstances. Unconfirmed reports in Russian media had suggested a fuel leak may have set fire to one of the aircraft's four engines, but investigators have not confirmed that account.

The An-12, known by the NATO reporting name Cub, is a Soviet-designed four-engine turboprop transport aircraft that first flew in the late 1950s. The type has been a workhorse of Soviet and Russian military transport ever since and remains in service with the Russian Air Force.
