New York Shortlists Ten Developers for First New Nuclear Plant in Decades

New York has named the ten companies that will compete to build the state's first new nuclear power plant in decades, picking them from 21 bids for at least one gigawatt of new atomic capacity in the north of the state.

The New York Power Authority said it had qualified the ten developers to move forward in its search for a partner to build at least one gigawatt of advanced nuclear power in Upstate New York. The authority chose them from 21 replies to a request for qualifications sent out in May, after two earlier rounds of interest in 2025.

Six of the firms qualified to work on either large reactors or small modular reactors: NextEra Energy, Avangrid, The Nuclear Company, Elementl Power, Turquoise Power and Oppenheimer Energy. The other four, X-energy, TerraPower, Blue Energy and Holtec International, qualified for small modular reactors only.

No site, reactor design or building partner has been chosen yet. Eight communities across the north of the state have said they would host the plant, and the authority said it would settle on a host community, a technology and a partner in the coming months. The project is the first stage of Governor Kathy Hochul's plan to add five gigawatts of new nuclear power across New York.

The authority's president, Justin Driscoll, said the agency had spent the past year building up its nuclear team, putting ten million dollars into workforce training and talking to communities across the state. He said qualifying ten established firms put the authority in a stronger position to deliver the first nuclear plant built in New York in a generation, supplying steady power without carbon emissions for decades to come.

Key points

  • New York's power authority qualified ten developers from 21 bids to build at least one gigawatt of new nuclear capacity upstate.
  • Six firms can bid on large reactors or small modular reactors; four qualify for small modular reactors only.
  • Eight upstate communities have offered to host the plant, but no site, technology or partner has been chosen yet.
  • The plant is the first stage of the state's plan to add five gigawatts of new nuclear power.
