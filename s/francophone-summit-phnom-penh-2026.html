French-Speaking Summit Opens in Phnom Penh as Congo-Rwanda Feud Threatens a Split

Leaders of the French-speaking world opened their summit in Phnom Penh on Sunday, with a fight over who should lead the organisation threatening to split it, and to drive out its biggest African member.

The 20th summit of the Organisation internationale de la Francophonie brings 20 leaders to the Cambodian capital, under the slogan 'Reinvest in Peace for Shared Prosperity and Lasting Stability'. King Norodom Sihamoni presided at the opening, while President Emmanuel Macron, who hands the chair to Cambodia, called for unity.

The dispute centres on Monday's vote for secretary-general. Rwanda's Louise Mushikiwabo, who has led the 90-member group since 2018, is seeking a third term. The Democratic Republic of Congo, which accuses Rwanda of backing the M23 armed group, a charge Kigali denies, has threatened to leave if she is re-elected.

Kinshasa withdrew its own candidate, Juliana Lumumba, on Saturday and backed Romania's former prime minister Dacian Ciolos, offering a unity choice if Rwanda did the same. Rwanda rejected a request from Macron to withdraw Mushikiwabo. Losing Congo, the world's second-largest French-speaking country, with 57.3 million speakers, would be a major blow.

Key points

  • The 20th Francophonie summit opened in Phnom Penh on Sunday, with about 20 leaders attending and Cambodia taking the chair from France.
  • Leaders vote on Monday for the next secretary-general; Rwanda's Louise Mushikiwabo seeks a third term, opposed by DR Congo.
  • DR Congo has threatened to quit the 90-member group if Mushikiwabo is re-elected, after withdrawing its own candidate in favour of a unity choice.
