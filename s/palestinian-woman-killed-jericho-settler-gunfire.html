Palestinian Woman, 72, Killed in Settler Gunfire Near Jericho, Health Ministry Says

A 72-year-old Palestinian woman was shot dead and 10 other people were wounded by gunfire from Israeli settlers in the village of Ad-Duyuk al-Tahta, west of Jericho, on Monday evening, the Palestinian health ministry said.

The health ministry named the woman as Fadha Wadha. Doctors at Jericho Government Hospital said she was shot in the head. Witnesses said she was standing at the doorway of her home with relatives when she was hit, and had not been involved in a nearby car-ramming incident. A hospital director had earlier given her age as 60; the ministry later confirmed she was 72.

The Israeli military said the shooting followed an attack on the outskirts of Jericho in which Palestinians drove a car into an Israeli vehicle, then got out with weapons and assaulted its occupants, leaving two Israelis lightly hurt. It said rocks were then thrown at the Israelis, who opened fire. The military did not comment on the woman's death itself and said it was still examining what happened.

Journalists who visited the scene on Tuesday morning found at least five rifle rounds still lying on the ground. The violence comes amid heightened tension around Jericho since settlers moved into Palestinian homes in the area in early November, preventing some residents from reaching their properties. The Israeli human rights group B'Tselem says there has been a prolonged campaign of settler attacks, intimidation and land seizures in the village, with troops now stationed nearby.
