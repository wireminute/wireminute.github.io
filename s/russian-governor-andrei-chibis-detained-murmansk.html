Russian Governor Detained in Rare Corruption Case Against Regional Leader

Russia has detained the governor of its northern Murmansk region on suspicion of embezzlement, the Interior Ministry said on Monday, in the first criminal case against a regional leader in five years.

Andrei Chibis, 47, was detained by Interior Ministry officers as he arrived in Moscow on a visit on Monday, according to the ministry and Russian media. Ministry spokeswoman Irina Volk said he was suspected of involvement in a group case involving large-scale fraud and theft of public funds.

Investigators say more than 39 million roubles, about $478,000, in budget money was taken from the regional construction ministry. They are also examining the alleged misuse of more than one billion roubles, about $12.2 million, set aside to rebuild a highway between the Arctic towns of Apatity and Kirovsk, about 15 kilometres apart. The case also covers alleged illegal currency transactions.

Investigators say the group was formed in 2022 and channelled regional funds overseas, with some of the money going to Kyrgyzstan, according to the reports. A court in Moscow is due to decide on Wednesday whether Chibis stays in custody. Chibis has not commented publicly, and no plea has been reported.

Chibis served as a deputy construction minister, then took charge of Murmansk in 2019. The region, inside the Arctic Circle, is home to Russia's Northern Fleet. He is under sanctions from Britain and the European Union over claims he played a part in taking Ukrainian children into Russia and placing them with Russian families.

He is the first regional governor detained since Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, and the first in five years. Several former governors have faced criminal cases in recent years, but sitting regional leaders have rarely been targeted.
