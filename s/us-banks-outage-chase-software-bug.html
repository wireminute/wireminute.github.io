United Airlines Grounds Departures as Chase Bug Hits US Banks in Day of Outages

United Airlines suffered a widespread systems outage on Monday that delayed departing flights across the United States, hours after a software bug at JPMorgan Chase slowed banking for customers at major American lenders, in the fourth big technology failure in the country in five days.

United Airlines suffered a widespread systems outage on Monday that delayed departing flights across the United States, hours after a software bug at JPMorgan Chase slowed banking for customers at major American lenders. The airline said the problem affected flights scheduled to depart, while planes already in the air and flights operated by United Express were unaffected. United said on Monday evening that the issue had been resolved. A memo from the union representing United pilots pointed to a fault in the airline's weight and balance calculation system, and the airline said it had found no sign of a cyberattack.

By Monday night, about 14 percent of United's flights were running late, though it was not clear how many of those delays stemmed from the outage. The airline's failure came on the same day thousands of customers at Chase, Wells Fargo, Bank of America, U.S. Bank, Truist and Capital One reported trouble logging in, viewing balances and completing transfers. Reports on the outage-tracking site Downdetector for Chase began at 9:53 a.m. Eastern Time and rose to nearly 5,000 during the afternoon before easing to about 1,450 by early evening.

JPMorgan Chase said a software bug in computer hardware it operates itself was slowing service for some customers, and that there was no evidence the problem was cyber-related. The bank said it was working on a fix and aimed to have service restored before the day was out, and that account access and funds remained available to customers. It said other lenders appeared to be affected only when JPMorgan was a party to a transaction with them, and that the disruption was not a systemic issue. Zelle said its network was running normally and pointed to Chase as the starting point of the delays.

The two outages were the third and fourth major American technology failures in five days. A global Microsoft outage on Thursday disrupted Outlook, Teams and other services, and a fault in air traffic control equipment on Friday grounded all departing flights across the United States for several hours, stranding thousands of travellers. Each organisation blamed a technical fault and said there was no evidence of a cyberattack. The central bank's main payment networks, Fedwire, FedACH and FedNow, operated normally through Monday's banking disruption, and no bank reported that customers had lost money.

Key points

  • United Airlines suffered a widespread outage on Monday that delayed departing flights; the airline said the problem was fixed on Monday evening.
  • A software bug at JPMorgan Chase slowed transactions at major US banks, with outage reports for Chase peaking near 5,000 on Downdetector.
  • It was the fourth major US technology failure in five days, after Microsoft and the Friday air traffic ground stop, and none was blamed on a cyberattack.
  • Federal payment systems ran normally and no bank reported customers losing money.
