Klopp Says FIFA Is Worse Under Infantino Than Blatter and Calls for Change

Germany coach Jurgen Klopp has said football's governing body has become worse under Gianni Infantino than it was under Sepp Blatter, and has called for a change of leadership before FIFA's presidential election in March 2027.

Speaking in Berlin on Sunday, a day before Germany's Nations League match against the Netherlands, Klopp said he had followed football for many years and believed most people now agreed that a change would make a lot of sense. "I didn't think things could get worse after Sepp Blatter, but someone has managed to do just that," he told a news conference. He added that the next person to lead FIFA could surely do a better job.

Klopp said the next president should be someone who truly cares about the future of football and is not driven by financial interests, and that such a person becoming president would be a significant improvement. He also questioned whether former Arsenal manager Arsene Wenger, who has ruled himself out of the race, would be the right choice, saying a great coach is not automatically suited to leading world football's governing body.

Blatter led FIFA from 1998 until he resigned in 2015 amid a corruption scandal, and was later cleared in two trials but banned from football by FIFA's ethics committee. Infantino, also Swiss, succeeded him in 2016 and is seeking re-election in March 2027. He has faced heavy criticism, including for an unsuccessful attempt in July to bring private investors into a new FIFA commercial entity holding rights linked to competitions including the World Cup, and for creating the FIFA Peace Prize last year and giving its first award to United States President Donald Trump.

Only former Swiss footballer Ramon Vega and Japan's Kozo Tashima have announced they will stand against Infantino, and candidates must register by 18 November with the backing of five member associations. The election will be held on 18 March 2027 at the FIFA Congress in Rabat, Morocco. European football's governing body is also reported to be searching for a candidate to challenge Infantino.

Key points

  • Germany coach Jurgen Klopp says FIFA has become worse under Gianni Infantino than under Sepp Blatter, and that a change of president would make a lot of sense.
  • Klopp says the next FIFA president should care deeply about football's future and not be driven by financial interests.
  • Infantino, who took over in 2016, has been criticised over a failed plan to sell part of the World Cup's commercial rights and over the FIFA Peace Prize awarded to Donald Trump.
  • The FIFA presidential election is set for 18 March 2027 in Rabat, with only Ramon Vega and Kozo Tashima so far declared against Infantino and candidates due to register by 18 November.
