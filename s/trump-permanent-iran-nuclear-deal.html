Trump Vows a Permanent Iran Deal That No Future President Can Undo

US President Donald Trump said on Saturday that he intends to secure a permanent agreement with Iran that would prevent Tehran from ever obtaining a nuclear weapon, and that no future American president should be able to reverse it.

Speaking in a telephone interview, Trump said any deal must outlast his own time in office. "I'm going to make a deal that is going to be permanent. I'm not going to let somebody allow them to have a nuclear weapon," he said. He added that the agreement had to hold even if an incompetent president took office in four, 15 or 20 years.

Trump said his approach was shaped by what he described as Iran's repeated failure to honour agreements. "We make a deal, and then they break the deal the next day," he said, and accused Tehran of violating a memorandum of understanding a day after it was signed. He said Iran wanted an agreement but had not offered terms he could accept, and accused its negotiators of misleading the international community for decades.

The president also played down the idea that Washington is pursuing regime change in Tehran, saying he had never been particularly interested in that goal and describing Iran's current leadership as more reasonable than its predecessors, though difficult to negotiate with. He said his administration had already dealt with successive groups of Iranian leaders. He also spoke about his recent trilateral meeting with the leaders of China and Russia, saying the three had agreed to reduce the risks of nuclear war.

Iran's foreign minister said messages were still being exchanged with Washington through mediators, and that Tehran was seeking a good, honourable and dignified agreement that would ensure there was no further war. He said Iran's red lines included keeping its highly enriched uranium inside the country, though he believed a fair solution was still possible, and that China and Russia opposed any change in Tehran's nuclear doctrine. Iran's deputy foreign minister separately called the American economic blockade a hostile act, said Tehran would take all necessary measures to break it, and said nuclear talks had not yet begun and Iran's nuclear material would not be exported.

Trump's envoys met senior Qatari officials in Berlin on Thursday to discuss mediation between the two sides. The international nuclear watchdog has estimated that Iran held about 441 kilograms of uranium enriched to 60 percent when its nuclear sites were attacked in June 2025.

Key points

  • Trump said he intends to secure a permanent deal with Iran that would stop it ever obtaining a nuclear weapon, binding on future presidents.
  • He accused Iran of breaking agreements, including violating a memorandum of understanding a day after signing it.
  • He played down regime change, calling Iran's current leadership more reasonable but difficult to negotiate with.
  • Iran's foreign minister said Tehran wants a dignified agreement that ensures no further war, but will keep its enriched uranium inside the country.
