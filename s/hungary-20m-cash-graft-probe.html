Nearly $20m in Cash Found Buried in Forests in Hungary Graft Probe

Hungarian investigators have recovered almost $20 million in cash — some of it buried in forests, the rest stashed in farm buildings, warehouses and a secluded safe — as part of a bribery inquiry into a former development minister.

Hungary’s asset-recovery office said late on Friday that searches had turned up large amounts of money in forests, farm buildings, warehouses and a home, as well as inside a high-value safe kept in a secluded place. The haul came to 5.07 billion forints, worth about $15.33 million, together with €2.66 million and $1.26 million in cash.

Counting the money took a dozen workers from the State Treasury eight hours, using five banknote-counting machines, the finance minister said. The inquiry centres on Miklós Seszták, who served as development minister from 2014 to 2018 in the government of Viktor Orbán. Seszták has denied any wrongdoing.

The case is part of a wider anti-corruption drive by Prime Minister Péter Magyar, who defeated Orbán in an April election after 16 years in power and pledged to rebuild ties with the European Union. Magyar has named the campaign “Operation Purgatory”, and a survey published earlier this month found that 70 percent of Hungarians considered it very important.

Two other former ministers from Orbán’s governments and the former central bank governor, György Matolcsy, are also under arrest over separate graft accusations. The investigation is continuing, and officials have not said whether further searches are planned.

The recovered money is now held by the state while prosecutors build their case, and the authorities say the sums seized so far make it one of the largest cash recoveries in a Hungarian corruption inquiry.

Key points

  • Investigators recovered nearly $20 million in cash — forints, euros and dollars — hidden in forests, farm buildings, warehouses and a secluded safe.
  • The money was seized during a bribery inquiry into former development minister Miklós Seszták, who denies wrongdoing.
  • Counting the haul took 12 treasury workers eight hours, using five banknote-counting machines.
  • The case is part of Prime Minister Péter Magyar’s “Operation Purgatory” anti-graft drive after he defeated Viktor Orbán in April.
