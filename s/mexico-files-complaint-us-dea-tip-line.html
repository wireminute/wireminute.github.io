Mexico Files Formal Complaint with US over DEA Tip Line on Alleged Drug Traffickers

Mexico has filed a formal complaint with the United States over a Drug Enforcement Administration tip line seeking information on suspected drug traffickers and allegedly corrupt Mexican officials, President Claudia Sheinbaum has said, in a fresh strain on relations between the two neighbours.

Sheinbaum announced the complaint on Friday at her morning news conference. Foreign Minister Juan Ramon de la Fuente contacted the US ambassador to Mexico to lodge it formally, she said, adding that the ambassador told him he would look into the matter.

The DEA announced the tip line this week, seeking information on alleged members of criminal organisations, corrupt officials and others collaborating with drug traffickers in Sinaloa, the northwestern Mexican state that is the stronghold of the Sinaloa Cartel.

Sheinbaum had already criticised the tip line publicly a day earlier, accusing US officials of interfering in Mexico's affairs. The complaint touches a particularly sensitive issue in bilateral relations: Mexico guards its sovereignty closely, and US law-enforcement steps on Mexican soil are a recurring point of friction.

The row comes as Washington presses Mexico hard over fentanyl trafficking and cartel violence, and as the two countries argue over who bears responsibility for a drug trade that runs across their shared border. Mexico has insisted that cooperation must run on equal terms and has pushed back against unilateral US action inside its territory.

Sinaloa has been at the centre of the dispute. The northwestern state is the stronghold of the Sinaloa Cartel, one of the groups Washington blames most for the flow of fentanyl into the United States, and the DEA tip line was aimed squarely at information about its members and the officials accused of working with them.

There was no immediate public response from Washington to the complaint beyond the ambassador's assurance that he would look into it. The complaint is a diplomatic protest rather than a legal step, and talks between the two governments on security and trade continue.

Key points

  • Mexico has filed a formal complaint with the United States over a DEA tip line seeking information on suspected drug traffickers and allegedly corrupt officials.
  • President Claudia Sheinbaum announced the move on Friday; Foreign Minister Juan Ramon de la Fuente lodged it with the US ambassador, who said he would look into it.
  • The DEA launched the tip line this week, asking for information on cartel members, corrupt officials and their collaborators in Sinaloa, stronghold of the Sinaloa Cartel.
