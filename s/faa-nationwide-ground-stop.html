Nationwide Ground Stop Over as US Flights Start Moving Again

The nationwide ground stop that grounded every departing flight in the United States is over, with the aviation regulator's live board showing no nationwide stop late on Friday night and departures moving again — though delays are expected to linger into the weekend.

The order landed just before 4 p.m. Eastern, when the aviation regulator told operators that every airport in the country was ground stopped and departing traffic should stay on the ground until further notice. The advisory ran from 3:54 p.m. to 11:30 p.m. Eastern, with no firm time given for flights to resume. Aircraft already in the air were allowed to continue to their destinations, and flights arriving from overseas were handled one by one.

By early evening the picture began to change, and late on Friday night the regulator's live status board listed no nationwide stop at all. The only ground stop shown was for Montreal, in Canada, with ground delays at Boston, New York's LaGuardia, San Diego and San Francisco, blamed on wind and airport volume. The homeland security secretary had warned earlier that recovery would take some time, and airlines said schedules would be disrupted for the rest of the day.

The scale of the disruption was vast. Flight-tracking data counted more than 1,100 flights cancelled and nearly 10,000 delayed by 5:40 p.m. Eastern, after an earlier count of more than 9,000 delayed and 440 cancelled by 5 p.m. The regulator had first halted traffic in Chicago, a busy connecting hub, before widening the order nationwide. With aircraft and crews out of position, delays are likely to run into the weekend.

What failed is still not clear. The transport secretary blamed a communications problem affecting every regional air traffic control centre, and the regulator's website described it as an equipment failure. One report pointed to a telecommunications network overseen by a defence contractor, and another, citing an unnamed source, suggested a software upgrade may have played a part — neither confirmed by the regulator. Officials said there was no sign so far of a hostile attack. It was the first nationwide stop since a pilot-notification failure in January 2023, and only the second such nationwide halt since the September 11 attacks.

Key points

  • The nationwide ground stop is over: the regulator's live board, checked late Friday night, lists no nationwide stop — only a ground stop at Montreal and delays at Boston, LaGuardia, San Diego and San Francisco.
  • More than 1,100 flights were cancelled and nearly 10,000 delayed by 5:40 p.m. Eastern, according to flight-tracking data.
  • The cause is still unknown: a communications failure hit every regional air traffic control centre; officials say there is no sign so far of a hostile attack.
  • The first nationwide stop since a pilot-notification failure in January 2023 — and only the second such halt since the September 11 attacks.
