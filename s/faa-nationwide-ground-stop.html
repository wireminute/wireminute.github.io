Flight Chaos Spills Into Second Day as 3,700 More US Flights Are Cancelled

More than 3,700 flights were cancelled across the United States on Saturday and 2,000 more delayed, as airlines struggled to recover from the longest nationwide shutdown of flights in 25 years and tens of thousands of passengers remained stranded.

Airlines cancelled more than 3,700 flights across the United States on Saturday and delayed over 2,000 more, as the recovery from Friday's nationwide shutdown dragged into a second day and tens of thousands of passengers remained stuck at airports. Two of the biggest carriers imposed their own ground stops to ease the pressure — one at Chicago's O'Hare airport and the other at New York's LaGuardia — as gates filled with aircraft that had nowhere to go.

Friday had already been one of the worst days in American aviation history. The regulator halted every departing flight nationwide for almost five hours, from 3:54 p.m. to 8:15 p.m. Eastern, forcing airlines to cancel close to 10,000 flights and hold back more than 7,000 others. It was the first nationwide order since January 2023 and the longest shutdown of flights since the September 11 attacks 25 years ago. More than 4,000 aircraft were already in the air when the order landed; they were allowed to continue to their destinations.

Officials say the failure began at 3:36 p.m. Eastern, when air traffic control centres started seeing unstable radar and communications. Ageing routers and switches — some of them decades old — were overwhelmed when new technology and software patches were introduced into the old system, returning readings controllers could no longer trust. The transport secretary has called on Congress to fund a 50-billion-dollar overhaul of the system, and says he expects flights to be back to normal before the busy Thanksgiving travel period.

Investigators have ruled out foul play. The homeland security secretary said there was no evidence of a cyberattack, and the failure was unrelated to a separate outage at a large software company the same day. Because the regulator caused the delays, airlines are not required to pay compensation to stranded passengers.

The disruption is expected to linger. Aircraft and crews remain out of position across the country, and airlines have warned that cancellations will continue as schedules are rebuilt. About 1.5 million passengers were affected on Friday alone, and travellers have been urged to check their flight status before heading to the airport.

Key points

  • More than 3,700 US flights were cancelled on Saturday and 2,000 more delayed, as the recovery from Friday's nationwide ground stop dragged into a second day.
  • Friday's stop lasted almost five hours, with nearly 10,000 flights cancelled and 7,000 delayed — one of the worst travel days in American aviation history.
  • Two major carriers imposed their own ground stops at Chicago O'Hare and New York LaGuardia to handle congestion and gate pressure.
  • Officials blame ageing routers overwhelmed by new technology; there is no evidence of a cyberattack, and airlines are not required to compensate stranded passengers.
