US Air Travel 'Back at Full Operations' as Officials Name Cause of Historic Ground Stop

US air travel is back at full operations after the longest countrywide halt to departures since the 2001 attacks, with officials blaming a contractor's copper-wire replacement work for the failure and promising a full recovery by Sunday.

The transport secretary said on Saturday that the failure began when a contractor was replacing old copper wiring with fibre-optic lines at a remote air traffic facility. The new router that was installed started flooding other routers with data, knocking out communications and radar feeds across the system. Officials are investigating whether the contractor did the work badly or installed a faulty router. 'We are back at full operations,' the transport secretary said, adding that the work of swapping copper for fibre has been going on for decades.

Friday had already been one of the worst days in American aviation history. The regulator halted every departing flight nationwide for almost five hours, from 3:54 p.m. to 8:15 p.m. Eastern, after problems first appeared at 3:36 p.m. About 9,000 commercial flights were cancelled along with roughly 3,000 private jets, and more than 24,500 flights were delayed. More than 3,500 aircraft were already in the air when the order landed; they were allowed to continue to their destinations.

The disruption spilled into a second day. More than 4,600 flights were cancelled on Saturday by late morning, and officials said 4,000 to 5,000 of the 41,000 flights scheduled for the day would probably not fly. Two of the biggest carriers imposed their own ground stops at Chicago's O'Hare airport and New York's LaGuardia to ease congestion as gates filled with aircraft that had nowhere to go. Airlines warned that cancellations would continue while aircraft and crews were moved back into place.

Officials say the breakdown came from new technology being patched into a system built on decades-old analogue equipment. The regulator has already replaced 68 percent of its legacy copper telecom network with high-speed fibre, and the transport secretary has called on Congress to fund a 50-billion-dollar overhaul, saying the old kit cannot cope with modern demands. The aviation chief said there was no evidence the failure was caused by artificial intelligence or anything nefarious, and the homeland security secretary said there was no indication of a cyberattack. European countries suspended US-bound flights while the stop was in force. Because the regulator caused the delays, airlines are not required to pay compensation to stranded passengers.

Travellers have been urged to check their flight status before heading to the airport, with officials expecting schedules to be fully back to normal by Sunday.

Key points

  • US flights are back at full operations after Friday's nationwide ground stop, the longest countrywide halt since the 2001 attacks, with full recovery expected by Sunday.
  • Transport Secretary Sean Duffy blamed work by contractor L3Harris replacing copper wire with fibre at a remote air traffic facility, which flooded the system with bad data.
  • About 9,000 commercial flights were cancelled on Friday and more than 24,500 delayed; over 4,600 more were cancelled on Saturday.
  • Officials say there is no evidence of a cyberattack or anything nefarious; the failure came from new technology overloading decades-old equipment.
