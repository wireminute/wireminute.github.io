Iran Says Fifth Tanker in 24 Hours Hit a Mine in Hormuz as It Claims US Drone Downed

Iran's navy said on Saturday that a second tanker had caught fire that morning after striking a mine in the eastern Strait of Hormuz, the fifth vessel hit in 24 hours by Tehran's count, as the Revolutionary Guards claimed they had shot down an American drone over a nearby island.

The navy said the tanker had entered a route Tehran considers unauthorised and was forced to stop after the explosion and fire. It gave no name, flag or origin for the ship and said nothing about its crew. Earlier on Saturday, the Guards said two oil tankers, one of them a supertanker, had exploded and caught fire after striking mines in the strait. None of the accounts has been independently confirmed, and no shipowner, flag state or tracking service has verified them.

The Guards also claimed their forces had brought down an American MQ-1 drone over Qeshm Island, in the strait, describing it as the 17th such drone they had destroyed. The United States has not commented on the claim. The Guards said on Friday that they had targeted an American early-warning radar system in Bahrain; Washington said its forces had shot down two Iranian drones headed for the island, with no damage or casualties.

The Saudi-led coalition in Yemen said it had intercepted two drones launched by the Houthis towards Riyadh and southern Saudi regions, while the Houthis said they had fired two ballistic missiles at Aden, the Yemeni government's interim capital. The Houthis have also claimed missile attacks on Riyadh airport and a pumping station on the pipeline that carries Saudi oil to the Red Sea; Saudi Arabia has not confirmed those claims.

The incidents are the latest in a wave of reported attacks on shipping in the waterway. Iranian media said four oil tankers were hit by explosions earlier in the week, two on Wednesday and two more on Thursday afternoon, and data from the Washington Institute for Near East Policy counted at least 10 ships hit in the first 10 days of November, after 37 attacks in October. The United Nations said at least 25 seafarers had been killed in and around the strait since the war with the United States began in February. The strait is one of the world's most important routes for oil tankers, and Tehran has insisted since the war began that vessels must obtain its authorisation to cross, attacking ships that do not comply. Shipping through the waterway has fallen to roughly 65 per cent below its pre-war level, according to the UK Maritime Trade Operations office.

  • The Revolutionary Guards claim they shot down an American MQ-1 drone over Qeshm Island; the claim has not been independently verified.
