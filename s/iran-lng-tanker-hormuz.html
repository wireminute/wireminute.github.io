Gas Tanker Explodes After Striking Mine in Strait of Hormuz, Iran Says

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said on Friday that a gas tanker struck a naval mine and exploded in the Strait of Hormuz, in the latest unverified claim of an attack on shipping in the waterway.

The Guards' navy said the vessel was trying to cross a southern passage east of the strait that Iran considers unauthorised and dangerous when it hit the mine and exploded. It gave no name, flag or origin for the ship, and said nothing about its crew. Reports differed on the cargo: Iranian state media and some wires described it as liquefied natural gas, while others called it liquefied petroleum gas.

No outside source had confirmed the incident, and the US military had said nothing about it. The claim came a day after Iranian media reported explosions on four oil tankers in the same waters, and follows at least 10 ships hit in the first 10 days of November, after 37 attacks in October, according to data from the Washington Institute for Near East Policy.

Key points

  • Iran's Revolutionary Guards say a gas tanker struck a mine and exploded in the eastern Strait of Hormuz while using a route Tehran calls unauthorised.
  • The vessel has not been named, and no flag, origin or crew details were given; the claim has not been independently verified.
  • The incident follows reported explosions on four oil tankers on Thursday and at least 10 ships hit in the first 10 days of November.
