COP31 Deal in Doubt as Approval Session Slips and EU Warns Summit Could End With No Agreement

The COP31 climate summit's last-minute deal was in doubt on Sunday after the session called to approve it slipped by hours, with European officials warning there was a very real possibility the talks in Antalya would end with no agreement at all.

The closing plenary that was due to begin at 06:00 GMT on Sunday had still not opened by late morning, and organisers pushed it back to 11:00 local time in Antalya, 13:00 in Pakistan, as objections to the text grew. The presidency had given delegations until 03:00 GMT to lodge objections, but several governments were reported to be unhappy with the new wording, and under the talks' rules a single formal objection can block a deal.

An EU official described the latest text as a "backwards step" and said there was a "very real possibility" COP31 would end with no deal. "I am more pessimistic today than I was yesterday," the official said. Antigua and Barbuda's environment minister, Michael Joseph, said he was "very disappointed" with the text and could not honestly say he was comfortable with it passing. Members of the coalition pressing hardest for fossil-fuel language were also reported to be unhappy with the new text.

The fight remains over fossil fuels. The first draft, drawn up by co-host Australia, called for a faster phase-out of coal, oil and gas. Thursday's slimmer version removed every mention of the fuels after objections from Saudi Arabia and other big oil and gas producing states. More than 130 countries want phase-out language restored. The latest text again fails to name fossil fuels, but it points back to the 2023 global deal to move the world away from them.

The text has now been published officially as a 94-paragraph package. It keeps the goal of raising electricity to 35 percent of final energy use by 2035, saying that goal helps the Paris temperature target most when the power is clean. A hard target for carbon capture has been dropped in favour of an aspiration to reach gigatonne scale by 2035. The 120-billion-dollar adaptation finance figure is still absent, and the draft expresses deep concern that warming could breach the 1.5-degree goal, the first time a COP text has acknowledged the target will be missed.

On Saturday workers packed up electronics in the pavilions while corridors that had bustled with tens of thousands of participants stood largely empty. The United States, one of the world's biggest polluters, did not take part directly in this year's negotiations. Ethiopia was confirmed during the session as host of the next summit, COP32, in Addis Ababa in November 2027. The story is still developing.

Key points

  • The approval session for COP31's last-minute deal slipped from 06:00 GMT to 11:00 local time on Sunday as objections grew, with the outcome still undecided.
  • An EU official called the text a "backwards step" and warned of a "very real possibility" the summit ends with no deal; small island states said they were very disappointed.
  • The text still does not name fossil fuels, but points back to the 2023 deal to move away from them; more than 130 countries want phase-out language restored.
  • The draft keeps the 35 percent electrification goal for 2035 and drops a hard carbon-capture target, while the 120-billion-dollar adaptation finance figure remains absent.
