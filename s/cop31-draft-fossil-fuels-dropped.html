COP31 Heads for No Deal as Overtime Talks Stall With No New Text

The COP31 climate summit may close without any agreement, the European Union's climate chief warned on Saturday, as negotiations in Antalya dragged deep into overtime, still without a fresh draft text and with tempers fraying among nearly 200 countries.

Two weeks of talks in Antalya, Turkiye, were meant to wrap up on Friday, but the closing session was suspended late that night after approving only technical matters, and negotiations resumed on Saturday. A third draft had been expected on Saturday morning, yet by the afternoon no new text had appeared, and hopes of major changes were fading. Kurum told delegates the summit was near the end but not there yet.

Hoekstra said the summit would end with either a strong agreement or none at all. Kurum held talks with officials from the European Union, Australia and Saudi Arabia to try to end the impasse, while conceding an agreement might still slip away. The last major breakdown in United Nations climate talks came in Copenhagen in 2009.

The fight is over fossil fuels. Monday's first draft, drawn up by co-host Australia, called for a faster phase-out of coal, oil and gas. Thursday's slimmer version removed every mention of the fuels after objections from Saudi Arabia and other big oil and gas producing states. The European Union warned the summit risked becoming a "fossil summit", and twelve nations spanning Europe and the developing world, including France, Panama and the Solomon Islands, sent a joint letter on Friday demanding the agreement spell out a clear move away from fossil fuels.

What the draft does instead is lean on carbon capture as a central pillar of the energy transition, and it calls for electricity to reach 35 percent of final energy use by 2035, without saying that electricity must come from renewables. The 120-billion-dollar figure for adaptation finance has also disappeared, replaced by a vague commitment to discuss tripling adaptation funds by 2035, with no baseline or delivery plan.

The draft does express deep concern that warming could breach the 1.5-degree goal, after a year of stark reminders from heatwaves in Europe to devastating floods in Nepal. During the late Friday session delegates passed a banner reading "failure", and a demonstrator interrupted Kurum, shouting "you are killing the future". Countries agreed in Dubai two years ago to move away from fossil fuels, but the language has been fought over at every summit since. Ethiopia was confirmed during the session as host of the next summit, COP32, in Addis Ababa in 2027.

Key points

  • COP31 ran deep into overtime on Saturday with no fresh draft text and no agreement, and the EU said it would accept only a strong deal.
  • The latest draft makes no mention of fossil fuels after objections from Saudi Arabia and other big oil and gas producing states; twelve nations demanded the move-away language be restored.
  • The text leans on carbon capture instead, drops the 120-billion-dollar adaptation finance figure, and weakens the 35 percent electrification target by removing the renewables requirement.
  • Ethiopia was confirmed as host of COP32 in Addis Ababa in 2027, while campaigners rejected the draft as it stands.
