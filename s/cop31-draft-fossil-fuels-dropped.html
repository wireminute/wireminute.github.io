COP31 Runs Into Overtime as Fossil Fuel Deadlock Blocks a Deal

The COP31 climate summit has run past its Friday deadline with no deal, as nearly 200 countries stayed deadlocked over a draft agreement that makes no mention of fossil fuels, forcing negotiators into overtime talks in Antalya.

The two-week talks in Antalya, Turkiye, were due to end on Friday, but a late closing session approved only technical matters, not the wider political deal, and some decisions were put off to next year. A third draft is expected on Saturday morning after overnight talks. "We are close to the finish line, but we are not there yet," COP31 President Murat Kurum told delegates.

The fight is over fossil fuels. Monday's first draft, drawn up by co-host Australia, called for a faster phase-out of coal, oil and gas. Thursday's slimmer version removed every mention of the fuels after Saudi Arabia and other major oil and gas producers objected. The European Union warned the summit risked becoming a "fossil summit", and an EU official called the latest draft "not acceptable and not ambitious enough".

What the draft does instead is lean on carbon capture, utilisation and storage as a central pillar of the energy transition, with bracketed targets for storing hundreds of millions of tonnes of carbon a year by 2035. It calls for electricity to reach 35 percent of final energy use by 2035, but no longer says that electricity must come from renewables, leaving the door open for fossil-fuelled power to count. The 120-billion-dollar figure for adaptation finance has also disappeared, replaced by a vague commitment to discuss tripling adaptation funds by 2035, with no baseline or delivery plan.

The draft does express deep concern that warming could breach the 1.5-degree goal, after a year of stark reminders from heatwaves in Europe to devastating floods in Nepal.

During the late session delegates passed a banner reading "failure", and a demonstrator interrupted Kurum, shouting "you are killing the future". Tasneem Essop of Climate Action Network International said her network was "unambiguously" rejecting what is on the table, warning of "the most harmful and dangerous options of any COP so far". Brazil's climate secretary Aloisio Lopes Pereira de Melo said simply: "At this point, no one is happy."

Countries agreed in Dubai two years ago to move away from fossil fuels, but the language has been fought over at every summit since, and last year's meeting in Brazil ended with no mention of the fuels. Australian Climate Minister Chris Bowen, who is steering the negotiations, said: "We will be consulting and negotiating until it is very clear to us that we have a consensus."

Key points

  • COP31 ran past its Friday deadline with no deal; a third draft is expected on Saturday morning after overnight talks in Antalya.
  • The latest draft makes no mention of fossil fuels after objections from Saudi Arabia and other major oil and gas producers; the EU called it not acceptable and not ambitious enough.
  • The text leans on carbon capture instead, drops the 120-billion-dollar adaptation finance figure, and weakens the 35 percent electrification target by removing the renewables requirement.
  • Campaigners rejected the draft as it stands, with delegates passing a banner reading "failure" during Friday's late session.
