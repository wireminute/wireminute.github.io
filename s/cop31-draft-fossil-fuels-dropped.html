EU Formally Opposes COP31 Draft in Plenary as Deal Hangs in Balance

The European Union formally opposed the COP31 climate deal in the closing plenary on Sunday, deepening the deadlock in Antalya, as the summit ran into a second day of overtime with no agreement in sight.

The closing plenary finally opened on Sunday, hours late, and the European Union used it to formally oppose the draft deal. Darragh O'Brien, Ireland's climate minister, speaking for the EU, told delegates: 'We wish to formally express our opposition to the Antalya Pact because it is not appropriate at this time. We express our deep disappointment.' He added that the EU would not support a decision that does not even mention fossil fuels.

An EU delegation spokesman put it more bluntly: 'We will not endorse a cover decision or an Antalya pact that does not even mention fossil fuels, which is the key driver of climate change... the European Union cannot accept it.' Belgium's climate minister, Jean-Luc Crucke, called the text unacceptable, saying 'there has to be courage to say no.' COP31 President Murat Kurum urged consensus, saying that while no one obtained everything they wanted, 'everyone can find a place for themselves within this text.'

The fight remains over fossil fuels. The first draft, drawn up by co-host Australia, called for a faster phase-out of coal, oil and gas. Thursday's slimmer version removed every mention of the fuels after objections from Saudi Arabia and other big oil and gas producing states. More than 130 countries want phase-out language restored. The latest text again fails to name fossil fuels, but points back to the 2023 deal to move away from them.

The text is now published officially as a 94-paragraph package. It keeps the goal of raising electricity to 35 percent of final energy use by 2035, saying it helps the Paris temperature target most when the power is clean. A hard target for carbon capture has been dropped in favour of an aspiration to reach gigatonne scale by 2035. The 120-billion-dollar adaptation finance figure is still absent, and the draft expresses deep concern that warming could breach the 1.5-degree goal, the first time a COP text has acknowledged the target will be missed.

Under the talks' rules a single formal objection can block a deal, and with no consensus in sight, negotiating teams due to fly home on Sunday were moving their flights to Monday. If the deadlock holds, the summit could be suspended and reconvened next year. Ethiopia was confirmed during the session as host of the next summit, COP32, in Addis Ababa in November 2027. The story is still developing.

Key points

  • The EU formally opposed the Antalya Pact in the closing plenary on Sunday, saying it cannot accept a deal that does not mention fossil fuels.
  • The plenary opened hours late after the approval session slipped; COP31 President Murat Kurum still urged countries to find consensus in the text.
  • The 94-paragraph draft keeps the 35 percent electrification goal for 2035 but drops the 120-billion-dollar adaptation finance figure and any fossil-fuel phase-out language.
  • No deal has been adopted; if consensus fails the talks could be suspended and reconvened next year, with negotiating teams already moving flights to Monday.
