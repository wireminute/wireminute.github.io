COP31 Presidency Tables Last-Minute Deal as Climate Talks Enter Second Day of Overtime

The COP31 climate summit entered a second day of overtime on Sunday as its presidency tabled a last-minute draft deal, telling nearly 200 countries it had a text they could all agree on, even though it still does not name fossil fuels.

Two weeks of talks in Antalya, Turkiye, were due to end on Friday, but the closing session was suspended late that night after approving only technical matters, and negotiations ran through Saturday without a new text. Early on Sunday, COP31 President Murat Kurum, who is also Turkiye's environment minister, told a late-night plenary session that the presidency had put forward a text all parties could agree on, and said the conference would reconvene on Sunday morning to approve it.

The new text was not immediately made public, and it was unclear whether delegations had received the wording overnight, leaving them only hours to submit objections. Delegations had until 03:00 GMT on Sunday to raise objections, with a session scheduled for 06:00 GMT to begin approving the deal. The presidency had convened hurried meetings between opposing camps, bringing together the European Union and Saudi Arabia, deeply divided over the future of fossil fuels, in a bid to bridge the gap.

The fight remains over fossil fuels. Monday's first draft, drawn up by co-host Australia, called for a faster phase-out of coal, oil and gas. Thursday's slimmer version removed every mention of the fuels after objections from Saudi Arabia and other big oil and gas producing states. More than 130 countries want phase-out language restored. The latest text again fails to name fossil fuels, but it points back to the 2023 global deal to move the world away from them.

What the draft does instead is lean on carbon capture as a central pillar of the energy transition, and it calls for electricity to reach 35 percent of final energy use by 2035, without saying that electricity must come from renewables. The 120-billion-dollar figure for adaptation finance has also disappeared, replaced by a vague commitment to discuss tripling adaptation funds by 2035, with no baseline or delivery plan. The draft expresses deep concern that warming could breach the 1.5-degree goal.

The venue itself told the story of a summit running on empty: on Saturday workers packed up electronics in the pavilions while corridors that had bustled with tens of thousands of participants stood largely empty. The United States, one of the world's biggest polluters, did not take part directly in this year's negotiations. Ethiopia was confirmed during the session as host of the next summit, COP32, in Addis Ababa in November 2027. The story is still developing.

Key points

  • COP31 entered a second day of overtime on Sunday after its presidency tabled a last-minute draft deal, with delegations given until 03:00 GMT to object and an approval session set for 06:00 GMT.
  • The new text still does not name fossil fuels, but it points back to the 2023 deal to move the world away from them; more than 130 countries want phase-out language restored.
  • The draft leans on carbon capture, keeps the 35 percent electrification goal for 2035 without a renewables requirement, and drops the 120-billion-dollar adaptation finance figure.
  • The EU and Saudi Arabia were brought together for compromise talks, while the venue emptied and campaigners warned progress had stalled; the US did not take part directly.
