Blair Says Britain Should Only Rejoin a Reformed EU That Puts Technology First

Former prime minister Tony Blair has said Britain should only seek to rejoin the European Union if the bloc reforms to become more competitive on technology, arguing that returning to the EU as it stands today would be a mistake.

The argument is set out in a report from Blair's think tank, the Tony Blair Institute for Global Change, titled Beyond Leave and Remain: A Path to Rejoining a Reformed Europe. It says Britain should set a clear, public objective of rejoining the EU within a decade, but only if the bloc itself changes. In a foreword, Blair writes that the red lines drawn in 2016 are not the same in 2026, and that Britain needs a new approach that treats its future relationship with Europe as a question of national interest, not simply of reversing Brexit.

Blair argues that the EU has moved in the wrong direction since Britain left, with its economy becoming less competitive and its rules holding back the artificial intelligence revolution rather than driving it. He says Britain should use any negotiation as a lever to push the bloc toward reform, and should be clear that it wants a formal relationship with the EU, but only with a Europe on a credible path to reform, a Europe of nation states, and a Europe capable of being a genuine global power. He adds that it would be wrong to seek a straight return to the single market and the customs union.

The report sets out practical steps for closer ties. It proposes a new UK-EU industrial council, rejoining the EU's SAFE defence fund, and joint work on AI, cyber security, defence and energy, including developing the North Sea as a strategic energy basin and rebuilding Europe's nuclear industry. It points to former European Central Bank chief Mario Draghi's report on EU competitiveness, and says a reformed Europe, with Britain part of it, could become a power able to stand alongside the United States and China. It also notes the EU inviting Canada to become its first associate member as a sign of new flexibility.

The intervention comes as the debate over Britain's future with Europe sharpens. On Friday, Prime Minister Andy Burnham agreed three deals in Brussels, covering food trade, emissions trading and a youth mobility scheme, and said the next year would be spent in informal talks about the longer-term relationship. Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband said all the options will be on the table, including rejoining, and Burnham is expected to set out his options in a ten-year plan next month.

Key points

  • Blair's institute says Britain should aim to rejoin the EU within a decade, but only a reformed EU, and should make that a clear public objective in the government's ten-year plan.
  • Blair argues the EU has grown less competitive since Brexit and has held back the AI revolution; Britain should use negotiation to push the bloc toward reform.
  • The report proposes a UK-EU industrial council, rejoining the SAFE defence fund, and joint work on AI, cyber security, defence, energy and nuclear power.
  • The warning comes as Burnham agrees three Brussels deals and Miliband says all options, including rejoining, will be on the table in talks over the next year.
