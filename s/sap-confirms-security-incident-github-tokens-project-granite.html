SAP Confirms Security Incident After Reports of Stolen GitHub Tokens and Altered Code

SAP has confirmed a security incident in its software development systems after reports that attackers stole employee access tokens and used them to copy and alter source code on GitHub, in an incident the company is tracking under the name Project Granite.

The German software maker said it had spotted suspicious activity and had moved at once to protect customers and keep its services secure. Nothing so far in its investigation pointed to unauthorised access to customer business data, it said.

The incident became public on Thursday through press reports citing an internal company memo and people inside SAP. The reports said attackers had obtained authentication tokens belonging to employees and used them to copy and change source code on GitHub, and that the attackers may have used AI systems to help. SAP has not confirmed the token theft, the code changes or the AI claim, and has not said which repositories were involved or whether any altered code reached software used by customers.

People inside the company told reporters that SAP had locked down its GitHub access, halted automated build and delivery work and rotated its code-signing keys, and that many development teams were affected. Employees were asked to check whether they needed to change their access credentials.

SAP, based in Walldorf, Germany, is one of the world's largest enterprise software makers, with about 110,000 employees and software that runs the finance, human resources and supply-chain systems of many large companies and public bodies. That makes any tampering in its development pipeline a wider supply-chain question for its customers. It is not the first time this year that code in SAP's supply chain has been tampered with: in April, official SAP software packages were compromised. The investigation is continuing.

Key points
