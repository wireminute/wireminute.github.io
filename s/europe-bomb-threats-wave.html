Bomb Threat Wave Sweeps Europe, Emptying Schools, Diverting Flights and Clearing Newsrooms

A wave of bomb threats sent mainly by email has forced evacuations at thousands of schools, diverted passenger flights and emptied newsrooms across Europe, even though every threat checked so far has turned out to be false.

Aviation was hit first. Between 2 and 6 November, more than 50 passenger flights received threats, including services run by Aer Lingus, LOT Polish Airlines and Aegean Airlines. Aircraft were diverted, grounded or searched, and fighter jets were scrambled in France and Hungary to escort threatened planes. An Aer Lingus flight from Dublin to Rome was escorted by French jets and put down in Normandy. A cruise ship was also threatened, and an airport outside Warsaw was briefly shut.

Schools became the next target. On Wednesday, the same threatening email reached about 6,300 schools in Italy, and about 3,800 more on Thursday. Hundreds of schools evacuated while police searched buildings and found nothing. Similar emails reached around 470 schools in Scotland and more than 300 in Northern Ireland, as well as schools in the Republic of Ireland, where police said the messages were likely hoaxes and classrooms stayed open. Newsrooms were hit too: staff were evacuated at RTE, Newstalk and the Irish Independent in Ireland, Nova in Bulgaria and Sky News in London.

Who is behind the wave is still unknown. Some messages were signed with the name of a Russian ultra-nationalist group, but investigators suspect the signatures may be false. Security firms found many of the emails were sent from legitimate webmail accounts that had been hijacked. The Irish government said the pattern looked like a coordinated disruption campaign, and the head of Wizz Air called for common European procedures to handle such hoaxes. France's president cautioned against blaming foreign powers before the evidence is in.

Italy has now changed how it responds. The prime minister said the messages were meant to spread fear rather than cause harm, and her government says public buildings should not be evacuated on the basis of a mass email alone without concrete evidence of danger. Police in Italy, Scotland, Northern Ireland and the Republic of Ireland are still investigating, and no explosive has been found in any of the incidents.

Key points

  • More than 50 flights across Europe faced bomb threats between 2 and 6 November; planes were diverted or searched and fighter jets scrambled to escort some.
  • About 6,300 Italian schools received the same threatening email on Wednesday and about 3,800 on Thursday; hundreds evacuated, and schools in Scotland, Northern Ireland and Ireland were also targeted.
  • Newsrooms including RTE, Newstalk, the Irish Independent, Nova and Sky News were evacuated after email threats.
  • No explosive has been found in any of the incidents; police across Europe treat the threats as hoaxes, and who sent them is still unknown.
