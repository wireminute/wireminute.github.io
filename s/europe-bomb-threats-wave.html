More Than 8,000 Hoax Bomb Threats Hit Europe in a Month

More than 8,000 fake bomb warnings have reached schools, airlines, newsrooms and other organisations across Europe in the past month, in what officials and security researchers now describe as one coordinated campaign.

The campaign began on 19 October, when an email claiming a bomb had been planted arrived at a primary school in Belfast. About 300 schools in Northern Ireland received the same message that day, along with at least 33 schools in Scotland and around 25 institutions in Bulgaria. The messages shared the subject line 'Death is near' and were sent from at least four email accounts that had been taken over.

After two quiet weeks, the threats came back on 2 November. A flight from London to Sofia was diverted to Budapest, and the next day seven more flights and a cruise ship off Greece were targeted. Another batch followed on 5 November. Between 2 and 6 November, hoax threats reached more than 50 flights run by about 15 airlines, and fighter jets were scrambled in France and Hungary to escort threatened planes. On 6 November the threats reached newsrooms, including broadcasters in France, Bulgaria and Ireland, and at least 12 media organisations were targeted around that time.

An even bigger wave came on 11 November. About 6,300 schools in Italy received the same threatening email, prompting evacuations nationwide, and around 1,000 schools in Scotland, 300 in Northern Ireland and about 300 in Ireland were targeted the same day. The November messages were sent in the name of an obscure Russian anti-feminist group, whose founder said he had nothing to do with them. Many schools hit on 11 November had received the earlier round too, and researchers say the November messages went to nearly the same Scottish schools as the October ones, pointing to one shared list.

Who is behind the campaign is still unknown, but several officials and experts suspect most or all of the messages are connected. France's interior minister said foreign interference was a clear possibility, naming Russia or other countries, and Europe's police agency is trying to determine the source. Ukrainian officials have linked similar bogus bomb threats in the past to a group of suspected Russian mercenary hackers, which a French cybersecurity firm had linked to bogus threats against Western government bodies in 2024. Italy has changed its response: its prime minister said the messages were meant to spread fear, and public buildings should no longer be evacuated on a mass email alone without concrete evidence of danger. No explosive has been found in any of the incidents, and police across Europe are still investigating.

Key points

  • More than 8,000 fake bomb warnings have reached institutions and businesses across Europe in the past month, most of them aimed at schools in Britain, Ireland and Italy.
  • The campaign began on 19 October with emails headed 'Death is near', sent from hijacked accounts; a second wave from 2 November hit more than 50 flights, at least 12 newsrooms and about 6,300 Italian schools on 11 November.
  • The November messages were sent in the name of an obscure Russian anti-feminist group whose founder says he had nothing to do with them, and many of the same schools were targeted in both waves.
  • Officials suspect a foreign hand, with France's interior minister calling foreign interference a clear possibility; no explosive has been found in any incident and the sender remains unidentified.
