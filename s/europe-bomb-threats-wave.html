US Embassies Warn of Holiday Terror Risk in Europe, Citing Hoax Bomb Threats

American embassies across Europe have issued a coordinated security alert warning US citizens of possible terror attacks at crowded holiday events, citing the wave of hoax bomb threats that has disrupted flights and schools across the continent this month.

The alerts were published on Monday by embassies in London, Berlin, Madrid, Tallinn and Pristina, with London's statement covering Britain and the rest of Europe. They warned that large gatherings could present a target of opportunity for terrorist groups and criminals, including lone actors who may strike with little or no warning. Tourist locations named include markets, transport hubs, shopping malls, places of worship and other crowded public areas. The alerts name no specific plot, city or date, and Pristina's embassy noted such Europe-wide alerts are routine pre-holiday guidance. Britain's national terrorism threat level remains at severe.

The alerts also warned of disruption to travel and daily life through the holiday season, pointing to strikes and demonstrations that have become more frequent and to the hoax bomb threats that targeted civil aviation in several European countries in November. Americans in Europe were urged to stay aware of their surroundings, follow police instructions, and enrol in the State Department's Smart Traveler Enrollment Program for security updates.

An investigation published on Tuesday put the hoax campaign at more than 8,000 fake bomb warnings in the past month. The campaign began on 19 October, when an email claiming a bomb had been planted reached a primary school in Belfast, and about 300 schools in Northern Ireland, at least 33 in Scotland and around 25 in Bulgaria received the same message that day. The messages shared the subject line "Death is near" and were sent from at least four email accounts that had been taken over.

After two quiet weeks the threats returned on 2 November, hitting flights, and on 11 November about 6,300 schools in Italy, around 1,000 in Scotland, 300 in Northern Ireland and 300 in Ireland were targeted in one day. The November messages were sent in the name of an obscure Russian anti-feminist group whose founder denied involvement, and researchers say many of the same schools were hit in both waves, pointing to one shared list. Who is behind the campaign is still unknown; France's interior minister has said foreign interference was a clear possibility, and Europe's police agency is seeking the source. No explosive has been found in any incident.

Key points

  • US embassies in London, Berlin, Madrid, Tallinn and Pristina issued a coordinated alert on Monday warning of possible terror attacks at crowded holiday events across Europe.
  • The alerts name markets, transport hubs, shopping malls and places of worship as possible targets, but identify no specific plot, city or date.
  • The alerts explicitly cite the hoax bomb threats that targeted civil aviation in several European countries in November and disrupted flights.
  • More than 8,000 fake bomb warnings have hit Europe in the past month, mostly aimed at schools; no explosive has been found and the sender remains unidentified.
