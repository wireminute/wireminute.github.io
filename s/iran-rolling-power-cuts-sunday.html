Iran's Rolling Power Cuts Take Effect as War Damage Leaves Gas Output Short

Iran's scheduled power cuts have taken effect, with households facing outages of about two hours every other day, after the head of the national electricity company said attacks on four gas-processing facilities had knocked out about 230 million cubic metres a day of gas capacity.

The restrictions began on Sunday, as announced by Mohammad Allahdad, head of the national power company Tavanir, on state television on Saturday. He said fuel supply limits left the power sector with no choice but to ration supply, with outages of about two hours every second day, or as much as two hours daily in some areas. The cuts have no fixed end date.

The scale of the damage to the gas network is now clearer. Iran's National Gas Company said attacks on four gas-processing facilities cut about 230 million cubic metres a day of processing capacity, and that repairs have so far restored roughly 100 million cubic metres of it. Allahdad said gas output was still below last year's level, which makes supplying fuel to power plants more complicated in the coming months.

President Masoud Pezeshkian said attacks by the United States and Israel on energy sites had added to the country's power troubles as winter approaches. He pointed to damage to gas facilities in particular and warned that Iranians should prepare for a hard winter.

Iran was already struggling with a severe power shortage before the fighting, and the electricity system was under strain long before the first strikes. The war, which began in late February with American and Israeli strikes on Iran, has since hit power stations and fuel networks, cutting deeper into the electricity available for homes and businesses. Strikes have also damaged gas infrastructure, which feeds many of the country's power plants.

The announcement comes as fighting continues across the Gulf, with attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz and strikes on both sides through the weekend. The power cuts are the clearest sign yet of how the conflict is reaching everyday life inside Iran. Mediation efforts are continuing through Qatar, whose delegation held talks in Tehran on Friday evening, but there is no sign yet of a deal to end the war. The restrictions stay in place while fuel supplies remain tight.

Key points

  • Rolling power cuts began in Iran on Sunday, with outages of roughly two hours, on alternate days in some places and daily in others.
  • Attacks on four gas-processing facilities cut about 230 million cubic metres a day of capacity; repairs have restored roughly 100 million.
  • National electricity chief Mohammad Allahdad said gas output remains below last year's level and fuel supply will be harder in the coming months.
  • President Pezeshkian said US and Israeli strikes on energy and gas sites worsened the crisis as winter approaches.
