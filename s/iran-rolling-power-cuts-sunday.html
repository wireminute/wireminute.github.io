Iran Announces Rolling Power Cuts From Sunday as War Damage Strains Energy Grid

Iran will start scheduled power cuts on Sunday after strikes on its energy network during the war with the United States and Israel worsened a deepening electricity shortage, the head of the national power company has said.

Iran's national electricity chief, Mohammad Allahdad, told state television on Saturday that fuel supply limits left the power sector with no choice but to ration supply from Sunday. He said households should expect outages of about two hours every second day, or as much as two hours daily in some areas.

President Masoud Pezeshkian said attacks by the United States and Israel on energy sites had added to the country's power troubles as winter approaches. He pointed to damage to gas facilities in particular and warned that Iranians should prepare for a hard winter.

Iran was already struggling with a severe power shortage before the fighting, and the electricity system was under strain long before the first strikes. The war, which began in late February with American and Israeli strikes on Iran, has since hit power stations and fuel networks, cutting deeper into the electricity available for homes and businesses. Strikes have also damaged gas infrastructure, which feeds many of the country's power plants.

The announcement comes as fighting continues across the Gulf, with attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz and strikes on both sides through the weekend. The power cuts are the clearest sign yet of how the conflict is reaching everyday life inside Iran.

Mediation efforts are continuing through Qatar, whose delegation held talks in Tehran on Friday evening, but there is no sign yet of a deal to end the war. The restrictions take effect on Sunday regardless, with the electricity company saying the rationing will stay in place while fuel supplies remain tight.

Key points

  • Rolling power cuts begin in Iran on Sunday, with outages of roughly two hours, on alternate days in some places and daily in others.
  • National electricity chief Mohammad Allahdad said fuel supply limits leave the sector no choice but to restrict distribution.
  • President Pezeshkian said US and Israeli strikes on energy and gas sites worsened the crisis as winter approaches.
  • Iran's power system was already under severe strain before the war, which began with US and Israeli strikes in late February.
