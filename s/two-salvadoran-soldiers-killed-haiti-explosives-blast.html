Bukele Names Three Salvadoran Soldiers Killed in Haiti and Details Drone Blast That Killed Them

El Salvador's President Nayib Bukele has named the three Salvadoran soldiers killed in Haiti last week and described how two of them died trying to dislodge a grenade from a drone, in the first detailed account of the blast.

Bukele wrote on Sunday night that the soldiers who died on Friday were Lieutenant Cristian Daniel Palacios Santos and Corporals Emerson Alexander Zepeda Zelada and Wilber Manuel Serrano Medina. The three were the first officially reported deaths among Salvadoran troops since the large-scale deployment to Haiti began about a month ago.

According to Bukele, the two corporals were trying to dislodge a grenade from a drone as its battery was about to run out. He wrote that it was not possible to steer the drone toward a completely safe area for it to crash, and that Serrano Medina held the drone off the ground while Zepeda Zelada manually disarmed the grenade.

They were fully aware of the immense risk they were taking, yet they chose to act to protect others. And they succeeded, Bukele wrote. Palacios Santos was killed in the same incident, but Bukele provided no details of how he died.

Bukele said Palacios Santos was admired after he recently took control during a critical moment in an operation in which some contractors allegedly retreated. He acted with speed and determination, managing to save several lives, including those of his own colleagues, Bukele wrote. He did not identify the contractors.

Bukele said his government would award the families of the three soldiers $200,000 each immediately, in addition to a monthly income. He also said any soldier who wants to leave Haiti immediately may do so without fear of reprisal and without concern that their return might jeopardise the mission, adding that many of their comrades have volunteered to relieve anyone who wishes to be relieved.

Around 2,500 Salvadoran troops were sent to Haiti in late October in a mission to find four aviation technicians who went missing on 18 October after an attack on their vehicle near the capital, Port-au-Prince. The leader of the gang in the area where the technicians went missing has suggested in video messages that the four men were killed, but has offered no evidence. El Salvador's government maintains that they are alive, and officials have said the troops plan to leave once the technicians are found.
