Anthropic Hits $2 Trillion in Record Nasdaq Debut

Anthropic's shares soared on their first day of trading on Thursday, pushing the maker of the Claude chatbot to a valuation of about $2 trillion in the biggest stock market debut ever recorded.

The shares opened at $111.50 on the Nasdaq, 34 percent above the $83 sale price, putting the company at roughly $2 trillion. The stock climbed to almost $116 in the first minutes of trading before easing back to about $105, still more than a fifth above the offer price.

The listing raised $81.3 billion from the sale of 980 million shares, the largest amount ever raised in a stock market debut, passing the $75 billion SpaceX raised in June. A separate $26 billion private placement from large technology investors took the total raised past $107 billion. Anthropic is the first leading maker of frontier AI models to sell shares to the public; its main rival OpenAI is not expected to seek a listing before 2027.

The debut caps a turbulent week for the AI industry, days after the sudden departure of Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang. Investors rushed in despite questions about the cost of building AI systems: Anthropic has committed about $900 billion to computing power and infrastructure over the coming years, including $70 billion in the next 12 months, and carries $6.2 billion of debt. Revenue reached $33.24 billion in the first nine months of 2026, up from $2.55 billion a year earlier, while the company reported a $104 billion net loss for the period, driven largely by accounting adjustments. The listing came in the same week Nvidia reported its earnings, the other big test facing the sector this week.

Chief executive Dario Amodei said the technology would drive scientific breakthroughs, while warning of what he called very real risks. The shares trade under the ticker ANT.
