Death Toll Rises to 20 After Mexico Captures FBI Most Wanted Cartel Boss 'El Chapo Isidro'

The death toll from the violence that followed the capture of Fausto Isidro Meza-Flores, known as 'El Chapo Isidro', has risen to 20, Mexico's defence minister said on Monday, as soldiers came under fire again in Sinaloa while trying to restore order.

The defence minister said 20 people had been killed, four of them soldiers and the rest members of organised crime, updating the toll of 11 given on Saturday. Security Secretary Omar Garcia Harfuch said the soldiers died as gunmen tried to stop the arrest.

The violence continued into Sunday, when soldiers patrolling Sinaloa de Leyva came under fire from suspected cartel members, the defence ministry said. The troops detained eight people, including one minor, and seized six vehicles, two of them fitted with bullet-proof armour and gun turrets. The ministry said the eight had taken part in the retaliatory violence that followed the arrest.

The operation began at about 7 p.m. on Friday, when troops moved in to detain Meza-Flores and came under attack with gunfire and explosives. Armed men threw up roadblocks, set fire to trucks and businesses, and military helicopters were filmed firing at gunmen from the air. The violence was so intense that the army and National Guard special forces could not fly Meza-Flores out of Guasave until 4 a.m. on Saturday.

A professional baseball game at Kuroda Park in Guasave was abandoned as fans fled the stands, and Sinaloa's government told residents to stay indoors while an interagency operation worked to restore order. Residents described a night of terror, taking shelter as gunfire rang out and helicopters flew overhead.

Eight people were detained in the arrest operation, including Meza-Flores, his second-in-command, known as 'Serafin', and the group's financial operator. All were flown to Mexico City and handed to federal organised crime prosecutors. Mexico's government said intelligence from the Trump administration had helped locate and capture him, though it gave few details of the coordination.

Meza-Flores leads the Meza-Flores criminal organisation, known as Los Mazatlecos, based in Guasave. The FBI accuses him of moving large quantities of heroin, methamphetamine, cocaine, fentanyl and marijuana into the United States, and had offered a reward of up to $5 million for information leading to his arrest. He is expected to face extradition to the United States, where he is charged with drug trafficking.

The capture is a major success for Mexico's military and for the FBI, which had pursued Meza-Flores for more than a decade. But experts warned that picking off cartel leaders can set off another wave of violence in a region already caught up in a power struggle between warring cartel factions. The story is still developing.
