Mexico Captures FBI Most Wanted Cartel Boss 'El Chapo Isidro' After Gun Battles in Sinaloa

Mexican forces have captured Fausto Isidro Meza-Flores, known as 'El Chapo Isidro', one of the FBI's ten most wanted fugitives, after a military operation in the northwestern state of Sinaloa set off hours of gun battles, the country's defence ministry said.

The ministry said elite forces, using stealth and surprise, detained Meza-Flores in Guasave on Friday with help from the United States, and flew him to Mexico City. His alleged second-in-command, identified only as German N and known as 'Serafin', and the group's alleged financial operator, Jose N, were also detained.

The operation began with troops coming under attack with gunfire and explosives, the ministry said. Armed men threw up roadblocks in several towns near the arrest site, and military helicopters were filmed firing at gunmen from the air. A professional baseball game at Kuroda Park in Guasave was abandoned as fans fled the stands, and Sinaloa's government told residents to stay indoors while an interagency operation worked to restore order.

Meza-Flores leads the Meza-Flores criminal organisation, known as Los Mazatlecos, based in Guasave. The FBI accuses him of moving large quantities of heroin, methamphetamine, cocaine, fentanyl and marijuana into the United States, and had offered a reward of up to $5 million for information leading to his arrest. He is expected to face extradition to the United States, where he is charged with drug trafficking.

The capture is a major success for Mexico's military and for the FBI, which had pursued Meza-Flores for more than a decade. The defence ministry said the operation was carried out by elite forces with American assistance, though it did not say which US agency was involved. The arrest was announced by the Mexican authorities and had not been independently confirmed by other sources at the time of reporting.

Key points

  • Mexican elite forces captured Fausto Isidro Meza-Flores, 'El Chapo Isidro', in Guasave, Sinaloa, on Friday, with US help; he was flown to Mexico City.
  • The FBI lists him among its ten most wanted fugitives, with a reward of up to $5 million, accusing him of trafficking heroin, methamphetamine, cocaine, fentanyl and marijuana into the United States.
  • The raid set off gun battles and roadblocks across several towns; a baseball game in Guasave was abandoned as fans fled, and residents were told to stay indoors.
  • Two other men were detained, including his alleged second-in-command; Meza-Flores faces extradition to the United States on drug trafficking charges.
