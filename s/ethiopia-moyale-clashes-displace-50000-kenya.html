Fighting in Ethiopia's Moyale Drives 50,000 Into Kenya as Mediation Fails

Fighting between forces linked to Ethiopia's Oromia and Somali regional administrations has driven as many as 50,000 people across the border into Kenya, with mediation efforts failing and residents reporting nightly gunfire in the border town of Moyale.

The latest escalation began on 18 October, when a new Somali office opened and its flag was raised in a contested part of Moyale, sparking clashes between Somali regional police and Oromo militia members. A 13-year-old girl was killed. Fighting flared again on 22 October and spread to Arero and Wachile.

As many as 50,000 people have fled into Kenya since 22 October, most of them women and children, according to the UN refugee agency, citing local authorities. Kenya said on 9 November that about 40,000 had crossed. At least nine people were killed in the first days of fighting, a hospital official said.

A security official said talks between the two regional administrations had broken down and that direct action by the federal government was needed. Residents say night shooting and closed roads have brought the livestock trade to a standstill, with no sign of normal life returning.

Key points

  • Fighting between Oromia and Somali regional forces in Moyale began on 18 October over a new Somali office and flag-raising in a disputed part of the town, and spread to Arero and Wachile
  • As many as 50,000 people have fled into Kenya since 22 October, most of them women and children, according to the UN refugee agency citing local authorities
  • Talks between the two regional administrations have broken down, and residents report nightly gunfire and a halt to the livestock trade
