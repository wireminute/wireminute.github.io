Bus and Truck Collide Near Kaziranga, Killing at Least 24 in Assam

A highway collision between a passenger bus and a container truck in north-eastern India has killed at least 24 people, in one of the worst road crashes Assam has seen.

The crash happened on Friday on a highway in Golaghat district, near Kaziranga National Park, about 225 kilometres east of the state capital, Dispur. Police said at least 24 people were killed and 11 others injured when the bus collided head-on with the truck. Several children and the bus driver were among the dead, and the state government said two women and at least one child were among those killed.

The bus was travelling from Bokakhat towards Nagaon with about 30 people on board, many of them college students returning from classes. Several of the dead were college students, a state minister said, though they had not yet been publicly identified. The injured were taken to Jorhat Medical College and Hospital, where some were in a critical condition, and officials said the death toll was likely to rise. The identities of the dead were still being established, with bodies sent for post-mortem examination.

A state minister, Atul Bora, described the crash as one of the worst in Assam's history, and police have opened an investigation into the cause. Witnesses told reporters the bus had been speeding before the collision. Assam's chief minister, Himanta Biswa Sarma, said he was deeply distressed by the accident and offered condolences to the families of the dead.

The highway runs past Kaziranga, a protected park famous for its one-horned rhinos and listed as a world heritage site. The crash is the latest deadly collision on India's roads, which record some of the highest numbers of traffic deaths in the world, with speeding, poorly maintained roads and ageing vehicles among the causes cited by safety experts.
