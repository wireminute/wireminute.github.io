Fiona, the Internet-Famous Hippo, Dies at 9 After Swallowing a Visitor's Hat

Fiona, the hippopotamus whose fight for life as a premature calf made her famous around the world, has died at the Cincinnati Zoo at the age of nine, days after swallowing a knit hat that fell into her enclosure.

The zoo announced her death on Thursday, November 19, saying its veterinary and keeper teams had worked for days to help her pass the hat through her digestive system. Despite repeated attempts and a day of intensive supportive care, she did not recover, and the zoo said the object had proved too much for her to overcome.

The hat entered the enclosure on November 8, when a child threw it up in the air beside the viewing windows. A ceiling fan caught it and carried it through a narrow one-foot opening above the 12-foot glass, and Fiona swallowed a large part of it. Zoo officials have stressed that it was an accident.

Keepers first hoped she would pass the object on her own, giving her fluids and medication to help it move along. Surgery was ruled out for an animal weighing more than 2,000 pounds, after the zoo consulted veterinary and human specialists. An ultrasound showed sections of her small intestine bunching as the hat moved through, and a colonoscopy using a scope about nine feet long could not find it. Staff warned that a blockage could lead to infection and sepsis.

Fiona was born six weeks early in January 2017, weighing only 29 pounds, less than half the usual weight for a newborn hippo. Zoo staff hand-raised her, and her progress was followed by millions of people online, making her one of the best-known animals in the world. She would have turned 10 in January. Her younger brother Fritz, born at the zoo in August 2022, survives her.

Key points

  • Fiona, the Cincinnati Zoo hippo who became famous around the world after surviving a premature birth, died on November 19 at the age of nine.
  • She swallowed a knit hat on November 8 after a child tossed it into the air and a ceiling fan carried it through a gap above the viewing windows; the zoo says it was an accident.
  • Vets spent days trying to help her pass the hat with fluids and medication, but surgery was not an option for an animal of her size.
