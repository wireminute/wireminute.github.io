Molly Meldrum Dies at 83, the Australian Music Legend Who Championed a Nation's Sound

Ian "Molly" Meldrum, the Australian music critic and broadcaster who presented the television show Countdown for 13 years and spent six decades championing homegrown music, has died at the age of 83, his family has announced.

Meldrum died peacefully at 5.30am on Monday at Epworth Hospital in Richmond, Melbourne, after a long period of ill health. His family said it was "with profound sadness" that they announced the passing of the "Australian music legend, journalist, producer and cultural icon". He is survived by his long-term partner, Yan Wongngam, and his adopted son, Morgan Scholes. His family has asked for privacy.

Meldrum co-created and hosted Countdown, the ABC's Sunday-evening music programme, from 1974 to 1987. The show brought the world's biggest pop stars into Australian living rooms and gave Australian acts a national stage, and Meldrum, instantly recognisable in his trademark Stetson hat, became the face of the country's music scene. His influence was so great that he could make or break music careers, and his sign-off, telling viewers to "do yourself a favour", became part of the national vocabulary.

Born Ian Alexander Meldrum in Orbost, Victoria, on 29 January 1943, he began as a music journalist and critic in Melbourne in the 1960s before moving into record production and talent management. He later became a regular music presenter on Hey Hey It's Saturday, interviewing world-famous stars across five decades while continuing to argue that Australian artists deserved the same billing as the British and American acts who dominated the charts.

He was made a Member of the Order of Australia in 1986 and received the ARIA Special Achievement Award in 1993. He entered the Logie Awards Hall of Fame in 2012, and in 2014 he was inducted into the ARIA Hall of Fame alongside Countdown, the first inductees who were not recording artists.

The Australian Recording Industry Association said Meldrum had spent six decades convincing a country raised on British and American records that its own artists could stand alongside the greats. His beloved football club, St Kilda, and the Melbourne Storm rugby league club were among the first to pay tribute, calling him a lifelong supporter and a cultural icon.
