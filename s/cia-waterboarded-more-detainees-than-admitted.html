CIA Waterboarded More Detainees Than It Admitted, Newly Released Files Suggest

The CIA may have waterboarded more detainees than the three it has officially acknowledged, according to newly released documents that describe an additional case at a secret prison in Afghanistan.

The New York Times reported on Wednesday that the files, obtained through a freedom of information lawsuit, come from the investigation of John Durham, a special prosecutor appointed in 2008 to examine the agency's detention programme and its destruction of videotapes showing waterboarding. The inquiry ran until 2012 and ended without criminal charges against agency staff.

The key document is a summary of a 2012 interview in which a contract translator who worked with the agency told the FBI he had seen a man he called Bahir held on a tilted board while water was poured over his face. He said three or four people were carrying out the procedure in a hallway, and that he had witnessed it in passing without taking part himself. The documents do not name the site, but the newspaper concluded it was most likely a prison outside Bagram Air Force Base near Kabul, known as Detention Site Cobalt, or the Salt Pit, which became notorious for abusive conditions. The translator also described a detainee death that matched the facility.

Waterboarding involves restraining a person and pouring water over the face through a cloth to create the sensation of drowning. The CIA described it as an enhanced interrogation technique during its detention programme after the attacks of 11 September 2001. The agency has said it waterboarded only three men: Abu Zubaydah, Khalid Sheikh Mohammed and Abd al-Rahim al-Nashiri, all of whom were held at secret prisons in Thailand and Poland and are now detained at Guantanamo Bay. A 2014 report by the Senate Intelligence Committee found that Zubaydah alone was waterboarded 83 times.

The CIA has never acknowledged waterboarding detainees at any site in Afghanistan, and it did not respond to requests for comment from the newspaper. Rights groups have long argued that the practice was used far more widely than the agency has admitted, and the new account lends weight to that claim.

Key points

  • Newly released files suggest the CIA waterboarded more detainees than the three it has officially acknowledged.
  • A contract translator told the FBI in 2012 that he saw a detainee called Bahir waterboarded at a secret prison in Afghanistan, most likely the Salt Pit outside Bagram.
  • The CIA has said it waterboarded only Abu Zubaydah, Khalid Sheikh Mohammed and Abd al-Rahim al-Nashiri, at black sites in Thailand and Poland; Zubaydah was waterboarded 83 times, a Senate report found.
  • The documents come from special prosecutor John Durham's 2008-2012 investigation, which ended without charges; the CIA did not respond to requests for comment and has never acknowledged waterboarding in Afghanistan.
