US Hits Iran's Shadow Banking Networks With Sweeping New Sanctions

The United States has imposed sweeping new sanctions on more than two dozen Iranian exchange houses, front companies and individuals, stepping up its campaign to cut Iran off from the global financial system and choke off what Washington calls its shadow banking networks.

The Treasury Department said on Monday that the designations, made under its Operation Economic Outcast, target the financial infrastructure that keeps much of Iran's remaining foreign trade running. The targets include exchange houses and front companies based in Iran, the United Arab Emirates, Turkey and Hong Kong, as well as key personnel who move billions of dollars through the international financial system on Tehran's behalf.

Among those named was the Iran Center for Exchange of Currency and Gold, along with Dubai-based H N B Trading, which the Treasury said helped arrange a payment for a ship charter involving a company linked to Iran's Armed Forces General Staff. At least eight of the companies targeted are based in Dubai, with others in Turkey and Hong Kong.

Treasury's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network also released an analysis identifying nearly 15 billion dollars of financial activity between January 2025 and August 2026 that it said was potentially linked to Iranian shadow banking. At least 2.6 billion dollars of the funds came from banks in China, 981 million dollars from Singapore and 922 million dollars from Britain, routed onward to financial institutions in the Emirates.

Treasury said the shadow banking system is overseen by Iran's central bank and relies on dozens of Iran-based exchange houses and so-called rahbar companies, bank affiliates that manage sanctions-evasion networks for their Iranian clients. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Iran's economy was underwater and its leaders were panicking, calling the shadow banking system the last financial lifeline of a bankrupt regime.

The action is the latest step in Operation Economic Outcast, the campaign Bessent announced in August to sever the remaining economic lifelines sustaining the Iranian government. It comes as diplomatic efforts continue, with US Secretary of State Marco Rubio meeting Qatar's prime minister in Washington on Monday, and after President Donald Trump said on Sunday he was leaning toward resuming military action if no deal is reached.

Key points

  • The Treasury sanctioned more than two dozen Iranian exchange houses, front companies and key personnel under Operation Economic Outcast.
  • The action targets firms in Iran, the United Arab Emirates, Turkey and Hong Kong that move billions through the international financial system for Iran's foreign trade.
  • Treasury's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network identified nearly 15 billion dollars of activity between January 2025 and August 2026 potentially linked to Iranian shadow banking.
  • Treasury says the networks are overseen by Iran's central bank and run through exchange houses and rahbar companies that manage sanctions evasion.
