Trump May Hold Off New Iran Strikes Unless Tehran or Houthis Provoke

President Donald Trump has told senior advisers he may hold off on new strikes against Iran if Tehran and its allies in Yemen give him no fresh reason to act, according to people familiar with the discussions.

Trump has told senior advisers in recent days that he may hold off on attacking Iran if the United States faces no new provocations from Tehran or the Iran-backed Houthis in Yemen, according to three people familiar with the matter. The president has alternated between threatening to escalate the war and winding it down, and current and former officials say he does not appear to have made up his mind about how to end a conflict he launched with Israel in February.

In meetings with his national security advisers, and after intelligence briefings, Trump said he was considering holding off on strikes, the people said, speaking on condition of anonymity. He has also shown sympathy for allies in the region who have asked him not to return to full-scale war, and his private remarks build on public comments playing down the chances of major fighting resuming.

Among those urging restraint are Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman and Turkish President Tayyip Erdogan, who both pressed Trump not to resume attacks in phone calls over the past two weeks. Trump told the crown prince on October 31 that he was planning to attack after the midterm elections, but the crown prince pleaded with him to hold off, arguing that economic sanctions against Iran were working. Trump has spoken with Erdogan three times since October 28, and a readout of their November 7 call stressed that tensions between Iran and the United States need to end.

Trump said on Thursday that he could end the war immediately but was taking a slower and more restrained path at the request of the nations he was looking to protect. American intelligence agencies have assessed that the naval blockade and economic sanctions are hurting Iran's economy and would keep putting pressure on Tehran, while renewed strikes would not significantly shift Iran's demands in negotiations.

A White House official said Trump, as commander-in-chief, is the sole decision-maker and that only the president knows what he will do, and when. The war has proved unpopular at home: a poll taken shortly before the November 3 elections found 55 percent of Americans said it was not worth the cost, and voters handed control of Congress to the Democrats. Separately, an Israeli newspaper reported that Israel had warned Washington of an alleged Iranian drone plot against Trump and senior officials, a claim that has not been independently confirmed.

Key points

  • Trump told senior advisers in recent days he may hold off new strikes on Iran if there are no new provocations from Tehran or the Houthis in Yemen.
  • Saudi Arabia's crown prince and Turkey's president both urged restraint in calls over the past two weeks, arguing that sanctions and the naval blockade are already pressuring Iran.
  • US intelligence agencies assess the blockade and sanctions are hurting Iran's economy, and that renewed strikes would not shift Tehran's negotiating demands.
  • Trump has not made a final decision, and a White House official said only the president knows what he will do, and when.
