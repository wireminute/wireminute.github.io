Tusk Calls Russian Missile Crash in Poland a Deliberate Provocation

Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk has called the Russian missiles and drone that crashed in Poland a deliberate provocation, saying it was no coincidence they fell beside a major Western military exercise.

Poland's Defence Minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz said the weapon that came down near the village of Nowy Lipowiec, about 60 kilometres from the Ukrainian border, was a Kh-101 cruise missile. A second object was also a missile and a third was most likely a jet-powered drone, he said, with debris found at three sites in the region. No one was hurt and no infrastructure was damaged.

Air raid sirens sounded across parts of eastern Poland before the alert was lifted in the morning. Polish F-16 jets and Swedish Gripen fighters patrolled through the night, and Patriot air defence batteries were activated around Rzeszow and Swidnik. Prime Minister Donald Tusk convened an emergency meeting and said there was no current threat from the air. Warsaw said it would ask NATO for extra forces and surveillance aircraft for the alliance's eastern flank operation and seek an emergency meeting of member states.

Tusk said he was in constant contact with NATO leaders and that the alliance's secretary general, Mark Rutte, visited Warsaw on Saturday. He said he was choosing his words carefully because the matter was serious, but that it appeared Poland was dealing with a deliberate provocation. Defence Minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz said allies shared the assessment that the incursion was intentional.

The objects came down near Nowa Deba, where troops from 11 countries are taking part in Exercise Koalicja, a drill under way since November 9 preparing for a possible postwar mission in Ukraine. Debris was recovered at three sites, Nowy Lipowiec, Rudnik nad Sanem and Molodycz, between 50 and 100 kilometres from the exercise area. Rzeszow airport suspended flights while military aircraft were activated, and investigators have been sent to secure the sites.

Rutte visited Warsaw on Saturday and said NATO would defend every inch of Polish territory, calling the incursion clearly dangerous. Polish prosecutors confirmed that one of the objects that crashed was a Russian Kh-101 cruise missile, manufactured in the last quarter of this year, after examining propulsion and guidance parts recovered at Nowy Lipowiec. No one was injured, and Poland did not invoke Article 4, the treaty provision for consultations when an ally feels its security is threatened, though it asked for more NATO forces and air defence.

Key points

  • Tusk said the Russian objects that fell in Poland were a deliberate provocation, not an accident, pointing to their landing near a Western military exercise.
  • A Kh-101 cruise missile crashed near Nowy Lipowiec, a second missile came down in Rudnik nad Sanem and a jet-powered drone was found in Molodycz, with no injuries or damage.
  • Rutte visited Warsaw on Saturday and said NATO would defend every inch of Polish territory; prosecutors confirmed one crashed object was a Russian-made Kh-101 cruise missile, and Poland did not invoke Article 4.
  • Troops from 11 countries are drilling at Nowa Deba in Exercise Koalicja, preparing for a possible postwar mission in Ukraine; Rzeszow airport suspended flights during the alert.
