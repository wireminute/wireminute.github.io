Prosecutors Confirm All Three Objects That Crashed in Poland Were Russian Missiles

Polish prosecutors have confirmed that all three objects that crashed in south-eastern Poland on Friday were Russian Kh-101 cruise missiles, revising earlier accounts that one was probably a drone, as NATO prepares to discuss the incursion on Monday.

District prosecutors in Lublin said on Sunday that preliminary analysis of debris recovered from three sites showed all three objects were Kh-101 cruise missiles. The missiles were built in the final three months of 2026 at a factory in Dubna, in the Moscow region, the prosecutors said, after the government asked them to examine what had fallen on Polish soil.

The finding revises the account given by Defence Minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz on Friday, when he said one Kh-101 had come down near the village of Nowy Lipowiec, a second missile had fallen elsewhere, and a third object was most likely a jet-powered drone. Debris was recovered at Nowy Lipowiec, Rudnik nad Sanem and Molodycz, between 50 and 100 kilometres from the Ukrainian border. No one was hurt and no infrastructure was damaged.

Air raid sirens sounded across parts of eastern Poland before the alert was lifted in the morning. Polish F-16 jets and Swedish Gripen fighters patrolled through the night, and Patriot air defence batteries were activated around Rzeszow and Swidnik. Rzeszow airport suspended flights while military aircraft were activated, and investigators were sent to secure the sites.

Prime Minister Donald Tusk said the objects fell beside Nowa Deba, where troops from 11 countries are taking part in Exercise Koalicja, a drill under way since November 9 preparing for a possible postwar mission in Ukraine. He called the incursion a deliberate provocation, saying it was no coincidence the objects fell beside a major Western military exercise. NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte visited Warsaw on Saturday and said the alliance would defend every inch of Polish territory.

Poland has not invoked Article 4, the treaty provision for consultations when an ally feels its security is threatened, though it has asked for more NATO forces and surveillance aircraft for the alliance's eastern flank. The North Atlantic Council, NATO's main decision-making body, is due to meet in Brussels on Monday to discuss the violation of Polish airspace.
