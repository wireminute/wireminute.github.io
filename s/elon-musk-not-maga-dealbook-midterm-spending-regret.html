Musk Says He Regrets Record Midterm Spending and Is 'Not MAGA'

Elon Musk has said he regrets spending hundreds of millions of dollars backing Republican candidates in the US midterm elections, and described himself as a political moderate who is not part of the MAGA movement.

Speaking by video link at a business summit in New York on Wednesday, Musk said he felt "pretty foolish" about the money he had spent and had seen little for it. He was joining from China, where he is travelling with the US president's delegation for a summit of Asia-Pacific leaders. Campaign finance data puts his spending in support of Republican candidates and conservative groups in the 2026 midterms at at least $445 million, making him the largest individual political donor in US history.

The Democrats won control of both chambers of Congress in the 3 November elections, along with several governorships, in a result widely described as a sweeping defeat for the Republicans. Musk said the outcome showed that American elections were not decided by money alone, and that if he could go back he would have spent less.

Musk, who spent about $277 million helping Donald Trump win re-election in 2024 and once called himself "Dark MAGA", struck a very different tone on Wednesday. "I think I'm ideologically quite moderate. I don't think I have any extreme political views," he said. "I'm not MAGA. I'm not sure what MAGA means, but if that means blind adherence to a set of policies by the Republican Party, then I certainly don't have that." He added that there were positives and negatives in both parties.

He also praised Gina Hinojosa, the Democrat who won the Texas governorship, saying she would be "quite moderate" and that things would be fine in Texas under her leadership. Musk, who led an effort to cut the federal workforce after the 2024 election, said he did not need a pardon because he had committed no crimes.

Key points

  • Musk said he feels "pretty foolish" about spending at least $445 million backing Republicans in the 2026 midterms, and would have spent less
  • Democrats took control of both chambers of Congress in the 3 November elections, along with several governorships
  • He called himself "ideologically quite moderate" and said "I'm not MAGA", a sharp change from his "Dark MAGA" label in 2024
  • He praised Texas governor-elect Gina Hinojosa as a moderate and said things would be fine in Texas under her
