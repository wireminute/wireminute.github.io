West Bank Olive Harvest Marred by Attacks as 286,000 Trees Damaged Since 2023

This year's olive harvest in the occupied West Bank has opened under a wave of attacks on farmers and their groves, with United Nations monitors recording at least 160 assaults linked to the harvest in the first two weeks of October alone.

The attacks — involving live ammunition, arson, the felling of centuries-old trees and the theft of harvested olives and farm equipment — have been recorded across the West Bank, often with Israeli forces present or taking part, according to the UN aid coordination office. The UN human rights office in the territory described the start of the season as an “ominous start” and urged states and donors to act to protect Palestinian farming communities.

The toll on the olive sector has been heavy. The Palestinian Ministry of Agriculture says 98,293 olive trees were damaged in the West Bank in the first nine months of 2026, and 286,000 since 2023, including 100,000 this year. Direct losses to the olive crop have passed $66 million, and the ministry puts cumulative losses since 2023 at more than $300 million. In Gaza, it says 90 per cent of the territory's two million olive trees have been destroyed since October 2023.

Farmers say the damage goes beyond a single season's crop. Olive trees take about a decade to mature and can bear fruit for generations, making a grove a family's long-term livelihood. Thousands of farmers are also unable to reach their trees at all, cut off by movement restrictions that the UN says are at their tightest in twenty years. Olive oil production this year is expected to fall by about half, to between 10,000 and 11,000 tonnes, against 21,000 tonnes in a normal year.

The harvest, which began in early October and runs into November, is one of the most important dates in the Palestinian farming calendar, providing income for thousands of families. Palestinian officials say attacks have been recorded in governorates across the West Bank, including Ramallah, Nablus and Jenin, and warn that incidents tend to intensify as the season reaches its peak.
