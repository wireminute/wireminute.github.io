Reprisal Attacks Kill Dozens in Nigeria's Niger State After Village Head's Murder

Dozens of people have been killed in reprisal attacks on Fulani communities in Nigeria's Niger State, community groups and residents said, after gunmen shot dead a village head and his family during dawn prayers on Saturday.

Gunmen attacked Lumman-Ba'are village in Borgu district at about 5am on Saturday, killing the village head, Mallam Bawa Muhammad, his wife and three of his children, the Niger State government said. Fulani community groups said suspected vigilantes then struck back at Fulani settlements around the towns of Wawa and Gada-Oli on Saturday and Sunday, killing 64 people, and residents said 38 victims were buried in a mass grave in Wawa on Sunday.

A security report seen by AFP counted 35 dead in Wawa alone, with burials still under way, and said the final toll was expected to more than double. Police and the state government have not commented on the reported reprisal killings, and the reports have not been independently verified. Residents said the reprisals were still going on, in an area hit by recurring attacks, cattle rustling and kidnappings. The story is still developing.

Key points

  • Gunmen killed the village head of Lumman-Ba'are, his wife and three of his children during dawn prayers on Saturday, the Niger State government said; a security report put the initial toll at 11.
  • Fulani community groups say 64 people were killed in reprisal attacks on Wawa and Gada-Oli over the weekend, and residents said 38 victims were buried in a mass grave in Wawa.
  • Police and state authorities have not commented on the reprisal toll, and the reports have not been independently verified.
