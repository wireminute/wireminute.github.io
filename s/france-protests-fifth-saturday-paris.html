France's Fifth Saturday of Protests Draws 106,000 in Lowest Turnout Yet

About 106,000 people marched across France on 14 November in a fifth straight Saturday of protests against the cost of living and a contested police bill, the lowest turnout of the weekend rallies so far, as police fired tear gas in Paris, Bordeaux and Nantes.

The interior ministry put nationwide turnout at about 106,000 people, including 24,000 in Paris, the lowest figure for the weekend rallies in recent weeks. Student unions gave a much higher count, about 200,000 nationwide and almost 100,000 in the capital. The main Paris march set off from Place de la Republique in the early afternoon and moved through Bastille toward Nation, alongside two other demonstrations in the city: a march against violence against children and a Yellow Vest rally. Police said 23 people were detained in Paris and more than 2,000 identity checks were carried out.

The rallies were largely peaceful, but officers used tear gas in the capital and in the western cities of Bordeaux and Nantes. About 70,000 police had been deployed across the country, roughly 10,000 more than the previous weekend, with elite units, mounted police, helicopters, drones, armoured vehicles and water cannon on standby. The interior minister had warned the authorities would be uncompromising with anyone committing violence.

The unrest began in late September as school blockades and demonstrations over the 2027 budget, then widened into a broader movement against rising prices, strained public services and the police bill. The bill, already approved by the lower house and now awaiting the Senate, would give officers a presumption that they acted in legitimate self-defence when they use their weapons. Supporters, including the far-right National Rally, say it would stop officers being automatically suspected of excessive force. Critics call it a licence to kill.

The movement's sharpest turn came on 17 October, when the Yellow Vest movement returned to the streets and parts of Paris, Marseille and Lyon saw rioting and looting. More than 4,900 people have been detained since that day, and internal police watchdogs have opened 161 inquiries into claims of violence by officers. Turnout has faded from its peaks: about 146,000 people marched last Saturday, against more than half a million during a union-led strike the previous Thursday. Trade unions have called a fresh strike for Tuesday after rejecting a proposed 0.6% rise in the minimum wage. The capital has extended a night curfew until Monday, while Lyon has banned gatherings in central areas and restricted public transport. The protests come months before a presidential election in April 2027 in which the incumbent will not stand again.

Key points

  • About 106,000 people marched nationwide, 24,000 of them in Paris, the lowest weekend turnout yet, though unions put the figures far higher.
  • The rallies were largely peaceful, but police used tear gas in Paris, Bordeaux and Nantes, and 23 people were detained in Paris.
  • About 70,000 police were deployed across France, with elite units, drones, armoured vehicles and water cannon on standby.
  • Unions have called a fresh strike for Tuesday after rejecting a proposed 0.6% minimum-wage increase.
