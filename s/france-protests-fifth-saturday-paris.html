France Braces for Fifth Saturday of Protests as 70,000 Police Deploy Nationwide

France braced for a fifth straight Saturday of nationwide protests on 14 November, with about 70,000 police officers deployed as crowds gathered in Paris and dozens of other cities against the cost of living and a contested bill on police use of weapons.

The main Paris march was due to set off at 1pm local time, with between 30,000 and 60,000 people expected in the capital alone and 50 to 60 marches planned across the country. Interior Minister Laurent Nunez said the deployment was roughly 10,000 officers larger than the previous weekend, with elite units, mounted police, helicopters, drones, armoured vehicles and water cannon on standby. He warned the authorities would be uncompromising with anyone committing violence.

The unrest began in late September as school blockades and demonstrations over the 2027 budget, then widened into a broader movement against rising prices, strained public services and the police bill. The bill, already approved by the lower house and now awaiting the Senate, would give officers a presumption that they acted in legitimate self-defence when they use their weapons. Supporters, including the far-right National Rally, say it would stop officers being automatically suspected of excessive force. Critics call it a licence to kill.

The movement's sharpest turn came on 17 October, when the Yellow Vest movement returned to the streets and parts of Paris, Marseille and Lyon saw rioting and looting. More than 4,900 people have been detained since that day, and internal police watchdogs have opened 161 inquiries into claims of violence by officers. Turnout has faded from its peaks — about 146,000 people marched last Saturday, against more than half a million during a union-led strike the previous Thursday — but trade unions have called a fresh strike for Tuesday after rejecting a proposed 0.6% rise in the minimum wage.

The capital has extended a night curfew until Monday, while Lyon has banned gatherings in central areas and restricted public transport. In Orléans, 20,000 to 30,000 people were expected to demonstrate against the National Rally as the party held its congress there. The protests come months before a presidential election in April 2027 in which the incumbent will not stand again.

Key points

  • About 70,000 police were deployed across France for a fifth straight Saturday of protests, with the main Paris rally due to start at 1pm.
  • Demonstrators are protesting the cost of living, conditions in schools and a bill giving police a presumption of legitimate self-defence when they use weapons.
  • More than 4,900 people have been detained since the Yellow Vests returned on 17 October, and 161 inquiries have been opened into alleged violence by officers.
  • Unions have called a fresh strike for Tuesday after rejecting a proposed 0.6% minimum-wage increase.
