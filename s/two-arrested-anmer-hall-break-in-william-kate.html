Two Arrested After Break-In at William and Kate's Country Home in Norfolk

Police in eastern England said on Tuesday that two men had been arrested following a reported break-in at the country residence of Prince William and Princess Catherine.

Norfolk Police said officers were sent to the royal family's Sandringham estate on Monday evening after suspicious activity was reported at a property there. Officers searched the grounds with police dogs, drones and a helicopter, and took two men into custody on suspicion of burglary and trespass. Police said the men remained in custody and inquiries were continuing.

Police did not name the property involved, but British news reports identified it as Anmer Hall, the ten-bedroom house where William and Catherine stay with their three children, particularly during school holidays. The reports said masked men entered the house on Monday night while the family were away. Police said nobody was in the house when it happened.

Anmer Hall was given to the couple by Queen Elizabeth II as a wedding gift after their marriage in 2011. The house stands on the Sandringham estate in Norfolk, which belongs to King Charles. William and Catherine's main home is on the Windsor estate, west of London.

Officers were called to the estate shortly before 6pm on Monday, police said. They searched the grounds with police dogs, drones and a helicopter. Police have not said whether anything was taken from the house.

The estate is owned by King Charles, and the house has served as the couple's country retreat since it was given to them in 2011. The names and ages of the two men have not been released.

News of the break-in emerged on Monday night, and police confirmed the arrests on Tuesday.

Key points

  • Two men were taken into custody on the Sandringham estate after a break-in was reported at Prince William and Catherine's country home.
  • Police were called on Monday evening and searched the estate with dogs, drones and a helicopter; the two men are held on suspicion of burglary and trespass.
  • The house, identified in British news reports as the ten-bedroom Anmer Hall, was empty at the time.
  • Anmer Hall was a wedding gift from Queen Elizabeth II in 2011, and the couple's main home is on the Windsor estate.
