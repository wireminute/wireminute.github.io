Spain Votes on Sunday in Snap Election That Could Bring the Far Right to Power

Spaniards head to the polls on Sunday in a snap general election that could hand the far right a share of power for the first time since Spain became a democracy after Franco.

Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez called the vote in early October, eight months ahead of schedule, after lawmakers voted down his housing relief package. The call came as mass protests over rents and house prices swept Spanish cities, and opinion polls have barely moved in his favour since.

The conservative People's Party, led by Alberto Núñez Feijóo, is ahead in the polls at around 31 percent, with the Socialists on 26 to 29 percent. Vox, the far-right party led by Santiago Abascal, stands at roughly 18 percent, lifted by younger and male voters. Pollsters say a People's Party-Vox alliance is the only pairing forecast to win the 176 seats required to govern outright in the 350-member chamber.

Every one of the 350 lower-house seats is contested, along with 208 of the 266 Senate seats, although only the lower house picks the prime minister. Polling stations open at 9am local time and close at 8pm, and the Interior Ministry will release provisional results within an hour of closing, with a clear picture of the outcome expected before midnight.

Sánchez has led Spain since 2018 and is one of Europe's last left-wing leaders. He has cast the vote as a choice between a far-right surge and keeping Spain open and socially just. A right-wing victory could reshape Madrid's positions on military spending, migration, China and ties with Washington and Latin America. If no candidate secures a majority, the new lower house meets on December 23 to elect a speaker, the king nominates a candidate for prime minister, and a government must be formed within two months of the investiture vote or Spain votes again.

Key points

  • Spaniards vote on Sunday, November 29, in a snap election called by Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez after lawmakers rejected his housing relief package.
  • The People's Party leads the polls at about 31 percent, with Vox at roughly 18 percent; together they are the only pairing forecast to win the 176 seats needed to govern outright.
  • A People's Party-Vox government would give the far right a share of power for the first time since Spain returned to democracy after Franco.
  • Polls open at 9am and close at 8pm local time, with provisional results within an hour of closing; the new parliament meets on December 23 to choose a speaker.
