Trump Weighs Fresh Strikes on Iran as Netanyahu Pushes Week-Long Air Campaign

Donald Trump is weighing another round of extensive military strikes on Iran as Benjamin Netanyahu presses Washington to join a four-to-seven-day air campaign, according to reports, though the president has not yet made a decision.

The president is considering the move after Israeli warnings that Tehran has rebuilt its missile and drone arsenals and is advancing chemical and biological weapons programmes, according to American and Israeli officials briefed on the discussions. Netanyahu sent Mossad chief Roman Gofman to Washington last week to present the intelligence to senior officials, including CIA Director John Ratcliffe, and Israel's military intelligence chief has argued for resuming them. A senior Israeli official disputed the description of Netanyahu as pressuring Trump.

American intelligence officials are said to have evidence of stepped-up Iranian efforts to strengthen chemical and biological weapon capacities, though officials disagree on how strong the Israeli material is, and the prevailing view is that Tehran would use such weapons to deter attacks rather than strike first. Trump said in a television interview on Wednesday there were indications Iran was looking at chemical agents and biological weapons because the nuclear route had been closed off by American and Israeli strikes. He gave no evidence, named no specific programme and said Americans did not need to be concerned, adding that a strike was not imminent and that they would see what happens after January 1.

The administration is deeply divided over the intelligence and the wisdom of striking again, especially after midterm elections in which voters showed displeasure with a war that has pushed up prices. Some officials believe the Israeli warnings are driven by Netanyahu's political concerns during the transition after last month's Israeli election, according to one report. Possible targets could include nuclear facilities, including an underground site known as Pickaxe Mountain that has not yet been hit, while Israel's planning is also said to include energy infrastructure, which Trump has not agreed to. Officials said the United States has gathered warships, fighter jets and bombers for a short, intense campaign, and the Pentagon declined to comment.

The war began in February, and a fragile ceasefire has held since 8 April. Just last week Trump said another offensive was quite unlikely, heeding Gulf allies who favour economic pressure over renewed fighting. A US official said the economic pressure campaign was devastating Iran so badly that the president had no need for major combat operations. Iran's Revolutionary Guard warned on Thursday that any country whose bases are used for American operations against Iran would itself become a target, and said attempts to strike Iran's dams would draw a comparable response.

Key points

  • Trump is weighing another round of extensive strikes on Iran; Netanyahu is pressing for a four-to-seven-day air campaign, but no decision has been made.
  • Israel warns Iran has rebuilt missile and drone stocks and is advancing chemical and biological weapons work; US officials disagree on how strong the evidence is.
  • Possible targets include the Pickaxe Mountain nuclear site; Israel also wants energy infrastructure hit, which Trump has not agreed to.
  • Tehran warned on Thursday that any country hosting US bases used in an attack would itself become a target, and that strikes on its dams would draw a comparable response.
