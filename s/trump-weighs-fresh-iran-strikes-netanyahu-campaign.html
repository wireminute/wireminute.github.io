Netanyahu Calls Fresh Iran Operation Unavoidable as Joint Strike Plans Await Trump

Israel's prime minister has told security officials a new operation against Iran is unavoidable, with joint strike plans said to be approved by the Israeli military and United States Central Command while President Donald Trump weighs whether to give the go-ahead.

Israeli broadcaster Channel 13 reported that Mr Netanyahu told senior security officials on Wednesday evening that another operation against Iran was unavoidable. An unnamed senior Israeli military officer was quoted as saying joint plans had been approved by the Israeli military and United States Central Command, and that the two sides were now awaiting Mr Trump's decision. CBS News reported that Israel preferred joint action, but that Mr Trump's decision and the scope of any operation remained unclear.

Mr Trump has signalled he is in no hurry. He told Axios on November 19: "I am not rushing into any decision. We will see what happens after the beginning of the year." At a White House news conference on Friday, he said Tehran wanted an agreement to end the fighting, but not one he considered acceptable. In an interview earlier in the week he raised concerns that Iran was looking at gas and biological weapons options after its nuclear programme was damaged, and he has asked the Pentagon and intelligence agencies to examine attack scenarios drawn up by Central Command.

Fighting continued on Friday, when a United States-launched missile struck the outskirts of Qeshm city, according to Iran's Tasnim news agency, with no immediate reports of casualties. American jets had hit a missile launch site on Qeshm Island on Tuesday. Iran has struck American military facilities in Bahrain and Kuwait several times in the past week and stepped up attacks on ships passing through the Strait of Hormuz, while the Houthis in Yemen, backed by Iran, have launched daily attacks on Saudi Arabia.

Israel's spy chief, Roman Gofman, lobbied for a new multi-day air campaign during a visit to Washington last week, meeting the CIA director and the American envoy Steve Witkoff. The head of Central Command, Admiral Brad Cooper, met Israel's military chief and Mr Gofman in Tel Aviv on November 18. Gulf states, Pakistan and Turkiye have stepped up diplomacy to prevent a return to all-out war. The Senate voted 51 to 48 against a measure that would have forced the president to seek Congress's approval for further military action. The Pentagon puts the cost of the war for the United States military at $48.3 billion since late February, and Brent crude stands at $92.60 a barrel.

Key points

  • Channel 13 reports Netanyahu told security officials a new Iran operation is unavoidable, with joint plans said to be approved by the Israeli military and US Central Command.
  • Trump says he is not rushing a decision and will decide after the new year; on Friday he said Tehran wanted a deal he does not consider acceptable.
  • A US-launched missile struck the outskirts of Qeshm city on Friday, according to Iran's Tasnim news agency, with no immediate reports of casualties.
