Trump Signals He Will Wait Until 2027 on Fresh Iran Strikes as War Cost Passes $48 Billion

President Donald Trump has signalled he is in no hurry to order a new round of strikes on Iran, saying he will decide after the new year, as the Pentagon put the cost of the war so far at more than $48 billion.

Speaking as he returned from a summit in China, Trump said he was not rushing into a decision on Iran and would see what happens after the start of the new year, without explaining why he named that date. He had said a day earlier that Iran appeared to be looking at chemical and biological weapons because the nuclear route had been closed off by American and Israeli strikes, but he gave no evidence and named no specific programme. The remarks follow weeks of pressure from Israel, whose prime minister is urging a four-to-seven-day air campaign and sent his intelligence chief to Washington last week to make the case for joint strikes.

The Senate has voted down a measure that would have forced the president to seek Congress's approval before ordering further military action against Iran. The war powers resolution failed by 51 votes to 48, in the chamber's first vote on the conflict since the midterm elections. Three Republicans backed it and one Democrat opposed it. Even if it had passed, the president could have vetoed it, and Congress would have needed a two-thirds majority in each house to override him.

The war has cost the United States military $48.3 billion since the conflict began in late February, the Pentagon's deputy comptroller told lawmakers on Thursday. The figure covers munitions, equipment replacement and operations, and is about 26 percent higher than the $38 billion estimate the budget office gave in August. It does not include damage to American bases across the Middle East, some of which have been hit hard by Iranian drone and missile attacks, nor the wider harm to the global economy.

Fighting around the Gulf has continued while the decision waits. A tanker was struck in its engine room about six nautical miles north of Khasab, in Oman, the fourth attack on a tanker in the Strait of Hormuz in 96 hours, according to maritime monitors, who keep the waterway at a severe threat level. The crew were reported safe. Earlier in the week Iran fired on an American airbase in Kuwait and launched attacks towards Bahrain, after American jets hit a missile launch site on Qeshm Island. Diplomacy has produced little: Qatar, the main mediator, says talks continue but warns escalation endangers any deal, and Tehran wants the blockade lifted, some sanctions eased and frozen assets released before it will talk, terms Washington has rejected.

Key points

  • Trump says he is not rushing a decision on new strikes against Iran and will decide after the start of 2027.
  • The Senate voted 51-48 to reject a war powers resolution on the Iran war; three Republicans backed it and one Democrat opposed it.
  • The Pentagon says the war has cost the US military $48.3 billion as of November 13, up from $38 billion in August.
  • A fourth tanker was hit in the Strait of Hormuz in 96 hours, and maritime monitors keep the waterway at a severe threat level.
