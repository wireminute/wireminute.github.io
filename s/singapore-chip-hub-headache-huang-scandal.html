Huang Scandal Puts Singapore's Chip Hub Reputation Under Strain

A scandal tying Nvidia founder Jensen Huang to a Singapore data centre firm has put the city-state's prized reputation as a trusted, business-friendly chip hub under uncomfortable scrutiny.

Nvidia's founder quit as chief executive on Thursday after admitting to an affair. Reports connect him with Alice Huang, who headed Megaspeed, a Singapore company running data centres and buying large volumes of Nvidia chips. For decades Singapore has built its name as a welcoming place to do business, open to both American and Chinese markets and a leading hub for the global trade in advanced chips.

Last year Singapore ranked as Nvidia's second-biggest billing centre, taking in nearly a fifth of the chip giant's earnings. Yet almost none of that money paid for chips that physically entered Singapore: the island is a hub where Asian companies place orders and send the hardware on. Since Washington curbed exports of advanced American semiconductors to China in 2022, US officials have worried Singapore could be used as a back door.

Megaspeed is the case in point. It became the biggest regional purchaser of Nvidia chips, suspected of letting Chinese firms reach them through the back door via its facilities in Malaysia, Indonesia and Thailand. In 2024, US officials visited a Megaspeed-owned data centre in Malaysia and found Nvidia hardware sitting unopened in its packaging. Singapore has brought fraud charges against four firms and five people, among them Chinese citizens, after US concerns that Chinese AI firm DeepSeek had bought Nvidia chips via third parties in Singapore.

Singapore's government said on Thursday it has examined Megaspeed since February 2025 over misstated paperwork for servers bought and shipped out through Singapore. It also looked into Alice Huang, not over spying but over any part she may have played in the suspected fraud, and found too little evidence to bring a case. She was allowed to leave Singapore in February, after officials had kept hold of her passport for months. Nearly two years on, no charges have been brought against Megaspeed or anyone linked to it, and both inquiries remain open.

The episode leaves Singapore caught between two powers. It says it will not apply export controls imposed unilaterally by other countries, though it expects companies there to respect them. Singapore has said it asked the United States repeatedly for information from Supermicro, which supplied servers to Megaspeed, but received only limited help. Nvidia, for its part, stopped listing Singapore separately in revenue reports late in 2025, and in June 2025 it barred sales to Megaspeed on an internal list, according to reports.

Key points

  • Jensen Huang left Nvidia after admitting an affair reportedly involving Alice Huang, who headed Singapore data centre operator Megaspeed.
  • Singapore was Nvidia's second-biggest billing centre last year, yet almost none of that revenue paid for chips that physically entered the island.
  • Megaspeed is suspected of letting Chinese firms reach advanced chips through the back door, via its facilities in Malaysia, Indonesia and Thailand.
  • Singapore has examined Megaspeed since February 2025 but brought no charges, saying it found too little evidence against Alice Huang.
