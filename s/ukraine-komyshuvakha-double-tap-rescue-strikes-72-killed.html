Ukraine Says Russia Struck Rescue Teams After Double-Tap Attack Killed 72

Ukraine has accused Russia of deliberately striking rescue workers as they recovered bodies from a bombed aid centre, after the death toll from the attack on the village of Komyshuvakha rose to 72.

Search and recovery work in Komyshuvakha, in the Zaporizhzhia region, came to an end on 16 November, two weeks after the attack. Ukrainian officials said 72 people had been killed and four were still missing, making it among the deadliest single attacks of the war so far.

Ukraine says Russian aircraft hit the site twice in quick succession on 2 November. The first bombs fell on a school site that served as a distribution point for humanitarian aid, and then a bomb weighing 3,000kg struck the shelter in the building's underground rooms, where residents had taken cover. President Volodymyr Zelensky said nine more bombs then fell on the shelter, and a direct hit brought down its concrete structure.

Rescue teams worked at the site for two weeks under repeated shelling. Interior Minister Ivan Vyhivskyi said Russian forces shelled the village 11 times during that period, and nine of those attacks struck the rescue site itself. The shelling wounded three police officers and four rescue workers, injured two rescue dogs and damaged five vehicles, the emergency service said.

Ukraine's military intelligence service has named the officer it says planned the attack and six pilots it says carried it out, and the security service is investigating. Russia has not answered the specific accusation. Its defence ministry said in a general statement on 2 November that its forces had hit energy and transport sites used by the Ukrainian military.

The final toll is the highest from a single strike in Ukraine for years. Only the bombing of the Mariupol theatre in March 2022 is thought to have killed more people in one attack.

Key points

  • Rescue work in Komyshuvakha ended on 16 November, two weeks after the attack, with 72 people confirmed dead and four still missing
  • Ukraine says the aid centre was hit twice on 2 November, with a 3,000kg bomb falling on the shelter where residents had taken cover
  • Russian forces shelled the village 11 times while rescuers worked, and nine of the attacks hit the rescue site directly, the interior minister said
  • Russia has not responded to the specific accusation; its defence ministry said it had hit energy and transport sites used by the Ukrainian military
