Ukraine Fires Almost 1,200 Drones at Russia in One of the War's Biggest Air Attacks

In one of the largest air attacks since the war began, Ukraine sent almost 1,200 drones toward Russia overnight, as both sides traded heavy strikes while American and Russian negotiators widened peace talks to cover the future of European security.

Ukraine launched the wave of long-range drones between late Thursday and early Friday, in one of the biggest air assaults of the war so far. Moscow said its air defences brought down 1,198 of them, though some still got through, leaving seven dead across three regions — among them a 12-year-old — and 17 wounded, according to local officials.

Ukraine's general staff said its forces hit an oil refinery in the city of Volgograd, setting it on fire, in a strike aimed at the fuel supplies that feed Russia's war effort. The overnight barrage was the latest in a campaign of deep strikes against Russian energy sites, which Kyiv has stepped up even as diplomats push for a settlement.

Russia answered with 117 drones of its own overnight, killing at least two people in Ukraine, including a 17-year-old, and knocking out power in 12 regions. A single Russian strike on Friday hit two five-storey apartment blocks in the southern city of Zaporizhzhia, wounding 30 people, including six children, officials said.

The exchange came as the diplomacy around the war took a new turn. American envoys held two days of meetings in Abu Dhabi this week with delegations from both Russia and Ukraine, before travelling to Moscow to brief President Vladimir Putin, and negotiators agreed to widen the discussions to cover Europe's post-Cold War security arrangements — a step that has alarmed European leaders, who fear their interests could be bargained away.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy also renewed a warning on Friday that speed limits on Starshield, the military-grade satellite service run by SpaceX, were holding back Ukraine's defences, capping its drones at 161 kilometres per hour along the front line and in Russian-held territory. Kyiv's forces are increasingly relying on drones and ground robots to hold a front line of about 1,200 kilometres, as both sides lean on uncrewed systems to do the fighting that soldiers once did.

Key points

  • Almost 1,200 Ukrainian drones were fired at Russia overnight — one of the war's biggest air attacks. Moscow says its defences brought down 1,198 of them.
  • Seven people died across three Russian regions, among them a 12-year-old, and 17 were wounded; Ukraine says it set an oil refinery in Volgograd on fire.
  • Russia fired 117 drones back, killing two people in Ukraine, including a 17-year-old; a strike on two apartment blocks in Zaporizhzhia wounded 30, including six children.
  • The exchange came as US and Russian negotiators widened peace talks to cover European security, alarming European leaders; Zelenskyy warned Starshield speed limits were slowing Ukraine's drones.
