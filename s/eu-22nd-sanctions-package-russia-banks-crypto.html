EU Unveils 22nd Sanctions Package Targeting Russian Banks, Crypto and Shadow Fleet

The European Commission has presented its 22nd package of sanctions against Russia, aiming to cut off more of the money funding the war in Ukraine by hitting banks, crypto channels, the shadow fleet and Russian access to European sport and culture.

The Commission presented the package in Brussels on Monday, with Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas setting out the measures. Von der Leyen said Brussels wants to sustain the squeeze on Moscow until it shows a real willingness to work towards a lasting peace, and that new measures will keep coming until then. Kallas said the pressure must keep pace with diplomacy, as Russia continues to escalate its strikes on Ukrainian cities.

The package takes aim at the money that funds the war. A transaction ban would be extended to about 60 more Russian banks, cutting them off from the SWIFT messaging system, while controls would tighten on crypto platforms and payment networks, including firms in third countries that help Russia work around the restrictions.

Energy is another focus. Dozens of actors in Russia's energy sector would be listed, along with more than 60 vessels from the shadow fleet of ageing tankers that carries Russian oil around Western restrictions. The Commission also proposes barring port entry for tankers insured in Russia, and stopping European firms from assisting with the construction, operation or repair of Russian energy plants, including in the electricity sector for the first time.

The package would add over a hundred new listings aimed at the Russian and Belarusian arms industry, including firms accused of helping to circumvent sanctions. It also proposes a visa ban for Russian combatants, shutting Russian teams out of European sport and cultural events, and barring Europeans from sitting on the governing bodies of companies operating in Russia. As a proposal, it still needs the approval of all 27 member states before any of it takes effect.

Key points

  • The Commission presented its 22nd sanctions package against Russia on Monday, aimed at the money funding the war in Ukraine.
  • About 60 more Russian banks face a transaction ban and removal from SWIFT, with tighter controls on crypto platforms and payment networks.
  • More than 60 shadow-fleet vessels and dozens of energy-sector actors are targeted, with a proposed ban on port entry for Russian-insured tankers.
  • Russian combatants face an EU visa ban, and Russian teams are to be shut out of European sport and cultural events; the package still needs approval from all 27 member states.
