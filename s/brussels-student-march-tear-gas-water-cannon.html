Tear Gas and Water Cannon as Brussels Student March Turns Tense

Thousands of students and teachers marched through Brussels on Tuesday against higher university tuition fees, education reforms and funding cuts, in a demonstration that ended with police firing tear gas and turning water cannon on the crowds.

Thousands of students and teachers marched through the streets of Brussels on Tuesday to protest against higher university tuition fees, education reforms and funding cuts. The march, held on International Students' Day, drew students and teachers from across Belgium's French-speaking community, who say the planned changes will put higher education further out of reach.

Demonstrators blocked parts of the capital's inner ring road, bringing traffic to a standstill around Arts-Loi, Botanique and Place Rogier, as they chanted against the reforms. The demonstration was largely peaceful, but police moved in to break it up as the afternoon went on, firing tear gas and deploying water cannon vehicles to disperse the crowds. Several protesters were arrested as officers cleared the streets.

The protest is the latest in a wave of student demonstrations sweeping Europe over education funding and the rising cost of living, with similar marches in France and Italy in recent weeks. In Belgium, students have been demonstrating for weeks against reforms approved by the Wallonia-Brussels Federation, which oversees French-speaking education, and earlier protests in Brussels in October also ended with police using tear gas and water cannon.

More than 250 people were arrested during two weeks of unrest in Brussels in October, according to reports, and rights groups have questioned police tactics during the demonstrations, including the frequent use of tear gas at marches attended by young people. The police oversight body has received dozens of complaints about alleged misconduct during the October protests, and the dispute over education funding shows no sign of easing.

Key points

  • Thousands of students and teachers took to the streets of Brussels on Tuesday to oppose higher tuition fees, education reforms and funding cuts.
  • The march, held on International Students' Day, blocked parts of the inner ring road around Arts-Loi, Botanique and Place Rogier.
  • Police fired tear gas and turned water cannon on the crowds to break up the march, and several protesters were arrested.
  • The protest is part of a wider wave of student demonstrations across Europe over education funding and living costs.
