Nvidia Brings Back Jensen Huang as CEO as Shares Surge by a Third

Nvidia has reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive, 11 days after he resigned, in a reversal driven by President Donald Trump that sent the chipmaker's shares up by nearly a third and lifted stock markets around the world.

The company said on Monday that its board had voted unanimously to bring Huang back as chief executive, president and board director with immediate effect. The board said it was confident, based on input from senior levels of the United States government, that his return posed no national security risk.

Huang resigned on November 12 after revelations of extramarital affairs, including one with a woman alleged to be a spy for China. His return followed pressure from Trump, industry leaders including Elon Musk, employees, shareholders and business partners. Trump said he had been represented at the board's discussion and called Huang a national treasure, and welcomed the decision as correct and proper.

Investors welcomed the news. Nvidia shares closed up 31.5 percent on Monday, their biggest one-day gain in more than 23 years, adding almost $1 trillion in market value and making the company the second biggest in the world by value after Apple. The Nasdaq rose 6.5 percent and the S&P 500 rose 4.7 percent, their biggest daily gains since markets surged after Trump paused his tariff programme, while the Dow added 3 percent. The gains followed a sharp sell-off last week in which the S&P 500 and the Dow entered corrections and the Nasdaq came close to a bear market.

The decision drew criticism from lawmakers of both parties, who said Congress should tighten restrictions on China's access to the company's advanced artificial intelligence chips. The fallout from the scandal continued elsewhere, with the United States set to blacklist the Asian AI firm Megaspeed, which is under investigation over the diversion of chips.

Huang co-founded Nvidia 33 years ago and built it into the centre of the global artificial intelligence trade, supplying the chips behind the industry's buildout. His sudden resignation had roiled markets and shaken the AI ecosystem that relies on the company for chips and funding.

Key points

  • Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive on Monday, 11 days after his resignation, with immediate effect.
  • The board voted unanimously and said it was confident his return posed no national security risk, citing input from senior levels of the US government.
  • Nvidia shares closed up 31.5 percent, adding almost $1 trillion in value; the Nasdaq rose 6.5 percent and the S&P 500 rose 4.7 percent.
  • Lawmakers from both parties criticised the decision and called for tighter restrictions on China's access to advanced AI chips.
