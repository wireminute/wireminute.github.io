Qatar Emir Pledges 15bn Euros in Germany as Berlin Visit Pushes Diplomacy to End Iran War

Qatar's Emir Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani pledged an additional 15 billion euros in investment in Germany on Friday, as his first visit to Berlin in 16 years put diplomacy to end the war with Iran at the top of the agenda.

At a joint news conference with Chancellor Friedrich Merz, the emir said lasting peace and security could only be reached through a political agreement, and that dialogue was the only way forward. Merz said the new investment, worth about 16.5 billion dollars, would deepen cooperation in artificial intelligence, computer chips and aviation, and that the two countries would work more closely on defence, technology and protection against cyberattacks.

Merz acknowledged Qatar's role in mediating between the United States and Iran, called for the Strait of Hormuz to reopen permanently, and urged Tehran to halt its military nuclear programme. The emir also met President Frank-Walter Steinmeier, who proposed a regional security conference for the Middle East. A joint statement said their talks covered efforts to end the Iran war, lift the blockade in the Strait of Hormuz and build a new regional security architecture.

Steinmeier affirmed Germany's solidarity with Qatar after Iranian attacks on the Gulf state, and the emir invited him to the Doha Forum on 5 and 6 December to discuss the proposal with other countries. The visit was the first by a Qatari emir to Germany since 2010 and builds on a 2023 agreement to hold formal strategic talks between the two countries.

Qatar has been pressing for a diplomatic end to the war between the United States and Israel and Iran, which has disrupted shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, a route for about a quarter of the world's traded energy. On Thursday, Qatar's foreign ministry warned the closure of the strait had become a global crisis affecting energy security, supply chains and the world economy, and called for renewed diplomacy with Iran. The emir's push for talks comes as the war, now in its ninth month, continues to disrupt Gulf shipping and as regional states press for a negotiated settlement rather than a wider conflict.

Key points

  • Qatar pledged an additional 15 billion euros, about 16.5 billion dollars, in investment in Germany, covering artificial intelligence, chips and aviation, with closer defence and cyber cooperation.
  • The emir said lasting peace could only come through a political agreement and pressed for dialogue to end the war with Iran.
  • Germany's president proposed a regional security conference for the Middle East, and the emir invited him to the Doha Forum on 5 and 6 December.
  • Merz called for the Strait of Hormuz to reopen permanently and urged Tehran to halt its military nuclear programme.
