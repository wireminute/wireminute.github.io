Twelve Militants Killed in Four Days of Raids Across Balochistan, Military Says

Security forces killed 12 militants in intelligence-based operations in four districts of Balochistan from November 20 to 23, the military said on Thursday.

The military's media wing, Inter-Services Public Relations, said troops targeted a militant hideout in Nushki district on November 20, killing eight men after an intense exchange of fire and recovering weapons and ammunition. The next day, troops intercepted militant movement in Sibi district and killed two more. In two further engagements on November 22 and 23, hideouts in Panjgur and Kech districts were destroyed and two more militants were killed.

The military described the dead as members of Fitna al-Hindustan, the label it uses for Baloch separatist groups it says are backed by India. The statement said the raids were part of the continuing counter-terrorism campaign under the National Action Plan, and that operations would continue until what it called foreign-sponsored terrorism was wiped out.

The raids follow a run of similar operations in the province. State media reported on November 21 that 15 militants had been killed in two operations in Mastung district under an operation the military calls Radd-ul-Fitna-3. A week earlier, the military said two soldiers were martyred and 16 militants killed in operations in North Waziristan, a day after it reported 74 militants killed in operations across Balochistan and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.

Balochistan, Pakistan's largest province by area, has faced a long-running separatist insurgency alongside other militant violence, and security forces have stepped up raids there in recent weeks. Monitoring groups say militant attacks across the country have risen year on year since the banned Tehreek-i-Taliban Pakistan abandoned a ceasefire in late 2022, although one monitor recorded fewer attacks in the week of November 6 to 12 even as deaths rose.

Key points

  • Twelve militants were killed in operations in Nushki, Sibi, Panjgur and Kech districts between November 20 and 23, the military said.
  • Eight were killed on November 20 when troops targeted a hideout in Nushki; weapons and ammunition were recovered.
  • Two more were killed in Sibi on November 21, and two in Panjgur and Kech on November 22 and 23.
  • The military described the dead as members of Fitna al-Hindustan, which it says is backed by India, and said the campaign would continue.
