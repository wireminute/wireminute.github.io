Anthropic Shares Slide Toward IPO Price as AI Sell-Off Deepens

Anthropic shares fell sharply on their second day of trading on Friday, sliding toward their $83 listing price as a wider sell-off in artificial intelligence stocks deepened.

The shares were down about 12 percent in early trading on Friday, changing hands at around $88, after falling as much as 13.5 percent before the market opened. The drop wiped out most of the gains from Thursday's debut, when the stock closed at $97.17, 17 percent above the $83 sale price, after opening at $111.50 and briefly valuing the company at $2 trillion.

The slide came as the wider market fell again. The Nasdaq Composite was down about 3.5 percent in afternoon trading, erasing its gains for 2026 and leaving it 16 percent below its record closing level, while the S&P 500 fell 2.1 percent for a seventh straight daily decline. The indexes were heading for their worst week in about a year and a half.

Anthropic's debut had been seen as a test of confidence in the AI trade after weeks of losses for the sector, but it failed to steady the market. Investors have grown nervous about the cost of building AI systems, rising borrowing costs and the high valuations of AI companies, and neither the listing nor strong earnings from chipmakers this week revived appetite for the sector's shares. Retail investors traded about $772 million of Anthropic shares on Thursday, the most for a first day of trading since records began in 2012, according to market data.

Anthropic reported a $567 million operating loss in the third quarter, driven largely by $5 billion of computing costs under short-term commitments. The company behind the Claude chatbot raised a record $81.3 billion in its listing, the largest stock market debut ever, and attention now turns to a possible listing by its rival OpenAI, which would be the next major test of investor appetite for AI shares.

Key points

  • Anthropic shares fell about 12 percent on Friday, trading near $88, close to the $83 IPO price.
  • The stock closed Thursday at $97.17 after opening at $111.50 and briefly reaching a $2 trillion valuation.
  • The Nasdaq fell about 3.5 percent, wiping out its 2026 gains, while the S&P 500 dropped for a seventh straight day.
  • The slide shows the record IPO failed to steady a market worried about AI valuations and infrastructure costs.
