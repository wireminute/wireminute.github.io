US Lawmakers Push to Raise Minimum Wage to $25 and End Tipped Pay

A group of 17 lawmakers in Washington has stepped up its campaign to lift the federal minimum wage to $25 an hour and scrap the lower rate paid to tipped workers, pressing for a vote on a bill that has waited months for action.

The push centres on the Living Wage For All Act, introduced in the House in April by Representative Delia Ramirez of Illinois, with Representatives Jesus Garcia and Analilia Mejia among its lead backers. A companion bill in the Senate was introduced by Senator Chris Murphy, and the House version now has 35 co-sponsors.

Under the plan, the federal floor would rise in stages to $25 an hour, reaching that level by 2031 for large employers, those with more than 500 staff or at least $1bn in annual revenue, and by 2038 for smaller employers. The rate would then be tied to two-thirds of the national median wage, so it could not freeze again. The bill would also end every lower rate, including the $2.13 an hour tipped rate, which has not changed since 1991.

The federal minimum has stood at $7.25 an hour since 2009, the longest stretch without a rise since the wage was created. Supporters point to a new survey by the campaign group One Fair Wage, which found 78 percent of voters said a $25 wage was an important factor in choosing candidates. The group says about half of American workers earn less than $25 an hour.

The renewed push comes as Democrats prepare to take control of both chambers in the new Congress. Congressman-elect Brad Lander of New York said he would sign on as a co-sponsor when the next session opens in January. The bill has not yet had a committee hearing, and it would need to pass before the current Congress ends in January 2027 or be introduced again.

More than half the states already require more than the federal rate. Washington, DC, has the highest minimum of any US jurisdiction at $18.40 an hour, followed by Washington state at $17.13.

Key points

  • Seventeen lawmakers are pressing for passage of the Living Wage For All Act, which would lift the federal minimum wage to $25 an hour in stages.
  • Large employers would reach $25 by 2031 and smaller employers by 2038, with the rate then indexed to two-thirds of the national median wage.
  • The bill would end the $2.13 an hour tipped rate, unchanged since 1991, along with other lower rates for young and disabled workers.
  • The federal minimum has been frozen at $7.25 since 2009; the bill has 35 House co-sponsors but has not yet had a committee hearing.
