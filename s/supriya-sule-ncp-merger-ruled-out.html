Supriya Sule Rules Out NCP Reunion, Saying Merger Talks Ended With Her Brother's Death

Supriya Sule has ruled out any reunion of the two rival factions of Maharashtra's Nationalist Congress Party, saying talks on a merger ended the day her brother and former deputy chief minister Ajit Pawar died in a plane crash.

Sule, working president of the Nationalist Congress Party (Sharadchandra Pawar) and the Lok Sabha member for Baramati, told reporters in Mumbai on Saturday that merger discussions had been under way while her brother was alive, and that a date for the reunion had even been fixed. "The merger of both factions of the party was discussed until my brother Ajit Pawar was alive. The day he left us, it put a full stop to those discussions," she said. She added that she would not speak on the matter again, and asked that personal relationships not be mixed with politics.

Ajit Pawar died on 28 January, after the aircraft carrying him crashed while attempting to land at Baramati airport in Pune district. His death left the future of his faction open: his widow Sunetra Pawar, now a deputy chief minister of Maharashtra, has taken charge of his political legacy, while Sharad Pawar leads the rival faction, with his daughter Sule as its working president.

Sule said no one from the other faction had approached her about a merger — "not a single phone call, WhatsApp message, SMS, letter or email has come to me" — and she rejected speculation that her party might join the Bharatiya Janata Party-led National Democratic Alliance. "There has been no discussion on joining the NDA," she said. "When there has been no beginning, where does the question of a full stop arise?"

The rival faction moved a day earlier to close the question down. Maharashtra NCP president Sunil Tatkare said on Friday there was "absolutely no discussion regarding a merger in our party at any level." Shiv Sena (UBT) MP Sanjay Raut said the "merger drama" would be over by the evening, adding that Sharad Pawar and Sule would not join the Ajit Pawar-led faction. Sunetra Pawar has meanwhile told the BJP leadership that her faction will continue as a separate entity rather than merge.

Key points

  • Sule says merger talks between the two NCP factions ended with Ajit Pawar's death: "The day he left us, it put a full stop to those discussions."
  • Ajit Pawar died on 28 January, when his aircraft crashed while attempting to land at Baramati airport in Pune district.
  • Sule says she has received no call, message or email from the other faction about a merger, and denies any talks on joining the BJP-led NDA.
  • The rival faction's state president, Sunil Tatkare, said on Friday there is "absolutely no discussion" of a merger at any level.
