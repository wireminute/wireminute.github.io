Russell Brand Faces Prison Rules Inquiry After Wife Reads Out Message Claiming His Innocence

British authorities are urgently investigating a message from prison by Russell Brand, after his wife read out a statement in which the convicted comedian insisted he was not guilty, days after he was jailed for ten and a half years for rape and sexual assault.

A British government inquiry is under way into a message from prison by Russell Brand, after his wife read out a statement in which the convicted comedian insisted he was not guilty. Laura Gallacher read the message on Sunday during the couple's weekly online show, days after Brand was jailed for ten and a half years for rape and sexual assault. The statement said he was not guilty of the offences he had been jailed for, and that something strange was happening.

Brand, 51, was convicted on 5 November at Southwark Crown Court in London of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault against four women, for offences committed between 2004 and 2009. The jury cleared him of two further counts of rape and one count of attempted indecent assault. On 9 November, Mr Justice Joel Bennathan sentenced him to ten years and six months in prison. He must serve two-thirds of the sentence, seven years, before he can be released, and he must stay on the sex offenders register for life.

In the message, Brand also revealed that he had been moved from HMP Wandsworth in London to HMP Littlehey in Cambridgeshire, a lower-security prison for men convicted of sex offences. A spokesperson for the prime minister called the message clearly appalling and said it was being urgently investigated, noting that prisoners are barred from making statements that cause distress to victims and from speaking to the media except in exceptional circumstances, and cannot get around the rules by having someone else publish their words. Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband said the message showed no remorse and no sympathy for the victims.

One of Brand's accusers, Mia Latter, who has waived her right to anonymity, called the message an insult to the women who gave evidence, to the jurors and to the criminal justice system, and questioned why the video had been allowed to stay online. Police said detectives were also assessing new reports about Brand in the days after he was sentenced. No sanction over the prison message has been announced, and the investigation will decide whether the message broke prison rules and what it will cost him if it did.
