EU Answers Russia's NATO Demands With Its Own List and Record Ukraine Aid

The European Union has answered Russia's demand for NATO to retreat to its 1997 lines with a list of its own maximum demands, fresh sanctions and the largest single payment ever made to Ukraine, accusing Moscow of using the talks to prolong the war.

The EU's foreign policy chief, Kaja Kallas, called Moscow's demands absurd and a pretext for keeping the war going, and said a ceasefire must come before any talk of a new European security order. She said EU foreign ministers meeting in Brussels on Monday would draw up their own maximum demands, with sanctions and frozen Russian assets as leverage. The Kremlin said again on Monday that it does not want a temporary truce.

The Council of the EU approved on Monday its largest single payment ever to Ukraine, just under 8 billion euros, after Kyiv met more than 20 reform steps. Around 5 billion euros of it comes from the Ukraine Support Loan. The bloc also imposed new sanctions on nearly 50 banks and financial institutions, along with energy-linked businesspeople and people accused of spreading pro-Russian propaganda.

The demands are in a Russian document dated November 10, obtained from a person involved in the talks, with two sources confirming it was genuine. American envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff saw it; Europeans did not. It calls for NATO to withdraw troops from its eastern flank, including Poland and the Baltic states, halt enlargement, and end military activity in Ukraine. Finland would quit the alliance, no NATO troops would go to Ukraine, arms deliveries to Kyiv would end, and Ukraine would be barred from joining.

Moscow also wants nonaggression pacts with individual European nations. Russian negotiator Alexey Drobinin said such pacts could be struck with individual NATO states, which would then get extra guarantees from Moscow. Analysts warn the pacts would stop a signatory joining a future Russia-Europe conflict, hollowing out the alliance's promise that an attack on one is an attack on all. Former Russian diplomat Boris Bondarev, who quit in protest at the 2022 invasion, said "NATO will collapse" if the plan went ahead. Ukraine has proposed holding the war at the current front lines, but Moscow has not agreed. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said his Russian counterpart "lives in a different reality", and said Washington had raised Ukraine joining NATO in exchange for leaving Donbas, unconfirmed by Kushner. Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk rejected the terms outright as a threat to his country's security.

Russian attacks killed three people in Ukraine on Monday, in the Kherson, Dnipro and Zaporizhia regions, regional officials said, and a Ukrainian drone attack in Russia's Belgorod region killed one person, local authorities said.

Key points

  • Kallas calls Russia's demands absurd and a pretext to prolong the war; the EU is drafting its own maximum demands in reply, with a ceasefire first.
  • The EU Council approves its largest-ever Ukraine Facility payment, just under 8 billion euros, after Kyiv met more than 20 reform steps.
  • New EU sanctions hit nearly 50 banks and financial institutions, plus energy-linked businesspeople and people accused of spreading pro-Russian propaganda.
  • Three people were killed in Russian attacks on Ukraine on Monday and one in a Ukrainian drone attack in Belgorod, as the Kremlin repeats it wants no truce.
