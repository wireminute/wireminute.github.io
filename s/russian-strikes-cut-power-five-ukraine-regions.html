Russian Strikes Cut Power Across Five Ukrainian Regions as Nuclear Plant Switches to Diesel

Russian attacks on Ukraine's energy system overnight and on Wednesday morning have left consumers without power in five regions, and forced the Zaporizhzhia nuclear power plant onto diesel generators, Ukraine's energy ministry said.

The ministry said Russian forces struck power plants as well as electricity transmission and distribution substations during the night and into the morning. The attacks caused new outages in the Dnipropetrovsk, Donetsk, Zaporizhzhia, Odesa and Kharkiv regions. The situation was most difficult in Zaporizhzhia and Dnipropetrovsk, where a significant number of energy facilities essential to keeping both regions supplied were damaged.

A thermal power plant run by the energy company DTEK was badly damaged in the strikes and stopped generating electricity. Sixteen specialised vehicles were damaged at a DTEK production site in Odesa, and about 9,000 customers were left without power in parts of the Odesa and Bolhrad districts. Ukraine's grid operator said the strikes damaged many facilities important for supplying electricity to the regions, without naming the sites hit.

Shelling also cut the external power supply to the Zaporizhzhia nuclear power plant, which is under Russian occupation, and the plant switched to power from its diesel generators. The loss of off-site power has happened repeatedly at the plant during the war, and backup generators are used to keep its safety systems running. The plant's six reactors are shut down, and the site relies on outside electricity or its diesel generators to run cooling and other safety systems.

Hourly power cut schedules are in effect in most regions through Wednesday, alongside limits on electricity use by industrial consumers, while repair crews work to restore supply. Russian forces also struck energy infrastructure and industrial sites in the Zhytomyr and Poltava regions overnight. The attacks are part of a campaign against Ukraine's energy system that began in late September, ahead of the heating season.

Key points

  • Overnight and Wednesday-morning attacks hit power plants and substations, causing new outages in five regions.
  • Zaporizhzhia and Dnipropetrovsk are the worst affected, with many energy facilities damaged.
  • A DTEK thermal power plant was badly damaged and stopped generating; about 9,000 customers lost power in parts of Odesa region.
  • The Zaporizhzhia nuclear plant lost external power and switched to diesel generators; hourly outage schedules apply across most regions on Wednesday.
