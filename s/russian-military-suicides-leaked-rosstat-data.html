Leaked Records Show More Than 1,000 Russian Soldiers Died by Suicide During Ukraine War

More than 1,000 Russian soldiers have died by suicide since the full-scale invasion of Ukraine began, leaked records from Russia's state statistics agency show, in a rare measure of the strain the war has placed on Moscow's armed forces.

More than 1,000 Russian soldiers have died by suicide since the full-scale invasion of Ukraine began, according to confidential records from Russia's state statistics agency, leaked and analysed. The figure, 1,087 cases identified between February 2022 and September 2026, analysts describe as almost certainly an underestimate.

The records come from a cache of more than 11 million death certificates obtained by two hacking groups, compiled by Rosstat from civil registry offices across Russia. The Kremlin does not publish figures on suicides in the military and treats casualty numbers as a state secret, so the leak offers a rare window into the strain on Russia's armed forces nearly five years into a war of heavy losses and harsh discipline.

Behind the numbers are individual cases. Sergeant Alexei Bolgov, 29, guarding a military headquarters in the occupied Donetsk region, told his wife Natalya by phone in late October 2024 that conditions were horrific and he would explain when he returned. Five weeks later he walked about 300 metres from his command post near the village of Stepove into woodland and shot himself, according to a written account by his commander.

At least 614 took place in occupied areas of Ukraine or in Russian regions bordering it, mainly Belgorod and Bryansk, and more than half involved firearms, particularly near the front lines. Another 108 men under 25 died by suicide elsewhere in Russia, away from the front. Recorded suicides have risen as the war has gone on: more than 50 in 2022, more than 200 in 2024 and a peak of 365 in 2025. The database already records almost 300 for 2026, the second-highest yearly total, even though the year's records are incomplete and lag behind the battlefield.

A further 885 soldiers in or near Ukraine were recorded as dying from firearm injuries of "undetermined intent", a category experts say can conceal suicides and that was used in several cases independently identified as suicides. Together, the suicides amount to roughly a regiment of dead. A second analysis of the same records counted at least 1,229 suicides and found Russian soldiers more than twice as likely to die by suicide as the population at large. The same leak also underpins a wider count of the war's dead: more than 460,000 deaths linked to the conflict were identified, and independent Russian outlets estimate about 700,000 soldiers dead or missing once unregistered deaths are counted.

Key points

  • Leaked Rosstat death records identify 1,087 Russian military suicides between February 2022 and September 2026, with analysts saying the true number is almost certainly higher.
  • A further 885 soldiers in or near Ukraine died from firearm injuries recorded as of "undetermined intent", a category experts say can hide suicides.
  • Suicides rose through the war, peaking at 365 in 2025, with almost 300 already recorded in incomplete 2026 data; more than half involved firearms.
  • The same leaked records point to more than 460,000 war-linked deaths, with independent estimates putting the total dead or missing at about 700,000.
