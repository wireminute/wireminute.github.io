Wales End 73 Years of Hurt With Stunning 35-28 Win Over New Zealand in Cardiff

Wales beat New Zealand 35-28 at the Principality Stadium in Cardiff on Saturday, ending 73 years without a win over the All Blacks in one of the great upsets in the sport's history.

Wales raced into a 14-0 lead inside the opening minutes, with tries from Tomos Williams, Dewi Lake and Archie Griffin putting them 21-7 ahead. New Zealand fought back to level the scores at 21-21 by half-time, with Cortez Ratima, Wallace Sititi and Fehi Fineanganofo all crossing, and the visitors threatened to take control after the break.

But Wales refused to fold. Eddie James and Louis Rees-Zammit added further tries in the second half - the fifth of the match for the hosts, the first time Wales have ever scored five tries against New Zealand - and Sam Costelow converted all five. New Zealand's Peter Lakai crossed late on, but the home side's defence held firm through a frantic finish.

The numbers told the story of the defensive effort: Wales made 346 tackles as wave after wave of New Zealand attacks was repelled, and the home crowd of 55,152 rose to celebrate a win few had dared to predict. Flanker James Botham was named player of the match, and head coach Steve Tandy called his players phenomenal after the final whistle.

The result ends a run of 34 successive defeats to New Zealand stretching back to 1953, and it is only the fourth time Wales have beaten the All Blacks in 39 meetings. New Zealand took two losing bonus points from the match, which kept them ahead in the standings, but the night belonged to Wales - a side that had won only eight of its previous 26 matches and had been written off by almost everyone before kick-off.

Key points

  • Wales beat New Zealand 35-28 at the Principality Stadium in Cardiff, their first win over the All Blacks since 1953.
  • Wales scored five tries - through Tomos Williams, Dewi Lake, Archie Griffin, Eddie James and Louis Rees-Zammit - the first time they have managed five against New Zealand.
  • New Zealand levelled at 21-21 by half-time, but Wales held on with 346 tackles as the visitors threw everything at them late on.
  • The win ends a 34-match losing run to New Zealand; flanker James Botham was named player of the match.
