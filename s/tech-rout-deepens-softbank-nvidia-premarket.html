SoftBank Sinks 15% as Global Tech Rout Deepens and Nvidia Slides Before US Open

SoftBank shares fell about 15 percent in Tokyo on Wednesday as a renewed sell-off in artificial intelligence stocks spread from Wall Street across Asia, dragging chipmakers down with it.

SoftBank Group shares fell about 15 percent in Tokyo on Wednesday, among the stock's worst sessions in more than two decades, as a renewed sell-off in artificial intelligence shares spread from Wall Street across Asia. The drop left the Japanese investment group at its lowest level in more than a year, wiping out the previous day's gains and leaving the shares roughly 70 percent below their 52-week high. It was the second near-15 percent fall for SoftBank in four trading sessions.

The selling was broad. Japan's Nikkei 225 index fell 3.6 percent, South Korea's Kospi dropped about 6 percent, and chipmakers across the region fell sharply. SK Hynix lost 11 percent and Samsung Electronics fell close to 10 percent, while in Japan Advantest and Tokyo Electron each dropped more than 12 percent and memory maker Kioxia fell more than 15 percent. Ibiden, which makes the packaging materials used in AI processors, fell 17.5 percent.

The rout followed another rough session in New York, where Nvidia fell 5.9 percent on Tuesday, giving back part of Monday's 31.5 percent surge after Jensen Huang returned as chief executive. AMD fell 8.9 percent and CoreWeave, which leases computing power built on Nvidia chips, dropped 11.9 percent. Futures on the Nasdaq 100 were down more than 2 percent on Wednesday morning, and Nvidia was about 4 percent lower in premarket trading.

Investors have grown doubtful about the pace of spending on AI, and the brief lift from Huang's return faded within a day. Questions about governance at Nvidia and the new chief executive's relationship with the White House have added to the unease, while the shaky stock market debut of the AI company Anthropic has shaken confidence further. SoftBank, which has committed tens of billions of dollars to OpenAI and owns a majority of British chip designer Arm, has become a proxy for the AI trade, so its shares swing hardest when sentiment turns. Arm shares also fell overnight.

At an all-hands meeting at Nvidia's headquarters on Tuesday, Huang apologised to staff and said he had violated the company's code of conduct, which he said he had helped to write. He said he had made terrible personal mistakes, telling employees he had been unfaithful to his wife and had hurt his children and his colleagues, and said he was now in therapy for about an hour and a half a day. He also rejected suggestions that his return had come about because of pressure from the White House, saying the board had examined the facts and that the administration had confirmed he posed no national security risk.

Traders will now watch the US open to see whether the slide extends or steadies. The sell-off has erased most of the gains from Tuesday's rally across Asia, and with the story still moving, further falls in US trading would deepen a rout that has already cut billions from the value of the world's biggest AI names.

Key points

  • SoftBank fell about 15 percent in Tokyo on Wednesday, among its worst sessions since 2000 and its lowest level in more than a year.
  • The Nikkei 225 fell 3.6 percent and South Korea's Kospi about 6 percent, with chipmakers including SK Hynix, Samsung, Advantest and Kioxia all down sharply.
  • Nvidia fell 5.9 percent on Tuesday, giving back part of Monday's 31.5 percent jump on Jensen Huang's return, and was about 4 percent lower before the US open.
  • Governance questions at Nvidia deepened after Huang told staff he had violated the company's code of conduct, apologised and said he was in therapy, while doubts over AI spending and a shaky Anthropic debut left investors treating SoftBank as a proxy for the AI trade.
