Tehran Signals It May Strike First If Another US Attack Looks Imminent

Iran's top commanders have warned that Tehran may not wait to be hit before responding, as the country's military says its doctrine is shifting from defence to offence and its supreme leader has ordered retaliation without geographic limits if the United States attacks again.

Iran's most senior military officers have signalled that Tehran may strike first if they conclude another American attack is coming. The chief of staff of the armed forces, Major General Ali Abdollahi, wrote that once signs of hostile action were confirmed, Iran's forces would decide when and how to respond without waiting for the other side to strike. The army's spokesman said Iran's military doctrine was moving from a defensive posture towards an offensive one, and pointed to an earlier Iranian strike on an American base in Jordan as an example of pre-emptive action.

The warnings follow an order from Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei, who told commanders that any renewed attack would draw a response that was immediate and effective and not confined to one region, and instructed them to draw up a fresh list of targets. Security chief Mohsen Rezaei said Iran would hit American bases near and far if attacked again, including bases in Europe if aircraft flew from them to bomb Iran, and suggested even islands thought to be beyond Iranian missiles' reach could be struck. A senior adviser to the parliament speaker has also raised pre-emptive action if Washington keeps tightening economic pressure.

Fighting is already under way on several fronts. The Revolutionary Guards said on Sunday that six drones had targeted the radar of an American air-defence site at the Riffa base in Bahrain, the second such claim in three days. The American military said it shot down two Iranian drones on Friday and reported no casualties or damage, while Bahrain issued safety alerts. American forces also struck Iranian missile and drone storage sites and radar positions after Iran attacked commercial shipping, and two oil tankers hit sea mines in the Strait of Hormuz on Saturday. In Yemen, the Houthis said they had targeted the airport and financial district in Riyadh and pumping stations on a major oil pipeline.

Washington has sent mixed signals. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said last week that the United States saw no need for another round of major operations, and President Donald Trump has not authorised a new campaign, according to people familiar with the deliberations. But American officials have warned Gulf allies to be ready for a possible short and intense campaign. The war began on February 28, when the United States and Israel launched strikes on Iran that killed the then supreme leader, Ayatollah Ali Khamenei.

Key points

  • Iran's armed forces chief says troops will not wait for an enemy attack once hostile action is confirmed, and the army says its doctrine is shifting from defence to offence.
  • Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei has ordered retaliation with no geographic limits if the United States attacks again, and a fresh list of targets.
  • Security chief Mohsen Rezaei warns American bases in Europe and on distant islands could be hit if they are used in an attack on Iran.
  • The warning comes as the Revolutionary Guards claim new drone strikes on a US radar site in Bahrain, the US hits Iranian targets, and two tankers strike mines in the Strait of Hormuz.
