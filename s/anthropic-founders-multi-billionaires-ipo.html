Anthropic's Seven Founders Set to Become Multi-Billionaires as AI Giant Goes Public

The seven people who founded Anthropic are hours away from becoming multi-billionaires, as the company behind the Claude chatbot lists its shares in one of the largest stock market debuts in history.

Anthropic is selling its shares at between $70 and $78 each, with the price set on Wednesday and trading due to begin on Thursday on the Nasdaq stock exchange under the symbol ANT. At the top of the price range, each founder's stake would be worth at least $18.5 billion. At the $74 midpoint, the stakes would be worth about $17.8 billion to $18.1 billion each, and the listing would value the company at up to about $1.4 trillion.

The seven founders left OpenAI in 2021 after falling out over the direction of artificial intelligence safety, and built Anthropic around its Claude chatbot. They are chief executive Dario Amodei, 43, president Daniela Amodei, chief compute officer Tom Brown, 39, policy chief Jack Clark, chief scientist Jared Kaplan, 42, interpretability researcher Chris Olah, 34, and chief architect Sam McCandlish, 36. Each holds roughly 240 million to 244 million shares, about a tenth of the company between them, with Brown holding the largest disclosed stake.

The founders plan to sell about 3.4 million shares each in the offering, worth roughly $250 million apiece, while keeping nearly all of their holdings. They have pledged to give 80 percent of their personal stakes to charity, though the pledge can be revoked and is not legally binding. A special class of share will also let them keep control of key shareholder votes even after the company lists.

Demand for the offering has been strong, with orders reported at about six times the shares on offer, and strategic partners including Google and Nvidia have agreed to buy $26 billion of non-voting stock. Amazon's stake of about 1.92 billion shares would be worth about $142 billion at the midpoint price. Anthropic's annualised revenue run rate stood at about $81 billion at the end of October, according to its filings.
