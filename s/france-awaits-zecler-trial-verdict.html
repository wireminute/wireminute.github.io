Three French Officers Sentenced to Prison Over Racist Beating of Music Producer Michel Zecler

A Paris court has sentenced three police officers to prison over the 2020 beating of music producer Michel Zecler, recognising the attack as racist in a landmark ruling, with each man handed a five-year term, most of it suspended.

A Paris court has sentenced the three police officers who beat music producer Michel Zecler in his studio in 2020, handing each man a five-year prison term, most of it suspended. Aurelien Lapersonne, 29, must serve two years behind bars and Philippe Tondeux, 50, one and a half years, while Pierre Plantier, 37, will serve his one year under electronic monitoring instead of in a cell. Lapersonne and Tondeux will be taken into custody at a later date, and all three men were barred from police work for life.

The court found the officers guilty of intentional violence as a group, breaking into Zecler's studio and forging an official document, and accepted that a racist motive aggravated the attack. The ruling is a landmark in France, where courts have rarely recognised racism in a police violence case, and it followed more than ten hours of deliberation by a panel of five judges. Prosecutors had asked for six years for Lapersonne, five for Tondeux and four for Plantier, with no time suspended and a permanent ban from police work.

The attack took place on 21 November 2020, when the officers stopped Zecler, now 47, outside his Paris studio over a missing face mask. Cameras in the building filmed the officers hitting him around 29 times in four minutes, then six more times in the face once he was handcuffed. Zecler suffered 45 days of incapacity and a torn biceps tendon, and told the court the officers used a racist slur during the attack. The officers have admitted the beating had no justification.

The case, filmed and shared widely, became a symbol of the debate over police violence in France, and campaigners who backed Zecler said the decision marked a shift in how the country treats racist violence by officers. The ruling comes as the Senate weighs a bill that would presume officers acted in self-defence when using firearms. The three men were tried over two weeks in Paris, six years after the assault that first brought the case to national attention.

  • A Paris court sentenced all three officers to five years in prison, with most of the time suspended: Lapersonne must serve two years, Tondeux one and a half, and Plantier one year under electronic monitoring.
  • The court found them guilty of group violence, breaking into Zecler's studio and forging an official report, and accepted racist motive as an aggravating factor.
