Three French Officers Convicted Over 2020 Beating of Michel Zecler as Court Upholds Racist Motive

A Paris court has convicted three police officers over the beating of Black music producer Michel Zecler at his studio in November 2020, upholding the racist motive as an aggravating factor and handing down prison sentences.

The Paris criminal court delivered its verdict on Friday, six years after surveillance cameras recorded Aurelien Lapersonne, Philippe Tondeux and Pierre Plantier striking Zecler about 29 times in four minutes, and six more times in the face after he was handcuffed. The court found the officers guilty of aggravated violence and forging a public document, and accepted that racist motive aggravated the attack, a point investigators had earlier set aside.

Prosecutors had asked for prison terms of six years for Lapersonne, five for Tondeux and four for Plantier, with no suspended sentences and a permanent ban from police work. Zecler, who suffered 45 days of incapacity and a torn biceps tendon, told the court the officers used a racist slur during the attack. The officers have admitted the beating had no justification.

The case, filmed and shared widely, became a symbol of the debate over police violence in France, and the verdict comes as the Senate weighs a bill that would presume officers acted in self-defence when using firearms.

Key points

  • A Paris court convicted all three officers and upheld racist motive as an aggravating factor in the 2020 beating.
  • The officers were sentenced to prison for aggravated violence and forging a public document.
  • Prosecutors had sought six, five and four years in prison, with a permanent ban from police work.
  • Surveillance cameras recorded about 29 strikes in four minutes, plus six more after Zecler was handcuffed.
