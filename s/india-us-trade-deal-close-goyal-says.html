Goyal Says India-US Trade Deal Is Nearly Done as Tariff Threat Hangs Over Talks

India's commerce minister says a long-delayed trade agreement with Washington is close to being finished, days after the United States warned it would raise tariffs on Indian goods to 25 per cent if the two sides fail to agree.

Piyush Goyal told an industry conference on Friday that the agreement was nearing completion, saying it was close to culmination. He added that the process for closing the deal, which had held up progress for months, was now taking shape. Goyal was speaking by video link to the India Debt Capital Market Summit.

His remarks came two days after the US Trade Representative set out a plan for new import duties reaching 25 per cent on Indian goods, following an investigation into what Washington calls structural excess capacity in manufacturing. Under the same plan, the rate would be held at 17 per cent if New Delhi and Washington reach an agreement.

The proposed tariffs are now open for public comment and would not take effect until next year. India's trade ministry said it was studying the notice, and that it had told Washington during the investigation that Indian industry did not suffer from structural overcapacity.

The two countries agreed the outline of a trade pact in February, under which Washington was to cut its tariff on Indian goods to 18 per cent. That framework was thrown into doubt after the US Supreme Court voided the emergency-powers tariffs on which it rested, and Indian exporters currently pay an extra 10 per cent duty under a separate American finding on forced labour.

A 17 per cent rate would leave Indian exporters slightly better off than rivals in several other Asian economies, although some exporters have said the advantage is too narrow to change much. Goyal also told the conference that India's free trade agreement with the European Union should be signed before the end of the year and take effect by March or April 2027.

Key points

  • Goyal says the India-US trade deal is close to completion, with the path to a final agreement now taking shape.
  • Washington has put forward duties of as much as 25 per cent on Indian goods, falling to 17 per cent if the two sides strike a deal.
  • The tariff plan follows a US investigation into excess manufacturing capacity and faces public consultation before taking effect next year.
  • The two sides agreed a trade framework in February, but the US Supreme Court has since struck down the emergency tariffs it was built on.
