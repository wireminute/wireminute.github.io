Malaysia to Send More Than 6,500 Myanmar Nationals Back to Myanmar

Malaysia will send more than 6,500 Myanmar nationals back to Myanmar in December, Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim told parliament on Tuesday, five days after the World Court ruled that Myanmar committed genocide against its Rohingya minority.

Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim told parliament on Tuesday that 1,500 Myanmar nationals will be taken home on three naval vessels on December 6 and 7, and a further 5,303 will be flown back during December, with Myanmar's government paying the cost of the flights. The announcement covers more than 6,500 people in total.

The repatriations come five days after the International Court of Justice ruled that Myanmar's security forces committed genocide against the Rohingya minority. The court found that about 10,000 Rohingya were killed in a 2017 military operation that also involved mass rapes and the destruction of thousands of homes.

Anwar told lawmakers that Malaysia respected the court's ruling but would keep working with Myanmar's leadership to carry out the returns, saying Malaysia had secured a guarantee of a safe and dignified return for those sent back. Malaysia already sent about 1,500 Myanmar nationals home in late September under the same scheme, which rights groups say may put people in danger by returning them to a country still at war.

Malaysia hosts nearly 200,000 refugees from Myanmar, including Rohingya who fled the military crackdown and members of other ethnic minorities. A total of 10,476 Myanmar nationals have already been sent back this year, including 1,476 taken home aboard a Myanmar naval vessel on September 29.

Anwar also told parliament that Turkiye was not prepared to take in 10,000 Rohingya refugees from Malaysia, after an announcement by his deputy suggesting such a deal was later rowed back. Rights groups have raised concerns about the repatriation scheme, saying its workings are opaque and that returnees face an uncertain fate in Myanmar.

Key points

  • Malaysia will send more than 6,500 Myanmar nationals back to Myanmar in December, Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim told parliament on Tuesday.
  • 1,500 people will travel on three naval vessels on December 6 and 7, and 5,303 more will be flown back during the month at Myanmar's expense.
  • The announcement comes five days after the International Court of Justice ruled that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya minority.
  • Rights groups say the repatriation scheme is opaque and may put returnees in danger; Malaysia says it has a guarantee of a safe and dignified return.
