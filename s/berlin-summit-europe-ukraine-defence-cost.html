Europe Wins a Seat at Ukraine Peace Talks as Trump Questions Billions in Military Aid

European leaders left the Berlin summit with a seat at the Ukraine peace talks and a pledge to keep funding Kyiv, hours after Donald Trump phoned the meeting to question the billions Europe spends on military aid and to say Vladimir Putin is ready to negotiate.

The Coalition of the Willing summit in Berlin ended with Europe securing its own place in the negotiations: Germany, France, Britain, Poland and Italy, together with the European Union and NATO, will speak for the continent. Leaders said the next step should be a first four-way meeting of Ukraine, Europe, the United States and Russia, possibly in the United Arab Emirates or another neutral location, though no date has been set.

British Prime Minister Andy Burnham said Europe was ready to enter negotiations, but insisted a peace agreement between Ukraine and Russia must come first, and only then serious talks on European security. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said Moscow's maximalist demands were not realistic, that the line of contact should be the starting point for any territorial deal, and that a settlement imposed by great powers over Europe's head, in the style of the 1945 Yalta conference, would not bring peace.

Hours after his three-way meeting with Putin and Xi Jinping in Shenzhen, Trump called into the Berlin meeting from Air Force One. According to European officials cited in reports of the call, he questioned why Europe keeps spending billions on military aid, said Putin was genuinely interested in negotiating an end to the war, and argued the money would be better spent at home. Merz pushed back, saying the support was meant to enable negotiations from a position of strength, not to prolong the war, and Burnham rejected the idea that Trump was being overly optimistic about Putin.

The summit's declaration reaffirmed the NATO allies' commitment to provide 70 billion euros in military assistance in 2026 and at least as much in 2027, alongside the European Union's 90-billion-euro Ukraine support loan running through 2027. The leaders of the E3 countries and Ukraine also called on Russia to agree a ceasefire on energy infrastructure for the winter. The American envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner attended the Berlin talks, and Kushner said bringing Europe to the table would be essential to ending the war.

Key points

  • Europe won a seat at the talks: Germany, France, Britain, Poland and Italy plus the EU and NATO will speak for the continent, with a first four-way meeting proposed.
  • Leaders say Europe is ready to negotiate, but a Ukraine-Russia peace deal must come first, before wider talks on European security.
  • Trump phoned the summit from Air Force One, questioned billions in military aid and said Putin is ready to negotiate; Merz said the aid exists to negotiate from strength.
  • Allies reaffirmed 70 billion euros in military aid for 2026 and at least as much in 2027, plus the EU's 90-billion-euro support loan.
