Europe Gathers in Berlin to Set Ukraine's Defence Bill as Trump Meets Putin in China

European leaders are gathering in Berlin on Thursday to agree funding for Ukraine's defence and to press Washington's envoys for a European seat at the peace talks, as the leaders of the United States, Russia and China hold their first three-way meeting in Shenzhen.

The gathering is hosted by German Chancellor Friedrich Merz, with French President Emmanuel Macron and British Prime Minister Andy Burnham attending. The talks are due to cover long-term security guarantees for Kyiv and continued military and financial support, with air defence at the top of President Volodymyr Zelenskyy's list of needs as Ukraine heads into winter under the threat of Russian drone attacks.

The Berlin meeting comes as the American, Russian and Chinese presidents hold their first three-way talks in Shenzhen, on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific summit. The American envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner are due in Berlin to meet Ukrainian officials and a newly formed European negotiating team drawn from France, Germany, Britain, Italy and Poland, alongside the heads of the European Commission and the European Council. The White House has endorsed a European role in the next round of negotiations, though Moscow has not yet agreed to it.

The European Commission proposed on Wednesday a further loan of 45 billion euros for Ukraine in 2027, worth about 49.3 billion dollars, with up to 25 billion euros for military aid and 20 billion euros to support the state budget. It follows a payment of 1.71 billion euros on Monday under an existing loan, for drones, ammunition and air defence. Even if the new loan is approved, a financing gap of about 25 billion euros would remain next year. Four member states have said they will stay out of the new funding: Hungary, Slovakia, the Czech Republic and Bulgaria.

Germany and Norway have each announced 300 million dollars for a NATO-coordinated programme that buys American weapons for Ukraine. Analysts say the test of the Berlin meeting is whether it produces solid pledges for Ukraine's immediate defence and next year's budget, and brings Washington and European capitals closer together. With Europe likely to pick up much of the bill for guarantees, sanctions and Ukraine's route into the European Union, its leaders want a say in decisions that will shape their security, budgets and markets.

Key points

  • European leaders meet in Berlin on Thursday to agree funding for Ukraine's defence and to push for a role in the peace talks.
  • The American, Russian and Chinese presidents hold their first three-way meeting in Shenzhen on the same day.
  • The European Commission has proposed a further 45 billion euro loan for Ukraine in 2027, with up to 25 billion euros for military aid and 20 billion euros in budget support.
  • Germany and Norway have each pledged 300 million dollars for a NATO programme that buys American weapons for Ukraine.
