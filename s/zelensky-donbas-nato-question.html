Zelensky Says US Floated Donbas-for-NATO Swap for the First Time

Ukraine's president says American negotiators have raised, for the first time, the idea of Ukraine withdrawing from its eastern Donbas region in exchange for membership of NATO, following the latest round of peace talks with Russia in Abu Dhabi.

Volodymyr Zelensky made the disclosure in remarks to journalists on Friday, released by his office on Saturday. He said the question came from the American side after the talks in Abu Dhabi on 10 and 11 November, and that it had never been put to Kyiv before. "The Americans raised a question: 'What if Ukraine joins NATO? Would Ukraine withdraw from Donbas?'" he said. "This was the first time this had been raised."

Zelensky said Kyiv's position had not changed: Ukraine will not give up its own land, and it will never recognise territory seized by Russia as Russian. But he said that if Washington and other NATO allies confirmed such an offer, he would take it to the Ukrainian people. "I will ask the opinion of our society: do we accept or do we not accept?" he said, adding that he had no right to decide such a question alone.

Any deal, he said, would have to come as part of a full security package: firm guarantees from NATO and the European Union, a strong Ukrainian army with its numbers, weapons and funding secured for a set period, and a reconstruction plan backed by money from named sources. He also said he had asked the Americans whether their guarantees would put US soldiers on Ukrainian soil, and had been told no.

There is no formal proposal yet, and the United States has not publicly confirmed Zelensky's account of the exchange. The disclosure comes days after President Vladimir Putin told President Donald Trump's envoys in Moscow that the only border line Russia accepts is the state border, and as European leaders agreed in Dublin that five nations — Britain, France, Germany, Italy and Poland — will speak for Europe at the next round of talks.

Zelensky said the very fact the question was asked showed NATO membership was back on the table. "That is why I believe NATO is back on the agenda," he said. "But you have also heard Putin's signals, and this is an extremely difficult conversation."

Key points

  • US negotiators asked, for the first time, whether Ukraine would withdraw from Donbas in exchange for NATO membership, Zelensky said.
  • Kyiv has not agreed to give up any territory and would never recognise occupied land as Russian, he stressed.
  • Zelensky would put a confirmed offer to the Ukrainian public, but only alongside a full security package: NATO and EU guarantees, a strong funded army and reconstruction money.
  • No formal proposal exists and Washington has not confirmed the account; the question followed the Abu Dhabi talks with Russia.
