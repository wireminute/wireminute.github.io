Ábrego García Arrives in Spain, Ending Two-Year Deportation Battle

Kilmar Ábrego García has arrived in Madrid, ending a nearly two-year legal fight over his deportation that at one point reached the country's highest court.

He landed in the Spanish capital on Wednesday morning on a commercial flight, travelling with his wife, who is an American citizen, and his attorney. A federal judge had told him to leave the United States by Tuesday under a negotiated agreement for a voluntary departure. His lawyers say he does not plan to settle in Spain and ultimately hopes to reach Mexico, though they have not said where he will finally live.

The case became one of the defining disputes of the current administration's deportation drive. Ábrego García, who came to the United States illegally as a teenager in 2011, was detained in March 2025 and sent to a high-security prison in El Salvador, even though a court order barred his return to his home country because he faced a risk of persecution there. An official later described the removal as an administrative error. He was brought back to the United States in June 2025.

After his return, he faced a criminal human-smuggling charge, which was later dismissed. Immigration officials then tried to remove him again, this time to a country with which he had no connection, naming Uganda, Eswatini, Ghana and, most recently, Liberia. His lawyers fought each attempt in court, and a hearing in October ended abruptly when both sides left the courtroom and the session was cancelled.

The homeland security department said it was delighted that he had self-deported, and said he had received no incentives and would not be allowed back into the United States. His lawyer said the departure was temporary and that he had many options to return. Maryland Senator Chris Van Hollen, who travelled to El Salvador last year to press for Ábrego García's release, said he was travelling to a country of his choice, a free man, unharmed and with dignity. His departure ends the government's nearly two-year effort to deport him, though his lawyers have not said where he will finally settle.

Key points

  • Ábrego García, 31, has arrived in Madrid with his wife and his attorney after leaving the US voluntarily for Spain under a court-approved agreement, ending a nearly two-year legal battle.
  • He was wrongly sent to a prison in El Salvador in March 2025 despite a court order barring his return; an official later called it an administrative error.
  • After his return to the US, officials tried to deport him to Uganda, Eswatini, Ghana and then Liberia, countries where he has no ties.
  • His lawyers say he does not plan to stay in Spain and hopes eventually to reach Mexico; his lawyer called the departure temporary and said he had many options to return to the US.
