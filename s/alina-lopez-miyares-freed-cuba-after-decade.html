American Freed After Nearly a Decade in Cuban Detention Returns to US

An American teacher held in Cuba for nearly a decade has been freed and returned to the United States, US officials confirmed on Friday, in a release that had been kept quiet for months.

Alina Lopez Miyares, a 68-year-old Cuban American teacher, was flown back to the United States in September by a US government team that travelled to Havana to collect her, officials said. The State Department said it welcomed her return and stressed that no payment was made and no exchange took place to secure her freedom.

Lopez was detained in 2017 after returning to Cuba, accused of espionage and sentenced by a military court to 13 years in prison. She was born in Cuba and left as a child, later becoming a US citizen and working as a special education teacher. Her lawyers declined to comment and asked for privacy for their client.

Advocates for Lopez have said she was lured back to Cuba in 2017 by her estranged husband, who they say was a Cuban spy. The group that campaigns for detained Americans said she had returned to the island at her former husband's urging.

Little was known publicly about her case until Friday, when the release was confirmed. Her family in Florida had spoken over the years of her wish to come home and be close to them, and campaigners for detained Americans had pressed her case while she served the sentence. Her lawyers said they had no further comment and asked for her privacy to be respected now that she is back.

The release came as tensions between Washington and Havana have risen sharply, with the US administration stepping up sanctions and an oil blockade in a pressure campaign aimed at forcing change in Cuba. The island has been under a US embargo since 1962.

Key points

  • Alina Lopez Miyares, 68, a Cuban American teacher, was freed after being held in Cuba since 2017.
  • A US government team flew to Havana in September to bring her back to the United States.
  • US officials said there was no payment and no exchange for her release.
  • She had been sentenced to 13 years in prison by a military court on espionage charges.
