Trump Official Suggested Venezuela Take Part of Guyana's Essequibo, Sources Say

A senior official in Donald Trump's administration raised the idea of Guyana handing Venezuela partial control of the Essequibo region, a vast territory that covers about two-thirds of Guyana, according to a report published on Thursday.

Jeremy Lewin, who was the State Department's policy chief until the end of October, put the idea to Guyana's Foreign Secretary Robert Persaud during a meeting in Washington in September, three people familiar with the discussions told the news agency that first reported the story. Lewin also told Persaud he could help arrange territorial talks with Venezuela's acting president, Delcy Rodriguez.

Essequibo, which lies west of the river of the same name, covers roughly 160,000 square kilometres and holds deposits of gold, diamonds and bauxite. Caracas has pressed its claim to the territory at intervals for well over a century, while Guyana says an 1899 arbitration award settled the boundary. The dispute is now before the International Court of Justice, where Guyana is asking judges to confirm the award.

Lewin confirmed to the news agency that he had held preliminary discussions with officials from both countries. He said the contacts followed a letter Rodriguez sent to Trump in May asking Washington to mediate direct talks on the territory, and said the White House had directed and approved the outreach. He did not publicly confirm the suggestion about partial control. The news agency said it had not been able to verify the letter independently.

Guyanese officials were taken aback by the suggestion and sought urgent clarification from Washington, according to the report. Guyana's foreign ministry said on Thursday that the United States had assured it that its position on the border dispute had not changed, and stressed that Essequibo's status was not negotiable. A State Department official also said Washington's position had not changed. The White House passed questions to the State Department without saying whether it had directed the talks or received the letter.

The report came a day after US Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth visited Caracas and signed a military cooperation agreement with Rodriguez, the first visit to Venezuela by an American defence secretary since US forces captured then-president Nicolas Maduro in January.

Key points

  • A former senior US State Department official raised the idea of Venezuela gaining partial control of Essequibo, which covers about two-thirds of Guyana, during talks with Guyanese officials in September, according to sources.
  • Jeremy Lewin confirmed he held preliminary talks with both sides and said the White House directed the outreach, but he did not publicly confirm the territorial suggestion.
  • Guyana says the United States has assured it that its position has not changed, and insists Essequibo's status is not negotiable.
  • Venezuela has pressed its claim to Essequibo at intervals for well over a century; Guyana relies on an 1899 arbitration award, and the dispute is before the International Court of Justice.
