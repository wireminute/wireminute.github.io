US Clashes With Russia and China at UN Over Iran Nuclear Programme

The United States has clashed with Russia and China at the United Nations Security Council over Iran's nuclear programme, as Washington pushes to keep sanctions on Tehran in force while the war over Iran continues.

At a meeting of the 15-member council, chaired this month by the United States, Russia and China moved to block discussion of the committee set up to oversee and enforce UN sanctions on Iran. The attempt was overruled by a vote of 11 to 2, with two abstentions.

The United States envoy to the United Nations, Mike Waltz, accused Moscow and Beijing of trying to shield Tehran by obstructing the work of the so-called 1737 Committee. He said every member state should be carrying out an arms embargo against Iran, banning the transfer and trade of missile technology and freezing relevant financial assets.

Waltz said the measures to be reimposed were narrowly aimed at Iran's nuclear, missile and conventional arms programmes and its support for terrorism. He said Russia and China opposed a functioning sanctions committee because they wanted to protect their partner, Iran, and keep up defence cooperation that is now once again prohibited.

He added that the UN nuclear watchdog had recently restated that Iran is the only country without nuclear weapons to have produced and stockpiled uranium enriched to 60 percent, and that Tehran had refused to give inspectors access to the stockpile.

Russia's ambassador to the UN, Vasily Nebenzya, rejected the claims and accused the United States and its allies of whipping up hysteria over Iran's nuclear ambitions in order to justify another military venture against Tehran and a wider escalation across the Middle East. China's representative, Fu Cong, called the United States the instigator of the nuclear crisis and said Washington had used force against Iran in the middle of negotiations, undermining diplomacy.

Iran's ambassador, Amir Saeid Iravani, told reporters afterwards that Tehran's nuclear programme has always been exclusively peaceful and that Iran would not recognise any attempt to reimpose sanctions. Britain and France told the council that restoring sanctions was justified because Tehran had failed to answer international concerns about its nuclear work.

President Donald Trump has repeatedly cited Iran's nuclear programme as justification for the war. The council session showed how far apart the great powers remain: Washington and its Western allies want the sanctions machinery kept alive, while Moscow and Beijing say the pressure campaign itself is the problem.

Key points

  • Russia and China failed 11-2 to block discussion of the UN sanctions committee on Iran, with two abstentions.
  • US envoy Mike Waltz accused Moscow and Beijing of shielding Tehran and obstructing the 1737 Committee.
  • The nuclear watchdog says Iran is the only non-nuclear-weapons state to have stockpiled uranium enriched to 60 percent.
  • Iran insists its programme is exclusively peaceful and says it will not recognise reimposed sanctions.
