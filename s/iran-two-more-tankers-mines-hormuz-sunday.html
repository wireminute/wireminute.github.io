Iran's Guards Claim Supertanker Sank After Mine Strikes in Strait of Hormuz

Iran's Revolutionary Guards say two oil tankers struck naval mines on an unauthorised route in the Strait of Hormuz on Sunday, and claim one of them, a supertanker carrying more than two million barrels of crude oil, sank. There is no independent confirmation of the sinking.

The Guards' navy said the two tankers were travelling on an unauthorised route in the southern part of the strait, north of Ghanam Island and east of Hormuz, when they struck mines and caught fire. It said the vessels had switched off their identification and navigation systems. One of them was a supertanker carrying more than two million barrels of crude oil, which sank after the explosion, the Guards said, according to Iranian state television. No names or flags of the ships were given, and nothing was said about the condition of their crews.

Iran's regular army navy gave a separate account of the same incident, saying two supertankers caught fire after hitting mines on Sunday afternoon after entering a prohibited area despite repeated warnings. It said both vessels stopped moving after the blasts and warned ship owners and operators that they would bear responsibility for sailing through unsafe waters with their tracking systems switched off. It gave no casualty figures and did not name either vessel.

There is no independent confirmation of the reported sinking. Britain's maritime trade office said it was aware of a report by Indian shipping authorities of an attack on a tanker in the Gulf, but it could not say whether that was the same incident the Guards had announced. The office said the crew were safe and that the damage and environmental impact were unknown. The Guards made a similar claim a week earlier that a supertanker had sunk after a mine strike, which also went unconfirmed.

The incident came a day after two tankers were hit near the Omani town of Khasab, and it is the latest disruption to shipping through the strait, which carried about a fifth of the world's seaborne crude before the war between the United States, Israel and Iran began. At least 25 tankers have been hit in and around the strait in the past seven days, and tanker traffic through the waterway has fallen sharply as owners avoid the route.

Key points

  • Iran's Revolutionary Guards say two tankers hit mines on an unauthorised route in the Strait of Hormuz, and claim one, a supertanker carrying over two million barrels of crude, sank.
  • Iran's army navy separately said two supertankers caught fire after hitting mines; neither account named the vessels or gave casualty figures.
  • There is no independent confirmation of the sinking; Britain's maritime trade office said it could not link a reported attack on a tanker to the Guards' announcement.
  • The incident follows strikes on two tankers near Oman on Saturday, with at least 25 tankers hit in and around the strait in the past week.
