Saudi Arabia Shuts Key East-West Oil Pipeline After Drone Attack Launched From Iraq

Saudi Arabia has shut down its East-West oil pipeline, the kingdom's main bypass around the blocked Strait of Hormuz, after drone attacks launched from Iraq, and Baghdad has confirmed the strikes came from its territory and dismissed a military commander.

The pipeline was shut on Friday as a precaution after drones struck pumping stations in the Riyadh and Medina regions, Saudi Arabia's energy ministry said. Several people were injured and pumping infrastructure was damaged, and emergency teams were sent to secure the line and assess the damage. The 1,200-kilometre pipeline carries crude from the kingdom's eastern fields to the Red Sea port of Yanbu, with a capacity of seven million barrels a day. It has been moving four to five percent of the world's oil supply, and it became Saudi Arabia's main export route after the Strait of Hormuz was effectively blocked during the war with Iran.

Iraq's government said on Saturday that the drones were launched from a site in Maysan province, on the border with Iran. The prime minister ordered an investigation into the province's operations command and dismissed its commander. Iraq also closed two key border crossings with Iran until further notice. The Saudi foreign ministry said the drones came from Iraq but the kingdom would not retaliate for now, to give Baghdad a chance to take the necessary measures.

No group has claimed the attack, and officials have blamed Iran-backed militias operating in Iraq. The same militias were blamed for drone attacks on Saudi oil facilities in July, which the Houthis had claimed, and Saudi and American forces bombed militia targets in Iraq after those strikes. The pipeline has been hit repeatedly in recent months: an attack on a pumping station in the Medina region in early November caused a fire, and drone attacks in September halted crude loadings from Yanbu. On Friday, the Houthis also seized an island in the Bab el-Mandeb strait, the narrow shipping lane through which around 12 percent of global trade passes.

The shutdown removes the main alternative route for Saudi crude while the Strait of Hormuz stays blocked, squeezing the world's largest oil exporter from both sides. Brent crude settled at a six-month high of $117.64 a barrel as the attacks mounted. Saudi officials said teams were working to secure the pipeline and assess its safety, but there was no word on when it would reopen.

Key points

  • Saudi Arabia shut its 1,200-kilometre East-West pipeline on Friday as a precaution after drone attacks on pumping stations; the line carries up to seven million barrels a day to Yanbu and moves four to five percent of global oil supply.
  • Iraq said on Saturday the drones were launched from its Maysan province, on the border with Iran; the prime minister ordered an investigation and dismissed the province's operations commander.
  • Riyadh said it would not retaliate for now to give Baghdad a chance to act, and Iraq closed two border crossings with Iran; no group has claimed the attack, and officials blame Iran-backed militias.
