Italy Fines Meta €805 Million Over Facebook Photo Access Without Consent

Italy's data protection authority has fined Meta €805 million — about $880 million — after finding that Facebook's Android app reached into users' phone galleries without their specific consent, in one of the largest privacy penalties the country has imposed.

The regulator said on Saturday that the Facebook app for Android devices had accessed photographs, videos and related metadata belonging to Italian users without clear consent and without telling them properly what was happening. The penalty centres on a feature offered since 2023 that suggests pictures and clips from a person's own gallery as ideas for posts while they browse.

The authority said the app asked for a single broad permission to open the gallery, without making clear that switching it on also switched on the suggestion feature. Around 20 million Android users in Italy were affected. In setting the size of the fine, it pointed to the number of people involved, the fact that the breach was still going on for users who had not updated the app or had already agreed, and what it described as a lack of cooperation from the company.

Meta now has 60 days to turn the feature off by default and ask users for a separate, specific permission, spelling out what the feature does, that it involves access to the photo gallery, and how to switch it off. The fine amounts to roughly 0.47% of the group's worldwide turnover. The regulator added that it reserved the right to look separately at a newer feature, launched this year, that lets gallery content be processed in the cloud.

The investigation began in July 2025 after a complaint from a consumer protection group in Bolzano, and included the regulator's own technical checks. It did not examine whether gallery content was used to train artificial intelligence systems — a practice the company denies. Meta had not responded publicly by Saturday afternoon, and the company can appeal the decision.

Key points

  • Italy has fined Meta €805 million (about $880 million) over Facebook's Android app accessing users' photos without specific consent.
  • The case centres on a gallery-suggestions feature running since 2023, which reached about 20 million Android users in Italy.
  • Meta has 60 days to switch the feature off by default and seek a separate, specific consent from users.
  • The investigation, opened in July 2025, did not cover whether gallery content was used to train AI systems, which Meta denies.
