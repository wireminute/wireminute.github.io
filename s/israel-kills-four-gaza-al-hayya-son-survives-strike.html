Hamas Chief's Son Escapes Gaza Strike as Israel Kills Two Near Medical Clinic

Hamas leader Khalil al-Hayya's last surviving son has escaped an Israeli air strike on his family in Gaza, as Israeli attacks killed more Palestinians across the territory on Friday, including two men near a medical clinic in Gaza City.

The strike hit at dawn on Friday, targeting a residential apartment where Izzeldeen al-Hayya, 29, was with his family, according to Hamas. He and his family survived, though several relatives were wounded, including the Hamas leader's daughter-in-law. No one was killed in that strike. The family of another of al-Hayya's sons, Azzam, who was killed earlier this year, also survived.

Izzeldeen al-Hayya is the last of the Hamas leader's sons still alive. Four of his brothers have already been killed by Israeli forces. Hamza died in 2008 and Osama in 2014. Humam was killed in September last year. Azzam died this May. Izzeldeen is the twin brother of Humam, who died in a 2025 Israeli strike on Hamas leaders in Qatar, a strike his father narrowly survived. Khalil al-Hayya was named the group's overall leader in July.

Elsewhere in Gaza on Friday, Palestinians were killed in separate attacks despite a ceasefire agreed more than a year ago. Medical sources said three people were killed and two wounded in a strike on the southern city of Rafah, while one person was killed when a drone hit a vehicle in the north of the territory. Rescue officials later put Friday's death toll at seven, with two more people killed in a strike near Gaza City's main hospital.

The Israeli military said later on Friday that it had killed two Hamas members in Gaza City who also worked as guards at a clinic run by the medical charity Doctors Without Borders. It named the two men and said the strike had not targeted a hospital and had caused no damage to medical infrastructure. Palestinian reports said the strike hit near the clinic, close to Shifa Hospital. The military also said 10 operatives had been killed in strikes across Gaza over the past week, including four it said took part in the October 2023 attacks on Israel.

Hamas condemned the attack on the al-Hayya family as an attempt to assassinate Izzeldeen, and said it was linked to threats made against his father by Israeli Defence Minister Israel Katz. A day earlier, al-Hayya had said the group could respond after Israeli attacks left at least 10 people dead in Gaza. The Israeli military had no immediate comment on Friday's strike on the family.

Key points

  • Izzeldeen al-Hayya, 29, survived a dawn strike on a residential apartment in Gaza; relatives were wounded, including his father's daughter-in-law, but no one in the family was killed.
  • He is the Hamas chief's last surviving son: four brothers were killed by Israeli forces between 2008 and this May, including his twin in a 2025 strike in Qatar.
  • Seven Palestinians were killed in separate attacks across Gaza on Friday, rescue officials said, three in Rafah, one in the north and two near Gaza City's main hospital, despite a ceasefire agreed more than a year ago.
  • The Israeli military said two Hamas members killed near a Doctors Without Borders clinic in Gaza City also worked as guards there; Hamas called the strike on the al-Hayya family an assassination attempt linked to threats by Israel's defence minister.
