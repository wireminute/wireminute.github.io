Israel Launches First Large-Scale Gaza Operation Since Ceasefire as Hamas Calls It a Flagrant Violation

Israel has launched its first large-scale military operation in central Gaza since the October 2025 ceasefire, sending tanks toward two refugee camps and closing part of the territory's main road, as Hamas called the move a flagrant violation of the truce.

The Israeli military said on Wednesday that its forces had begun what it described as a concentrated operation in three areas of central Gaza, west of the Yellow Line, the demarcation line set under the ceasefire deal. The military's civil affairs branch said the operation covered blocks numbered 660, 661 and 666, near the Al-Bureij and Al-Nuseirat refugee camps, and that sections of Salah al-Din Road, Gaza's main north-south route, would be closed in those areas until the operation ended. No timetable was given.

Tanks and military bulldozers advanced east of Al-Bureij amid artillery shelling and continuous gunfire, according to reports from the area. The military said the operation aimed to remove a threat to its forces in the area, and its civil affairs branch said it was directed at what it called terrorist infrastructure. It was the first time Israeli forces had announced such an operation since the ceasefire took effect in October 2025, and the first large-scale ground move in central Gaza since the truce was signed.

Hamas said the operation was a flagrant violation of the ceasefire agreement. In a statement, the group said the forced evacuation threats and the displacement of civilians were war crimes and a flagrant violation of international humanitarian law. Gaza's health ministry said 1,658 people had been killed and 6,000 wounded since the ceasefire began on October 11, 2025.

The operation came a day after the Board of Peace, the body overseeing the ceasefire deal, warned the United Nations Security Council that the year-old truce could collapse without immediate action, saying a ceasefire that delivers nothing visible cannot be sustained indefinitely. The board said the wider plan, which calls for Hamas to disarm, Israeli troops to withdraw and reconstruction to begin, had effectively stalled, with near-daily Israeli strikes continuing and Hamas fighters also still attacking Israeli troops.

Key points

  • Israel began a concentrated operation in three areas of central Gaza west of the Yellow Line, near the Al-Bureij and Al-Nuseirat refugee camps.
  • Tanks and bulldozers advanced as sections of Salah al-Din Road were closed; no timetable was given for the operation.
  • Hamas called the move a flagrant violation of the ceasefire and said forced evacuation threats were war crimes.
  • Gaza's health ministry says 1,658 people have been killed and 6,000 wounded since the ceasefire began in October 2025.
