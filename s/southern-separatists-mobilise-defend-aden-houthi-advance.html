Southern Separatists Mobilise to Defend Aden as Houthi Forces Close In

Southern separatists in Yemen have launched a mobilisation campaign to defend Aden as Houthi fighters advance to within about 60 kilometres of the city, the seat of the country's internationally recognised government.

Houthi fighters have seized the Al-Maqatirah and Al-Madaribah districts in Lahj province and are positioned at the Al-Kharaz camp, according to military sources cited by news agencies, bringing them within striking distance of the port city. Other reports put their coastal position about 25 kilometres from government lines near Qawah, west of Aden. Forces aligned with the government withdrew from Dhubab airport and the Bab el-Mandeb coastline on Sunday to reinforce the city's defences, ending a six-week counteroffensive to retake ground along the Red Sea coast.

The Southern Transitional Council, known as the STC, which campaigns for an independent south Yemen, has moved to fill the gap. Its leader, Aidarous al-Zubaidi, has called up fighters across the south to prepare to defend Aden and the neighbouring provinces of Lahij and al-Dhalea, and forces linked to the movement have stepped up to defend the city. The council is widely seen as backed by the United Arab Emirates, and its emergence as Aden's defender gives the separatist movement new leverage in the south.

The sudden withdrawal of government forces from the Red Sea coast this week followed their collapse there in September, when Houthi fighters seized large areas of the coastline, including the port city of Mocha. Amr al-Bidh, a special representative for the STC president, blamed the collapse on the government's campaign against the southern separatists earlier this year. Analysts say divisions among the factions opposed to the Houthis, and weak coordination between them, lie behind the setbacks.

Saudi Arabia, which has backed the Yemeni government since 2015, replaced its coalition commander on Monday as the fighting intensified. The Yemeni army said its forces had carried out 661 attacks on Houthi positions and that 173 Houthi fighters had been killed or wounded, figures that have not been independently verified. Yemen's parliament has voted unanimously to designate the Houthis a terrorist organisation. The war began when the Houthis, also known as Ansar Allah, seized the capital Sanaa in September 2014, forcing the government to flee south to Aden.

On Wednesday, Rashad al-Alimi, the head of Yemen's Presidential Leadership Council, signed the measure into law as Law No. 1 of 2026. The law designates the Houthis a terrorist organisation across their political, military and administrative structures and makes their activities and financing a criminal offence, with powers to freeze and seize assets connected to the group.

Key points

  • Houthi fighters are reported to be within about 60 kilometres of Aden after seizing districts in Lahj province, with their coastal position about 25 kilometres from government lines
  • Government forces withdrew from Dhubab airport and the Bab el-Mandeb coast on Sunday to defend Aden, ending a six-week counteroffensive
  • The Southern Transitional Council, which campaigns for an independent south Yemen, has called up fighters to defend Aden, Lahij and al-Dhalea
  • Saudi Arabia replaced its Yemen coalition commander on Monday, and Yemen's parliament voted to designate the Houthis a terrorist organisation; on Wednesday the council head signed the designation into law
