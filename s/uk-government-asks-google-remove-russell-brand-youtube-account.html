UK Government Asks Google to Remove Russell Brand's YouTube Account After Prison Message

The British government has asked Google to remove Russell Brand's YouTube account after the jailed comedian used the channel to publish a message from prison in which he maintained his innocence.

A justice minister met representatives from Google to make the request, according to Sky News. The move followed a message read out on Sunday by Brand's wife, Laura Gallacher, on the couple's weekly online show, in which Brand insisted he had done nothing wrong. Downing Street described the message as clearly appalling and said it was being urgently investigated.

Brand, 51, was jailed for ten and a half years last week after being convicted of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault against four women, for offences committed between 2004 and 2009. He is serving his sentence at HMP Littlehey in Cambridgeshire, a lower-security prison, after being moved from HMP Wandsworth. Prisoners are barred from making statements that cause distress to victims, and from speaking to the media except in exceptional circumstances.

Meta disabled Brand's accounts on Facebook and Instagram last week after his conviction, under rules that bar convicted sex offenders from its platforms. Google stopped Brand earning money from his YouTube account in 2023, after allegations against him were first reported, but the channel itself has stayed online. It has 6.6 million subscribers, and the video carrying the prison message had been viewed about 465,000 times. Google has not publicly responded to the government's request.

Officials are also considering wider plans to press social media companies to bar convicted sex offenders from their platforms, following Meta's example. Prison officials are separately reported to be considering moving Brand to another prison and reviewing his security category after the message. No decision on the YouTube account has been announced.
