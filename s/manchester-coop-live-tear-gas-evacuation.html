Don Toliver Postpones London Show After Manchester Arena Evacuation Over Unknown Substance

Don Toliver has postponed his London concert after more than 12,000 people were evacuated from his Manchester show on Sunday night, when an unknown substance was released into the crowd, and police say they still do not know what it was.

The American rapper had been due to play The O2 in London on Monday night, a day after the evacuation at Manchester's Co-op Live arena, but the show has been moved to December 15. The promoter said the change was caused by production problems after the Manchester show, and that all tickets for Monday's concert would remain valid for the new date. Full refunds for the Manchester show will be processed automatically, it said.

Toliver, whose real name is Caleb Zackery Toliver, addressed the incident for the first time in a message on Instagram, saying he was very sad to have to move the London date and that keeping everyone safe came first. He added that his thoughts were with everyone hurt in Manchester. Greater Manchester Police said the substance was let off in the crowd towards the end of the concert, shortly after 10pm, and declared a major incident. Plumes of smoke filled the arena as concertgoers left, and officers escorted more than 12,000 people safely out of the venue.

Ambulance crews assessed more than 80 people at the scene, some of them with eye and breathing symptoms, and decontamination was carried out as a precaution. One person was taken to Manchester Royal Infirmary for further assessment. The service deployed several crews, including its Hazardous Area Response Team, and advised anyone who attended the concert and later felt unwell to seek medical advice. Police first described the substance as believed to be tear gas, but later statements referred to it only as an unknown substance, and officers said work was under way to establish what it was and how the incident happened.

Assistant Chief Constable Steph Parker said the priority had been the safety of everyone at the concert and thanked concertgoers for their patience. "We will now begin to fully understand what this substance was and how this incident came about," she said. No arrests have been announced and police have not said who released the substance. The mayor of Greater Manchester, Bev Craig, said the major incident had now ended and thanked concertgoers and emergency services, while inquiries into the cause continue. Co-op Live said it was working closely with police to identify the cause. Phoebe Bridgers is due to play the arena on Thursday. Police said inquiries were continuing and the story is still developing.

Key points

  • Don Toliver has postponed his London show at The O2 to December 15 after more than 12,000 people were evacuated from his Manchester concert on Sunday night.
  • Police still do not know what substance was released into the crowd; it was first described as believed to be tear gas, but is now called an unknown substance.
  • Ambulance crews assessed more than 80 people at the scene for eye and breathing symptoms, and one person was taken to hospital.
  • The major incident has now ended, according to Greater Manchester's mayor, but the police investigation is continuing and no arrests have been announced.
