Belgium's Airports Shut Down as Air Traffic Controllers Join National Strike

Belgium's airports are shutting down for most of Monday after the country's air traffic controllers joined a nationwide strike, cancelling nearly all flights.

The company that runs Belgium's air traffic control, Skeyes, said there would not be enough controllers on duty to keep normal services running safely, so it suspended them from 7am to 9pm local time, a 14-hour halt.

Brussels Airport, the country's largest, said all departures and arrivals scheduled between 7am and 9pm would have to be cancelled. About 429 flights carrying around 59,500 passengers had been scheduled there between 6am and 10pm. Brussels South Charleroi Airport, a major base for low-cost flights, said all of its flights were cancelled for the day and warned the walkout would shut every airport in the country for the whole day.

Only flights needed for emergencies and other safety-critical purposes will continue. The suspension also covers the airports at Antwerp, Charleroi, Kortrijk, Liege and Ostend, all of which rely on Skeyes for air traffic control.

The controllers are taking part in a national day of action called by Belgium's main trade unions against the federal government's austerity measures and economic reforms. The unions oppose Prime Minister Bart De Wever's programme to cut 10 billion euros from spending over the next few years as a way of reducing the country's deficit. Airline representatives said it was the 11th strike to hit Belgian airspace in 11 months.

The shutdown was announced on Saturday, giving airlines and passengers less than two days to adjust. Airlines are rebooking affected passengers, and the knock-on effects on aircraft and crew schedules are expected to last beyond the 9pm end of the suspension. The story is still developing.

Key points

  • Nearly all flights in Belgium are cancelled on Monday as air traffic controllers join a national strike; Skeyes suspended regular services from 7am to 9pm local time.
  • Brussels Airport cancelled all departures and arrivals between 7am and 9pm; the airport had about 429 flights and close to 59,500 passengers booked for the day.
  • Charleroi Airport cancelled all flights for the day; only emergency and safety-critical flights will operate.
  • The walkout is part of a nationwide protest against the government's austerity measures; it is the 11th strike to hit Belgian airspace in 11 months.
