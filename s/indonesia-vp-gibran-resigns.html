Indonesia's Vice President Resigns in Diploma Row as His Father Rallies Behind Prabowo

Indonesia's vice president, Gibran Rakabuming Raka, has resigned two years into his five-year term, handing his resignation to parliament and to President Prabowo Subianto as a dispute over his school qualifications reached breaking point — while his father, former president Joko Widodo, publicly backed the president he once helped to power.

On Thursday, Gibran, 39, spent about 20 minutes with parliamentary leaders in Jakarta, handing over his resignation letter and asking them to act on it, the speaker of the upper house, Ahmad Muzani, told reporters. He had already sent a letter to Prabowo. Hours later the two men appeared together at the presidential palace, where Prabowo said he had accepted the resignation with a “heavy heart”. Gibran bowed, embraced the president and left without speaking to reporters.

The resignation ends weeks of pressure over whether Gibran met the schooling requirement to stand for office. Last month, Indonesia's Constitutional Court concluded that his papers did not show he had completed senior high school abroad, and on 30 October the Supreme Court annulled the equivalency certificate issued for his overseas education. Gibran was schooled in Singapore and Australia.

The court findings fed a push in parliament, where 120 lawmakers from eight parties signed a petition to open impeachment proceedings. Gibran has insisted the decision was his own and had nothing to do with his diploma. The senior law minister said he quit for personal reasons, pointing to growing public sentiment.

No Indonesian leader of his rank has stepped down since President Suharto left office in 1998. The departure also strains the alliance between Prabowo and Jokowi, who beat Prabowo in two presidential elections before backing him in 2024 — a race in which Jokowi was accused of using a court ruling to change the rules so his son could stand. Speaking outside his home in Solo, Jokowi said he would keep supporting Prabowo's government, above all its programmes for the wider public.

Parliament must now meet to declare the post vacant and ask the president for the names of two candidates; the assembly then elects a replacement within 60 days. The turmoil has already rattled markets, with the rupiah slipping to a fresh record low against the dollar.
