Zelenskyy Says US Is Too Focused on the Middle East and Not Pressing Russia Hard Enough

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has accused the United States of focusing too much on the Middle East and not pushing Russia hard enough in peace talks, saying delays in delivering anti-ballistic missiles are costing Ukrainian lives.

Speaking in an interview at his office in central Kyiv, Zelenskyy said it was "very strange" that US President Donald Trump had posted a photograph of himself embracing Vladimir Putin and describing the Russian leader as "my friend". "He's an aggressor, and a killer," he said, adding that Putin "can't be a friend", and comparing it to calling Adolf Hitler a good friend during the Second World War.

His sharpest complaint was about air defence. Zelenskyy said Ukrainian forces now destroy about 70 percent of Russia's fastest drones, a figure he said was improving, but face a huge shortfall of interceptors for shooting down ballistic missiles. He said American delays in delivering Patriot systems were costing lives, adding that the missile shortage "is people's lives".

He also confirmed that Starlink, and its military version Starshield, remain restricted in occupied areas of Ukraine, leaving drone units unable to catch up with Russian Shahed drones as they fly deeper into the country. Asked who had taken that decision, he said it was not clear. "Technically SpaceX, politically," he said, describing the situation as "like ping pong", with each side pointing to the other.

On diplomacy, Zelenskyy said the peace talks held in Abu Dhabi earlier this month had made no obvious progress, and that talks were expected to resume in December. He said Trump's two envoys often seemed more in tune with Moscow's position than with Kyiv's, and he called for "real helpful allies, who can push Russia". He defended Ukraine's sinking of the Russian warship Merkury off the Libyan coast as an "absolutely fair goal", and warned of a difficult and dangerous winter as Russian strikes target Kyiv's bridges and power grid, plunging much of the city into darkness.

Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, and the war is now in its fifth year. Washington was Kyiv's main backer under former President Joe Biden, but Trump has cut military support since returning to office and has pressed both sides toward a negotiated settlement. Zelenskyy said Ukraine would defend its capital "in any way", adding: "Without a capital, you don't have a country."

Key points

  • Zelenskyy said US delays in delivering Patriot anti-ballistic missiles are costing Ukrainian lives, with a huge interceptor shortfall.
  • He called it "very strange" that Trump posted a photograph embracing Putin as "my friend", saying Putin "can't be a friend".
  • He confirmed Starlink and Starshield remain restricted in occupied areas of Ukraine, hampering drone units.
  • Peace talks in Abu Dhabi made no obvious progress; talks are expected to resume in December, and he warned of a difficult winter ahead.
