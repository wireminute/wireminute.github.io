Four Bodies Recovered After Migrant Boat Capsizes Off Senegal as Dozens Remain Missing

Four bodies have been recovered after a migrant boat carrying about 100 people capsized off the coast of Yenne in western Senegal, with dozens still missing and only one survivor identified so far, local officials said.

Four bodies have now been recovered after a migrant boat capsized off the coast of Yenne in western Senegal, as the search continues for dozens of people still missing. Three bodies were found on the beach at Sendou, near the town, and a fourth was found at Yenne itself, local officials said. The victims included two women, aged about 35 and 45, and a man aged about 50. Only one survivor has been identified so far.

The wooden fishing boat, known locally as a pirogue, capsized in the early hours of Wednesday after striking rocks, at around 2 a.m. local time. A survivor questioned by police said about 100 people were on board, including five women. The badly damaged boat later washed ashore near Yenne, south of the capital Dakar, scattered with personal belongings. Police recovered identity documents, several mobile phones and a school document believed to belong to a high school student from the south of the country.

Search teams worked through Wednesday and were due to resume on Thursday, with dozens of people still unaccounted for. The three bodies found at Sendou were taken to a hospital morgue, and prosecutors have opened an investigation into the sinking. The exact number of people on board has still not been confirmed, and officials fear the death toll will rise as the search continues.

The wreck comes ten days after 43 people died when a boat went down off the north-western town of Thieppe. The Atlantic crossing from West Africa to Spain's Canary Islands is one of the world's deadliest migration routes. The International Organization for Migration said last month that at least 454 people had died or gone missing along the route off the West African coast this year, and Senegal has seen a sharp rise in departures this month.

Key points

  • Four bodies recovered after the Yenne capsizing: three at Sendou beach and one at Yenne; only one survivor identified so far
  • About 100 people were aboard the pirogue, which struck rocks at around 2 a.m. local time; dozens remain missing
  • Search teams worked through Wednesday and resume Thursday; prosecutors have opened an investigation
  • The wreck follows the Thieppe sinking ten days ago that killed 43; at least 454 people have died or gone missing on the Atlantic route this year
