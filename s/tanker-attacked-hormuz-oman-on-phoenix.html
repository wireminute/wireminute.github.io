Oil Tanker Attacked Off Oman in Strait of Hormuz, Crew Safe

An oil tanker was attacked while crossing the Strait of Hormuz near Oman on Monday, leaving the vessel unable to move after fire broke out in its engine room, maritime officials said. All crew members were safe.

The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations agency said the attack happened about seven nautical miles from Khasab, on Oman's Musandam Peninsula, and that the extent of the damage was not yet known. Maritime security firm Vanguard named the vessel as the Panama-flagged ON PHOENIX, saying an unknown projectile struck it about three nautical miles off the Omani coast.

Iran's navy said the tanker had been moving through a restricted area with its navigation systems switched off and had sustained significant damage. The attack was the latest in a series on shipping in the strait, where Iran says six tankers have been hit since Saturday.

Key points

  • The tanker was attacked about seven nautical miles from Khasab, Oman, and left disabled with an engine-room fire; the crew is safe
  • Monitors named the vessel as the Panama-flagged ON PHOENIX, hit by an unknown projectile
  • Iran's navy said the tanker was in a restricted area with its navigation off; Iran says six tankers have been hit since Saturday
