US Army Detonates Five Tons of Toxic Lead Explosives in Iowa, Calls Blast a Success

The US Army has carried out the controlled detonation of nearly five tons of unstable lead azide at an ammunition plant in Iowa, saying the blast went better than expected and early air monitors showed no lead readings above zero.

The US Army has destroyed the stockpile in two successive blasts on Wednesday morning. The 68 barrels, holding nearly five tons of the chemical, were blown up at about 9am Central Time at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant near Middletown, after days of delay and a last-minute court battle. Residents nearby felt two booms and saw a cloud of white and grey smoke rise over the site.

The Army called the operation a success. Adam Telle, the acting secretary of the Army, said the controlled detonation had exceeded all expectations and that the threat of an uncontrolled spontaneous explosion had been eliminated. Lee Zeldin, the head of the Environmental Protection Agency, said an initial report showed a reading of zero on the agency's air monitors, and that the magazines holding the chemical had caved inward perfectly. The agency's sensor array across the area is being watched closely, and workers have begun collecting samples, with full sampling due to be finished by the end of the day on Thanksgiving.

A plume of lead dust from the blast is drifting southwest from the plant, across southeastern Iowa and into northern Missouri, more than 100 miles from the site. The Army's projections showed the dust settling over a wide area, and residents within 20 miles of the plant have been told to shelter indoors with windows and doors shut for 24 to 48 hours. Missouri's governor said early analysis shared by federal partners suggested the mitigation measures had worked and the explosion was well contained.

The stockpile had been stored at the plant since 2009, some of it decades old, and was found to be degrading in March, with crystals forming inside the containers and leaving the chemical at risk of exploding without warning. It could not be moved because it had become too volatile to transport. The blast was first scheduled for last Friday and postponed three times amid lawsuits from environmental groups and concern from residents. A court paused the permit last week, but on Monday an appeals court refused to keep the pause in place, saying the greater danger was a sudden explosion with no safety measures. The Army used a water wall, foam barriers and gel blocks to contain the blast, and the governor of Iowa had declared a disaster emergency across three counties before it went ahead.

Key points

  • Two controlled detonations at about 9am Central Time on Wednesday destroyed 68 barrels of degrading lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant.
  • The Army called the blast a huge success, and early readings on the EPA's air monitors showed no lead above zero.
  • A plume of lead dust is drifting southwest into northern Missouri, and residents within 20 miles were told to shelter indoors.
  • The blast was delayed three times by lawsuits and a court pause before an appeals court cleared it on Monday.
