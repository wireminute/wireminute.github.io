US Army to Detonate Five Tons of Unstable Explosives in Iowa on Wednesday

The US Army will carry out a controlled detonation of nearly five tons of unstable explosive material at an ammunition plant in Iowa on Wednesday morning, after a federal appeals court cleared the way for the operation on Monday evening.

The material is lead azide, a chemical used to set off munitions, stored in 68 drums at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant near the small town of Middletown. The Army says it found the stockpile degrading in March, with crystals forming inside the containers and leaving the chemical at risk of exploding without warning. Officials say it cannot be moved because it has become too volatile to transport.

The blast is set for 9am Central Time on Wednesday, with the material destroyed where it sits, under layers of earth and concrete. An emergency alert sent to mobile phones in the area said some residents within five miles to the south-west of the site may be moved out, while people living between five and 20 miles away have been told to stay inside with their windows shut. The Army says state, local and federal agencies are all behind the plan, and that the operation will be closely watched.

The plan has been fought in court and in public for days. Environmental groups sued to stop it, warning that the blast would throw lead particles into the air over a wide stretch of Iowa and across the border into Illinois. A court paused the permit last week, but on Monday the appeals court refused to keep the pause in place, saying the greater danger was a sudden explosion happening on its own, without any of the safety measures in the permit. The Army has argued throughout that the real choice is between a planned blast carried out with precautions and an uncontrolled explosion with none.

President Donald Trump had earlier told the military to wait for the court's decision and the incoming governor's approval before going ahead. After Monday's ruling, the Army said it would go ahead as planned, and Iowa's governor backed the decision. The state's emergency operations centre has been activated, and health officials have advised people living close to the site to remain indoors for a day or two after the blast.

Key points

  • The Army will detonate nearly five tons of degraded lead azide at its ammunition plant in Iowa on Wednesday morning.
  • A federal appeals court cleared the operation on Monday, rejecting environmental groups' bid to keep it paused.
  • Officials say the chemical could explode on its own and is too volatile to be moved.
  • Residents near the plant have been told to stay indoors, and some within five miles may be moved out.
