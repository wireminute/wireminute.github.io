Trump Calls Iran War 'Effectively Over' as Tehran Claims Strike on US Radar in Bahrain

President Donald Trump has said the war with Iran is effectively over and that large-scale strikes are now unlikely, even as Tehran said it destroyed an American radar system in Bahrain and the vice president he removed from the Iran file stays sidelined.

Trump told a radio interviewer on Thursday that it would count as a defeat if Iran's leaders were still in power when he leaves office, but added that he saw no need for military force for now because the United States controlled the Strait of Hormuz. The same day he had written online that the US no longer needed a deal with Iran and told Vice President JD Vance to turn his full attention to alleged corruption in Democrat-run cities and states.

Vance had been Washington's chief negotiator with Tehran since the spring, leading the American side in talks in Islamabad in April and helping secure a memorandum of understanding in June that briefly paused the fighting and reopened shipping through the strait. The Wall Street Journal reported this week that Trump had privately questioned whether that June agreement was a costly mistake that gave Tehran time to regroup.

Analysts say losing him from the file could make a new escalation more likely. Nasser Hadian, a professor of international relations at the University of Tehran, said the reassignment was mainly a signal of confrontation with the Democrats after their sweeping midterm victories, but it also told Iran a deal was not a priority.

Tehran said early on Friday that it had destroyed a US military early-warning radar system in Bahrain, describing the attack as retaliation for an airstrike on a telecommunications tower in the southern port city of Sirik. Iran's parliament speaker and top negotiator, Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, said this week that the Strait of Hormuz would never return to its pre-war status, when commercial shipping could pass through freely.

Mediated contacts between the two sides are still going on, but no breakthrough is expected. Tehran is demanding seven conditions and a clear order of concessions to end the war — including an end to the American naval blockade, the lifting of sanctions, the release of frozen funds and an explicit end to the war — terms Washington has so far found unacceptable.

Trump has meanwhile warned that the American military is ready to act and is watching a facility linked to Iran's nuclear programme for signs of activity. With the negotiator who favoured a settlement sidelined, Tehran must plan for a Washington that has publicly taken the deal off the table.

Key points

  • Trump said the Iran war was effectively over and big strikes were unlikely, while warning the US military was ready and watching a nuclear-linked site.
  • Tehran said it destroyed a US early-warning radar system in Bahrain on Friday, calling it retaliation for a strike on a telecom tower in Sirik.
  • Vance, the chief negotiator behind the June memorandum, stays sidelined after Trump said no deal with Iran was needed.
  • Mediated contacts continue with no breakthrough expected; Tehran demands seven conditions including an end to the naval blockade and sanctions relief.
