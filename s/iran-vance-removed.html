Tehran Reads Vance's Removal as a Warning as Trump Says No Iran Deal Is Needed

Decision-makers in Tehran are weighing what the removal of Vice President JD Vance from the Iran file means for a war now in its ninth month, after President Donald Trump said the United States no longer needs a deal with Iran and told his chief negotiator to turn to domestic issues instead.

Trump announced the move on Thursday in a post on his Truth Social platform, directing Vance to give his full attention to alleged corruption and crime in cities and states run by Democrats. In the same message he said a deal with Iran was no longer a priority for his government, adding that the days of seeking one had passed.

Vance had been Washington's lead negotiator with Tehran since the spring. He led the American side in talks in Islamabad in April and helped secure a memorandum of understanding in June that briefly paused the fighting and reopened shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, before that window closed without a settlement. In late October he wrote to Iran's security chief, Mohsen Rezaei, as regional mediators tried again to narrow the gaps between the two sides.

In Tehran, Vance is seen as one of the more pragmatic voices in the American administration, and analysts say losing him from the file could make a new escalation more likely. Nasser Hadian, a professor of international relations at the University of Tehran, said the reassignment was mainly a signal of confrontation with the Democrats after their sweeping midterm victories, but it also told Iran a deal was not a priority.

The timing has sharpened the reading in Tehran. The day before Trump's post, a newspaper report said Vance was weighing a fresh round of talks with a senior Iranian official, while a source said the president knew of the plan and believed Tehran was not negotiating in good faith. Vance had also publicly blamed the midterm losses on the war, while Trump conceded the conflict had contributed to the defeat.

Mediated contacts between the two sides are still going on, but no breakthrough is expected. Tehran is demanding seven conditions and a clear order of concessions to end the war, terms Washington has so far found unacceptable. Special envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner remain the main American negotiators, and Vance could return to the file if talks ever gain traction.

Trump has meanwhile warned that the American military is ready to act and is watching a facility linked to Iran's nuclear programme for signs of activity. With the negotiator who favoured a settlement sidelined, Tehran must plan for a Washington that has publicly taken the deal off the table.

Key points

  • Trump told Vance to leave the Iran file and focus on alleged corruption in Democrat-run cities and states, saying the US no longer needs a deal with Iran.
  • Vance was Washington's chief negotiator with Tehran, leading the Islamabad talks and the June memorandum that briefly paused the war.
  • Analysts in Tehran say his removal could raise the chances of conflict, with the man seen as the more pragmatic American voice now moved aside.
  • Mediated contacts continue but no breakthrough is expected, with Tehran demanding seven conditions Washington has so far rejected.
