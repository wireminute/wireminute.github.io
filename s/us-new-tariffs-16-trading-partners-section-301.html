US Set to Unveil New Tariffs on 16 Trading Partners After Excess-Capacity Probe

The United States is poised to unveil new tariffs on 16 major trading partners, including China and the European Union, after an investigation into excess industrial capacity, with the announcement expected as soon as Tuesday.

Trade Representative Jamieson Greer is expected to announce the findings of an investigation into excess industrial capacity in 16 major trading partners, with new import duties to follow. People familiar with the plans said the results could come as soon as Tuesday evening, though the timing may still shift.

The duties would sit on top of tariffs already imposed during Trump's second term and partly replace the global import taxes the Supreme Court struck down in February. The probe, opened in March under Section 301 of the 1974 Trade Act, covers China, the European Union, India, Japan, South Korea and Mexico, and 10 others.

Final rates, product coverage and the start date are not yet decided. The administration is expected to honour caps from earlier trade deals, keeping China's overall rate at or below 20 percent and the EU's below 15 percent. The trade representative's office declined to comment.

Key points

  • The US is poised to unveil new tariffs on 16 major trading partners after a Section 301 probe into excess industrial capacity.
  • The announcement from Trade Representative Jamieson Greer could come as soon as Tuesday evening; final rates and product coverage are not yet decided.
  • The duties would add to existing tariffs and partly replace the global levies struck down by the Supreme Court in February; agreed caps would keep China at or below 20 percent and the EU below 15 percent.
