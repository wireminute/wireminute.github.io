US Proposes New Tariffs on 14 Trading Partners, With China Facing the Highest Rate

The United States has now published the per-country rates behind its proposed tariffs on 14 major trading partners, with China facing a 20 percent duty that would rise to 40 percent if a trade truce lapses, and no duties able to take effect before a public hearing on January 25.

The Office of the United States Trade Representative set out the rates on Wednesday after completing an eight-month Section 301 investigation into what it calls structural excess industrial capacity. The administration proposed headline rates of 20 to 40 percent, but said duties would be capped at lower levels agreed under trade deals already concluded or still under negotiation.

China would face the highest capped rate, 20 percent, on top of roughly 25 percent in tariffs imposed during President Donald Trump's first term. That rate matches the cap in a trade truce with Beijing that expires on January 10; if the truce unravels, the Chinese rate would rise to 40 percent, the trade representative's office said.

Vietnam would face a capped rate of 20 percent if it reaches a trade agreement with Washington, rising to 35 percent if negotiations fail. India would face 17 percent if a deal is finalised and 25 percent if talks fail. Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand and Bangladesh would face 18 percent, while Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, the European Union and Switzerland would face 15 percent, including existing duties. Singapore would face a capped rate of 10 percent.

Trade Representative Jamieson Greer, who unveiled the measures, told CNBC the caps were consistent with trade agreements reached over the past year. The duties would not apply to about 30 billion dollars of Chinese imports under consideration for tariff reductions through a bilateral mechanism known as the Board of Trade, and they would not stack on top of the duties imposed in July on a wider group of trading partners.

Beijing pushed back on the move. Foreign ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun called overcapacity a false issue, and China has rejected the claim that its export growth reflects anything other than the competitiveness of its industries. Markets were weighed down on Wednesday amid a renewed sell-off, and a new poll found 62 percent of Americans would support repealing the tariffs after the midterm losses.

The proposed duties will go through a public notice and comment period, with the hearing on January 25 standing before any of them can take effect, a step officials said could push implementation into January. The move is the second stage of an effort to rebuild the tariff programme after the Supreme Court struck down the administration's global import taxes in February. Section 301 duties carry no expiry date and have survived earlier court challenges.

The reaction from the targeted economies came swiftly on Thursday. Japan denied having excess production capacity, with the government's top spokesman, Minoru Kihara, calling it regrettable that a measure targeting Japan had been announced at all. The European Union said it rejected the premise of the new tariffs and expected the United States to stick by a deal struck last year, while Singapore said it was very disappointed and disagreed with the findings. India rejected the claim that it had structural excess capacity in any sector and said it was working towards an early conclusion of a bilateral trade agreement, and Vietnam said it was working to finalise a deal as soon as possible.

China's commerce ministry said Beijing reserved the right to take necessary measures, accusing Washington of using overcapacity concerns as a pretext for protectionism. In a separate announcement, the trade representative's office also proposed additional tariffs on goods from Brazil, including aircraft, over alleged inaction on American trade concerns; the Brazilian president's office called the measures unacceptable and said Brasilia would not yield to this type of threat.

Key points

  • The US has published per-country capped rates for its proposed Section 301 tariffs on 14 trading partners; headline rates run from 20 to 40 percent, capped at levels agreed in trade deals.
  • China faces the highest capped rate at 20 percent on top of about 25 percent in first-term tariffs, rising to 40 percent if the trade truce expiring January 10 lapses; Vietnam faces 20 percent with a deal or 35 percent without one.
  • India faces 17 percent with a deal or 25 percent without, five Southeast Asian economies face 18 percent, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, the EU and Switzerland face 15 percent, and Singapore faces 10 percent.
  • No duties can take effect before a public hearing on January 25; Beijing calls overcapacity a false issue, and a new poll found 62 percent of Americans would support repealing the tariffs.
  • Major economies including Japan, the European Union, Singapore, India and Vietnam pushed back against the proposals, China said it reserved the right to respond, and Brazil called a separate new tariff proposal unacceptable.
