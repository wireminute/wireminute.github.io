Shehbaz Sharif and Field Marshal Munir Share Top Spot in Global Muslim Influence Ranking

Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif and Field Marshal Asim Munir have jointly taken the top spot in the latest ranking of the world's 500 most influential Muslims, sharing the number one position with three other leaders in a first for the annual list.

The 2027 edition of The Muslim 500, compiled by the Amman-based Royal Islamic Strategic Studies Centre, places five leaders at joint number one: Saudi Arabia's King Salman bin Abdulaziz and Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, Pakistan's Sharif and Munir, and Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan. The centre said the shared top spot reflected the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement between Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Türkiye, a pact that commits the three countries to defend one another and which the edition presents as a turning point in how Muslim nations work together on security.

The rise is striking. A year ago, Sharif and Munir sat in the list's unranked group of 450 names, while King Salman was eighth, the crown prince 14th and Erdogan seventh. With five names sharing first place, the ranking skips second and third altogether and resumes at fourth.

The list has been published every year since 2009 and assesses the ability of individuals to shape opinion and events across the Muslim world, drawing on nominations and expert judgement. Its editors called the 2027 edition a historic turning point, saying the year had been remade by war and by the new defence pact.

For Pakistan, the ranking is a symbolic moment. Its prime minister and its army chief, listed together as a single entry, now sit above every other name in a publication that for 17 years opened with a single name at the top. The shared position puts Pakistan's civilian and military leadership at the centre of a story the editors frame as a new era of Muslim-world cooperation.

Key points

  • Shehbaz Sharif and Asim Munir share the No. 1 spot in the 2027 edition of The Muslim 500, alongside Saudi Arabia's king and crown prince and Türkiye's president.
  • Five leaders share the top position for the first time in the list's history; the ranking has been published annually since 2009.
  • The compilers cite the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement between Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Türkiye as the reason for the shared ranking.
  • A year ago, Sharif and Munir were in the list's unranked 450, while King Salman was eighth, the crown prince 14th and Erdogan seventh.
