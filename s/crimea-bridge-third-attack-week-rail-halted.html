Putin Vows 'Far More Painful' Response After Third Strike on Crimean Bridge

President Vladimir Putin has vowed a response 'far more painful' than Ukraine's strikes on the Crimean Bridge, dropping any idea of a truce on energy and Black Sea attacks after the crossing was hit for a third time in a week.

President Vladimir Putin has vowed that Russia's response to Ukraine's strikes on the Crimean Bridge will be far more painful than the attacks themselves, and said Moscow is dropping any idea of a truce covering energy sites and shipping in the Black Sea. Speaking in Shenzhen after his first three-way meeting with Donald Trump and Xi Jinping, Putin said he had asked his Security Council to consider halting strikes at sea and on energy facilities, but that the bridge attacks had ended that discussion. 'We will not negotiate,' he said.

Ukraine's military confirmed it struck the bridge again overnight, saying its navy used Neptune missiles to damage the railway tracks. Russian officials said train services had been halted while the damage was assessed, and that nobody was hurt. Sergei Aksyonov, the Russian-installed head of Crimea, said traffic across the bridge was suspended. It was the third strike on the crossing in a week, and Putin's first public reaction to the attacks. He insisted the bridge was used only by civilians and had not carried military equipment or fuel for a long time. Kyiv disputes that: Ukraine's General Staff calls the crossing a supply route for Russian forces in the south.

The latest strike follows two far heavier attacks earlier in the week. On Monday, drones hit the bridge in what Moscow called the largest drone barrage of the war, claiming 1,336 Ukrainian drones were shot down. A day later, a far heavier strike knocked out the middle of the roadway, sending its main arch into the sea, according to verified images and video. Ukraine's security service said the metal arch and the road span beneath it were completely destroyed, and President Volodymyr Zelenskyy confirmed the attack, calling it the final stage of a weeks-long operation and a response to Russian strikes on Ukrainian infrastructure. Aksyonov said two people were killed in the attack on Crimea on Tuesday.

The 19-kilometre link, opened in 2018, is one of Putin's flagship projects and a vital logistics route for Russian troops in southern Ukraine. With the crossing out of action, free ferry services between Kerch and Taman will start on Friday, the occupation authorities said. The bridge attacks came on the day Putin sat down with Trump and Xi in Shenzhen for the first three-way summit of the three leaders.

Key points

  • Putin vowed a response 'far more painful' than the bridge attacks and ruled out any truce on energy and Black Sea strikes, saying 'we will not negotiate'.
  • Ukraine confirmed its navy used Neptune missiles to damage the bridge's railway tracks in the third strike in a week; train services are halted.
  • Tuesday's strike knocked out the middle of the roadway and sent the main arch into the sea; two people were killed in Crimea that day, Aksyonov said.
  • Free ferries between Kerch and Taman start on Friday while the 19-kilometre crossing, opened in 2018, is out of action.
