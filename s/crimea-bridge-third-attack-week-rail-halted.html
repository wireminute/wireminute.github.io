Kerch Bridge Arch and Main Beam Damaged, Russia Says, as Rail Reopens and Repairs Are Weighed

Russia has given its first detailed account of the damage to the Kerch Bridge, saying the main beam of its arched span and the arch itself were damaged in Ukraine's strikes, as rail traffic resumed on one track and officials weighed how to rebuild the crossing.

Russian Deputy Prime Minister Marat Khusnullin said on Friday that the main beam of the bridge's arched span and the arch itself were damaged, and that designers were working on two options for restoring the crossing. He said the exact repair timeline would only be known once engineering decisions were made, though some sections could reopen within one to two months. A structural engineer cited in reports said replacing the road span and arch could take more than a year.

Road traffic across the 19-kilometre bridge remains closed, while rail traffic resumed on one track on Thursday evening after the latest strike, according to Russian state media. Free ferry services between Kerch and Taman began on Friday, and the Russian-installed authorities in Crimea said supplies of food, medicine and fuel had not been interrupted.

Ukraine struck the bridge three times in a week, on November 16, 17 and 19, in what President Volodymyr Zelenskyy described as the seventh stage of an operation under way for several weeks. The heaviest blow came on Tuesday, when a strike knocked out the middle of the roadway and sent its main arch into the sea, according to images verified by news agencies. Ukraine's security service said the metal arch and the road span beneath it were completely destroyed. On Thursday, Ukraine's navy used Neptune missiles to damage the railway tracks, halting trains until the partial reopening.

President Vladimir Putin has vowed a response 'far more painful' than the attacks and said Moscow is dropping any idea of a truce covering energy sites and shipping in the Black Sea. Speaking in Shenzhen after his first three-way meeting with Donald Trump and Xi Jinping, Putin insisted the bridge was used only by civilians and had not carried military equipment or fuel for a long time. Kyiv disputes that: Ukraine's General Staff calls the crossing a vital supply route for Russian forces in the south and said the short supply route from Russian territory had been destroyed. Ukraine also struck the M-14 highway, Russia's main overland route to its southern forces, on the same day as the main bridge strike.

Key points

  • Russia said the main beam of the bridge's arched span and the arch itself were damaged, with two repair options being calculated and no firm timeline yet.
  • Road traffic remains closed, but rail traffic resumed on one track on Thursday evening; free ferries between Kerch and Taman started on Friday.
  • Ukraine struck the bridge three times in a week, on November 16, 17 and 19; Tuesday's strike knocked out the middle of the roadway and sent its main arch into the sea.
  • Putin vowed a response 'far more painful' than the attacks and ruled out a truce on energy and Black Sea strikes, while Kyiv says the crossing was a vital military supply route.
