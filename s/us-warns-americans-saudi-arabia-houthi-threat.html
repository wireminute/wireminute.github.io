Houthi Drones Reach Mecca and Medina as Saudi Defences Intercept Missiles Over Riyadh

Saudi air defences intercepted two ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh and drones headed for Mecca and Medina on Sunday, as the Houthis widened their attacks to the kingdom's holy cities and fighting intensified on the ground in Yemen.

The coalition backing Yemen's government said its forces destroyed the two missiles heading for the capital and shot down drones over the Eastern Province, with two more drones intercepted on their way to Mecca and another destroyed over the Medina region. Reports on Sunday put the number of drones destroyed across the kingdom at six. Saudi civil defence sounded an alert in Riyadh shortly after 4am warning of an incoming threat, then said the danger had passed. There was no immediate word of damage or casualties.

The strikes followed a day of warnings and interceptions on Saturday, when the coalition said it had shot down two drones launched towards Riyadh and the kingdom's southern regions. The United States mission in Saudi Arabia issued a nationwide security alert the same day, urging Americans across the Middle East to exercise heightened vigilance and warning of an increased threat of armed attacks by the Houthis and of Iranian efforts to target American officials in the region. The State Department ordered non-emergency government employees and their families to leave Saudi Arabia on 23 October, and American officials now need special permission to use any airport there.

Inside Yemen, fighting intensified in Marib and al-Jawf, where government forces said they had gained ground in the al-Hazm and Madghal districts. A Yemeni army spokesman said 321 Houthi leaders had been killed or wounded in 723 operations across Taiz, Lahij, Marib and al-Jawf, with 117 military vehicles destroyed, and the coalition carrying out 37 air strikes, 56 drone attacks and 541 artillery strikes. The Houthis said the coalition had launched 72 air strikes in the past 24 hours and that they had fired ballistic missiles at government forces in the Kharaz area of Lahj. The Houthis also claimed to have shot down a Saudi coalition reconnaissance aircraft over al-Jawf, the fifth aircraft they say they have downed since the escalation began.

Houthi-affiliated media reported two women and two children injured by artillery shelling in a residential area of Sanaa's Shuaub district. More than 340,000 people have been displaced by the fighting in Yemen since August, according to the International Organization for Migration. Saudi authorities say at least 26 people have been killed in suspected Houthi missile and drone attacks in the kingdom since 23 September. The Houthis have repeatedly targeted Riyadh's international airport, the East-West pipeline and oil infrastructure in the east and south of the country.
