Houthis Say They Fired Missiles at Riyadh Airport and Financial Centre

Yemen's Houthis said on Sunday they fired two ballistic missiles at Riyadh's international airport and a financial centre in the Saudi capital, naming their targets for the first time as their campaign against the kingdom widens, while the Saudi-led coalition said its defences intercepted the missiles.

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said the two missiles were aimed at King Khalid International Airport and a financial centre in Riyadh, and claimed the operation had achieved its aim. The Saudi-led coalition said its air defences intercepted and destroyed two ballistic missiles launched toward the capital, and shot down three drones over the kingdom's Eastern Province. There was no immediate Saudi confirmation that the airport or the financial centre had been targeted, and no word of damage or casualties. Neither side's account of what the missiles hit has been independently verified.

Saree also claimed six more ballistic missiles were fired at Saudi troop positions at Qaawa camp and nearby sites in Lahj province, in southern Yemen, and said Houthi drones had struck two pumping stations on the East-West pipeline, which carries Saudi oil to the Red Sea. He dismissed Saudi reports that Houthi drones had been aimed at Mecca or Medina, calling them fabricated and saying Houthi operations targeted only oil facilities and military bases. The coalition said it had intercepted a drone in the Medina region.

The claims followed interceptions on Saturday, when the coalition said it had shot down two drones launched towards Riyadh and the kingdom's southern regions. The United States mission in Saudi Arabia issued a nationwide security alert the same day, urging Americans across the Middle East to stay vigilant over the threat of armed attacks by the Houthis and of Iranian efforts to target American officials. The State Department ordered non-emergency government employees and their families to leave Saudi Arabia on 23 October.

Inside Yemen, fighting intensified in Marib and al-Jawf, where government forces said they had gained ground in the al-Hazm and Madghal districts. A Yemeni army spokesman said 321 Houthi leaders had been killed or wounded in 723 operations across four provinces. The Houthis said the coalition had launched 72 air strikes in the past 24 hours and claimed to have shot down a Saudi coalition reconnaissance aircraft over al-Jawf, the fifth they say they have downed since the escalation began.

Houthi-affiliated media reported two women and two children injured by artillery shelling in a residential area of Sanaa. More than 340,000 people have been displaced by the fighting in Yemen since August, according to the International Organization for Migration. Saudi authorities say at least 26 people have been killed in suspected Houthi missile and drone attacks in the kingdom since 23 September.

Key points

  • The Houthis say they fired two ballistic missiles at Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport and a financial centre; the coalition says both missiles were intercepted.
  • Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree also claimed six missiles at Saudi troops in Lahj and drone strikes on two East-West pipeline pumping stations.
  • Saree dismissed Saudi reports of drones aimed at Mecca or Medina as fabricated; the coalition says it intercepted a drone in the Medina region.
  • Fighting inside Yemen intensified in Marib and al-Jawf, with more than 340,000 people displaced since August.
