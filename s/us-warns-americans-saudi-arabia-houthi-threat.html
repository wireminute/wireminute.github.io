Houthi Ballistic Missiles Intercepted Over Riyadh as Bahrain Tells Residents to Seek Shelter

Saudi defences intercepted two ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh on Sunday morning, a day after Houthi drones were downed over the kingdom, as Bahrain told its residents to move to the nearest safe place amid a widening threat across the Gulf.

Saudi air defences intercepted and destroyed two ballistic missiles launched towards Riyadh on Sunday morning, the Saudi-led coalition said, in the latest attack on the capital in a campaign of strikes that has intensified in recent weeks. The coalition's spokesman, Turki al-Maliki, announced the interception, and Saudi Civil Defence issued an alert warning residents of potential danger in the capital before announcing that the threat had passed. There was no immediate word of damage or casualties.

On the same morning, Bahrain's Interior Ministry issued an alert of its own, urging citizens and residents to remain calm and move to the nearest safe location. Reports said warning sirens sounded across the kingdom, and the ministry told the public to follow updates through official channels. It gave no details of a specific threat.

The missiles followed a day of warnings and interceptions on Saturday, when the coalition said it had shot down two drones launched towards Riyadh and the kingdom's southern regions. The United States mission in Saudi Arabia issued a nationwide security alert the same day, urging Americans across the Middle East to exercise heightened vigilance and warning of an increased threat of armed attacks by the Houthis and of Iranian efforts to target American officials in the region.

The State Department ordered non-emergency government employees and their families to leave Saudi Arabia on 23 October, and American officials now need special permission to use any airport in the country. The Houthis have launched repeated strikes on Saudi targets in recent weeks, including airports, energy infrastructure and Riyadh's financial district, as fighting between the United States, Israel and Iran grinds on and shipping through the Strait of Hormuz is disrupted by mine strikes on tankers.

Key points

  • Saudi-led coalition says it intercepted and destroyed two Houthi ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh on Sunday; Civil Defence issued an alert, then said the danger had passed.
  • Bahrain's Interior Ministry issued an alert on Sunday urging residents to remain calm and move to the nearest safe location; reports said sirens sounded across the country.
  • On Saturday the coalition said it downed two Houthi drones headed for Riyadh and the south, and the US mission warned Americans of a rising threat of Houthi attacks.
  • Non-emergency US government staff and their families were ordered out of Saudi Arabia on 23 October; the Houthis have repeatedly struck Saudi targets in recent weeks.
