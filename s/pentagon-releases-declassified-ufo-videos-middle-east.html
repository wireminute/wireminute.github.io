Pentagon Releases Three New Declassified UFO Videos Filmed in the Middle East

The United States has released three more declassified military videos of unexplained objects filmed over the Middle East, in the eighth batch of files published under its programme to open up government records on the subject.

The best known clip runs for 20 seconds and was recorded in 2020 by an infrared sensor on an American military platform in the region. It shows a bright patch moving across a grey screen while the operator pans the camera and changes magnification, then slipping out of view. The file was sent to the Pentagon’s UFO office by United States Central Command, and officials list the case as unresolved, meaning they have reached no conclusion about what was filmed. The department said it would give no assessment of the material, leaving the public to draw its own conclusions. The images on their own cannot establish how large the object was, how far away it was, or how fast it was moving, and the clip carries no radar track, pilot audio or second sensor to support it.

The other two videos were also recorded by Central Command sensors in the Middle East, one lasting 54 seconds and filmed this year, the other 13 seconds from 2023. The batch also holds 40 documents, including six 1952 intelligence files on flying saucers, a 1953 account by a senior aircraft designer, and 17 air force reports from 1956 and 1957. A separate radar slide records eight detections by civil radar over 84 seconds at heights estimated between 55,000 and 70,000 feet, which would imply a speed of about 1,650 miles per hour if the returns came from a single object, though the slide itself warns the sweep interval cannot prove that.

The release programme began after the president ordered the department in February to declassify its records on the subject, with the first batch appearing in May. The department says every case it publishes remains unresolved, a label that means it cannot determine what was seen, not that it has confirmed anything out of the ordinary. A former head of the Pentagon’s UFO office has cautioned that clips of this kind usually turn out to have ordinary explanations, and has said the public should not expect a document that settles the question.

Key points

  • The US Department of War published its eighth batch of declassified UAP files on Friday, containing 40 documents and three videos
  • The best-known clip is 20 seconds of infrared footage recorded in the Middle East in 2020, showing a bright patch crossing the sensor’s view
  • Two further videos from the region, lasting 54 seconds and 13 seconds, were also released, along with a radar slide showing eight detections over 84 seconds
  • The Pentagon lists every case as unresolved and says it will offer no assessment of any file, leaving the public to judge the material itself
