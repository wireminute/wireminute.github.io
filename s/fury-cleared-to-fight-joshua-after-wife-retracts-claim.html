Fury Cleared to Fight Joshua on December 11 After Wife Withdraws Schizophrenia Claim

Tyson Fury has been cleared to fight Anthony Joshua in Cardiff on December 11, after his wife Paris withdrew her claim that the former world heavyweight champion had been diagnosed with schizophrenia.

The British Boxing Board of Control gave Fury the all-clear on Friday, after saying it was examining the remarks and that it could have refused him a licence for the fight at Cardiff's Principality Stadium. Its general secretary, Robert Smith, said Fury had passed all of his pre-fight medical checks and that the promoter had been told on Friday morning. "Fury cleared last night. All up to date medicals cleared," Smith said.

In an early version of a new Netflix documentary about Fury, Paris Fury had said her husband was diagnosed with schizophrenia in the months after he beat Wladimir Klitschko to become world champion in November 2015. The film looks back at his struggles with depression, anxiety and bipolar disorder in that period. She corrected the account in a statement on Thursday night, saying she had been recalling a conversation from many years ago and had used the word "schizophrenic" loosely. "Whilst Tyson did show some symptoms associated with schizophrenia, he doesn't have this condition and has never received a diagnosis," she said.

Netflix said it had edited the documentary to reflect the correction before its release on Friday. Fury, a former two-time world heavyweight champion, stepped away from boxing for three years after the Klitschko win and has spoken openly about his mental health since returning to the ring.

The December 11 meeting with Joshua is one of the biggest fights in British boxing history, with the vacant WBA heavyweight title at stake. Victory would make Fury a three-time world heavyweight champion. The clearance ends a week of doubt over whether the fight, years in the making, would go ahead at all.

Key points

  • Tyson Fury has been cleared by the British Boxing Board of Control to fight Anthony Joshua in Cardiff on December 11.
  • Paris Fury withdrew her claim that her husband was diagnosed with schizophrenia, saying he has never received such a diagnosis.
  • Netflix edited its new documentary about Fury to reflect the correction before its release on Friday.
  • The vacant WBA heavyweight title is on the line, and a win would make Fury a three-time world champion.
