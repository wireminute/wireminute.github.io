Mongolia's President Khurelsukh Resigns Over Railway Corruption Claims

Mongolia's President Ukhnaa Khurelsukh said on Tuesday he would step down after the opposition accused him of taking bribes linked to a government railway project, denying any wrongdoing and saying he would leave office so the truth could be established.

Khurelsukh told a news conference in Ulaanbaatar that the accusations against him were false and aimed at damaging his reputation, and that he would give up the presidency to clear his name. He said he had never taken money from the people or the state while president, and that he would cooperate with investigators.

The accusations were made on Saturday by Nyambaatar Khishgee, a former mayor of the capital from a rival faction of the ruling Mongolian People's Party, who said Khurelsukh received 25 million dollars from the contractor Bodi International in connection with the Tavantolgoi-Gashuunsukhait railway, a coal line built while Khurelsukh was prime minister. The claim is that he ensured the contract went to the company without a competitive tender. The anti-corruption agency has said its inquiry found no evidence that 110 million dollars in bribes was paid in cash.

The opposition Democratic Party demanded on Monday that Khurelsukh resign or face impeachment, saying corruption ran deep in the country and calling for senior officials linked to the claims to step aside and face investigation. Khurelsukh, who became president in 2021, said he would hand over his duties and become an ordinary citizen.

Under Mongolian law his resignation must be accepted by the 126-member State Great Khural, which is expected to meet later this week. The parliament's speaker, Sandag Byambatsogt, will take on presidential duties, and an election for a successor must be held within four months. Two other officials named in the accusations, deputy justice minister Munkh-Erdene Dembereltseren and cabinet secretariat head Enkhbayar Battumur, also said on Tuesday they would resign. Mongolia has endured months of political turbulence, and lawmakers named a new prime minister in March, the third in less than a year, after weeks of protests against corruption.

Key points

  • President Ukhnaa Khurelsukh said on Tuesday he would step down after the opposition accused him of corruption linked to a railway project and threatened impeachment
  • He was accused of receiving 25 million dollars from contractor Bodi International over the Tavantolgoi-Gashuunsukhait railway contract; he denies any wrongdoing
  • His resignation must be accepted by the 126-member State Great Khural; the parliament speaker will take on presidential duties and an election must be held within four months
  • Two other officials named in the accusations also said they would resign, after months of political turbulence and anti-corruption protests
