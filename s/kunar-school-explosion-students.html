Seven Students Killed, Up to 99 Injured in Kunar School Blast as Probe Blames Mining Explosives Depot

Seven students were killed and up to 99 injured in Saturday's explosion beside a middle school in Afghanistan's Kunar province, an Afghan government spokesperson said, raising the official toll as investigators traced the blast to a depot where mining companies stored explosives. Hospital sources cited by an Afghan outlet still put the dead far higher, at at least 25, a figure officials have not confirmed.

The government spokesperson said seven students were killed and up to 99 injured, and an investigation found the blast happened at a depot near the school where mining companies stored explosives. The figure is higher than the hospital head's earlier preliminary count of six killed and 97 injured, and it still differs sharply from hospital sources cited by the Afghan outlet Hasht-e Subh, who put the toll at at least 25 killed and nearly 50 injured, all students. That higher figure has not been confirmed by provincial officials.

The blast struck at about 10 a.m. local time in the Andar Ser area, in a house beside the middle school. Kunar's director of information and culture, Mawlawi Sami-ul-Haq, said the house belonged to people involved in mining work who had stored dynamite there, and that the explosives were also used for work along the Kunar-Nuristan road. The force of the blast damaged the school building and several nearby homes.

Most of the injured students were taken to Nangarhar Regional Hospital in the neighbouring province for treatment, and medical staff were still counting the injured as more arrived. Earlier in the day, police spokesperson Farid Dehqan had said only that several students were killed or wounded, giving no exact figure. Investigators and security forces reached the scene to examine the unsafe storage of commercial explosives in a residential area near a school.

No group has claimed responsibility, and officials have not described the blast as an attack. Kunar, which shares a border with Pakistan, has lived with insecurity and the dangers of explosives for years, and incidents involving stored or leftover explosives near schools and places where children gather have happened before. The story is still developing, and further details are expected as officials complete their checks at the scene.
