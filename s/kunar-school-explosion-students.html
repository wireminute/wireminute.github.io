Ten Students Killed, 110 Injured in Kunar School Blast, Interior Ministry Says

At least 10 students were killed and 110 injured in Saturday's explosion beside a middle school in Afghanistan's Kunar province, the Interior Ministry said, raising the toll again as investigators blamed negligence at a depot where mining companies stored explosives. Provincial officials had earlier put the toll at seven killed and up to 99 injured, and hospital sources cited by an Afghan outlet still put the dead far higher, at at least 25, a figure officials have not confirmed.

Interior Ministry spokesperson Abdul Matin Qani said preliminary findings showed the blast happened at an explosives storage site belonging to mining companies near the school in the Andar Ser area of Asadabad, the provincial capital. The ministry said the incident was apparently caused by negligence and insufficient precaution. The new toll of 10 killed and 110 injured is higher than the provincial figures given earlier in the day: hospital head Mawla Dad first counted six killed and 97 injured, and a government spokesperson later raised that to seven killed and up to 99 injured. It still differs sharply from hospital sources cited by the Afghan outlet Hasht-e Subh, who put the toll at at least 25 killed and nearly 50 injured, all students. That higher figure has not been confirmed by provincial officials or the ministry.

The blast struck at about 10 a.m. local time in a house beside the middle school in Andar Ser. Kunar's director of information and culture, Mawlawi Sami-ul-Haq, said the house belonged to people involved in mining work who had stored dynamite there, and that the explosives were also used for work along the Kunar-Nuristan road. The force of the blast damaged the school building and several nearby homes.

Most of the injured were taken to hospital in Jalalabad, in the neighbouring Nangarhar province, while the rest were treated in Kunar, according to local reports. Earlier in the day, police spokesperson Farid Dehqan had said only that several students were killed or wounded, giving no exact figure. Investigators and security forces reached the scene to examine the unsafe storage of commercial explosives in a residential area near a school.

No group has claimed responsibility, and officials have not described the blast as an attack. Kunar, which shares a border with Pakistan, has lived with insecurity and the dangers of explosives for years, and incidents involving stored or leftover explosives near schools and places where children gather have happened before. The story is still developing, and further details are expected as officials complete their checks at the scene.

Key points

  • The Interior Ministry now puts the toll at 10 students killed and 110 injured, up from the provincial figures of seven killed and up to 99 injured given earlier in the day.
  • The ministry blamed negligence and insufficient precaution at an explosives depot belonging to mining companies near the school in Asadabad.
