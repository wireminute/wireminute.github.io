Capsized Ibiza Tanker to Be Towed to Cartagena as Fuel Tanks Found Intact

The diesel tanker that capsized off Ibiza will be towed to the Spanish port of Cartagena after inspections found its fuel tanks intact, with the vessel expected to arrive on Monday as crews work to remove its 7,000-tonne cargo without a spill.

Spain's transport ministry said on Friday that the Malta-flagged Med Emre will be towed to Cartagena, the only port that meets the technical requirements to take the diesel off the ship and carry out repairs. The tow began on Friday after checks on the vessel's structure, and the rescue ship Clara Campoamor attached a tow line that steadied the tanker and stopped it drifting. The ship is expected to reach Cartagena on Monday.

The Med Emre keeled over about 25 nautical miles (46 kilometres) off Ibiza's north-western coast in the early hours of Thursday, while sailing from Algeciras to Barcelona. A distress call reached rescuers in Valencia at about 22:30 local time on Wednesday, and minutes later the crew reported a leak and said the vessel was leaning at 90 degrees. All 17 crew members were brought to safety, but the chief engineer later died in hospital. Seven were taken to hospital, six of them with minor injuries, mainly hypothermia.

So far the fuel has stayed contained. Officials said an earlier slick seen on the water had disappeared and there was no sign of an active spill, and Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez said the information so far suggested there was no leak. Regional emergencies director Pablo Garriz said the vessel was not lying on the seabed but floating semi-submerged at the surface, its keel sticking out of the water, which makes salvage and containment work far easier than recovering a wreck from a depth of 1,000 metres. Spain's maritime pollution plan is at level two, its second-highest setting.

Taking the cargo off and putting the ship upright will take several weeks once it reaches Cartagena, the ministry said, adding that leaving the vessel in open water over the winter would unacceptably raise the risk of it sinking and spilling its load. The cause of the capsizing is not yet known and an investigation is under way. The wreck sits close to waters that matter: Ibiza's marine environment is on the UNESCO World Heritage List, home to dense meadows of Posidonia oceanica seagrass found only in the Mediterranean. Spain's worst spill came in 2002, when the Prestige tanker broke apart off Galicia and released over 60,000 tonnes of heavy fuel oil.

  • The Med Emre will be towed to Cartagena, the only port able to take off its cargo and repair it, and is expected to arrive on Monday.
  • All 17 crew were rescued, but the chief engineer later died in hospital; seven were taken to hospital, six with minor injuries, mainly hypothermia.
