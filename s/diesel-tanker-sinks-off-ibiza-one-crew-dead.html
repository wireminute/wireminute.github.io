Ibiza Tanker Found Only Partly Submerged as Crews Watch for Diesel Spill

The diesel tanker that capsized off Ibiza has been found only partly submerged, with its keel sticking out of the water, as Spanish crews work to stop up to 7,000 tonnes of diesel leaking into the sea.

The Malta-flagged chemical tanker Med Emre keeled over about 25 nautical miles (46 kilometres) off Ibiza’s north-western coast in the early hours of Thursday, while sailing from Algeciras to Barcelona. A distress call reached rescuers in Valencia at about 22:30 local time on Wednesday, and minutes later the crew reported a leak and said the vessel was leaning at 90 degrees. All 17 crew members were brought to safety, but one later died in hospital. Seven were taken to hospital, six of them with minor injuries, mainly hypothermia.

At first, officials believed the ship had gone down completely. Regional emergencies director Pablo Gárriz later said the vessel was not lying on the seabed but floating semi-submerged at the surface, its keel protruding above the water. He said that made salvage and containment work far easier than recovering a wreck from a depth of 1,000 metres.

Spain’s maritime rescue service sent a plane and the pollution control vessel Clara Campoamor to survey the area, and the national maritime pollution plan is at level two, its second-highest setting. Officials said small sheens had been seen on the water but there was no sign yet of a major leak, and no impact so far on Balearic waters or the coastline. Five of the crew were taken to Valencia and five stayed aboard a rescue vessel in good condition. The cause of the capsizing is not yet known.

The wreck sits close to waters that matter. Ibiza’s marine environment is on the UNESCO World Heritage List, home to dense meadows of Posidonia oceanica seagrass found only in the Mediterranean. Spain’s worst spill came in 2002, when the Prestige tanker broke apart off Galicia and released over 60,000 tonnes of heavy fuel oil.
