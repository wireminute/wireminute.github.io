New Migrant Caravan Crosses Into Southern Mexico and Presses North Despite Tariff Threat

Roughly 300 migrants have crossed from Guatemala into southern Mexico and merged with a bigger group heading north, a week after Mexico's president said no caravan was on the move and Washington threatened to double tariffs unless the movement stopped.

The group, most of them Central American, entered Mexico early on Friday and merged with a bigger column of close to 1,500 people that had gathered the previous week. That column has since divided into at least two groups moving through Arriaga and Tonala in Chiapas, a source who follows the movement said.

Migrants on the road said local officials ordered them out of Pijijiapan, a town further south where they had planned a two-day rest. One Honduran man travelling with his wife and two small daughters said the officers paid no attention to the tired children and told everyone to keep walking. Another Honduran in the group said he was weighing staying in Mexico to work until Mr Trump leaves office.

The crossing is the seventh migrant group to enter Chiapas this year. The state borders Guatemala and serves as a key entry point for people making their way north. It comes a week after President Donald Trump warned that tariffs on Mexican goods would double at once if the caravans were not stopped, and after US border czar Tom Homan urged Mexico to deploy soldiers along its northern border and its southern frontier with Guatemala. Mexico's president, Claudia Sheinbaum, had said last week that no caravan was on the move, that an earlier group had broken apart, and that a new one was only gathering in the south.

Key points

  • Roughly 300 people, most of them Central American, entered southern Mexico from Guatemala early on Friday and merged with a bigger northbound group of close to 1,500.
  • The bigger group has split into at least two columns moving through Arriaga and Tonala in Chiapas; it is the seventh such crossing into the state this year.
  • Migrants said officials forced them out of Pijijiapan, where they had planned a two-day rest, and some said they were considering staying in Mexico to work.
  • The crossing comes a week after President Trump threatened to double tariffs on Mexican goods unless the caravans were stopped, and after the US border czar urged Mexico to send troops to both its borders.
