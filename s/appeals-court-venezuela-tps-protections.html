Appeals Court Keeps Venezuelan Deportation Protections Frozen for Now

A federal appeals court in San Francisco has kept deportation protections for hundreds of thousands of Venezuelans suspended, hours after the Trump administration asked the Supreme Court to intervene in the case.

The 9th US Circuit Court of Appeals acted on Wednesday, putting on hold a lower-court order that would have restored the protections, known as Temporary Protected Status. The freeze will last until seven days after the appeals court hands down its full ruling in the case.

The order came the same day the Justice Department asked the Supreme Court to step in again. A federal judge in San Francisco, Edward Chen, had ruled that the administration lacked the authority to end the protections for Venezuelans, and his order was due to take effect on Friday.

The Supreme Court had already paused Judge Chen's ruling once, in October 2025, but that pause expired this month. In June, the court backed the administration's separate decisions to end the same protections for Haitians and Syrians, and it told the 9th Circuit to look again at Judge Chen's decision in light of that ruling.

Government lawyers told the courts that about 1,800 Venezuelans who once held the protected status are in detention and around 330 face removal proceedings. They said restoring the protections would halt the removal of hundreds of people with final orders against them and force the release of those held in detention.

Temporary Protected Status shields people from deportation and lets them work legally in the United States. It was granted to Venezuelans under the previous administration, and the current administration moved to end it, arguing the programme had run its course.

The administration says the programme is temporary by design and that conditions in Venezuela no longer justify renewing it. Wednesday's order settles nothing about the final outcome: the legal challenge continues, and the protections stay suspended only while the courts decide.

Key points

  • The 9th Circuit suspends a judge's order that would have restored deportation protections for Venezuelans, until seven days after its full ruling
  • The move came hours after the Justice Department asked the Supreme Court to step in again, with the lower-court order due to take effect on Friday
  • About 600,000 Venezuelans held the status at its peak; around 1,800 are in detention and about 330 face removal proceedings
  • The Supreme Court had paused the same order in October 2025; the legal challenge continues and the freeze is temporary
