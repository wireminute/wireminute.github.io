Jordan Blocks Telegram App, Pointing to Extremist Content and National Security

Jordan has moved to block the Telegram messaging app across the kingdom, with security officials saying the step is needed to stop the spread of illegal and extremist material and to protect national security.

The Public Security Directorate announced the decision in a statement on Monday, saying it was part of wider efforts against online crime and to make digital services safer. The directorate urged people to be careful when using social media apps, to stay away from accounts, links and groups they do not recognise, and to report unlawful content through official channels.

The ban comes during a security crackdown that followed a deadly attack on a Greek Orthodox church last month, in which nine people were killed in the village of Hammoud in the western governorate of Karak. On Sunday, a court handed death sentences to three men convicted over the attack. Jordan has also said its warplanes hit targets in Syria, killing 20 fighters from the ISIL group that Amman links to the church attack. Separately, an officer was killed and a second wounded in the capital this month, in an incident the authorities described as another terrorist attack.

Telegram was launched in 2013 by the Russian-born entrepreneur Pavel Durov, and it is widely used across the Middle East and North Africa, a region that accounts for about eight percent of its users worldwide. The app offers end-to-end encrypted chats, though not every conversation on it is encrypted, and security agencies have long said it is used by armed groups including ISIL. Durov is currently believed to be in Dubai, and in July Russia issued an arrest warrant for him, accusing him of aiding terrorism. He also faces investigations in several other countries.

The statement gave no date for when the block would take effect, and there was no immediate public response from Telegram.

Key points

  • Jordan's Public Security Directorate announced on Monday that Telegram will be blocked in the kingdom, citing national security and the spread of illegal and extremist material.
  • The move follows a crackdown after a church attack in Karak last month that killed nine people; three men were sentenced to death over the attack on Sunday.
  • Jordan says it also struck ISIL-linked targets in Syria after the attack, and a security officer was killed in Amman this month in a separate incident.
  • Telegram, founded in 2013 and popular across the Middle East and North Africa, offers encrypted chats; its founder Pavel Durov faces a Russian arrest warrant and investigations elsewhere.
