Classified NSA Network Back Up After Outage, US Official Says

The National Security Agency's classified internal communications network is back up after an outage on Tuesday that forced the agency to stagger staff arrivals at its Fort Meade headquarters, a US official said.

NSA Net, the system agency employees use to share classified information internally, stopped working on Tuesday, CNN reported, citing six people familiar with the matter. The agency staggered arrival times for staff at its Fort Meade campus in Maryland while it assessed how widely operations were affected, and said redundancies would keep urgent intelligence reaching the president during his trip to Asia.

The network has since been restored, the New York Post reported, citing a US official, who said the system had experienced temporary degradation and that mission-critical capabilities remained fully operational throughout. The agency has not commented publicly, and no official cause has been given.

Nothing so far points to a cyberattack, the sources said, and some suggested the failure may be linked to software upgrades on the network. A person familiar with the incident told the Wall Street Journal the agency was updating software to patch the system and that the network would only be offline temporarily. The White House said it saw no evidence of a cyberattack.

The outage was the latest in a run of technology failures across the United States this week, including a global Microsoft outage and a software fault that disrupted banking services. The country's top homeland security official said on Tuesday that the failures were blamed on ageing computer systems, not cyberattacks.
