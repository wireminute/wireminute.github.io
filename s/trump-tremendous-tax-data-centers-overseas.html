Trump Vows Tremendous Tax for US Firms Building Data Centers Abroad

President Donald Trump said on Friday that US technology companies will face a "tremendous tax" if they build artificial intelligence data centers in other countries, in a move he said was aimed at keeping the facilities, and the AI race with China, on American soil.

Speaking to reporters at the White House on Friday, Trump said the government would penalize companies that build data centers abroad. "We're not allowing data centers to go into other countries because China will take all of them, and we're not allowing that to happen," he said. "And we're going to put tremendous penalties on companies that try and do it." He said the administration would fight very hard to encourage companies to build the facilities in the United States.

Trump gave no details of how the tax would work and said he had not yet told his own officials about the plan. He pointed to a proposed data center in Finland as a project he would not allow. The remarks came days after his administration tightened rules on advanced AI chips, requiring licenses for chips and servers sent to data centers anywhere in the world that may provide computing power to companies in China and other restricted countries.

Data centers have become a political flashpoint in the United States, with host communities pushing back over electricity use, water and local disruption. Trump urged Americans to welcome construction in their communities and extract concessions from developers. "You ought to take advantage of the data centers. Make them pay you, make them pay your community, but don't shut them off," he said.

The world's largest technology companies have responded to the backlash by promising to take fewer taxpayer handouts, be more open about their projects and offer more money to host communities. Opposition to data centers was a potent issue in the midterm elections, in which Democrats took control of Congress.

Key points

  • Trump said US firms building AI data centers abroad will face a "tremendous tax" and penalties.
  • He gave no details and said he had not yet told his own officials about the plan.
  • He cited a proposed data center in Finland as one he would not allow, framing the move as competition with China on AI.
  • He urged US communities to welcome data centers and make developers pay, as tech giants promise more concessions to hosts.
