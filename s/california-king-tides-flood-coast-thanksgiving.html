King Tides Flood California Coast as Record Surf Pounds Beaches Over Thanksgiving

Record-high tides and powerful surf flooded parts of California's coast on Wednesday, smashing a section of the Pacifica Pier and pushing seawater into streets and homes as millions of Americans travelled for Thanksgiving.

Water levels in San Francisco Bay reached their highest November mark in over four decades on Tuesday morning, the National Weather Service said, and waves of up to 30 feet were reported at Ocean Beach on Wednesday. Waves smashed a section of the Pacifica Municipal Pier into the sea on Wednesday morning, and water pushed over a seawall into nearby neighbourhoods and roads. Cars were partly submerged and residents waded through knee-deep water in places.

Coastal flood warnings were in effect along the Pacific coast and around San Francisco Bay through Thursday afternoon, with breaking waves of 20 to 30 feet forecast. Evacuation warnings in Stinson Beach were lifted on Wednesday, while communities in Marin County sandbagged homes. The same storm system forced the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade in New York to be postponed to Saturday, the first time the parade has ever been delayed by weather.
