Trump Calls Pre-Election GDP Figures 'Fake' and Demands Investigation

Donald Trump has accused his own Commerce Department of deliberately releasing incorrect economic growth figures before this month's midterm elections, calling the numbers 'fake' and demanding an immediate investigation.

The Bureau of Economic Analysis, part of the Commerce Department, said on Wednesday that the economy grew at an annual rate of 4.3 percent in the third quarter, revising up the 3.4 percent advance estimate it published on 29 October, five days before the midterm elections on 3 November. In those elections, the Republican Party suffered heavy losses, with Democrats winning control of both houses of Congress.

Trump made the accusation on Thursday, Thanksgiving Day, in posts on his Truth Social platform, writing from his golf course in West Palm Beach. 'It was just revealed that the REAL Numbers on the Economy, published before the Election, were FAKE,' he wrote, offering no evidence for the claim. 'Why wasn't the correct number issued before the Election? I am asking for an immediate investigation of this situation. This is not a small error, this is a massive, and purposeful, error.'

The agency has said such revisions are routine. Advance estimates are built from incomplete source data and are updated as fuller information arrives on investment, consumer spending, trade and inventories. It said the 0.9 percentage point upward revision mainly reflected stronger investment and consumer spending as more complete data became available. The agency publishes three estimates for each quarter, with the final third-quarter figure due on 23 December.

The attack fits a pattern. Trump has repeatedly questioned official economic figures that do not match his account of a historic boom, a stance that risks shaking confidence in numbers businesses, financial markets and consumers rely on to make decisions. In a second Thanksgiving post, he wished a happy holiday even to those who issued the figures, calling the revision a 'record setting error' that should be investigated immediately.

Key points

  • The Bureau of Economic Analysis revised third-quarter growth to 4.3 percent a year, up from the 3.4 percent estimate published five days before the midterm elections.
  • Trump called the earlier figures 'fake' and demanded an immediate investigation, describing the error as 'massive, and purposeful', without providing evidence.
  • The agency says revisions are routine: advance estimates use incomplete data and are updated as fuller figures on investment and consumer spending arrive.
  • Republicans suffered heavy losses in the 3 November midterms, with Democrats winning control of both houses of Congress.
