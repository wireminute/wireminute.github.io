Leaked Russian Records Put Soldier Death Toll in Ukraine at Nearly 500,000

At least 490,334 Russian soldiers have died since the full-scale invasion of Ukraine began in February 2022, leaked records from Russia's state statistics agency show, in the fullest measure yet of the war's human cost for Moscow.

The figure comes from an analysis of more than 11 million death records held by Rosstat, Russia's federal statistics agency, covering 2021 to September 2026. The records were obtained after two hacking groups broke into the agency's systems. Researchers filtered the records by place of death, keeping only Ukraine and Russian border regions, and found 425,174 entries that explicitly listed a military cause of death, such as war injuries. A further 14,093 were identified by employment, and 51,067 by place of death, such as a military hospital or the front line, bringing the total to 490,334.

The records were checked against a separate list of 290,012 named military deaths compiled from official reports, social media posts and cemetery records, and the two matched in 90 percent of cases. Experts estimate that list captures only about 60 percent of the true toll. Adding deaths established through court rulings, death notices and medical certificates takes the total close to 500,000; counting the missing could push it past 700,000.

Almost all of the dead are men, and those aged 30 to 49 account for nearly two-thirds of them. In 2025, 65 percent of Russians aged 18 to 24 who died lost their lives in the war, and the average age of a dead soldier has risen from 27 in the first year of the war to 43 now. Last year was the deadliest of the war, with more than 165,000 deaths, an average of about 3,200 a week. About 20,000 Russian soldiers died fighting for the town of Pokrovsk in the Donetsk region, and 1,280 died for Andriivka, a village of 1,000 people south of Bakhmut. At least 5,017 foreigners are among the dead, including 2,304 North Koreans.

Moscow has not published casualty figures since September 2022, when it said 5,937 servicemen had been killed. Rosstat admitted its systems had been attacked but denied that protected information had leaked, and declined to comment on the documents. The Kremlin said only the defence ministry could comment on losses, and did not confirm or deny the figures. The records cover only officially registered deaths; soldiers listed as missing are often registered only months or years later, so the true toll is likely higher. For comparison, about 15,000 Soviet troops died in the ten-year war in Afghanistan. Ukrainian losses were last put at about 50,000 by President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in July 2026.

Key points

  • Leaked Rosstat records identify at least 490,334 Russian soldiers killed since February 2022; with court rulings, death notices and medical certificates the toll approaches 500,000.
  • The records are more than 11 million death certificates from 2021 to September 2026, obtained after hacks; cross-checks against a named-deaths list matched 90 percent of records.
  • 2025 was the deadliest year, with more than 165,000 deaths, about 3,200 a week; the average age of the dead has risen from 27 to 43.
  • Moscow has not published casualty figures since September 2022; the Kremlin did not confirm or deny the data, saying only the defence ministry could comment.
