Russian Strikes on Odesa's Historic Centre Kill Four, Wound Thirteen

Russian forces struck the historic centre of Odesa three times on Wednesday morning, killing four people and wounding thirteen, Ukrainian officials said.

The attacks, carried out with jet-powered drones, fell on the city's UNESCO-protected old centre, regional governor Oleh Kiper said, with the casualty count climbing through the morning as rescue teams reached each site. The first strike killed three people, two women and a man, and injured seven, according to the city military administration; two further strikes during the morning raised the toll to four killed, with the injured figure later revised to thirteen, according to the regional governor.

The city council said the strikes damaged a residential building, an educational institution and the Museum of Modern Art, along with other cultural sites. A car caught fire, and hundreds of windows were blown out in at least seven buildings. Ukraine's State Emergency Service said a three-storey administrative building was destroyed, and emergency crews were still working at the scene.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy described the attack as brutal and deliberate, saying it had also targeted the rescue workers who responded to the first strikes.

The strikes came a day after a Ukrainian attack on the Kerch Bridge, which links Russia with the annexed Crimean peninsula, killed two people and caused part of the structure to collapse. Across Ukraine as a whole, Russian air attacks killed at least five people on Wednesday, officials said.

Odesa has been under repeated attack through the summer and autumn, as Russian forces stepped up strikes on the city and the surrounding region with drones and missiles, including weapons aimed at port infrastructure. The city is a major export hub on Ukraine's Black Sea coast, and its historic centre, listed by UNESCO, has been hit several times since the full-scale invasion began.
