Bahrain Holds Iran Responsible for Drone Attacks and Rejects Being a Battleground in Its Feud With the US

Bahrain has formally held Iran responsible for Sunday's drone attacks on the island and rejected the use of its territory to settle scores with the United States, as American forces said they shot down two of the incoming drones.

In a statement, Bahrain's foreign ministry denounced what it called a resumption of Iranian aggression, saying the strikes violated international law and that no pretext could justify breaching the sovereignty of a state. It said Bahrain rejected the use of its territory to settle accounts and held Iran fully responsible for the attacks and their consequences. Manama said it would coordinate with its Gulf neighbours under the bloc's joint defence agreement, which treats an attack on one member as an attack on all, and Saudi Arabia condemned the strikes and expressed solidarity with the kingdom.

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said their aerospace force had sent six drones against an American air-defence radar at the Riffa helicopter base, south of the capital, calling it retaliation for hostile American action against Iran. Bahrain's military said its air defences intercepted several of the incoming attacks before they could reach their targets. The American military command for the region said its forces had shot down two Iranian drones bound for the island and reported no casualties and no damage. Explosions were heard around the capital, Manama, in the early hours, sirens sounded across the island, and the interior ministry issued two nationwide safety alerts urging people to stay calm and move to the nearest safe place.

Iran defended the strikes. Its foreign ministry spokesman said attacks on countries cooperating with the United States were justified under international law and accused Gulf states of aiding American strikes against Iran. The speaker of Iran's parliament warned that Tehran would not remain in a state of neither war nor peace and threatened to widen the geographical scope of the conflict if Washington made a mistake. The Guards repeated their claim that the Strait of Hormuz was closed and warned of a harsher response to any further American aggression.

It was the second Iranian attack on Bahrain in three days. On Friday the Guards claimed to have destroyed an American early-warning radar on the island, while the American military said it had shot down two drones with no injuries and no damage. The incidents come in the ninth month of a conflict that began with American and Israeli strikes on Iran in late February, with diplomatic efforts still stalled. Bahrain is home to the headquarters of the American navy's Fifth Fleet.

Key points

  • Bahrain's foreign ministry has formally accused Iran over Sunday's drone attacks, saying it holds Tehran fully responsible and rejects its territory being used to settle accounts.
  • American forces say they shot down two Iranian drones bound for Bahrain, with no casualties or damage reported.
  • Iran's Revolutionary Guards say six drones targeted an American air-defence radar at the Riffa helicopter base, in retaliation for hostile American action.
  • Iran defends the strikes as justified against countries cooperating with the US, while Bahrain says it will coordinate with Gulf allies under their joint defence pact.
