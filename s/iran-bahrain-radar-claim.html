Iran Claims New Drone Strike on US Base in Bahrain as Saudi Defences Stop Missiles Aimed at Riyadh

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said on Sunday they had sent six drones against an American radar base in Bahrain, hours after the island kingdom sounded sirens and told residents to take shelter, while Saudi defences intercepted missiles and drones fired towards Riyadh and the kingdom's east in the same morning.

The Guards said their aerospace force targeted the air defence radar at the Riffa helicopter base, south of the capital Manama, describing the operation as a second phase of retaliation for American actions against Iran. Iranian state media carried the claim early on Sunday. Neither the United States nor Bahrain had confirmed an attack or reported any damage, and no independent account of what happened over the island has emerged. The American military command for the region had still not commented hours after the claim.

Bahrain's interior ministry issued a nationwide alert on Sunday morning, urging people to stay calm and move to the nearest safe place. Sirens sounded across the kingdom and explosions were heard around Manama, with residents reporting a second series of blasts later in the morning. The ministry later said one alert had been lifted and the danger had passed, but further alerts followed and precautionary measures stayed in place. It did not say what the threat was or where it had come from. Bahrain is home to the headquarters of the American navy's Fifth Fleet, which directs naval operations across the region.

In the same morning, the Saudi-led coalition fighting Yemen's Houthis said its air defences had intercepted two ballistic missiles headed for Riyadh and three drones over the kingdom's Eastern Province. A civil defence alert sounded in Riyadh at 4:08 a.m., warning residents of an incoming aerial threat. It was the fourth attack on the Saudi capital in six days. The Houthis, meanwhile, said they had shot down a Saudi armed reconnaissance aircraft over al-Jawf, in northern Yemen. Neither claim has been independently verified.

The claim follows a disputed exchange on Friday, when the Guards said they had destroyed an American early-warning radar in Bahrain in answer to a strike on a telecoms tower in the southern Iranian port town of Sirik. The American military gave a different account, saying its forces had shot down two Iranian drones bound for the island, with no injuries and no damage. United States intelligence assessments put Iran's remaining missile arsenal at between 1,500 and 2,500 weapons. The incidents come during a fragile lull in a conflict between Iran and the United States that began with American and Israeli strikes on Iran in late February and is now in its ninth month, with diplomatic efforts to end the fighting still stalled.

Key points

  • Iran's Revolutionary Guards say they sent six drones against a US radar base at Riffa in Bahrain on Sunday, calling it a second phase of retaliation; neither the US nor Bahrain has confirmed the attack.
  • Bahrain sounded sirens and told residents to seek shelter in a nationwide alert; one alert was later lifted but further alerts followed, and a second series of blasts was reported in the morning.
  • Saudi defences intercepted two ballistic missiles headed for Riyadh and three drones over the Eastern Province early on Sunday, the fourth attack on the Saudi capital in six days; the Houthis claim they downed a Saudi aircraft over al-Jawf.
  • US intelligence assessments put Iran's remaining missile arsenal at between 1,500 and 2,500 weapons, as diplomatic efforts to end the nine-month conflict remain stalled.
