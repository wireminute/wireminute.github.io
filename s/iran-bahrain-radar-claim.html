Iran Says It Sent Six Drones at a US Radar Base in Bahrain as Sirens Sound in Manama

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said on Sunday they had sent six drones against an American radar base in Bahrain, hours after the island kingdom sounded sirens and told residents to take shelter in its first nationwide alert in months.

The Guards said their aerospace force targeted the air defence radar at the Riffa helicopter base, south of the capital Manama, describing the operation as the second phase of retaliation for American actions against Iran. Iranian state media carried the claim early on Sunday. Neither the United States nor Bahrain had confirmed an attack or reported any damage, and no independent account of what happened over the island has emerged.

Bahrain's interior ministry issued a nationwide alert on Sunday morning, urging people to stay calm and move to the nearest safe place. Sirens sounded across the kingdom and explosions were heard around Manama, according to reports from the island. It was the first such alert in months. Bahrain is home to the headquarters of the American navy's Fifth Fleet, which directs naval operations across the region.

The claim follows a disputed exchange on Friday, when the Guards said they had destroyed an American early-warning radar in Bahrain in answer to a strike on a telecoms tower in the southern Iranian port town of Sirik. The American military gave a different account, saying its forces had shot down two Iranian drones bound for the island, with no injuries and no damage. Neither side has produced evidence outsiders can check.

The two incidents come during a fragile lull in a conflict between Iran and the United States that began with American and Israeli strikes on Iran in late February and is now in its ninth month. Diplomatic efforts to reach an agreement to end the fighting remain stalled, and both sides have warned of further action. With claims and denials now running in parallel, the gap between the two accounts is the story: six drones claimed on one side, no confirmation on the other, and a kingdom on alert in between.

Key points

  • Iran's Revolutionary Guards say they sent six drones against a US radar base at Riffa in Bahrain on Sunday, calling it a second phase of retaliation.
  • Bahrain sounded sirens and told residents to seek shelter in a nationwide alert, the first of its kind in months; explosions were heard around Manama.
  • Neither the United States nor Bahrain has confirmed the attack or reported any damage; the claim has not been independently verified.
  • The claim follows Friday's disputed exchange, when Iran said it destroyed a US radar in Bahrain and Washington said it shot down two drones.
