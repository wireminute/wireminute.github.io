Iran Says It Destroyed a US Radar in Bahrain. Washington Says Both Drones Were Shot Down

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said on Friday that they had destroyed an American early-warning radar in Bahrain, in answer to a strike on a telecoms tower inside Iran — a claim the United States flatly denied, saying it shot down two Iranian drones before they reached their target.

The Guards said their aerospace force had hit the radar on the island of Bahrain in the hours after what they called a hostile strike on a communications tower in Sirik, a port town in southern Iran on the Strait of Hormuz. They described the strike as a serious warning to Washington to stop its air attacks and what they called oil smuggling in the strait. It was the first such claim against American forces in a Gulf state since early September.

The American military gave a very different account. Its Central Command said the Guards had sent two one-way attack drones bound for Bahrain, which American forces brought down, with no injuries and no damage. Neither side has produced evidence that outsiders can check, and no independent account of what happened over Bahrain has emerged.

Tehran added a second claim on Friday, saying its navy had shot down an American MQ-1 drone off the coast near Sirik. The Guards said their forces were on full alert and ready to respond without limits if the attacks were repeated, and Iran also said a gas tanker had struck a mine while crossing the Strait of Hormuz.

The exchange comes during a fragile lull in a conflict between Iran and the United States that began with American and Israeli strikes on Iran in late February and has since spread across the region. The United States has carried out limited strikes in recent days on Qeshm island and other parts of southern Iran near the strait, and a report released on Friday by the UK Maritime Trade Operations office put shipping through the waterway at roughly 65 per cent below its pre-war level.

For now the two accounts cannot both be true, and the gap between them is the story: a claimed radar destroyed on one side, two drones intercepted on the other, and no wreckage, pictures or third-party check to settle it. With both sides warning of further action, the strait — one of the world's most important routes for oil tankers — is again the place where a misread signal could turn a lull back into open fighting.

The Guards added a further claim on Friday, saying a tanker had struck a naval mine while trying to sail through the Strait of Hormuz, causing an explosion. Neither that claim nor the radar claim has been independently verified. Bahrain itself has said nothing in public about an attack on its territory, and issued no public warning of one.

Key points

  • Iran's Revolutionary Guards said they destroyed a US early-warning radar in Bahrain, in answer to a strike on a telecoms tower in Sirik.
  • The US military said it intercepted two Iranian drones headed for Bahrain, with no injuries or damage.
  • Iran also said it shot down a US drone off its southern coast and warned of a wider response if attacks continue.
  • Neither side's account has been independently confirmed; Iran also claimed a tanker hit a mine in the Strait of Hormuz, and Bahrain has said nothing in public about an attack.
