Bahrain Says Its Defences Shot Down Iranian Attacks as Tehran Claims Strike on US Base

Bahrain said on Sunday its air defences stopped and destroyed a number of Iranian aerial attacks, hours after Tehran claimed its drones had hit an American radar base on the island, in the sharpest exchange yet around the Gulf kingdom since the regional conflict began.

The kingdom's military said its air defences engaged the incoming attacks before they could reach their targets, and accused Tehran of a systematic hostile approach towards the country. It was the first time since September that Bahrain's defences had fired on incoming attacks. Earlier, the interior ministry had issued two nationwide safety alerts, urging people to stay calm and move to the nearest safe place. Sirens sounded across the island and explosions were heard around the capital, Manama. The ministry later said one alert had been lifted, but further alerts followed.

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said their aerospace force had sent six drones at a United States air-defence radar at the Riffa helicopter base, south of the capital, calling it the next stage of its response to American action against Iran. Iranian state media carried the claim early on Sunday, saying the strikes had been heard across different areas of Bahrain. The Guards offered no evidence of damage. Neither the United States nor Bahrain has confirmed that any American facility was hit. The American military command for the region had still not commented hours after the claim.

In the same morning, the Saudi-led coalition fighting Yemen's Houthis said its air defences had stopped two ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh and three drones over the kingdom's Eastern Province. A civil defence alert sounded in Riyadh at 4:08am. It was the fourth strike aimed at Riyadh inside six days. The Houthis, meanwhile, said they had shot down a Saudi armed reconnaissance aircraft over al-Jawf, in northern Yemen. Neither claim has been independently verified.

The claim follows a disputed exchange on Friday, when the Guards said they had destroyed an American early-warning radar in Bahrain in answer to a strike on a telecoms tower in the southern port town of Sirik. The American military gave a different account, saying its forces had shot down two Iranian drones bound for the island, with no injuries and no damage. American intelligence assessments put Iran's remaining missile arsenal at between 1,500 and 2,500 weapons. The incidents come during a fragile lull in a conflict that began with American and Israeli strikes on Iran in late February and is now in its ninth month, with diplomatic efforts still stalled. Bahrain is home to the headquarters of the American navy's Fifth Fleet, which directs naval operations across the region.

Key points

  • Bahrain's military says its air defences stopped and destroyed a number of Iranian aerial attacks on Sunday, the first time its defences have fired since September.
  • Iran's Revolutionary Guards say six drones targeted a United States air-defence radar at the Riffa helicopter base; the US has not confirmed any damage or loss.
  • Bahrain issued two nationwide safety alerts and sounded sirens; residents were told to move to safe places, and explosions were heard around the capital.
  • Saudi defences stopped two ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh and three drones over the Eastern Province, the fourth strike aimed at the Saudi capital inside six days.
