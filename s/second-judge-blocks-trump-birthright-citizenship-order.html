Second US Judge Blocks Trump's Birthright Citizenship Order

A second federal judge has blocked President Donald Trump's executive order limiting birthright citizenship, ruling that the president cannot carve out new exceptions to a right the Supreme Court has already upheld.

US District Judge Christopher Cooper in Washington issued a preliminary injunction on Wednesday, barring the government from enforcing the order against children covered by a class-action lawsuit. The ruling is the latest setback for one of the flagship measures of the administration's hardline immigration crackdown.

The order, signed in August, sought to widen the categories of children born in the United States who the government says are not entitled to citizenship at birth. It came after the Supreme Court ruled 6-3 in June that children born in the country to parents who are unlawfully or temporarily present are citizens under the 14th Amendment. Cooper noted that the court's ruling had barely been issued before the president signed a second order, and wrote that the president cannot carve out new exceptions to birthright citizenship for children born in the United States and subject to its laws.

The injunction bars the Department of State, the Department of Homeland Security and the Social Security Administration from enforcing the order against any child in the class. It follows a similar ruling in September, when a federal judge in Maryland blocked the same order, describing it as almost certainly unconstitutional. The administration would now have to overturn both decisions before it could ever enforce the restrictions.

The lawsuit was brought by the American Civil Liberties Union and other advocacy groups on behalf of children who would lose citizenship under the order. The group's deputy director said the Supreme Court had twice rejected efforts to end birthright citizenship, in 1898 and again this year, and that a third attempt would not succeed. The case will now proceed while the injunction remains in place.
