US Says Russia Is Open to NATO-Style Guarantees for Ukraine, but Moscow Says No

American envoys say Russia has grown comfortable with the idea of NATO-style security guarantees for Ukraine, but Moscow pushed back on Friday, saying even a formula resembling alliance membership is unacceptable, leaving the centrepiece of the US peace effort in dispute.

Jared Kushner, one of the American envoys leading the peace effort, told reporters in Berlin on Thursday that Russia had grown comfortable with the concept of a security guarantee for Ukraine based on the alliance's Article 5, its collective defence clause. A US official later said Moscow was increasingly open to granting Ukraine protections similar to the alliance's Articles 4 and 5, provided it keeps at least some of the territory its forces have captured, and described the goal as getting Kyiv as close to NATO as possible without membership itself. The official said any settlement must leave Ukraine sovereign, secure, prosperous and free, and that freezing the current front lines would almost certainly be part of any such deal. Russia holds about a fifth of Ukraine's territory.

Steve Witkoff, the other American envoy, said the president was open to an Article 5-style guarantee, while cautioning that it would have to be in America's interest and part of a broader security package. The remarks came after the two envoys briefed President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and European leaders in Berlin, and after the president spoke into the session by phone from his aircraft. President Vladimir Putin has said Russia has invited European representatives to Moscow for talks, saying it needs to understand what Europe would bring to the negotiations.

Moscow moved quickly to narrow what it would accept. Deputy Foreign Minister Sergei Ryabkov said on Friday that even the idea of a quasi-membership formula for Kyiv, or the search for what he called a creative solution in that area, was unacceptable to Russia. He said Ukraine must accept that Moscow will not shift on its demand that Kyiv never join the alliance, and that Russia discusses security guarantees first in terms of its own security.

Zelenskyy said guarantees modelled on Article 5 still leave many questions, including what collective would stand behind them, how it would defend Ukraine and whether it would be ready to repel an attacker. He said such guarantees would have to be set out in writing and last for decades rather than depend on a single administration, and that membership of the alliance itself remains the clearest security guarantee for Kyiv. He also said the envoys had asked whether Ukraine would give up the Donbas region in exchange for membership, an idea Kyiv has rejected, and insisted the fair option is to hold the line where it stands.

Key points

  • Kushner said on Thursday in Berlin that Russia had grown comfortable with security guarantees for Ukraine based on NATO's Article 5 collective defence clause.
  • A US official said Moscow was open to NATO-like guarantees if it keeps at least some captured territory, and Witkoff said the president is open to an Article 5-style guarantee.
  • Moscow pushed back on Friday, with Deputy Foreign Minister Ryabkov calling even a quasi-membership formula unacceptable and insisting Ukraine will never join the alliance.
  • Zelenskyy said Article 5-style guarantees raise many questions and must be written down and last for decades, calling NATO membership itself the clearest guarantee.
