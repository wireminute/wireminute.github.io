Turkish Police Raid Opposition Channel Sozcu TV, Take It Off Air and Install State Trustee

Police in Turkey moved against the opposition channel Sozcu TV before dawn on Tuesday, searching its Istanbul offices, halting its broadcasts and placing the channel under a state-appointed trustee.

Officers searched the channel's building in the Levent district from about 4:30 in the morning, under a court order, and blocked off nearby streets. Its morning show was replaced by pre-recorded programming, and live news stopped once the state savings deposit insurance fund, the TMSF, took charge of the channel and its news website.

Prosecutors said four executives had been detained, including the board chairwoman Funda Tuncer Sidali and former general manager Mustafa Asim Akgul. They said the operation was part of an investigation into the channel's owner, Burak Akbay, over alleged links to the network of the late cleric Fethullah Gulen and alleged breaches of terrorism financing law. The government blames Gulen's network for a failed coup attempt in 2016, and the case against the media group dates back nearly ten years.

The channel's lawyer, Ersan Barkin, said the trusteeship covered Sozcu TV and its website only, and that the printed newspaper Sozcu and its sister papers Tavir and Korkusuz were not affected. He said the case had previously been thrown out by the country's highest court after it found no evidence of a crime. Presenter Damla Dogan Tuncel later said the channel planned to resume live broadcasts in the afternoon, and the main opposition leader condemned the raid.

It is the sixth big Turkish news outlet put under a state-appointed trustee since 2022, after Halk TV, Flash TV, Eko Turk, Haberturk and Tele1. Police raided Halk TV, the country's most-watched news channel, a month ago and halted its broadcasts in the same way. Sozcu TV has repeatedly been penalised by the broadcasting regulator, and was taken off air for ten days in July 2025 over its coverage of mass protests that followed the jailing of Istanbul mayor Ekrem Imamoglu.
