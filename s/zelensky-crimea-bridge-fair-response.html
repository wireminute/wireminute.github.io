Zelensky Calls Crimean Bridge Strikes a 'Fair Response' to 109 Russian Attacks on Ukrainian Bridges

President Volodymyr Zelensky has called Ukraine's strikes on the Crimean Bridge a fair response to Russia's attacks on Ukrainian bridges, saying Moscow hit bridges in Ukraine 109 times with missiles and drones.

Speaking alongside British Defence Secretary Wes Streeting in Kyiv on Saturday, Zelensky said the figure counted separate attacks, not individual missiles, and that this was why Ukraine's response was completely justified. He rejected any suggestion that the crossing is purely civilian infrastructure, saying Russian military convoys use it. 'Everyone has already seen the evidence,' he said, pointing to tanks, armoured vehicles and artillery moving across the bridge.

Ukrainian Defence Minister Yevhenii Khmara, who joined the meeting, said Russian crews will need several months simply to lift and tow away the collapsed road arch and the span destroyed in the November 17 strike. He said Russian engineering teams are carrying out underwater inspections to work out how to clear the wreckage.

The 19-kilometre bridge, which links Russia to occupied Crimea, was hit twice in three days. A Security Service of Ukraine operation on Tuesday, November 17, destroyed a main road arch and the roadway beneath it, halting vehicle traffic, before coastal Neptune cruise missiles damaged the railway tracks overnight into Thursday, November 19. Road and rail traffic across the crossing remains closed.

Russia has said it will rebuild the bridge. Deputy Prime Minister Marat Khusnullin said on November 19 that some road sections might reopen within one to two months, though a structural engineer cited in reports said replacing the road span and arch could take more than a year. Zelensky told NBC News on Friday that the strikes were a legitimate response and that the bridge would not remain standing after the war in any case.

Key points

  • Zelensky said Russia attacked Ukrainian bridges 109 times with missiles and drones, and called Ukraine's strikes on the Crimean Bridge a fair response.
  • He said the bridge is not purely civilian, pointing to Russian tanks, armoured vehicles and artillery crossing it.
  • Defence Minister Yevhenii Khmara said it will take Russian crews several months to lift and tow away the collapsed arch and road span.
  • The 19-kilometre crossing remains closed to road and rail traffic after the November 17 and November 19 strikes; Russia says it will rebuild it.
