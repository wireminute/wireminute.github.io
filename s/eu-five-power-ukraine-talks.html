Europe Names Five Nations to Speak for It at Ukraine Peace Talks

European leaders have agreed that Britain, France, Germany, Italy and Poland will speak for the continent at the next round of talks on ending the war in Ukraine, alongside the European Union's own institutions.

The arrangement was announced on Friday by António Costa, the European Council's president, after an informal summit in Dublin, where leaders settled what officials are calling the E5 format: the five national delegations plus the European Commission and the European Council.

The decision gives institutional form to a long-standing European demand for representation in a US-backed diplomatic process that has so far brought American, Ukrainian and Russian officials into direct contact but produced no agreement on territory or a ceasefire.

The Dublin gathering had been called to discuss artificial intelligence. The question of who represents Europe at the Ukraine talks took over the agenda instead, and the agreement on the format was the summit's main outcome.

Costa set out the limits of the group's role. It will coordinate Europe's contribution to the negotiations, working with all 27 EU member states and alongside Ukraine, the United States and NATO — but it will not settle questions of Ukrainian territory for Kyiv. Questions of territory and sovereignty are for Ukraine alone to decide, Costa said.

Four of the five — France, Germany, Italy and Poland — are EU members. Britain is not, but it belongs to the Coalition of the Willing, the group of countries backing Ukraine, and takes its place in the five on that basis.

Ukraine's president, Volodymyr Zelensky, and Jared Kushner both confirmed that Europe will take part in the next round of talks. No date or venue for that round has been announced, and the level at which the five countries will be represented has not been set out.

Russia has not agreed to the expanded format. On Friday, the Kremlin's spokesman, Dmitry Peskov, said Moscow was open to contact with Europe but first needed to see genuine willingness to contribute to peace, and that no decision had been taken. EU leaders will return to Ukraine at their next European Council meeting on 26 and 27 November, when they are due to discuss the country's financing needs for 2027 and beyond.

Key points

  • EU leaders agreed in Dublin that Britain, France, Germany, Italy and Poland, with the EU institutions, will represent Europe at the next round of Ukraine peace talks.
  • The E5 group will coordinate Europe's contribution; only Ukraine can decide on its territory and sovereignty, Costa said.
  • Zelensky and Kushner confirmed Europe will take part in the next round; no date, venue or delegation level has been announced.
  • Russia has not agreed to the expanded format, saying it first wants to see genuine willingness to contribute to peace.
