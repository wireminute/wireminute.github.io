Steering Failure Struck Tanker Two Minutes Before It Hit Docked Riverboat, Investigators Say

Federal investigators say the tanker that capsized a docked river cruise boat in New Orleans suffered a steering failure about two minutes before impact, leaving its rudder jammed in one position and the vessel veering towards the riverbank.

The National Transportation Safety Board said on Sunday that a preliminary review of the tanker Atlantic Asphalt's voyage data recorder showed a "steering casualty" at about 12:26 p.m. local time on Friday, near the Crescent City Connection bridge. The rudder stayed stuck to starboard, and the vessel turned towards the east bank of the Mississippi, where the riverboat American Heritage was tied up at the Thalia Street Terminal. The collision came at about 12:28 p.m., and the riverboat rolled onto its side in about 22 minutes.

Investigators have downloaded more than nine hours of data from the recorder, including bridge audio, rudder angle, engine performance, speed and course. The recording also captured the ship's whistle, radio warnings and requests for help, and orders to drop anchor and take emergency action. What caused the steering to fail is still not known, and the board stressed that its review is preliminary. A preliminary report is expected within about 30 days, with a full report taking between 18 and 24 months.

The NTSB said it would also examine what went right: nobody was killed, even though the American Heritage was carrying 140 passengers and 50 crew, and the tanker had 19 crew and a local pilot on board. Thirty-two people were taken to hospital, none with critical injuries, and six of them were still in hospital on Sunday. All 197 passengers and crew are accounted for. Investigators are looking at whether the tanker's anchors were deployed and will interview the crew over the coming week.

The Port of New Orleans has sued the tanker's owner, Nave Shipping SA, and its manager, Thome Ship Management, saying the crash caused more than $1 million of damage to its terminal, and a federal judge has ordered the tanker seized while the inquiry continues. The riverboat remains on its side against the terminal, and the Coast Guard says moving it could take weeks. A safety zone is in force on the river between mile markers 95.5 and 96.5, and the board has asked anyone with video or photos of the collision to come forward.

Key points

  • The tanker Atlantic Asphalt suffered a steering failure at about 12:26 p.m. on Friday, leaving its rudder stuck to starboard, and hit the docked American Heritage about two minutes later, investigators say.
  • The finding comes from a preliminary review of the tanker's voyage data recorder, which holds more than nine hours of data including bridge audio and rudder angle; what caused the failure is still unknown.
  • No one was killed. Thirty-two people were taken to hospital, none with critical injuries, and six were still in hospital on Sunday; all 197 passengers and crew are accounted for.
  • The Port of New Orleans has sued the tanker's owner and manager for more than $1 million of damage, and a federal judge has ordered the tanker seized while the investigation continues.
