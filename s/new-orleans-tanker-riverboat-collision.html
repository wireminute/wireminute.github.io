Thirty-Two Taken to Hospital After Tanker Rams Docked Riverboat in New Orleans

Thirty-two people have been taken to hospital after a tanker struck a docked river cruise boat on the Mississippi River in New Orleans, tipping the vessel onto its side and sending passengers into the water. None of them is reported to have critical injuries, the city's mayor said.

The tanker Atlantic Asphalt hit the American Heritage, a riverboat run by American Cruise Lines, at 12:30 p.m. local time on Friday while it was tied up at the Port of New Orleans. About 129 passengers were on board, along with crew members. The boat had docked roughly two hours earlier so passengers could start coming ashore, after arriving in the city from Natchez earlier in the day.

Footage from the riverfront showed the far bigger tanker driving into the paddlewheel boat, which ended up listing heavily and partly under water. Sources told one American network the tanker struck the riverboat broadside and hard, and that the vessel capsized. People in the water were seen wearing life jackets and holding on to life rings and the vessel's railings while rescuers worked around them.

Rescue crews took 32 people from the riverboat to hospitals, the city's mayor said at a late-afternoon briefing, and none of them is reported to have critical injuries. The fire department sent 23 units and 57 personnel to the port, and at least 14 people were pulled from the water. The operator said all guests and crew had been evacuated and that it was working with the Coast Guard and local authorities. A triage area was set up inside the port's Administration Building, where evacuated passengers were taken for medical checks and assistance.

Louisiana's governor, Jeff Landry, said he had been briefed on the incident and was heading to New Orleans. The Coast Guard closed the river to all traffic between mile markers 93 and 96. The cause of the collision has still not been confirmed, and officials have not said whether the tanker suffered a mechanical or propulsion failure before impact. The mayor said the tanker had 'lost control' before it struck the riverboat, and the National Transportation Safety Board is expected to arrive on Saturday morning to investigate the cause.

The 160-metre tanker is registered in the Marshall Islands and owned by a Liberian shipping company. Tracking data shows it arrived in New Orleans on Wednesday after sailing from New Jersey in late October. The American Heritage, which can carry 180 guests, had been on a cruise up the river before docking to let passengers off.

A witness said the riverboat rolled onto its side within about 15 minutes of the impact. The mayor said nobody was unaccounted for and described the outcome as a miracle on the Mississippi. The state's lieutenant governor said everyone was believed to have been accounted for. One emergency response source said it was possible the tanker had lost power before the collision, though that has not been confirmed.

Key points

  • Thirty-two people have been taken to hospital, none of them with critical injuries, according to the city's mayor; at least 14 were pulled from the water and everyone is believed to have been accounted for.
  • The tanker Atlantic Asphalt struck the docked American Heritage at 12:30 p.m. local time on Friday; about 129 passengers plus crew were on board.
  • Louisiana's governor is heading to New Orleans; a triage area was set up at the port and all guests and crew were evacuated.
