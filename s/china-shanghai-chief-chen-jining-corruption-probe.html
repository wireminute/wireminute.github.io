China Places Shanghai Party Chief Chen Jining Under Corruption Investigation

China has placed Chen Jining, the Communist Party chief of Shanghai and a member of its ruling Politburo, under investigation for suspected corruption, in the most senior fall yet of the party's widening anti-graft campaign.

State news agency Xinhua announced on Tuesday that Chen, 62, is being investigated by the party's anti-corruption body for suspected serious violations of discipline and law, the standard wording used for corruption cases. No details of the alleged wrongdoing were given, and Chen could not be reached for comment.

Chen is the fourth member of the 24-strong Politburo formed in 2022 to be investigated since last year, after two senior generals and a former regional leader. He is also the first serving Shanghai party chief to face such a probe since Chen Liangyu was removed from the post in 2006. His fall leaves the body with 21 active members.

An environmental scientist by training, Chen took a doctorate at Imperial College London, then led Tsinghua University before moving into government as environment minister and then mayor of Beijing. He took charge of Shanghai in October 2022, when his predecessor Li Qiang moved up to become premier. Chen had been widely seen as a rising figure and a possible candidate for promotion to the Politburo Standing Committee at the party congress due in the second half of 2027.

Shanghai's party leadership gathered on Tuesday evening to back the probe publicly. No successor has been named, and the city's mayor is expected to fill in until one is chosen. He was last seen in public on Thursday, visiting the offices of a state-owned shipping company.

The case comes weeks after the party's central committee formalised the expulsion of 33 senior figures, nearly half of them military officers, at its fifth plenum in late October. The campaign has thinned the senior leadership ranks and depleted the top military command, and the announcement of an investigation at Chen's level is generally taken as a sign that a political downfall will follow.

Key points

  • Chen Jining, 62, the Communist Party chief of Shanghai and a Politburo member, is under investigation for suspected serious violations of discipline and law.
  • He is the fourth Politburo member to be probed since 2025 and the first serving Shanghai party chief investigated since 2006.
  • A former Tsinghua University president with a doctorate from Imperial College London, Chen had been seen as a candidate for promotion at the 2027 party congress.
  • No successor has been named; Shanghai's party committee held a meeting on Tuesday evening to endorse the investigation.
