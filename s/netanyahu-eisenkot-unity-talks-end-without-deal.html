Netanyahu-Eisenkot Unity Talks End Without a Deal as Israel's Government Deadlock Deepens

Talks between the parties of caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot on forming a unity government have ended without an agreement, leaving Israel's political deadlock unresolved.

Delegations from Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud party and Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar party met for about four hours on Sunday at a hotel in Herzliya, near Tel Aviv, in a second round of negotiations on a unity government. After the meeting, the two sides said they had made no progress toward an agreement.

The negotiators remain divided on two central questions: who would lead the next government, and whether far-right National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir would be part of it. Likud pressed for Ben-Gvir to be included in any unity cabinet, while Yashar insisted on his exclusion. The teams were also at odds over judicial legislation and over how the leadership would rotate.

In a joint statement, the parties said they had set out their positions on several major questions and talked through them in detail, and that another meeting would follow. No date was given. Officials from both camps, quoted in Israeli media, saw little realistic prospect of the negotiations producing a deal.

Eisenkot, a former chief of staff of the armed forces, holds the mandate to form a government after the election on October 27 left no bloc with a majority in the 120-seat Knesset. His bloc won 48 seats, 13 short of a majority, while Netanyahu's right-wing bloc won 53 and Arab-majority parties won 19. Eisenkot must assemble a coalition by December 6, though he can ask for two more weeks.

The talks were striking because Eisenkot had earlier ruled out serving in a government with Netanyahu, before changing course and inviting Likud to a series of marathon negotiations in an effort to build a broad government. With this round over and no breakthrough, the caretaker government stays in place while the deadline for forming a new one draws closer.

Key points

  • Likud and Yashar delegations met for about four hours in Herzliya on Sunday and left without an agreement.
  • The sides are split over who would lead the government and whether far-right minister Itamar Ben-Gvir would join it.
  • A follow-up meeting was promised, but no date was given.
  • Eisenkot has until December 6 to form a government after an election that gave no bloc a majority.
