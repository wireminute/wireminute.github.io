Crimea Bridge Road Span Falls Into the Sea After Second Ukrainian Strike in Two Days

The road section of the bridge linking Russia with Crimea has collapsed into the sea after Ukraine struck the crossing for a second day in a row, killing two people in the surrounding district, the Russian-installed head of Crimea said on Tuesday.

Sergei Aksyonov said in a Telegram message at about half past one in the morning local time that the bridge had been attacked again and that traffic was suspended. Morning photographs posted by Ukrainian channels showed the road span lying in the water, with only the rail arch still standing, and shipping through the Kerch Strait was blocked. Aksyonov said two people had been killed in the Leninskyi district, the area around the town of Kerch. Ukraine has not commented on the strike.

The first strike came on Monday morning, the first time in more than a year that Ukraine had hit the 19-kilometre bridge, which links Russia with the peninsula Moscow seized and annexed in 2014. The Kremlin described Monday's damage as minor, and road traffic was still suspended when the second strike landed. The bridge is the main road and rail link Russia uses to move fuel, ammunition and troops onto the peninsula and onward to its forces in southern Ukraine.

Russia's defence ministry said its air defences shot down 1,441 Ukrainian drones overnight across 22 regions, the largest such attack of the war by its own count, passing Monday's figure of 1,336, which had itself set a record. Ukrainian drones also struck energy sites, including an oil refinery at Ilsky in the southern Krasnodar region, where falling debris set off a fire and injured six people, including children. Ukraine's president said on Sunday that recent attacks had disabled more than 55 percent of Russia's oil-refining capacity. The figures come from Moscow and Kyiv and could not be independently verified.

Russia answered the drone barrage with its own strikes late on Monday, hitting Kyiv's North and South bridges across the Dnipro and damaging the subway system, the city's mayor said, cutting water and heating in parts of the capital. Kyiv came under a record 17 air raid alerts in a single day, and Russian strikes killed at least seven people and cut power to 50,000. The exchange comes three days before the leaders of the United States, Russia and China are due to meet in Shenzhen on Thursday, days after American envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff visited Moscow and Kyiv for talks on ending the war.
