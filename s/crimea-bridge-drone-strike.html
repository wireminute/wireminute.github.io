Zelensky Says Crimea Bridge Arch Destroyed in Seven-Phase Operation Weeks in the Making

Ukraine's president has confirmed that Ukrainian forces destroyed a metal arch and a road span of the Crimea bridge in an overnight strike, saying the attack was the final phase of an operation prepared over several weeks, as Russia said two people were killed and road traffic on the crossing was suspended, with rail services partly resuming later in the day.

Volodymyr Zelensky said on Tuesday that the strike was the seventh phase of an operation carried out over several weeks and developed by the Security Service together with the armed forces' General Staff. He said Ukrainian forces had first worn down Russian air defences around the crossing. Drones of the Palianytsia type struck the road section, he said, while Neptune missiles, Ruta drones and FP-5 Flamingo missiles hit the railway section. The Security Service said one of the bridge's metal arches and the road span beneath it were completely destroyed and no vehicles could cross. Zelensky published video of the strike. The Security Service said it was the fourth time the bridge had been attacked.

By Tuesday afternoon, rail traffic had partly resumed after a pause, while the road section stayed closed and no vehicles could cross. Video verified by a news agency showed the bridge's main road arch crumbled into the sea, leaving a gap in the crossing. The Kremlin said President Vladimir Putin had been briefed and vowed the bridge would be rebuilt, two days before he is due to meet the leaders of the United States and China. Russia's Investigative Committee confirmed structural damage to the bridge and opened a criminal case on terrorism charges, blaming Ukraine's armed forces for the attack.

The Russian-installed head of Crimea, Sergei Aksyonov, said in a Telegram message at about half past one in the morning local time that the bridge had been attacked again and that traffic was suspended. He said two people had been killed in the Leninskyi district around Kerch. Russia's Transport Ministry said both road and rail traffic had been suspended and specialists were assessing the damage. The Kremlin said the first attack this week caused only minor damage.

The first strike came on Monday morning, the first time in more than a year that Ukraine had hit the 19-kilometre bridge, which links Russia with the peninsula Moscow seized and annexed in 2014. The bridge is the main road and rail link Russia uses to move fuel, ammunition and troops onto the peninsula.

Russia's defence ministry said its air defences shot down 1,441 Ukrainian drones overnight across 22 regions, the largest such attack of the war by its own count, passing Monday's record of 1,336. Ukrainian drones also struck energy sites, including a refinery at Ilsky in the Krasnodar region, where falling debris set off a fire and injured six people. The figures come from Moscow and Kyiv and could not be independently verified. Russia answered with its own strikes late on Monday, hitting Kyiv's North and South bridges across the Dnipro and damaging the subway system, cutting water and heating in parts of the capital. Kyiv faced a record 17 air raid alerts in a single day, and Russian strikes killed at least seven people and cut power to 50,000. The exchange comes three days before the leaders of the United States, Russia and China are due to meet in Shenzhen on Thursday.

Key points

  • Zelensky confirmed the overnight strike was the seventh phase of an operation prepared over several weeks; the Security Service says a metal arch and the road span beneath it were completely destroyed.
  • Two people were killed in the district around Kerch, the Russian-installed governor said; road traffic across the bridge is suspended, rail traffic partly resumed on Tuesday, and the Kremlin has vowed to rebuild it.
  • Palianytsia drones struck the road section while Neptune missiles, Ruta drones and Flamingo missiles hit the railway section, Kyiv said.
  • Russia said it shot down 1,441 Ukrainian drones overnight and struck Kyiv's bridges in reply, days before leaders meet in Shenzhen on Thursday.
