Putin Rules Out Talks and Promises Harsher Retaliation After Third Bridge Strike

Vladimir Putin has promised a much harsher retaliation for Ukraine's attacks on the Crimean Bridge and ruled out negotiations over the strikes, after Ukrainian forces hit the crossing for a third time in a week.

Ukraine struck the railway section of the Crimean Bridge in an overnight drone attack, Russia's transport ministry said on Thursday, suspending train services across the crossing for a third time in a week, with nobody hurt and specialists assessing the damage.

Sergei Aksyonov, the Russian-installed head of Crimea, said in a Telegram message that the bridge had been attacked again, traffic was temporarily halted while the damage was assessed, and all relevant services were on site. Ukraine's General Staff hinted in a morning statement that the bridge had been hit, in an indirect confirmation of the strike. Local channels reported a series of explosions near Kerch in the early hours, some possibly air defences at work.

The railway section was the part of the crossing that had survived the earlier strikes this week. On Tuesday, Ukrainian drones and missiles destroyed the central road section of the bridge, forcing its main road arch to crumble into the sea and leaving a gap in the crossing, according to video verified by a news agency. Two people were killed in the district around Kerch in that strike. Rail traffic had partly resumed on Tuesday afternoon, while the road section stayed closed.

Ukraine's president said the strikes were the seventh phase of an operation prepared over several weeks, and the Security Service said one of the bridge's metal arches and the road span beneath it were completely destroyed and no vehicles could cross. The first strike came on Monday morning, the first time in more than a year that Ukraine had hit the crossing. Ukraine said Palianytsia drones struck the road section, while Neptune missiles, Ruta drones and Flamingo missiles hit the railway section.

Speaking in Shenzhen after meeting the American and Chinese leaders, Putin said Russia would answer the attacks and that its response would be far more painful than the strikes on the bridge. He said there would be no negotiations after the attacks, and claimed Russian engineers had already rebuilt the railway section and would restore the whole crossing. He described the bridge as civilian infrastructure, a claim Kyiv disputes.

The bridge is the main road and rail link Russia uses to move fuel, ammunition and troops onto the peninsula, a flagship project inaugurated by Putin in 2018. Russia's Investigative Committee confirmed structural damage to the bridge and opened a criminal case on terrorism charges, blaming Ukraine's armed forces.

Key points

  • Putin said Russia's response to the bridge attacks would be far more painful than the strikes themselves, and ruled out negotiations after the attacks.
  • The overnight strike hit the Crimean Bridge's railway section, the part that had survived this week's earlier attacks; train services are suspended again and nobody was hurt, Russia's transport ministry said.
  • Earlier strikes this week destroyed the bridge's central road section, sending its main road arch into the sea; two people were killed near Kerch.
  • Putin claimed Russian engineers had already rebuilt the railway section and would restore the whole crossing; the bridge is Russia's main road and rail supply link to Crimea.
