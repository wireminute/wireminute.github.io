Three Men Sentenced to Death in Jordan for Church Shooting That Killed Nine

A court in Jordan on Sunday handed down death sentences to three men over a gun attack on a church funeral in the south of the country last month that killed nine people and wounded 34.

The State Security Court found the three guilty of terrorist killings, arms offences, plotting attacks and spreading extremist ideas. The sentences can be appealed within 30 days and will also go automatically to the Court of Cassation for review.

Gunmen fired on mourners at a Greek Orthodox church funeral in Hamoud, a village about 80 kilometres south of Amman in Karak governorate, on 23 October. Nine men were killed, most of them members of the Halasah, a prominent local Christian clan, and 34 people were wounded. Bells rang at the church after the sentences were announced on Sunday.

The three men — two brothers, Hazzaa and Mishaal Mohammad, and their brother-in-law Ahmad Hussein, all from the Thneibat clan — were taken into custody three days after the attack, following a gun battle at a derelict house in Karak. Two other men were jailed for six years each for selling the attackers the weapons used in the assault.

The attack was a rare strike on Jordan's Christian minority in a country long seen as one of the region's safer states. Investigators have said they found no evidence linking the three men to other militant cells, and the authorities say the file on the attack remains open. The trial ran for four sessions before Sunday's verdict, and the two men convicted of supplying the weapons were sentenced in the same ruling.

Key points

  • A Jordanian court handed death sentences to three men over the 23 October attack on a church funeral in Karak.
  • Nine people were killed and 34 wounded in the gun attack on a funeral at the Greek Orthodox church in Hamoud village, Karak.
  • The court convicted them of terrorist killings, arms offences, plotting attacks and spreading extremist ideas.
  • Two other men received six-year terms for selling the attackers their weapons; the death sentences face automatic review.
