Thanksgiving Storms Leave More Than 800,000 Without Power Across the US

More than 800,000 homes and businesses across the United States were without power by Thursday afternoon on Thanksgiving as a powerful nor'easter moved up the East Coast and heavy snow hit the Midwest, with states of emergency declared in New Jersey and New York.

The tracking site PowerOutage.us counted more than 800,000 customers without power by 3.30pm Eastern time on Thursday, up from about 600,000 late in the morning. Virginia had the most, with nearly 200,000, followed by Maryland with 177,000 and Missouri with more than 120,000. Pennsylvania, New Jersey and North Carolina each had tens of thousands out, and gusts of up to 70 miles per hour toppled trees and power lines.

In New England, the utility National Grid said it had restored nearly 300,000 customers since the nor'easter began on Tuesday night. As of 9am on Thursday, 160,113 customers were still without power, including 146,429 in Massachusetts and 13,684 in Rhode Island. Gusts topped 80 miles per hour, and the company said most customers would be restored by Saturday morning.

Macy's moved its Thanksgiving Day Parade, the 100th edition, to 8.30am on Saturday 28 November, the first time the parade has ever been postponed. A high wind warning runs from 10am Thursday to 1am Friday for New York City, Long Island and much of New Jersey, with coastal flood warnings in place. Forecasters expected one to three inches of rain in the New York area and gusts of 40 to 50 miles per hour, reaching 60 to 70 on exposed coasts. In Ocean City, New Jersey, strong winds tore roofs off houses, and coastal flooding hit the Chesapeake Bay and Virginia Tidewater areas.

New Jersey's governor, Mikie Sherrill, declared a state of emergency from 6pm on Wednesday, and New York's governor, Kathy Hochul, declared one for New York City, Long Island and Westchester County. Utilities staged repair crews, and winter storm warnings covered parts of the Midwest, where some areas could see six to 12 inches of snow. Forecasters said the storm would depart overnight into Friday, with poor weather expected through Friday for much of the Mid-Atlantic and Northeast.

Flights were heavily disrupted. By 3pm on Thursday, 419 flights in the United States had been cancelled and 1,708 delayed, according to flight tracking data. Baltimore/Washington airport was the worst hit, with 47 outbound and 46 inbound flights cancelled. Wednesday had been worse, with more than 6,800 flights delayed and more than 160 cancelled after a cargo plane overshot a runway at Miami. More than 80 million Americans are expected to travel for Thanksgiving, in what the Federal Aviation Administration has called the busiest holiday travel weekend in 16 years.

Key points

  • More than 800,000 homes and businesses were without power by Thursday afternoon on Thanksgiving, with Virginia, Maryland and Missouri the worst hit.
  • National Grid said it had restored nearly 300,000 customers in New England, but more than 160,000 were still out on Thursday morning after gusts of more than 80 miles per hour.
  • Macy's moved its 100th Thanksgiving Day Parade to 8.30am on Saturday, the first postponement in the parade's history, as high wind and coastal flood warnings covered the New York area.
  • States of emergency were declared in New Jersey and New York, and by 3pm on Thursday 419 flights had been cancelled and 1,708 delayed.
