Thanksgiving Storms Leave More Than 325,000 Without Power Across the US

More than 325,000 homes and businesses across the United States were without power on Thanksgiving morning as heavy snow hit the Midwest and a powerful nor’easter moved up the East Coast, with states of emergency declared in New Jersey and New York.

The tracking site PowerOutage.us counted 327,654 customers without power as of about 6:25am Eastern time on Thursday. Missouri had the most, with 115,762, after heavy, wet snow brought down trees in the St Louis area. Virginia had 111,524 customers out and North Carolina 45,850, with Illinois at 13,558 and Maryland at 7,140.

In New England, the utility National Grid said it had restored nearly 300,000 customers since the nor’easter began on Tuesday night, including more than 100,000 overnight. As of 9am on Thursday, 160,113 customers were still without power, including 146,429 in Massachusetts and 13,684 in Rhode Island. The storm brought wind gusts of more than 80 miles per hour, and the company said repairs would take several days, with most customers restored by Saturday morning.

Macy’s moved its Thanksgiving Day Parade, the 100th edition, to Saturday 28 November, the first time the parade has ever been postponed. A high wind warning is in effect from 10am on Thursday to 1am on Friday for New York City, Long Island and much of New Jersey, with coastal flood warnings also in place. Forecasters expected one to three inches of rain in the New York area and gusts of 40 to 50 miles per hour, reaching 60 to 70 along exposed coasts, and warned the storm could strengthen into a bomb cyclone.

New Jersey’s governor, Mikie Sherrill, declared a state of emergency from 6pm on Wednesday, and New York’s governor, Kathy Hochul, declared one for New York City, Long Island and Westchester County. Utilities staged repair crews across the region, with PSEG Long Island deploying more than 500 personnel. Winter storm warnings were also in effect in parts of the Midwest, where some areas could see six to 12 inches of snow.

Flights were heavily disrupted. By late morning on Thursday, nearly 400 flights in the United States had been cancelled and more than 1,000 delayed, according to flight tracking data. Baltimore/Washington airport was the worst hit, with 54 cancellations, and gusts at LaGuardia reached 45 miles per hour. Wednesday had been worse, with more than 6,800 flights delayed and more than 160 cancelled, after a cargo plane overshot a runway at Miami and briefly grounded flights bound for the airport. More than 80 million Americans are expected to travel for Thanksgiving, in what the Federal Aviation Administration has called the busiest holiday travel weekend in 16 years.

Key points

  • More than 325,000 homes and businesses were without power on Thanksgiving morning, with Missouri, Virginia and North Carolina the worst hit.
  • National Grid said it had restored nearly 300,000 customers in New England, but more than 160,000 were still out on Thursday morning after gusts of more than 80 miles per hour.
  • Macy’s moved its 100th Thanksgiving Day Parade to Saturday, the first postponement in the parade’s history, as high wind and coastal flood warnings covered the New York area.
  • States of emergency were declared in New Jersey and New York, and by late morning nearly 400 flights had been cancelled and more than 1,000 delayed.
