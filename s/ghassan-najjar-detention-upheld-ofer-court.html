Court Keeps Burin Olive Farmer Ghassan Najjar in Detention Until February

Judges at Israel's Ofer Military Court have confirmed a four-month detention order against Palestinian farmer and land activist Ghassan Najjar, keeping him behind bars until at least 26 February.

Najjar, 36, appeared before the Ofer Military Court on Monday, where judges confirmed the order first imposed after his arrest. His sister, Suha Najjar, said he would stay in Ofer Prison until 26 February at the earliest, and that the court would review his case before deciding whether the detention should be extended.

Najjar was arrested in a pre-dawn raid on his home village of Burin, south of Nablus in the occupied West Bank, on 27 October, at the start of the olive harvest. He has been held in administrative detention since, and the authorities have given no reason for his arrest. The Institute for Middle East Understanding said he was detained because he planned to pick olives on his own land and help neighbouring farmers pick theirs.

Najjar is a co-founder of the Burin Land and Farming Agricultural Cooperative and coordinates the Sumoud network, which supports farmers and shepherds facing settler violence and restrictions on their land. He has been arrested repeatedly over the years, was first detained at the age of 15, and has been under a travel ban since 2011. Burin sits between the settlements of Yitzhar and Har Bracha, and its farmers have long faced attacks and limits on reaching their groves, above all during the olive harvest.

Administrative detention allows Israel to hold Palestinians without charge or trial, with the order renewable on review. Amnesty International said 3,101 Palestinians were held under the measure as of October, and it has called for Najjar's immediate release, saying he was arrested only because of his peaceful work defending the olive harvest. Front Line Defenders has also demanded his release.

Key points

  • An Israeli military court at Ofer upheld the four-month administrative detention order on Monday, keeping Najjar in prison until at least 26 February, with a review to decide on any extension.
  • Najjar was arrested in a pre-dawn raid in Burin on 27 October, at the start of the olive harvest, and has been held without charge since, with no reason given for his arrest.
  • Rights groups say he was detained for planning to harvest olives on his land and help other farmers do the same, after warnings from Israel's security agency to stop his activism.
  • Administrative detention allows detention without charge or trial; Amnesty says 3,101 Palestinians were held under the measure as of October.
