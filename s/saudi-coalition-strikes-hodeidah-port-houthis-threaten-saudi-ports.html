Saudi-Led Coalition Strikes Hodeidah Port After Evacuation Order as Houthis Threaten Saudi Ports

The Saudi-led coalition has struck the Houthi-held Red Sea port of Hodeidah after ordering civilians to evacuate the area, as the Houthis threatened to hit Saudi ports including Jeddah in retaliation.

Coalition spokesman Maj Gen Turki al-Maliki posted the evacuation order on X early on Wednesday, asking civilians to leave the area "for their safety" and marking the zone on a map. Hours later it said it had struck "operational capabilities" at the port, and Houthi-run media reported intense strikes on the city and its airport. It also said it hit targets in Saada and Amran, and intercepted four drones over Ras Tanura in Saudi Arabia's Eastern Province.

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree warned that Jeddah and all Saudi ports on the Red Sea coast would become targets if Hodeidah was attacked, urging port workers to evacuate and ships to leave. The escalation follows Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia, including a Riyadh tower drone strike that injured 11 people, and comes as the wider US-Israel war on Iran grinds on. Washington urged Americans in the region to stay alert.

Key points

  • The Saudi-led coalition ordered civilians to evacuate Hodeidah port early on Wednesday, then said it struck "operational capabilities" there.
  • Houthi media reported intense strikes on the port city and its airport, with overflights continuing, and strikes were also reported near Sanaa airport.
  • The Houthis threatened to target Jeddah and all Saudi Red Sea ports if Hodeidah was attacked, and urged ships to leave.
