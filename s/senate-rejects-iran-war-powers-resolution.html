Senate Rejects Bid to Rein In Trump's War Powers on Iran

The United States Senate has voted down a resolution that would have told the president to halt the fighting with Iran unless Congress approved it, in the chamber's first vote on the conflict since the midterm elections.

Senators rejected the measure by 51 votes to 48 on Thursday, with the vote falling largely along party lines. It was the first time either house of Congress had taken up the war since voters went to the polls on 3 November and stripped the Republicans of their majorities in both chambers.

Three Republicans crossed the aisle to support the resolution: Rand Paul of Kentucky, Lisa Murkowski of Alaska and Susan Collins of Maine. John Fetterman of Pennsylvania was the lone Democrat to oppose it. Thom Tillis of North Carolina, who had backed a similar resolution in September, voted against this one.

The measure was put forward by Tim Kaine, the Democratic senator from Virginia who has led repeated attempts in the Senate to give Congress a greater say over decisions to wage war. It called on the president to withdraw American forces from the fighting with Iran unless lawmakers authorised continued military action. The House of Representatives had passed the resolution earlier in the year.

The vote does not change the course of the war. The resolution was a concurrent resolution, and even if it had passed it would not on its own have compelled the president to stop the fighting. But it was the clearest measure yet of how much backing the conflict still has in Congress after an election in which voters punished the governing party in part over the war and its economic costs.

The war began in February, when the United States and Israel opened a joint attack on Iran. Since then Democrats have forced a series of votes on the conflict, using them to put Republicans on the record. Thursday's vote was the latest in that series, and it showed the Republican leadership holding its ranks together despite the election setback.

Key points

  • The Senate votes 51-48 to reject a war powers resolution on the Iran war, its first vote on the conflict since the midterm elections
  • Three Republicans, Paul, Murkowski and Collins, back the measure; Fetterman is the only Democrat to oppose it
  • The resolution would have told Trump to end the war or seek Congress's approval, but it carried no legal force to compel him
  • The result shows Republican leaders holding their ranks despite election losses widely linked to opposition to the war
