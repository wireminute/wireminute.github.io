Britain Freezes Houthi Assets and Sanctions Four Senior Officials

Britain has imposed sanctions on Yemen's Houthi movement and four of its senior officials, freezing the group's assets and accusing the officials of torture, rape and other serious abuses of detainees.

The British government announced the measures on Tuesday, placing Ansar Allah, the formal name of the Houthi movement, under its Yemen sanctions regime. The group faces an asset freeze, while the four officials face asset freezes, travel bans and bans on serving as company directors. The Foreign Office said the Houthi authorities had repeatedly shown disregard for the basic rights of people under their control, as well as for British staff and aid workers.

Those named are Abdulkarim Amir al-Din Hussein al-Houthi, who has served as the Houthis' interior minister since 2019; Abdul Hakim Hashem Ali al-Khaiwani, who leads the movement's security and intelligence service; Mohammed Moufleh, a judge appointed by the Houthis; and Sarim al-Din Muffadal, a senior Houthi prosecutor.

Britain said the interior ministry under al-Houthi runs detention centres where prisoners were beaten, given electric shocks and sexually abused. It said Muffadal was suspected of involvement in sexual violence against women detainees, including rape, along with torture and other degrading treatment. Moufleh was accused of a serious breach of a person's right to liberty and security.

Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband said the Houthis' renewed attacks on Britain's partners and innocent civilians were unacceptable and that those behind them must be held to account. The Foreign Office said the measures were aimed at the Houthi leadership rather than the Yemeni people.

Britain said the Houthis had resumed fighting across Yemen and were behind strikes on commercial shipping in the Red Sea and the Gulf of Aden, as well as missile and drone attacks on countries in the region. The designations were made under Britain's Global Human Rights and International Humanitarian Law sanctions rules, the government said.

Key points

  • Britain has designated the Houthi movement, formally Ansar Allah, under its Yemen sanctions regime and frozen the group's assets.
  • Four senior officials face asset freezes, travel bans and director disqualifications: interior minister Abdulkarim al-Houthi, intelligence chief Abdul Hakim al-Khaiwani, judge Mohammed Moufleh and prosecutor Sarim al-Din Muffadal.
  • Britain accuses the officials of torture, sexual violence and other serious abuses of detainees in Houthi-run facilities.
  • London says the measures respond to the Houthis' renewed fighting, attacks on Red Sea shipping and missile and drone strikes on regional partners.
