Millions Locked Out of UK Benefits and Pension Services After Serious Cyber Incident

Millions of people in Britain have been left unable to reach online benefits and pension services after the government department that runs them shut its systems down following what its minister called a serious cyber incident.

The Department for Work and Pensions said it had shut down part of its online estate as a precaution after detecting a cyber incident in its systems. Unusual activity was first noticed on Thursday, and the department moved to take its public-facing services offline on Thursday evening. It is working with GCHQ's National Cyber Security Centre and the Government Cyber Coordination Centre to investigate and to bring the services back online.

Almost all of the department's public-facing services were affected, including the online portals for Universal Credit, the state pension, personal independence payments and pension credit, as well as the Tell Us Once service used to notify the government after a death. By midday on Friday the systems still could not be reached, and the government has not said when they will be back.

The department has stressed that scheduled payments will go ahead as usual and are unaffected. It also said people will not face penalties for journal entries they cannot complete while the systems are down. Some claimants have told reporters that money they depend on has still not arrived, and that they fear being penalised for being unable to update the online journals that show they are looking for work.

Work and Pensions Secretary Pat McFadden called the incident serious and said it was too early to say what had caused it. He said taking the systems down was the right step to protect the public and their personal information. Downing Street said there was currently no evidence that personal data had been lost, and no evidence that any other government department had been targeted, but it declined to rule out that the incident was a hacking attempt. The investigation is ongoing.

The department is one of Britain's largest, serving around 20 million people who claim the state pension or working-age, disability and ill-health benefits. The outage is the latest in a series of cyber incidents to hit British institutions in recent months, and follows repeated government warnings that attacks could knock essential services offline.

  • Universal Credit, state pension, personal independence payment and Tell Us Once portals were unreachable by midday on Friday, with no date given for their return.
