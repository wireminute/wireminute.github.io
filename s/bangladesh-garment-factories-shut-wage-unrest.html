Tear Gas in Gazipur as Bangladesh Garment Workers Reject 36% Pay Rise

Police fired tear gas and sound grenades at garment workers in Gazipur on Thursday after they blocked two highways to reject a 36 percent pay rise they say is too small, a day after the wage board announced the new rate.

Workers came out of factories in Gazipur on Thursday morning, the second day of protests since the wage board's announcement, and blocked the Dhaka-Tangail highway near Naojor at about 11:30 a.m., police said. Officers fired tear gas shells and sound grenades and used batons to clear the road after protesters set fire to rubbish piled on the highway and to a police motorcycle. Traffic stood still on both sides of the road for a time before police restored movement.

A second group blocked the Dhaka-Mymensingh highway near Sreepur at about the same time, in protest against an attack on workers there, and police fired tear gas at about 11:45 a.m. to disperse them. Workers threw bricks during the confrontation, injuring an additional superintendent of police and several other officers, police said. The industrial police superintendent in Gazipur said the situation was now calm but officers remained on alert.

The unrest spread beyond the highways. Thirty factories in Gazipur declared a holiday on Thursday, and more plants in Konabari, Sreepur and surrounding areas announced three-day closures, as owners moved to keep workers off site. The protests continued even though the wage board had settled the new minimum at 17,000 taka a month, about $139, up from 12,500 taka, after a session lasting nearly 12 hours that ended at about 2 a.m. on Wednesday.

Workers say the rise, the smallest in four decades, is not enough. They had sought 30,000 taka a month, and their representatives on the six-member board backed 18,000 taka before being outvoted 4-2. The new four-grade structure runs from 17,000 to 21,500 taka and takes effect on December 1, with the higher pay reaching workers with their January wages. Garments earn more than 80 percent of Bangladesh's export income and employ more than four million people, most of them women, so a prolonged stoppage threatens orders and pay alike.

Key points

  • Police fired tear gas and sound grenades in Gazipur on Thursday as garment workers blocked the Dhaka-Tangail and Dhaka-Mymensingh highways.
  • Protesters set fire to a police motorcycle and to rubbish on the highway; workers threw bricks, injuring a senior police officer and several others.
  • Thirty factories declared a holiday on Thursday and more announced three-day closures as the protests entered a second day.
  • Workers reject the wage board's 36% rise to 17,000 taka a month, the smallest in four decades; they had sought 30,000 taka.
