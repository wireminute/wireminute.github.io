Bangladesh Lifts Garment Workers' Minimum Pay 36% After Weeks of Protests

Bangladesh has lifted the monthly minimum for garment workers by 36 percent, to 17,000 taka, ending weeks of protests that shut factories and disrupted production in the country's biggest export industry.

The government-appointed wage board settled the new rate at about 2 a.m. on Wednesday after a session lasting nearly 12 hours, with four of its six members backing 17,000 taka (about $139) a month, up from 12,500 taka. The two worker representatives on the board had supported 18,000 taka. Board chairman Judge Syed Azad Subhani confirmed the majority decision, and workers' representative Babul Akhter gave the vote breakdown.

The rise is the smallest in more than a decade, and it falls well short of what workers had sought when the dispute began, an initial demand of 30,000 taka a month. Under a four-grade pay structure, monthly wages will run from 17,000 taka for the lowest grade to 21,500 taka for the highest. The new rates come into force on December 1, and the higher pay should reach workers with their January wages.

The decision follows protests that began in October and spread across the factory belts around Dhaka, closing plants, blocking a highway and bringing clashes with police. On Tuesday alone, the factory owners' association said 397 plants could not operate, while 84 more stayed closed under the labour law's provision allowing shutdowns without pay during stoppages. The association estimated the unrest had cost the industry about $428 million, with orders worth $254 million moving to other countries.

The stakes reach far beyond the factory floor. Garments generate more than 80 percent of Bangladesh's export earnings and employ more than four million people, most of them women, and its factories supply global brands such as H&M and Zara and US retailers Walmart, Target and Gap. Bangladesh is the world's second-largest garment exporter after China, so a prolonged shutdown threatened orders and pay alike. The board's decision now leaves factory owners to reopen plants and workers to judge whether the new rate is enough.

Key points

  • Minimum monthly wage rises 36% to 17,000 taka (about $139) from 12,500 taka, after a wage board vote early on Wednesday.
  • The board voted 4-2 for 17,000 taka; worker representatives had backed 18,000 taka, and workers initially sought 30,000 taka.
  • The new four-grade structure runs from 17,000 to 21,500 taka a month; it comes into force on December 1, with higher pay reaching workers from January.
  • Protests since October shut hundreds of factories and cost the industry an estimated $428 million; garments earn more than 80% of Bangladesh's export income.
