Microsoft Says Its Services Are Fully Back After Day-Long Global Outage

Microsoft has said its email, meetings and workplace services are fully restored after a worldwide failure that left businesses, hospitals and government offices struggling for more than a day.

The company said its consumer services were fully restored at 16:10 GMT on Friday, just over a day after the failure began. In a message posted online on Saturday, it said the work to fix the underlying problem was complete and service had been returned to the last affected customers and regions. It added that it would keep watching the restored systems for an extended period because of the scale of the disruption.

The failure started at around 16:00 GMT on Thursday and spread quickly around the world. Within an hour, reports of problems were coming in from 74 countries and territories, and the outage monitor Downdetector logged more than 7.4 million reports in total, peaking at almost 20,000 a minute, over 1,500 times the usual level. Outlook email, Teams meetings, the Copilot assistant and other Microsoft 365 tools were all affected, along with the Exchange Online systems behind them.

Microsoft traced the fault to its own network work. An earlier upgrade to increase network capacity had introduced a problem that only surfaced after a further change to the systems, leaving servers unable to cope with the load placed on them. The company said it had fixed the underlying fault and stressed that the incident was a technical failure: there was no sign of a cyber attack, and artificial intelligence tools played no part in it. A senior executive had said the same on Thursday evening, and the company says it will publish a full report once its review is finished.

The disruption reached far beyond office inboxes. Government agencies in Australia, Japan and the United States were hit, airlines warned travellers that emailed queries might go unanswered, trading firms reported delays, and some companies had to postpone earnings announcements. Schools and health services also struggled to communicate. Microsoft's shares ended Friday flat at $523.88, and the company apologised to customers and partners for the interruption.

For users, one loose end remains: messages sent while the systems were down. Microsoft said it would not treat the incident as fully closed until the backlog of undelivered mail had been cleared, leaving some organisations unsure when normal delivery would resume. The outage was one of the longest in the company's history and the second such failure this year.

Key points

  • Services were fully restored at 16:10 GMT on Friday, just over a day after the failure began.
  • The fault traces to a network capacity upgrade; the company says there was no cyber attack and no AI involvement.
  • The outage hit Outlook, Teams and Copilot across 74 countries, with more than 7.4 million failure reports.
  • Microsoft has apologised and says it will publish a full report once its review is complete.
