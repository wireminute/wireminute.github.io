Mosque Set on Fire in Northern Israel as Politicians Blame Incitement

A mosque in northern Israel was set on fire early on Thursday in a suspected arson attack, prompting opposition politicians to blame anti-Arab incitement by the government of caretaker prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu.

The fire broke out at dawn at a mosque in the Arab village of Dahi, near Afula in the Lower Galilee. The imam, Khaled al-Zoubi, said he saw smoke and fire at dawn prayer. Police said a room on the lower part of the mosque was damaged and graffiti was sprayed nearby.

Local reports said three unidentified people tried to force their way into the building and then threw a firebomb inside. Graffiti reading revenge was found at the scene, referring to a West Bank settler killed in March. Police have opened an investigation, with the Shin Bet security agency also involved.

Arab and opposition lawmakers accused Netanyahu and his ministers of fuelling hostility towards Arab citizens. Opposition leader Yair Lapid called the fire a heinous hate crime, while Mansour Abbas of the Raam party, called it a hate crime and a desecration of a holy site.

Key points

  • A mosque in Dahi, near Afula, was set on fire at dawn on Thursday; police and the Shin Bet are investigating a suspected hate crime.
  • Graffiti reading revenge was sprayed at the scene, referring to a settler killed in the West Bank in March.
  • Opposition politicians blamed anti-Arab rhetoric from Netanyahu's camp; the government has not responded to the accusations in the reports.
