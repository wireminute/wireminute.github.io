Eight Killed as Peruvian Military Helicopter Crashes in Coca-Growing Valley

Eight Peruvian service members have been killed after an air force helicopter crashed in a remote coca-growing valley in the country's south-east, the defence ministry said, with the pilot the only survivor.

The Peruvian Air Force Mi-17 helicopter, carrying nine people, came down on Thursday near the town of Sanabamba in the Ayacucho region, in the valley formed by the Apurimac, Ene and Mantaro rivers, known by its Spanish acronym VRAEM. The aircraft had been sent to extract a military patrol operating in the remote area, and contact was lost on Thursday evening. Search and rescue teams found the wreckage on Friday, a day after the crash, and located all nine people who had been on board.

Five of the dead were air force personnel and three were soldiers from the army. The three soldiers were members of the patrol the helicopter had been sent to rescue, and they also died in the crash. The pilot and commander, Enzo Espinoza Roque, was the only survivor. He was seriously injured and was flown to Lima for specialist medical treatment, the defence ministry said.

The ministry said the cause of the crash was not yet known and an investigation was under way. The VRAEM is Peru's largest coca-growing basin and a centre of the military's counter-narcotics operations, where troops have long operated against drug trafficking and the remnants of an armed group in an area with almost no road access.

It is the second fatal crash involving a Peruvian military Mi-17 helicopter this year. In February, 15 people, including seven children, were killed when an Mi-17 carrying flood-relief workers crashed in the southern Arequipa region after contact was lost on a flight from Pisco to Chala.
