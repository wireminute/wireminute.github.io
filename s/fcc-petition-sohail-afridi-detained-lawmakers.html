Opposition Asks Constitutional Court to Free Detained Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Chief Minister and 14 Lawmakers

Pakistan's opposition leaders have asked the Federal Constitutional Court to order the immediate release of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa's chief minister and 14 other elected lawmakers, who have not been seen in public since governor's rule was imposed in the province more than a month ago.

National Assembly opposition leader Mahmood Khan Achakzai and Senate opposition leader Raja Nasir Abbas filed the petition jointly on Saturday, through their lawyer Moazzam Butt. It seeks the release of Afridi, provincial assembly Speaker Babar Saleem Swati, seven other members of the provincial assembly and six members of the National Assembly, including Junaid Akbar Khan.

Afridi has not been seen publicly since the federal government imposed governor's rule and a state of emergency in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa on 11 October. A federal minister confirmed the next day that Afridi and some members of his cabinet had been taken into preventive custody. Executive powers in the province have since passed to Governor Faisal Karim Kundi, while Afridi's party maintains that he remains chief minister with the backing of its founder, former prime minister Imran Khan.

The petition argues that keeping elected representatives in custody undermines the mandate voters gave them and breaches fundamental rights, including due process, dignity, freedom of association and equality before the law. It says the emergency powers used to impose governor's rule do not suspend those rights or place the government's actions beyond judicial review. The petitioners ask the court to order the government to disclose where the lawmakers are being held, produce them before the court, and declare their custody unlawful.

In a separate move on Saturday, Abbas and Balochistan National Party chief Akhtar Mengal asked to join a case about an order restraining the Peshawar High Court from hearing petitions on the detention of party workers; that case is expected to be taken up on Monday. Another opposition lawmaker, Shandana Gulzar, filed a similar petition on Thursday seeking Afridi's release. Afridi also faces arrest warrants in a case over an attack on a state broadcaster's building in May 2023, which a Peshawar anti-terrorism court reissued earlier this week. A federal minister has said governor's rule will end after six months, while the emergency will remain until peace returns to the province.

Key points

  • Achakzai and Abbas filed the petition on Saturday, seeking the release of Afridi, the provincial assembly speaker and 13 other elected lawmakers.
  • Afridi has not been seen in public since governor's rule and emergency were imposed in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa on 11 October; a minister confirmed he was in preventive custody.
  • The petition says the detentions breach fundamental rights and asks the court to order the government to disclose where the lawmakers are held and produce them in court.
  • A related case on the courts' power to hear detention petitions is expected to be taken up on Monday; another opposition lawmaker filed a similar petition on Thursday.
