Bancroft Breaks Silence on Sandpapergate, Says Whole Australia Team Knew About Ball Tampering

Cameron Bancroft has broken his silence on the 2018 ball-tampering scandal, saying the whole Australian team knew sandpaper was being used against South Africa and that he became the scapegoat for the side.

Cameron Bancroft has broken his silence on the 2018 ball-tampering scandal, saying the whole Australian team knew sandpaper was being used against South Africa and that he became the scapegoat for the side. In a written statement published in The West Australian, Bancroft backed claims first made by Usman Khawaja in his autobiography Sabr that every member of the squad was aware of the plan. He wrote that it was not just the three of them, himself, Steve Smith and David Warner, who knew about him using sandpaper on the ball in South Africa. He said the final straw that pushed him to write the account was Australia captain Pat Cummins' press conference on Friday, in which Cummins rejected Khawaja's claims.

Bancroft admitted that his account to Cricket Australia's investigation in 2018 was incomplete and untruthful, saying he held back details including when the use of sandpaper began, how long it went on for, and the involvement and awareness of others. He said the tampering started during the second Test in Port Elizabeth in March 2018, before the Cape Town Test where he was caught on camera, and that he used sandpaper nearly every over. He added that his role in the scandal had been acknowledged in front of the group at the time.

He said former opener David Warner suggested using sandpaper, and described regular conversations with Cummins, Mitchell Starc, Josh Hazlewood and Mitchell Marsh about whether the ball was reversing enough, with former captain Tim Paine also involved in discussions about its movement. Bancroft also said bowling coach David Saker approached him during the Cape Town Test, and that his use of sandpaper had been celebrated within the group. He said he looks back on the affair as the episode that destroyed his career.

Cummins had called Khawaja's claims complete and utter nonsense in an hour-long press conference on Friday, and the bowling group issued a joint statement in 2021 insisting they had no prior knowledge of the plot. Cricket Australia chief executive Todd Greenberg has announced an independent forum to examine the scandal again, while making clear there will be no new investigations or sanctions. Bancroft was caught on camera working on the ball during the third Test in Cape Town in March 2018. Smith and Warner were banned for 12 months and Bancroft for nine, while the bowlers escaped punishment.

Key points

  • Bancroft says the whole Australian team knew sandpaper was being used in South Africa in 2018, not just Smith, Warner and himself.
  • He admits his account to Cricket Australia's 2018 investigation was incomplete and untruthful, and held back details about when the tampering began and who knew.
  • He says Warner suggested using sandpaper and describes regular conversations with Cummins, Starc, Hazlewood and Marsh about reverse swing.
  • Cummins has denied the claims and Cricket Australia has set up an independent forum, with no new investigations or sanctions planned.
