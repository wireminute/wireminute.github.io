Three Dead and 23 Trapped as Flood Swamps Unauthorised Gold Mine in Indonesia

Three miners have been found dead and about 23 more are believed trapped after floodwater poured into an unauthorised gold mine in Indonesia, as rescuers race to pump the shaft dry.

Gold diggers were working late on Monday when torrential rain pushed the Ciseureuh River over its banks and into their shaft in Bogor regency, West Java, about 60 kilometres south of Jakarta. Eleven men got out of one part of the workings before the floodwater took hold.

On Wednesday rescuers brought three bodies to the surface from 30 to 40 metres down, the Jakarta search and rescue agency said. Pumps are draining the shaft, which runs about 100 metres deep, but officials said the water level had barely moved and the cramped, unlit passage was slowing the search.

Police said 26 people were still unaccounted for. The state miner Antam, which operates gold mines in the Pongkor area, said the flooded shaft was an unauthorised working outside its operations and that all of its own staff were safe.

Key points

  • Three miners have been found dead and about 23 more are believed trapped in a flooded unauthorised gold mine in Bogor regency, West Java.
  • Torrential rain late on Monday pushed the Ciseureuh River into the roughly 100-metre-deep shaft; 11 miners escaped before it filled.
  • Rescuers are pumping water from the shaft, but officials say the water has barely moved and the cramped, unlit access is slowing the search.
