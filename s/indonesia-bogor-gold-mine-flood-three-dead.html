Sixteen Dead, Ten Still Trapped as Flooded Gold Mine Search Enters Fourth Day

Sixteen miners have been confirmed dead and ten more are still believed trapped in a flooded unauthorised gold mine in Indonesia, rescuers said on Thursday, as the search entered its fourth day with hopes of finding survivors fading.

The toll rose again on Thursday as rescue teams pulled more bodies from two adjacent shafts in Bogor regency, West Java, taking the confirmed dead from 13 to 16. Ten miners are still believed trapped underground. Six of the dead have been identified, West Java's police chief said, and the search will continue as long as conditions allow.

Twenty-six workers were trapped on Monday when heavy rain pushed the Ciseureuh River over its banks and into the shaft, about 60 kilometres south of Jakarta, while 11 others escaped from a different part of the workings before the floodwater took hold. The victims were found at depths of up to 40 metres. A team of 325 rescuers, emergency workers, police, soldiers, health officials and volunteers is pumping water from the shaft with submersible pumps and sealing openings where groundwater seeps in, and is now using ropes to reach the two shafts.

The rescue agency chief said the depth of the hole, the tight access and the lack of lighting were shaping how teams worked, and that the water level had barely moved despite the pumping. Heavy rain on Thursday forced the search to pause at times, with streams of water flowing back into the mine. The alarm came about 16 hours late because the mine was unlicensed: local miners and residents first tried to pump the water out themselves before the situation became unmanageable, and a formal report only reached Jakarta's search and rescue office in the early hours of Wednesday.

The mine sits near facilities run by the Pongkor gold unit of state-owned miner PT Antam, but the company said the incident was unrelated to its operations. Hand-dug, unlicensed gold mining is common in the area, and the flooded shaft had no permits or safety systems. A police spokesperson said the chances of finding survivors were now slim, adding that it would take a miracle for anyone still to be alive. The search will go on as long as conditions allow, officials said, with the ten missing miners now believed to have been underground for four days.

Key points

  • The confirmed death toll has risen to 16, with ten miners still believed trapped in the flooded unauthorised gold mine in Bogor regency, West Java.
  • Twenty-six workers were trapped on Monday when heavy rain pushed the Ciseureuh River into the roughly 100-metre-deep shaft; 11 others escaped from another part of the workings.
  • Heavy rain on Thursday forced the search to pause at times, and rescuers say the chances of finding survivors on the fourth day are slim.
  • The alarm came about 16 hours late because the mine was unlicensed; the shaft sits near a state-owned mining unit's facilities, but the company says the incident is unrelated to its operations.
