Thirteen Dead and 13 Still Trapped as Flood Swamps Unauthorised Gold Mine in Indonesia

Thirteen miners have been confirmed dead and 13 more are still believed trapped after floodwater poured into an unauthorised gold mine in Indonesia, as rescuers resume the search on Thursday with ropes and pumps in a shaft about 100 metres deep.

The toll rose overnight as rescue teams pulled more bodies from two adjacent shafts in Bogor regency, West Java, taking the confirmed dead from nine to 13. Six of the dead have been identified, West Java's police chief said, and the search will continue on Thursday if the weather allows.

Twenty-six workers were trapped on Monday when heavy rain pushed the Ciseureuh River over its banks and into the shaft, about 60 kilometres south of Jakarta, while 11 others escaped from a different part of the workings before the floodwater took hold. The victims were found at depths of up to 40 metres. A team of 325 rescuers, emergency workers, police, soldiers, health officials and volunteers is pumping water from the shaft with submersible pumps and sealing openings where groundwater seeps in, and is now using ropes to reach the two shafts.

The rescue agency chief said the depth of the hole, the tight access and the lack of lighting were shaping how teams worked, and that the water level had barely moved despite the pumping. The alarm came about 16 hours late because the mine was unlicensed: local miners and residents first tried to pump the water out themselves before the situation became unmanageable, and a formal report only reached Jakarta's search and rescue office in the early hours of Wednesday.

The mine sits near facilities run by the Pongkor gold unit of state-owned miner PT Antam, but the company said the incident was unrelated to its operations. Hand-dug, unlicensed gold mining is common in the area, and the flooded shaft had no permits or safety systems. The search will go on as long as conditions allow, officials said, with the 13 missing miners now believed to have been underground for three days.

Key points

  • The confirmed death toll has risen to 13, with 13 miners still believed trapped in the flooded unauthorised gold mine in Bogor regency, West Java.
  • Twenty-six workers were trapped on Monday when heavy rain pushed the Ciseureuh River into the roughly 100-metre-deep shaft; 11 others escaped from another part of the workings.
