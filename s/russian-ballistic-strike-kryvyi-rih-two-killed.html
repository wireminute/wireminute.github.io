Russian Ballistic Missiles Kill a Child and a Paramedic in Kryvyi Rih, Wounding 47

Two Russian ballistic missiles hit the centre of Kryvyi Rih on Friday evening, killing a seven-year-old boy and a 20-year-old paramedic. The number of wounded has risen to 47, including six children, local officials said on Saturday morning.

The missiles struck the car park of a popular restaurant in the city centre, in a densely populated area, on World Children's Day. It was the second attack on the city in a day, after a strike on an infrastructure facility earlier on Friday, and it followed a large Russian aerial assault that killed seven people in Kyiv the same morning. Kryvyi Rih is the home town of President Volodymyr Zelensky.

The boy died in hospital after being brought in with serious head injuries, and the paramedic, who had come to the scene to help the wounded, also died in hospital. Six children were among the wounded, including a three-year-old girl and a four-year-old boy, and 27 people were in a serious condition, according to Oleksandr Vilkul, who heads the city's Defence Council. Two paramedics and two members of the State Emergency Service were also among the injured.

Drones struck the same site again while rescuers were working there, in what officials called a deliberate attack on emergency workers, and a further drone hit an infrastructure facility in the city overnight. Rescue and repair work was hampered by continuing air raid alerts, Vilkul said. The regional prosecutor's office said the missiles were preliminarily identified as Iskander-M ballistic missiles.

The strikes damaged 23 apartment buildings, a hospital, schools, businesses, garages, ambulances and other vehicles, according to preliminary information. Hospitals in the city were treating the wounded through the night. President Zelensky said Ukrainian forces had hit three Russian ballistic missile launchers overnight, destroying one of them. The story is still developing.
