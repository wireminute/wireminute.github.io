OpenAI Hits Back at Apple With Trade Secrets Countersuit in AI Device Race

OpenAI has filed a countersuit against Apple, accusing the iPhone maker of stealing its trade secrets and leaning on suppliers to slow its push into AI-powered devices, in a legal fight that pits two of the biggest names in technology against each other.

The counterclaim was filed on Friday in a federal court in California, in the same case Apple opened in July. OpenAI says Apple has used its size and wealth to hold back a rival, pressing hardware suppliers that work with the ChatGPT maker and taking confidential details of devices OpenAI has yet to release. Both companies have asked for a jury to hear the case.

Much of the filing turns on how Apple handles staff files. OpenAI says Apple encouraged employees to keep company work on personal iCloud accounts and to run work messages through personal iMessage, then used the presence of those files to threaten people who left for rivals. The filing says the practice set traps for departing staff. OpenAI also says a former employee who went back to Apple sent over 100 private files about a device OpenAI has not yet released to personal accounts, a claim it first raised in October.

Apple has now answered the countersuit. A spokesperson said the case is about OpenAI staff deliberately taking and using Apple's secret and confidential information on unreleased products and processes, and described OpenAI's counterclaims as a transparent attempt to distract from Apple's own lawsuit, in which OpenAI has already been ordered to stop using Apple's confidential material. OpenAI also withdrew its motion to have Apple's case dismissed, without giving a reason. The claims on both sides have not been tested in court.

Apple sued OpenAI in July, after OpenAI bought the design studio io, founded by former Apple design chief Jony Ive, in a deal worth 6.4 billion dollars. Apple accused OpenAI and two former Apple staff members, Chang Liu and Tang Tan, of taking confidential engineering and hardware information to help build its own devices. OpenAI disputes those claims, and the trial in Apple's case is set for July 19, 2027.

The two companies worked together in 2024 to bring ChatGPT to the iPhone. Now both are racing to build the next wave of personal gadgets built around AI, and the court fight will help decide who controls that market. The filing runs 81 pages and Judge Edward Davila is presiding.

Key points

  • OpenAI filed counterclaims on Friday in a California federal court, accusing Apple of stealing its trade secrets and pressuring hardware suppliers.
  • The filing says Apple encouraged staff to keep work files on personal iCloud and iMessage accounts, then used that against employees who left for rivals.
  • Apple pushed back, saying the case is about OpenAI staff deliberately taking its confidential information and calling the counterclaims a distraction; OpenAI also withdrew its motion to dismiss Apple's case.
  • Apple sued OpenAI in July after its 6.4 billion dollar purchase of Jony Ive's design studio io; the trial in that case is set for July 19, 2027.
