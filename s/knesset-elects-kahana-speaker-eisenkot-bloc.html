Kahana Takes Speaker's Chair as Eisenkot Bloc Seizes Control of Israel's Parliament

Israel's parliament has elected Matan Kahana of Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar party as its new speaker, handing the opposition bloc control of the legislative agenda in the first major defeat for Benjamin Netanyahu since last month's deadlocked election.

Kahana won 66 votes against 52 for the rival candidate, according to the parliament spokesperson's office, replacing Amir Ohana of Netanyahu's Likud party. In his first speech from the chair, Kahana said the Knesset was the home of all of Israel's citizens and that he intended to be a speaker for everyone.

The speakership carries real power in Israel's 120-seat, single-chamber parliament, deciding which bills reach a vote. Kahana will be able to advance measures backed by Eisenkot's bloc and its supporters, including an independent inquiry into the 7 October 2023 Hamas attack, which Netanyahu has resisted, term limits for the prime minister, and the repeal of laws that critics say weakened the courts.

Netanyahu's Likud party denounced the result, saying Kahana had been elected with what it called the votes of terror supporters. Netanyahu spent the election campaign attacking the Arab-led parties, which hold 19 seats, as an existential threat, language those parties condemned as racist. Eisenkot's bloc has also taken the chairs of several key parliamentary committees, tightening its grip on the chamber's work.

The vote follows the 27 October election, in which Netanyahu's alliance with far-right and ultra-Orthodox parties won 53 seats, short of a majority, while Eisenkot's four-party bloc took 48. President Isaac Herzog has asked Eisenkot, a former military chief, to try to form a government, but he too lacks a majority and is likely to have to compromise on campaign promises. Netanyahu remains caretaker prime minister until a government is formed, and a fresh election will be called next year if no one succeeds.

Kahana, a retired air force colonel who served in the military's elite Sayeret Matkal unit before becoming a fighter pilot, was religious services minister from 2021 to 2022. Netanyahu has sought to cast doubt on the election result by alleging voter fraud among Arab voters without evidence; election officials say they are examining possible misconduct but have seen no sign of fraud that could have changed the outcome.

Key points

  • Matan Kahana of Eisenkot's Yashar party was elected Knesset speaker by 66 votes to 52, replacing Likud's Amir Ohana.
  • As speaker, Kahana controls which bills come to a vote and can advance an inquiry into the 7 October 2023 attack, term limits and repeal of laws curbing the courts.
  • Netanyahu's Likud condemned the vote, saying Kahana was elected with the votes of terror supporters.
  • Eisenkot, tasked by President Herzog with forming a government, holds 48 seats against Likud's 53 and still needs a majority of 61; Netanyahu stays on as caretaker.
