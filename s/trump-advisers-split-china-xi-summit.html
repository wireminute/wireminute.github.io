Trump’s Advisers Split Over China as Xi Summit Nears, With Nvidia Scandal Fuelling the Feud

President Donald Trump’s top advisers have spent the past week feuding over China policy, exposing a deep split inside the administration days before his meeting with Xi Jinping, as fallout from the Nvidia chip scandal continues to shake the White House.

Trump boarded Air Force One on Monday for a four-day trip to Asia, travelling first to the Philippines and then to the Chinese tech hub of Shenzhen, bringing with him a team of senior advisers who have been clashing over how to handle Beijing. “One wants to be friendly with China, and one wants to have a blowup with China,” the president said on Monday as he departed the White House. “I don’t think you want to insult China just before you get there.”

At the centre of the row is the scandal over Nvidia’s advanced chips. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has in recent days accused Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick of failing to act against Megaspeed, a Singapore-based company suspected of diverting Nvidia’s advanced chips to China despite months of warnings from national-security officials. Trump appeared to defend Lutnick on Monday, suggesting that delaying restrictions may have helped avoid a confrontation with China.

Bessent, Lutnick and Secretary of State Marco Rubio are all joining Trump on the trip. The Nvidia revelations have given new ammunition to more hawkish officials such as Rubio to push for a tougher approach ahead of the meeting with Xi, the president’s fourth with the Chinese leader in just over a year. The White House has sought to play down any effect the turmoil will have on the summit.

A senior American official said Trump was not expected to raise Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive who is suspected by the United States of having ties to Chinese intelligence. Trump has been focused on securing another economic agreement with Xi before a trade truce expires in January, and on an unprecedented three-way summit with Xi and Russian President Vladimir Putin to discuss nuclear arms reduction and the Iran war. The Commerce Department also announced a long-awaited rule on Monday to restrict exports of high-end chips to several countries, while an American official noted Trump had kept the ban on exports of Nvidia’s most advanced chip.

Key points

  • Trump’s advisers are split between engagement with Beijing and confrontation, days before his meeting with Xi Jinping in Shenzhen.
  • Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has accused Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick of failing to act against Megaspeed, a firm suspected of diverting Nvidia chips to China.
  • Trump defended Lutnick on Monday, while hawks such as Secretary of State Marco Rubio are pushing for a tougher line.
  • Trump is seeking an economic deal before a trade truce expires in January, and is also pursuing a three-way summit with Xi and Vladimir Putin.
