APEC Leaders Adopt Shenzhen Declaration, Pledging Closer Ties and Fewer Trade Barriers

Leaders of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation forum have adopted a joint declaration in Shenzhen pledging stronger regional connectivity and fewer barriers to trade and investment, closing their two-day summit in the southern Chinese city.

The 33rd APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting, chaired by Chinese President Xi Jinping on November 18 and 19, adopted the 2026 APEC Leaders' Declaration by consensus. The declaration sets out the Shenzhen Outlook on building an Asia-Pacific community, alongside three outcome documents covering trade, connectivity and innovation. The summit was held under the theme of building an Asia-Pacific community for shared prosperity.

The declaration calls for stronger land, sea and air transport links, better infrastructure networks, closer coordination of policies and regulations, and easier travel for business and tourism, alongside more exchanges between peoples. Leaders also pledged to cut institutional barriers that hold back trade and investment, and to support macroeconomic stability, productivity and growth across the region.

Xi set out four pillars for the community: economy and trade, connectivity, innovation and development, built on solidarity, openness, innovation and inclusive development. He presented a free trade area spanning the Asia-Pacific as a shared goal, urged steady progress towards it, and called for wider use of digital trade systems, stronger digital infrastructure and closer cooperation on artificial intelligence and cross-border data flows. No specific timetable or targets were set out.

China pledged $20 million to support the forum's practical cooperation and said it would set up a Centre for Asia-Pacific Community Studies in Shenzhen. Business leaders at the parallel chief executives' summit issued their own document, the Shenzhen Initiative, the first of its kind in the event's history, calling for trade barriers to come down and supply chains to be made more resilient.

Key points

  • APEC leaders adopted the 2026 Leaders' Declaration in Shenzhen, setting out the Shenzhen Outlook on building an Asia-Pacific community, with three outcome documents on trade, connectivity and innovation.
  • The declaration pledges stronger transport and infrastructure links, closer policy coordination, easier business and tourist travel, and fewer institutional barriers to trade and investment.
  • Xi Jinping set out four pillars, economy and trade, connectivity, innovation and development, and presented a region-wide free trade area as a shared goal, with closer cooperation on digital trade, data flows and artificial intelligence.
  • China pledged $20 million for the forum's practical cooperation and will establish a research centre in Shenzhen; business leaders issued their own Shenzhen Initiative calling for lower trade barriers.
