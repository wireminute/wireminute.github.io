Offices Worldwide Rewind to Phones and Fax as Email Outage Drags Into a Second Day

A global failure of the world's most widely used office software entered its second day on Friday, cutting email and meetings for millions and forcing hospitals, banks, airlines and schools to improvise.

The failure began on Thursday and stretched into a second day on Friday, knocking out Outlook email, the Teams meeting platform and the Copilot artificial intelligence assistant. The company blamed server problems, apologised to customers and partners for the disruption, and said it was seeing broad recovery and stability across its services, while warning that full restoration was still in progress.

Crucially for worried users, the company ruled out anything malicious: the outage was a technical issue, it said, not a cyber attack and not the work of AI systems. The disruption was recorded in 74 countries and territories, with users hit across the Americas, Europe, Asia and Africa — a reminder that more than 430 million people rely on the company's 365 services for daily work.

The knock-on effects reached far beyond office inboxes. In Britain, union officials said health service staff handling emergency calls were unable to pass patient details along in the normal way, leaving a large backlog. New Zealand's immigration agency halted the processing of all visa applications. In India, major airlines warned customers that emailed queries might not reach them, and in London's financial district companies scrambled to rearrange results announcements, with one large insurer circulating alternative phone numbers and moving recordings between devices over Bluetooth because email was down.

As screens stayed dark, older technology had a brief renaissance. Telephones, fax machines and Bluetooth connections limited the damage for many firms, and a European exchange operator simply advised the companies listed on its exchanges to use the telephone. Most core systems kept running, but trading floors and back offices slowed, and Japan's economy minister warned the incident would have a significant impact given how deeply the products are woven into working life.

For many users the unanswered question was a simple one: what happens to the messages sent into the void while the systems were down? The company has promised a full restoration, and analysts say the episode will sharpen pressure on big organisations to keep tested backup systems — and more than one provider — for the tools they cannot work without.

Key points

  • The outage began on Thursday and hit Outlook, Teams and Copilot; restoration was still under way on day two.
  • The company blames server problems and has ruled out a cyber attack.
  • Disruption was recorded in 74 countries, affecting health services, visa processing, airlines and company results.
  • Businesses fell back on telephones, fax machines and Bluetooth while core systems mostly kept running.
