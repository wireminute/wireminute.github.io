500 Million Indians Left Poverty Behind — but Millions Still Live on the Edge

A new World Bank report credits India with moving almost 500 million people above the poverty line during the 12 years to 2023 — yet millions who crossed it still live a single shock away from falling back.

The bank puts extreme poverty, defined as living on less than $3 a day, at 27.1 per cent of the population in 2011 and 2.6 per cent in 2023. The share below the $4.20-a-day line used for lower-middle-income countries shrank from 57.7 per cent in 2011 to 16.1 per cent in 2023, with about 496 million people moving above it. The only comparable reduction on this scale was China's, which moved over 800 million people above the extreme-poverty line across forty years.

The bank attributes the shift mainly to stronger household spending, more jobs — especially for women — and bigger state transfers, including free or subsidised grain for close to 800 million people each month. It expects the decline to continue, though more slowly, projecting the rate at the $4.20 line at 14.2 per cent in 2026 and 12.1 per cent by 2028.

But the line itself is the dispute. India's government has not released its own consumption-based poverty count for over ten years, so the bank built its figures from official household surveys. Economists who question the result say the bar is set so low that crossing it does not mean a dignified life: a family of three counts as out of poverty on roughly 7,380 rupees ($78) a month. Consumer prices, meanwhile, climbed by more than half between September 2016 and September 2025, while pay has not kept pace — and over 233 million people still sit below the $4.20 line.

Inequality has widened even as poverty has fallen. The 2026 World Inequality Report ranks India among the most unequal countries in the world: the richest tenth takes about 58 per cent of national income, while the poorest half gets about 15 per cent. For families just above the line, one lost job, one hospital bill or one failed harvest can erase a decade of progress.

Key points

  • A World Bank report says almost 500 million Indians moved out of poverty in the 12 years to 2023, as the extreme-poverty rate dropped from 27.1 per cent to 2.6 per cent.
  • The bank credits stronger spending, more jobs for women and expanded state support, including free grain for close to 800 million people a month.
  • Critics say the poverty line is too low to mean a dignified life — a family of three counts as out of poverty on about 7,380 rupees ($78) a month — and prices have climbed by more than half since 2016.
  • Inequality is among the highest in the world: the richest tenth takes about 58 per cent of national income, while the poorest half gets about 15 per cent.
