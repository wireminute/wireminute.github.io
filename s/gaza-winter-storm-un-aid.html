UN Says Gaza Storm Toll Was Preventable as Winter Aid Waits at the Border

The United Nations has said the deadly impact of the latest winter storm in Gaza was preventable, blaming restrictions on aid and access for leaving thousands of families exposed to flooding that swept away their shelters.

The UN Humanitarian Country Team in the occupied Palestinian territory issued an urgent appeal on Friday, saying children and adults had been killed, critical services suspended, and thousands of family shelters flooded, swept away or torn apart in the storm. The team, which brings together the heads of UN agencies and aid groups working in the territory, said the damage came "not by the storm alone, but by policy decisions".

The statement said Israeli policies continue to obstruct the entry of critical items and access to most of Gaza's land, including areas away from the shoreline that are less prone to flooding. Incoming supplies face multiple approval requirements, limited crossing capacity and delivery windows that are unpredictable or narrow. More than 150,000 tents are waiting outside Gaza to be dispatched, though funding has fallen far short of what is needed.

The UN's aid chief had warned two days earlier that restrictions, funding shortages and repeated delays had already cost lives. He said aid partners had tied progress on access to political developments, first asking the UN to wait until after Israel's elections and then until a new government was formed, and called the delays unacceptable under international humanitarian law. He called for all crossings to be opened and for aid partners to be allowed to work.

Gaza's media office said about 70,000 tents sheltering more than 300,000 people were flooded in about 12 hours of rain, with material losses of around $3 million. Local reports said five people, including three children, were killed on Sunday evening when a rain-soaked sand embankment collapsed onto a tent sheltering two families in a camp south of Deir al-Balah. With winter set to last for months, the UN said access to longer-lasting shelter must not be made conditional on political progress, and warned that cold, overcrowding and poor sanitation have created a high risk of disease spreading.

Key points

  • The UN's humanitarian team in Gaza said the storm's deadly impact was preventable, blaming policy decisions rather than the rain alone.
  • Israeli policies are still obstructing the entry of winter supplies and access to most of Gaza's land, the team said, including areas less prone to flooding.
  • More than 150,000 tents are waiting outside Gaza to be dispatched, but funding has fallen far short of what is needed.
