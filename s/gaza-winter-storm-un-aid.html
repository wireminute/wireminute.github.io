Four Nations Urge Israel to Let 150,000 Tents Into Gaza as Winter Bites

The foreign ministers of Australia, Canada, Italy and Britain have jointly urged Israel to let winter aid into Gaza without delay, saying 150,000 tents and other vital supplies are being held at the border while floods and cold deepen the crisis for families living in tents.

In a joint statement issued on Sunday, the four ministers said the situation on the ground in Gaza was dire and called on Israel to "stop the delays now" and allow "immediate, rapid and unimpeded humanitarian access throughout Gaza". They said recent floods had brought further suffering, that severe weather and heavy rain were compounding an already dire humanitarian situation as winter takes hold, and that the position was "entirely reversible" if the crossings were opened. The statement followed a similar appeal from the European Union on Saturday.

The call came days after the first death from the cold this winter. Gaza's health ministry said nine-month-old Kinan Ahmed Raqiq died of hypothermia in a tent in southern Gaza after being transferred from the Kuwaiti Hospital to the Nasser Medical Complex in Khan Younis, where he was dead on arrival. A forensic examination confirmed severe hypothermia. His father said he found the baby's skin had turned blue during the night in the family's tent. The ministry said it was the first recorded death from cold exposure this winter, after 10 such deaths last winter.

Heavy rain between 6 and 8 November flooded tens of thousands of tents across the territory. The UN aid office said more than 6,000 family shelters were destroyed and 17,400 damaged, leaving over 3,900 households with no shelter at all, and that about 26,000 households were affected by the storms. Five people, including three children, were killed when a rain-soaked sand embankment collapsed onto a tent south of Deir al-Balah. Aid groups estimate Gaza needs at least 121,000 tents and 1.7 million mattresses to face the cold, rainy season.

The UN Humanitarian Country Team issued an urgent appeal on Friday, saying the deadly impact of the storm was preventable and came "not by the storm alone, but by policy decisions". It said restrictions continue to obstruct the entry of critical items and access to most of Gaza's land, with multiple approvals, limited crossing capacity and narrow delivery windows. The World Health Organization warned of a surge in respiratory illnesses, and the UN children's agency said winter conditions put children at increased risk of hypothermia. A coalition of more than 50 countries and organisations led by the United Kingdom, Qatar and Jordan has called for the immediate entry of shelter, fuel, water, sanitation and health supplies.

Key points

  • The foreign ministers of Australia, Canada, Italy and Britain jointly urged Israel to allow "immediate, rapid and unimpeded" humanitarian access to Gaza.
  • About 150,000 tents and other vital supplies are being held at the border, while aid groups say at least 121,000 tents and 1.7 million mattresses are needed for winter.
  • Nine-month-old Kinan Ahmed Raqiq died of hypothermia in a tent, the first cold death of the winter; floods destroyed or damaged more than 23,000 shelters.
  • The UN says the storm's deadly impact was preventable, blaming restrictions on aid entry rather than the rain alone.
