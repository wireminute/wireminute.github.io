South Africa Moves Ahead With 5.2 GW Nuclear Build, First New Reactors in Decades

South Africa is pressing ahead with plans for 5,200 megawatts of new nuclear power, its biggest atomic programme since the Koeberg plant was built more than four decades ago.

Energy Minister Kgosientsho Ramokgopa told a nuclear and clean energy conference in Cape Town that his department is preparing a ministerial determination for 5.2 gigawatts of new nuclear capacity under the country's Integrated Resource Plan. The determination is the formal legal step that allows the procurement process to begin, and it still needs the agreement of the energy regulator and the finance minister.

State power utility Eskom has submitted its feasibility report for the programme. The government roadmap envisages construction starting in 2028, with the first new unit in operation by 2036 and the full capacity online by 2039. The plan is the first phase of a wider ambition to add 10,000 megawatts of nuclear power over time.

The likely sites are Duynefontein, next to the existing Koeberg station on the Western Cape coast, and Thyspunt in the Eastern Cape. Environmental approval for a 4 gigawatt plant at Duynefontein was upheld in court in August last year. Eskom would own and operate the new reactors, with the vendor, reactor design and financing chosen through competitive bidding.

The minister said a document setting out the case for nuclear power will be published by the end of November for 60 days of public comment, with community roadshows to follow in February. A final determination is aimed for April next year, though his speech put the outside date at before June 2027. The national nuclear corporation welcomed the move, and cabinet has also approved a new multipurpose research reactor at Pelindaba.

Koeberg, with two reactors totalling 1.85 gigawatts, has supplied about five percent of South Africa's electricity since the mid-1980s and remains the only nuclear power station on the African continent. A previous attempt to approve 2.5 gigawatts of new capacity in August 2024 was withdrawn after a legal challenge. Ramokgopa himself cautioned that South Africa has not built a nuclear plant in almost 50 years, calling the skills gap the biggest inherent risk. No official cost has been published, though one report put a preliminary estimate at about 250 billion rand.

Key points

  • Energy Minister Ramokgopa is preparing a ministerial determination for 5,200 MW of new nuclear capacity, with Eskom's feasibility report already submitted.
  • Construction is planned to start in 2028, the first unit to operate from 2036 and the full capacity on stream by 2039.
  • Likely sites are Duynefontein, beside Koeberg, and Thyspunt; Eskom would own and operate the plants, chosen by competitive bidding.
  • A public case for nuclear power is due by the end of November, with a final determination aimed for April 2027; no official cost has been published.
