Three Killed, 124 Arrested as Bangladesh Unrest Spreads Ahead of Hasina Return

Three people have been killed and 124 people linked to the Awami League arrested in Dhaka in the past 24 hours, police said, as political violence spread across Bangladesh ahead of ousted prime minister Sheikh Hasina's planned return from exile next month.

The violence erupted in 14 districts on Wednesday evening and in the early hours of Thursday, with vehicles torched, politicians' homes and branches of Grameen Bank attacked, and railway infrastructure targeted, Bangladeshi media reported. Two activists of the ruling party were killed in clashes in Narail on Wednesday night, and a third death was reported in Pirojpur. Police said 124 people linked to the Awami League had been arrested in Dhaka over the past 24 hours.

The unrest continued into Friday, when a petrol bomb was thrown at the home of a junior foreign minister in Sylhet, police said, and authorities restored communications with Gopalganj, Hasina's hometown, after days of unrest. Attackers had earlier torched a police station there and set fire to the ancestral home of former leader Muhammad Yunus in the port city of Chattogram late on Wednesday night. The fire broke out at about 11.30pm in an unused room on the ground floor of the four-storey house in the Panchlaish area, police said; family members upstairs were unhurt.

Prime Minister Tarique Rahman blamed India for the unrest, while police detained former education minister Nurul Islam Nahid, a member of Hasina's ousted administration. The government has deployed 97 platoons of border guards across seven districts, and street protests were held in 16 other districts after the Awami League called a nationwide lockdown to mark the anniversary of the day Hasina was sentenced to death.

Hasina, 79, has vowed to return to Bangladesh in December from India, where she has lived since a student-led uprising ended her 15-year rule in August 2024. A tribunal in Dhaka sentenced her to death in her absence in November 2025 for crimes against humanity over the crackdown on protesters during the uprising, and Dhaka has asked India to hand her over. Her now-banned Awami League says the lockdown is a protest against the sentence; the government says she would face execution if she returns.

Key points

  • Three people killed and two injured as violence spread across 14 districts; 124 Awami League-linked people arrested in Dhaka in 24 hours, police said.
  • Attackers torched Yunus's ancestral home in Chattogram late on Wednesday and a police station in Gopalganj; a petrol bomb hit a minister's home in Sylhet on Friday.
  • Prime Minister Tarique Rahman blamed India for the unrest; former education minister Nurul Islam Nahid was detained; 97 border-guard platoons deployed across seven districts.
  • Hasina has vowed to return in December despite a death sentence in absentia; Dhaka has asked India to extradite her, and the government says she would face execution on arrival.
