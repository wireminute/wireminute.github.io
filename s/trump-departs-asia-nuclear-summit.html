Trump Heads to Asia for First-Ever Nuclear Talks With Xi and Putin, Says He Will Raise Ukraine

President Donald Trump left Washington on Monday for Asia, heading first to Manila and then to Shenzhen for the first-ever three-way meeting with China's Xi Jinping and Russia's Vladimir Putin, with nuclear disarmament at the centre of the talks.

The trilateral meeting is set for Thursday on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen, and Beijing has described it as groundbreaking. The White House has said Trump plans to discuss reducing nuclear weapons stockpiles following the expiry of the New START treaty, with Iran also on the agenda. A US official said the meeting was meant to begin a conversation and that no agreements were expected to be announced.

Before departing, Trump told reporters he also planned to raise Russia's war in Ukraine with Xi and Putin, saying Ukraine was losing ground, even though administration officials had said on Friday that the conflict would not be on the agenda. He also said he may meet North Korean leader Kim Jong Un while in China, adding that it was up to Xi. Ukraine's president has warned against any arrangement that would let the three powers redraw global spheres of influence.

Key points

  • Trump departed Washington on Monday for Manila and then Shenzhen, where he will meet Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin on Thursday in the first-ever trilateral talks between the three leaders.
  • Nuclear disarmament is the central focus following the expiry of the New START treaty; Beijing called the meeting groundbreaking, while a US official said no agreements were expected.
  • Trump said he will also raise the war in Ukraine and may meet North Korea's Kim Jong Un in China, despite officials previously saying Ukraine would not be on the agenda.
