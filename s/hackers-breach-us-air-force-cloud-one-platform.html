Hackers Breach US Air Force's Main Cloud Platform, Putting Sensitive Military Data at Risk

Hackers have broken into Cloud One, the cloud system the US Air Force uses to run scores of its applications, in an intrusion found in recent weeks while the platform was being upgraded.

The breach was reported by three people familiar with the matter, and could put sensitive tactical, personnel and logistics data at risk. It is not known who carried out the attack, how far it reached, or whether any data was taken.

Cloud One, launched in 2017, was built as a single hub for the Air Force's computing needs, drawing on the resources of Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle and Alphabet. It powers applications that handle personnel records and pay, logistics and maintenance, and two systems at the heart of US air operations: one that gathers air defence radar and other feeds from across North America, and another that tracks bombs and missiles at more than 200 Air Force sites around the world. Two of the sources described it as one of the US military's largest cloud platforms, and one called the breach a major event for the service.

The discovery has caused disruption across the platform. Moving back-office applications onto Cloud One has been put on hold, and updates to applications already running there were blocked, leaving staff without a way to install fixes at the same time as major new software flaws were being disclosed across the technology industry. Some users also had their administrator rights revoked. The US military's cyber force, Cyber Command, has been asked to assess the damage.

One source said the intruders got in by exploiting a software flaw to reach the platform's control plane, which was described as working like a universal remote: in theory, that level of access could reveal whether aircraft are ready to fly or where spare parts are moving, though it is not clear whether the intruders did any of that. The Air Force declined to discuss specific networks and systems, saying it continuously takes prudent measures to protect its information and mission capabilities. The breach follows a run of cyber incidents hitting the US government, among them two at the FBI, a Pentagon data hack, a Department of Homeland Security personnel leak, an intrusion at two spy agencies and a Treasury Department hack.

Key points

  • Hackers have broken into Cloud One, the US Air Force's flagship cloud platform, in an intrusion uncovered during upgrade work in the past month or so.
  • The platform carries sensitive tactical, personnel and logistics data and tracks bombs and missiles at more than 200 Air Force sites worldwide.
  • Migrations onto Cloud One have been paused, app updates blocked and some users stripped of administrator rights; Cyber Command has been called in to assess the damage.
  • Who carried out the attack, how far it reached and whether any data was stolen are all still unknown; the Air Force declined to comment on specific networks.
