'Gaza Syndrome': Film Examines the Trauma Soldiers Carry Home From Gaza

A new documentary follows Israeli soldiers home from Gaza and asks what their nightmares, panic and grief mean when the people who bore the heaviest cost of the war are almost entirely absent from their accounts.

The film, Gaza Syndrome, released on Thursday, draws on hours of testimony from soldiers who served in Gaza, alongside interviews with psychologists, journalists and Palestinian trauma specialists. The soldiers speak of sleepless nights, panic, addiction and comrades lost to suicide, and some say they want to return to fight.

What the film keeps returning to is the absence at its centre: the Palestinians of Gaza barely appear in the soldiers' accounts, even though the war's heaviest toll has fallen on them. More than 74,700 Palestinians have been killed, including over 21,000 children, according to the reporting behind the film, and a United Nations commission of inquiry concluded in September 2025 that Israeli authorities had committed genocide in Gaza.

The scale of the psychological crisis inside Israel's military is laid out in official figures. Nearly 30,000 soldiers and security personnel wounded since October 7, 2023 have entered the Defence Ministry's rehabilitation system, and about 63 percent of them have psychological injuries, with thousands formally diagnosed with post-traumatic stress disorder and acute stress reactions.

The army says 22 soldiers died by suicide in 2025, one more than in 2024 and up from 17 in 2023. A parliamentary research centre recorded 279 suicide attempts between January 2024 and July 2025, and combat soldiers accounted for 78 percent of military suicide deaths in 2024.

One bulldozer driver told a parliamentary committee that crews had to run over people, dead and alive, in the hundreds. A separate examination of more than 2,500 soldiers' social media accounts documented wanton destruction, abuse of detainees and the use of Palestinian civilians as human shields.

A scholar of Israeli society interviewed for the film described the country as facing a psychological crisis among thousands of soldiers but no moral crisis. The film also notes a wider social strain: killings of women in Israel rose 48 percent in 2025, to 34 from 23 the year before.

Key points

  • Gaza Syndrome, released on Thursday, follows Israeli soldiers home from Gaza through testimony on nightmares, panic, addiction and suicide.
  • Nearly 30,000 wounded soldiers have entered the Defence Ministry's rehabilitation system; about 63 percent have psychological injuries.
  • The army recorded 22 soldier suicides in 2025, up from 21 in 2024 and 17 in 2023; 279 attempts were recorded between January 2024 and July 2025.
  • The film contrasts that trauma with the toll on Palestinians: more than 74,700 killed, including over 21,000 children, and a UN commission's 2025 finding of genocide.
