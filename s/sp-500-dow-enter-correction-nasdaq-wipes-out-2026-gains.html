S&P 500 and Dow Join Nasdaq in Correction as AI Sell-Off Wipes Out the Year's Gains

The S&P 500 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average closed in correction territory on Friday, joining the Nasdaq after a brutal week in which the tech-heavy index erased all of its gains for 2026.

The S&P 500 fell 3.7 percent on Friday to 6,910.39, its seventh consecutive daily decline and its longest losing streak since February 2020. The index closed below 7,000 for the first time since April and finished more than 10 percent below its record high, the threshold for a correction and its first since the tariff-driven sell-off in April 2025.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 949 points, or 1.9 percent, to 48,147.33, also closing more than 10 percent below its record peak. It was the Dow's fifth straight day of losses of more than 1 percent. The Nasdaq Composite plunged 5.3 percent, or more than 1,260 points, to 22,617.73, its worst week since March 2020. The index is now down about 18 percent from its record high in early October and has fallen into negative territory for the year.

For the week, the Dow lost 8 percent, the S&P 500 fell 8.6 percent and the Nasdaq tumbled 10 percent, the worst week for the first two since the tariff announcement in early April 2025. The sell-off has been driven by doubts over whether the huge sums companies are spending on artificial intelligence will pay off, and by high valuations across the sector.

Anthropic, the artificial intelligence lab behind the Claude chatbot, fell about 9 percent on Friday to $88.30, close to its $83 listing price, a day after the largest stock market debut on record raised $81.3 billion. The stock had opened at $111.50 on Thursday and closed that day at $97.17. Chipmaker Nvidia fell about 14 percent on Friday and 22.7 percent for the week, its worst week since October 2008, while CoreWeave dropped 12 percent to below its listing price. Analysts also pointed to a stronger dollar drawing liquidity out of the market and to worries about inflation and interest rates adding to the selling.

Key points

  • The S&P 500 fell 3.7 percent on Friday, its seventh straight daily loss, closing more than 10 percent below its record high and entering correction territory.
  • The Dow fell 949 points, or 1.9 percent, also closing in correction, while the Nasdaq plunged 5.3 percent and wiped out all of its gains for 2026.
  • For the week, the Dow lost 8 percent, the S&P 500 fell 8.6 percent and the Nasdaq tumbled 10 percent, their worst week since early April 2025.
  • Anthropic fell about 9 percent to $88.30, near its $83 listing price, and Nvidia dropped about 14 percent as doubts over AI spending deepened the rout.
