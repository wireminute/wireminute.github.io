Eisenkot Clinches First Coalition Deal as Lieberman Joins Bid to Form Government

Gadi Eisenkot has secured the first coalition agreement in his bid to form Israel's next government, with Avigdor Lieberman's party agreeing to join him after the two men met on Tuesday.

Eisenkot's Yashar party said the two leaders had agreed to sign a coalition pact after their meeting, the first such deal since the October 27 election left no party with a clear route to power. Lieberman leads Israel Our Home, a right-wing opposition party.

The election stripped Benjamin Netanyahu of his parliamentary majority, and the Knesset was sworn in on November 10 with no new government in place. President Isaac Herzog then gave Eisenkot the task of forming one, while Netanyahu remains in office as caretaker prime minister.

Yashar won 21 seats in the election, and Lieberman's party won seven. Together with the other parties in Eisenkot's patchwork bloc, the agreement takes his camp to 48 seats, still 13 short of the 61 needed for a majority in the 120-seat parliament, so more partners will be needed.

Eisenkot has until December 6 to put a government together under his 28-day mandate from the president, and he can ask for a further 14 days if he needs more time.

The deal came as a surprise in some quarters, because Lieberman had spoken publicly against forming a government that relied on support from Arab parties, and against joining Netanyahu in a unity cabinet.

Neither Netanyahu's bloc nor the opposition won a majority in the October 27 vote, and weeks of talks since then had produced no agreement before Tuesday. Eisenkot's deal with Lieberman is the first concrete step toward a government, but with 13 seats still to find, further rounds of negotiation lie ahead.

Key points

  • Eisenkot's Yashar party and Avigdor Lieberman's Israel Our Home have agreed to sign a coalition pact, the first since the October 27 election
  • Eisenkot's bloc holds 48 seats in the 120-seat Knesset, 13 short of the 61 needed for a majority
  • Lieberman had publicly opposed a government relying on Arab parties or a unity cabinet with Netanyahu
  • Eisenkot must form a government by December 6 under a 28-day mandate, with a possible 14-day extension
