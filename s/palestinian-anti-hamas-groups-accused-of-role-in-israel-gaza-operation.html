Anti-Hamas Palestinian Groups Accused of Taking Part in Israeli Gaza Operation

Palestinian security sources say Israeli-backed local armed groups took part in this week's Israeli ground operation in central Gaza, pressing families to flee their homes under the cover of Israeli fire.

Late on Tuesday evening, residents said, a group of armed men in plain clothes and carrying light weapons gathered outside a house where displaced women were sheltering on ad-Dawa Street, at the northern end of the Nuseirat camp. The men wore no uniforms or insignia, and people in the area took them for members of local militias backed by Israel.

The next day, Israeli forces began a ground operation around the Nuseirat and Bureij camps in central Gaza, on the far side of the Yellow Line, the boundary marking areas under Israeli military control since the October 2025 ceasefire. Troops shut Salah al-Din Street, the territory's main north-south road, and about 6,000 Palestinians fled the area, according to Gaza's Government Media Office, which put the number of affected families at more than 1,500. Israel said its troops were going after a tunnel it described as a danger to them, and on Thursday the military said the operation was over and the road had reopened. At least one Palestinian was killed in Israeli attacks near the site of the incursion, and Gaza's Civil Defence agency reported three more killed in separate drone strikes on Wednesday.

A Palestinian security source in Gaza said the security authorities had documented the local armed groups taking part in the operation, intimidating civilians and pressing families to leave their homes and shelters. The source, speaking on condition of anonymity, said the groups operated under Israeli fire cover while the closure of Salah al-Din Street also hampered humanitarian work, and described them as gangs funded and armed by Israel to sow disorder and weaken Palestinian cohesion. Israel has said openly that it backs Palestinian armed factions that oppose Hamas.

Palestinian political analyst Iyad al-Qarra said Israel had widened the groups' area of activity beyond the southern cities of Rafah and Khan Younis into central Gaza and Gaza City, and accused them of kidnappings and attempted assassinations carried out with Israeli aerial protection. He said the Israeli military sends the fighters into front-line operations before committing its own troops, and that the groups also gather intelligence through local social networks. Hamas said the advance beyond the Yellow Line and the forced evacuation of residents breached the ceasefire agreement. The operation was the first major ground offensive publicly announced by the Israeli military since the ceasefire took effect in October 2025.

Key points

  • Palestinian security sources say Israeli-backed local armed groups took part in this week's Israeli ground operation near the Nuseirat and Bureij camps in central Gaza.
  • About 6,000 Palestinians fled as troops shut Salah al-Din Street; Israel said its forces were going after a tunnel it described as a danger to them and declared the operation over on Thursday.
  • The security source said the groups intimidated civilians and pressed families to evacuate, operating under Israeli fire cover while the road closure hampered aid work.
  • An analyst said the groups' activity has spread beyond the southern cities of Rafah and Khan Younis into central Gaza and Gaza City, and Hamas called the advance beyond the Yellow Line a breach of the ceasefire.
