Peltola Beats Sullivan in Alaska, Handing Democrats a 56-44 Senate Majority

Democrat Mary Peltola has defeated two-term Republican Senator Dan Sullivan in Alaska, completing her party's sweep of the 2026 midterm Senate races and giving Democrats a 56-44 majority in the chamber.

The race was called on Tuesday, the last Senate contest of the midterms to be decided. State election figures show Peltola with 141,637 first-choice votes, or 49.98 percent, against 130,162, or 45.93 percent, for Sullivan, with all 401 precincts reporting and 285,439 ballots cast. Under Alaska's ranked-choice count, her lead is projected to widen to about 52 percent to 48 percent.

The win makes Peltola the first Democrat to take an Alaska Senate seat since 2008, when Mark Begich won the seat that Sullivan took from him in 2014. She is also the first Alaska Native woman to reach the Senate, and one of two Indigenous women elected to the chamber this year, alongside Peggy Flanagan of Minnesota.

The race was shaped by Alaska's unusual voting system and by a dispute over a second man on the ballot named Dan Sullivan. A retired schoolteacher running as an independent, Dan J. Sullivan, drew 4,684 votes, while Republican Gerald Heikes took 6,020. Days before polling day, reports said people linked to Peltola's campaign had helped recruit the second Sullivan; her campaign denied any involvement, as did the national Democratic Senate campaign committee. The Justice Department sued in October to stop certification if the incumbent lost by fewer votes than his namesake received, and Alaska's governor ordered an investigation. In the end, Peltola's margin of more than 11,000 votes was far larger than the namesake's total.

Peltola, who is Yup'ik, first won national attention in 2022, when she won a special election to succeed the late Representative Don Young, becoming the first Alaska Native member of Congress, and then won a full term that November. She lost her House seat in 2024 to Nick Begich III. Sullivan, a Marine Corps veteran and former state attorney general, had held the Senate seat since 2015. Alaska backed Donald Trump by about 13 points in 2024, which made the seat one of the cycle's most watched contests. The result gives Democrats nine net gains in the Senate without losing a seat of their own, their largest majority since 2009.

Key points

  • Peltola has unseated two-term Republican Senator Dan Sullivan in Alaska, the final Senate race of the 2026 midterms to be called.
  • She won 141,637 first-choice votes (49.98%) to Sullivan's 130,162 (45.93%), with all precincts reporting; the ranked-choice count projects her lead widening to about 52-48%.
  • The result gives Democrats a 56-44 Senate majority, a net gain of nine seats and their largest majority since 2009.
  • Peltola is the first Alaska Native woman elected to the Senate and the first Democrat to win the seat since 2008.
