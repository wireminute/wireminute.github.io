Centrica Boss Warns Energy Bill Non-Payment Will Worsen as January Rise Nears

The boss of British Gas owner Centrica has said he is hugely concerned about the impact of January's expected energy bill rise, warning that more households will stop paying and that the cost is already being spread across every electricity customer.

Chris O'Shea told the Sunday with Laura Kuenssberg programme that forecasts of a substantial increase in bills from January were materially correct. He said the first quarter of the year is when households use most of their energy, arriving just as people are paying for Christmas. Consultancy Cornwall Insight has forecast that the price cap will rise by 443 pounds a year, or 26 percent, taking a typical household bill to 2,166 pounds a year from 1 January. The regulator Ofgem announces the new cap on Wednesday.

O'Shea said there was a massive problem with non-payment in Britain. Some households cannot pay their bills, he said, and a growing number are choosing not to. The result, he said, is that every electricity consumer pays about 1.50 to 1.60 pounds a week to cover the cost of people who do not pay, and that burden will grow as bills rise. The amount households owe energy suppliers reached 5 billion pounds in the April to June period, according to Ofgem.

The price cap covers about 20 million households in England, Scotland and Wales and sets the maximum price for each unit of gas and electricity, so actual bills vary with how much energy a household uses. Wholesale energy prices paid by suppliers have risen this year as the conflict in the Middle East has pushed up costs for households.

The government has said some households will receive a 100 pound payment to help with energy bills, a policy announced in the Budget, and has temporarily cut VAT on electricity by 5 percent until the end of June next year, which is expected to save a typical household about 45 pounds a year. Consumer groups say the help is not enough. Citizens Advice called the 100 pound payment a drop in the ocean and warned of cold-related deaths this winter, while the Fuel Poverty Action Group said prices were driven by inflated company profits and called for stronger action. Energy firms operating in Britain are subject to a windfall tax introduced in 2022, and with other taxes the overall rate on oil and gas profits is 78 percent.

Key points

  • O'Shea says January bill forecasts are materially correct and he is hugely concerned; Cornwall Insight forecasts a 443 pound rise to 2,166 pounds a year.
  • Household energy debt hit 5 billion pounds in April to June; every electricity customer pays about 1.50 pounds a week to cover non-payment.
  • The price cap covers about 20 million households; Ofgem announces the new cap on Wednesday.
  • Government offers a 100 pound payment and a VAT cut worth about 45 pounds a year; Citizens Advice calls the payment a drop in the ocean.
