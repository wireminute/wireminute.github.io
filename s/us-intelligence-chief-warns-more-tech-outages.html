US Intelligence Chief Warns More Tech Outages Are Coming as Ageing Systems Are Upgraded

The United States' top intelligence official has warned Americans to expect further technology outages and service disruptions as companies and government agencies replace ageing computer systems, saying interruptions are an inevitable part of the upgrade process.

Jay Clayton, the Director of National Intelligence, said on Sunday that the wave of failures that hit American air travel, email services, banks and a spy agency network this month would not be the last. Speaking on CNN's State of the Union and ABC's This Week, he said the upgrades under way were needed to improve performance and cybersecurity as advances in computing strain older infrastructure, and that the public should be ready for more disruption while the work continues.

Clayton warned that the changeover would bring 'glitches, gaps, interruptions in service' and said that was bound to happen as systems are modernised. He was speaking days after President Donald Trump blamed the recent run of failures on companies' technology upgrades, and he echoed that explanation, saying the incidents were the product of technical change rather than hostile action.

The failures this month included a nationwide halt to air travel, a Microsoft outage that disrupted email services, a technical glitch that affected several major banks and a temporary disruption to the National Security Agency's network. The Federal Aviation Administration said router upgrades unleashed a flood of data that triggered the air travel ground stop, and that there was no indication of a cyberattack.

Clayton repeated his earlier position that the outages were not a coordinated or state-sponsored attack, saying the intelligence community had looked closely at the incidents and found separate failures linked to maintenance and technical changes. He declined to discuss specifics of separate reports of cyberattacks on the FBI and the Department of Agriculture, saying investigators would provide answers in due course. Clayton, a former top federal prosecutor in Manhattan and former chair of the Securities and Exchange Commission, was sworn in as the ninth Director of National Intelligence on August 3.

Key points

  • Jay Clayton warned Americans to expect more technology outages as ageing systems are upgraded, with 'glitches, gaps, interruptions in service' inevitable.
  • He said the upgrades are needed for performance and cybersecurity as computing advances strain older infrastructure.
  • He repeated that this month's failures, including the air travel halt, Microsoft outage, bank glitch and NSA disruption, were not a coordinated or state-sponsored attack.
  • He declined to discuss reported cyberattacks on the FBI and Agriculture Department, saying investigators would answer in due course.
