Supertanker Sank After Mine Strike in Strait of Hormuz, Iran's Guards Say

Iran's Revolutionary Guards say a supertanker loaded with more than two million barrels of crude oil sank after hitting a naval mine in the Strait of Hormuz, one of six vessels they say have been hit in the strait since Saturday, while maritime monitors have now named two other tankers struck over the weekend.

Britain's maritime trade office said a tanker was struck by an unknown projectile early on Monday while sailing out of the Gulf through the Strait of Hormuz, setting off a fire in its engine room. No injuries were reported, all crew were accounted for, and the damage was not yet known. The office did not name the vessel or say who was behind the attack.

Maritime security firm Vanguard Tech identified the vessel as the Panama-flagged On Phoenix, owned by Onex, and said it was hit about three nautical miles off the coast of Oman. The firm also named a second tanker, the Thenamaris-operated supertanker Al Dhannah, struck in its steering gear room on Saturday while approaching the strait; it turned back toward Dubai. Britain's maritime office reported that attack citing Indian shipping authorities; the crew were safe.

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said on Sunday that two oil tankers had hit naval mines on an unauthorised route in the southern Strait of Hormuz. The Guards said the blasts set off large explosions and fires, and that one vessel, a supertanker loaded with more than two million barrels of crude oil, went down in the Gulf.

On Monday the Guards said two more tankers had struck mines there. The Guards called the strikes punishment for the American blockade, said the tankers had moved under American guidance with United States jets overhead, and warned owners the Washington-backed southern route had been mined. The Guards said six tankers had now caught fire or sunk in the strait since Saturday, a count no outside source has confirmed.

In a separate statement reported on Monday, Iran's army said another tanker had struck a mine east of the strait after switching off its navigation system on a route Tehran had not approved. The vessel caught fire and was left unable to move. The army said the strait was closed and would stay closed as long as Iran judged necessary. It was not immediately clear whether this vessel was among the six the Guards had counted.

Neither Guards statement named any vessel, and it was not immediately clear whether the tankers the firm identified were among those in the Guards' account. Britain's maritime trade office advised ships in the area to transit with caution and to report suspicious activity. Two other tankers turned back toward the Gulf after warnings from the Guards' traffic control centre. The strait carries a large share of the world's seaborne oil.

Key points

  • A tanker hit by an unknown projectile in the Strait of Hormuz on Monday has been identified by a maritime security firm as the Panama-flagged On Phoenix; the strike set off an engine-room fire, with no injuries reported.
  • A second tanker, the supertanker Al Dhannah, was struck in its steering gear room on Saturday and turned back toward Dubai, the same firm said.
  • Iran's Revolutionary Guards say six tankers have now caught fire or sunk in the strait since Saturday, including a supertanker carrying more than two million barrels of oil; no vessel has been named by Tehran and nothing is independently confirmed.
  • Iran's army said separately that another tanker struck a mine east of the strait after switching off its navigation system, leaving it unable to move; Britain's maritime trade office has advised ships to transit with caution, and it is unclear whether the identified tankers are among those in Tehran's account.
