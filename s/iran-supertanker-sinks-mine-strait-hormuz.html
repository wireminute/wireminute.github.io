Two More Tankers Hit Mines in Strait of Hormuz as Iran Calls Strikes Punishment for US Blockade

Iran's Revolutionary Guards say two more oil tankers have hit naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz, a day after a supertanker loaded with more than two million barrels of crude oil sank there, and warn ship owners that the southern passage is mined.

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said on Sunday that two oil tankers had hit naval mines after taking a route the Guards described as unauthorised, in the southern Strait of Hormuz. The Guards said the blasts set off large explosions and fires, and that one of the vessels, a supertanker loaded with more than two million barrels of crude oil, went down in the Gulf.

On Monday the Guards said two more tankers had struck mines in the same southern passage, exploding a short distance from the vessels hit earlier. The Guards described the mine strikes as punishment for the American blockade of Iran, said the tankers had been moving under the guidance of American officers with United States fighter jets overhead, and warned ship owners that the southern route backed by Washington had been mined. The Guards said six tankers had now caught fire or sunk in the strait since Saturday, a count no outside source has confirmed.

Neither statement named any vessel or said where they were from, and no outside source has confirmed the accounts. Britain's maritime trade office said the crews of the first two tankers were safe, but that the damage and any environmental harm were still unknown. Two other tankers turned back toward the Gulf after warnings from the Guards' traffic control centre, according to the same statements. The Guards warned fishermen in the area to stay clear of the wreckage. The strait carries a large share of the world's seaborne oil, and tankers there have been attacked repeatedly since the war between Iran and the United States and Israel began in February.

Key points

  • Iran's Revolutionary Guards say two more tankers hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz on Monday, after a supertanker carrying more than two million barrels of oil sank there on Sunday.
  • The Guards call the mine strikes punishment for the US blockade and warn owners that the US-backed southern passage is mined.
  • The Guards say six tankers have now caught fire or sunk in the strait since Saturday; no vessel has been named and nothing is independently confirmed.
  • Britain's maritime trade office says the crews of the first two tankers are safe; the strait is a vital oil route that has seen repeated tanker attacks since February.
