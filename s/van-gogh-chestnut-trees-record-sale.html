Van Gogh Chestnut Trees Painting Sells for Record $190.4m After Nazi-Era Claim Settled

A Vincent van Gogh painting of blossoming chestnut trees has sold for $190.4 million at auction in New York, setting a new record for the Dutch artist a day after a dispute over its Nazi-era sale was settled.

Chataigniers en fleurs, painted in Auvers-sur-Oise in 1890 in the last weeks of the artist's life, went under the hammer at Sotheby's on Wednesday evening. Bidding opened at $135 million and climbed in $5 million steps before the gavel fell at $165 million, with fees taking the final price to $190.4 million. The whole contest lasted about three minutes.

The price smashed the old mark for a Van Gogh at auction, $117.2 million paid in 2022 for Orchard with Cypresses, painted in 1888. It was also the second-biggest sum any work has fetched at the auction house, behind the $236.4 million paid a year ago for Gustav Klimt's Portrait of Elisabeth Lederer. The painting had been valued at about $180 million before fees.

The sale almost did not happen. The painting was bought in Paris in 1909 by the German publisher Samuel Fischer, whose family sold it in 1936 after fleeing Berlin under Nazi persecution. His heirs called on Sotheby's to pull the painting from the sale on 12 November, saying the 1936 sale had been made under duress. On Tuesday, Sotheby's announced that the Fischer heirs and the Blaquier-Arrieta family, which has owned the painting since 1975, had reached an agreement, clearing the way for the auction.

The buyer was New York dealer Bill Acquavella, who confirmed the purchase after the sale. He did not say whether he was bidding for himself or for a client, so the painting's next home is not known. The work was the leading lot in a 12-painting sale from the Blaquier-Arrieta collection, which Sotheby's had guaranteed, and a bid had been placed in advance, so a sale was certain. It was the painting's first appearance at auction, having been out of public view for decades.

Key points

  • Chataigniers en fleurs sold for $190.4 million including fees at Sotheby's in New York, a new auction record for Van Gogh.
  • The gavel fell at $165 million after bidding opened at $135 million and rose in $5 million steps over about three minutes.
  • The old Van Gogh record was $117.2 million for Orchard with Cypresses in 2022, and the sale is the second-biggest sum for any work at Sotheby's.
  • The auction went ahead only after the Fischer heirs and the Blaquier-Arrieta family settled a dispute over the painting's forced sale in 1936; dealer Bill Acquavella was the buyer.
