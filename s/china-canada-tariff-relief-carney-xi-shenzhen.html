Carney and Xi Widen Tariff Relief as Canada and China Deepen Trade Reset

Canada and China have agreed to widen tariff relief on hundreds of millions of dollars of goods, as Prime Minister Mark Carney met President Xi Jinping in Shenzhen and both sides pushed their trade reset further.

The two leaders met on Tuesday on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen, their third meeting since Carney became prime minister. China will offer tariff relief on about C$400 million (US$280 million) of Canadian exports a year, including frozen pork cuts worth about C$116 million and fish and seafood worth about C$285 million, covering cold-water shrimp and prawns, molluscs including geoduck, sea cucumber and Greenland halibut.

In exchange, Canada will extend by two years remission measures for certain Chinese steel and aluminium products that were due to expire at the end of 2026, and add remissions for 32 more steel and aluminium tariff items where Canadian supply is insufficient or unavailable. The measures will stay in place until at least the end of 2028.

The deal builds on the strategic partnership the two leaders launched in January, which began repairing a strained relationship. Under that agreement China lowered tariffs on Canadian canola seed and other farm goods, and recently extended relief for canola meal, peas, lobster and crab for two more years. Canada, in turn, cut its 100 percent tariff on Chinese electric vehicles to about 6 percent, allowing 49,000 vehicles a year in, a part of the deal unchanged by Tuesday's announcement.

Carney said Canada was well ahead of schedule in its goal of lifting exports to China by 50 percent by the end of the decade, and praised the greater predictability and stability in the relationship. The leaders also agreed on a large Canadian business trade mission to China in 2027 and new direct flights between Vancouver and Guangzhou, as Carney works to reduce Canada's dependence on the United States amid US tariffs.

Key points

  • China will cut tariffs on about C$400 million of Canadian exports a year, including frozen pork and seafood.
  • Canada will extend steel and aluminium tariff remissions by two years and add 32 more items.
  • The deal builds on the January strategic partnership that began repairing ties after years of strain.
  • Carney said Canada is ahead of schedule on its goal of raising exports to China by 50 percent by 2030.
