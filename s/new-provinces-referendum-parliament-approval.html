Zardari and Shehbaz Agree Parliament Must Approve Any Vote on New Provinces

President Asif Ali Zardari and Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif have agreed that any move to create new provinces — including a proposed public referendum — must go through parliament, according to officials briefed on their meeting in Lahore on Saturday.

Officials familiar with the talks said the two leaders agreed to strictly follow the Constitution on creating new provinces, and that parliament could not be bypassed over whether a referendum is held or what it asks. The majority in parliament would decide, one official said, and without consensus the matter could not move. Mr Zardari made clear the PPP stands by the Sindh Assembly's unanimous resolution opposing the division of Sindh.

The debate follows Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi floating a referendum two days earlier, with an assurance that parties would be consulted first. Defence Minister Khawaja Asif has argued Pakistan could have as many as 12 provinces if the change follows administrative needs rather than linguistic, ethnic or political lines.

The accounts differ. The Prime Minister's Office statement described only general talks and did not mention provinces. One senator present has disputed that the subject was discussed at all.

Key points

  • Zardari and Shehbaz agreed in Lahore on Saturday that creating new provinces, or holding a referendum on it, must follow the Constitution and cannot bypass parliament.
  • Zardari restated the PPP's opposition to dividing Sindh, in line with a unanimous Sindh Assembly resolution.
  • The official statement on the meeting did not mention the provinces discussion, and one senator present has disputed that it was discussed at all.
