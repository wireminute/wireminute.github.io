Bessent Wins the China Chip Fight as Treasury Writes New Curbs Over Lutnick

A rift between Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick over sales of advanced computer chips to China spilled into public view on Monday, as the administration issued tough new curbs written by Bessent's department rather than the Commerce Department that normally oversees export controls.

The new rules, issued by the Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security, require licences before advanced chips can be shipped to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil, countries Washington suspects of acting as conduits for chips reaching China. They also restrict overseas data centres from renting large amounts of advanced computing power to firms based in China. The bureau confirmed the text was drafted at the direction of the Treasury and the National Security Council, an unusual admission for the department whose name is on the rule.

Bessent has pressed for far tougher enforcement. He has openly criticised the Commerce Department for holding back action against Megaspeed, a Singapore data-centre company under investigation over whether it helped move advanced Nvidia chips to China, saying other departments had favoured blacklisting the firm for months, “but commerce has the pen.” Lutnick, by contrast, has argued for selling enough advanced chips to keep Chinese developers “addicted to the American technology stack,” and was described by a former security official as the lone voice in the cabinet wanting to keep the loophole open.

The dispute is rooted in the scandal that forced Nvidia founder Jensen Huang to resign as chief executive last week after admitting to an affair with Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive whom American officials suspect of being recruited by China's intelligence services, a claim Beijing calls groundless. The Senate has called both Lutnick and Huang to give evidence on 3 December.

President Donald Trump has refused to take a side, saying one of his secretaries wanted friendly relations with China and the other wanted a confrontation, and that both views were acceptable. He added that he was not planning to remove Lutnick and that any delay may have been aimed at avoiding a rupture with Beijing. Trump is scheduled to sit down with Chinese President Xi Jinping in Shenzhen on Thursday at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit, with the chip question high on the agenda. Senior officials including Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Trade Representative Jamieson Greer have lined up behind Bessent's tougher approach.

Key points

  • New US rules require licences for advanced chip shipments to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil, and for data centres renting large computing power to China-based firms.
  • The rules were drafted by the Treasury and the National Security Council, not the Commerce Department that normally writes export controls.
  • Bessent has openly criticised Commerce for holding back action against Megaspeed; Lutnick has argued for selling enough chips to keep China “addicted to the American technology stack.”
  • Trump has refused to take a side, saying both views are acceptable, and meets Xi Jinping in Shenzhen on Thursday.
