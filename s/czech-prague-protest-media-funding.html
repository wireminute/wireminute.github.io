Huge Crowds Fill Prague Park to Defend Public Media From Funding Overhaul

Tens of thousands of Czechs filled Letna Park in Prague on Sunday to protest a government plan to scrap the fee that pays for public television and radio and fund the broadcasters from the state budget instead, a change critics say would put their independence at risk.

The rally filled the broad Letna plain overlooking the capital on Sunday afternoon, with Czech flags and placards reading 'Hands off the media'. It came two days before the 17 November holiday marking the start of the Velvet Revolution in 1989, and organisers said the crowd could become the largest Czech demonstration since that year.

The civic group Million Moments for Democracy, which organised the rally, said 350,000 people attended, already more than the 200,000-strong anti-government march it held in March. Those figures come from the organisers and have not been confirmed independently.

At issue is a bill from the government of Prime Minister Andrej Babis that would cancel the 205-crown monthly household broadcasting fee, worth about $9.26, and pay for Czech Television and Czech Radio out of the state budget. The change would cut their funding by about 15 percent, leaving television's budget at its 2008 level in nominal terms and radio's at its 2005 level. The bill has passed a preliminary vote in parliament and now faces further debate, after the ruling coalition pushed it forward on 24 October.

Critics, including opposition parties, say moving the broadcasters onto the state budget would expose them to political pressure and threaten their independence, and they have drawn comparisons with Hungary, where the government of Viktor Orban built up a network of loyal media. The Babis government, backed by the far-right SPD and the eurosceptic Motorists party, accuses private and public media of bias and says the broadcasters need to cut costs.

The dispute has already disrupted broadcasting. Staff at the public television and radio stations staged a one-day strike earlier in November that forced programme cuts, and thousands took to the streets the day after the bill moved forward in October. Organisers say the campaign will continue as the bill works its way through parliament.

Key points

  • Organisers said 350,000 people joined the rally at Letna Park in Prague, and predicted it could be the biggest Czech demonstration since the 1989 Velvet Revolution.
  • The bill would scrap the 205-crown monthly broadcasting fee and fund public television and radio from the state budget, cutting their money by about 15 percent.
  • Critics say the change would leave the broadcasters open to political pressure; the government says the media are biased and need to cut costs.
  • Public media staff held a one-day strike earlier in November, and the bill still faces further debate in parliament.
