Somalia Orders UAE Ambassador Out Within 72 Hours, Citing 'Open Invasion'

Somalia has ordered the ambassador of the United Arab Emirates to leave the country within 72 hours and recalled its own envoy from Abu Dhabi, accusing the Gulf state of violating its sovereignty in what Mogadishu called an open invasion.

The decision was taken at a meeting of the Council of Ministers on Thursday, chaired by Prime Minister Hamza Abdi Barre. The cabinet said the Emirates' actions amounted to a major violation of international law, above all the principles of respect for state sovereignty and independence, after reviewing reports on the scale of what it described as attacks and interference.

Information Minister Abdifatah Kasim Mohamud announced the move in a broadcast on the state-run news agency. He said measures would be taken against anyone who cooperates with the Emirates in the invasion against Somalia, and urged all Somalis to defend their country and unite against what he called an open invasion of its sovereignty, security, land and unity.

The minister did not spell out which actions by the Emirates had prompted the response. In a speech on Tuesday, President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud had accused the UAE of giving economic and political support to some regional Somali governments to oppose the federal government in Mogadishu.

The Foreign Ministry was instructed to recall Somalia's ambassador to the UAE for consultations with the national leadership and to tell the Emirati ambassador in Mogadishu that he must leave within 72 hours. There was no immediate response from the UAE foreign ministry.

The Emirates has helped fund and train Somalia's military in the past and has backed ports and other commercial ventures there, and has long denied meddling in the country's affairs. In January, Somalia said it had ended all agreements with the UAE, including port deals and defence and security cooperation, accusing it of undermining national sovereignty.

The expulsion deepens a rift that has been widening all year between Mogadishu and Abu Dhabi over the federal government's authority against regional administrations. With the 72-hour deadline running and no reply yet from the Emirates, the next move now rests with Abu Dhabi.

Key points

  • Somalia ordered the UAE ambassador to leave within 72 hours and recalled its own envoy from Abu Dhabi.
  • Mogadishu accused the Emirates of unspecified violations of sovereignty amounting to an open invasion; the cabinet called it a major breach of international law.
  • President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud had accused the UAE on Tuesday of backing regional governments against the federal government.
  • The UAE has not yet responded; in January Somalia ended all its agreements with the Emirates, including port and defence deals.
