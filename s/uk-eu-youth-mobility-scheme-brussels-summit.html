UK and EU Seal Brexit Reset in Joint Declaration, Adding Defence Ties and £17bn Boost

Britain and the European Union have published a joint declaration sealing their Brexit reset, adding closer defence cooperation to the youth mobility, food and carbon deals agreed at Friday’s summit in Brussels.

Britain and the European Union have published an 18-page joint declaration sealing their Brexit reset, after Prime Minister Andy Burnham, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and European Council President Antonio Costa held a closing press conference in Brussels. Burnham called the package a historic moment and a win for both sides, saying Treasury analysis put the boost to Britain’s economy at 17 billion pounds over this parliament, and that a bill to put the deals into law will be introduced next week.

The declaration also brings the two sides closer on defence. They promised to keep backing Ukraine with weapons, money and aid, and to keep up pressure on Russia through sanctions, including joint naval action against the tankers that carry Russian oil in defiance of sanctions. A new framework will also let Britain take part in the European Union’s civilian security and defence missions.

At the centre of the package is a youth mobility scheme allowing 100,000 young people a year to move in each direction. People aged 18 to 31 can spend up to three years across the Channel, living, working or studying there. The food deal will end checks on British food and drink exports to the European Union, a step Britain has valued at 5.1 billion pounds a year, with Britain aligning with EU rules on food safety and animal and plant health. The two sides will also link their emissions trading systems, so businesses on both sides face the same carbon price.

One issue is still open. The two sides have not agreed what EU students should pay to study at British universities, and the European Union has given Britain six months to settle the question. Von der Leyen said she was confident a solution could be found. The formal signing of the agreements will wait until the fees issue is resolved. Critics in the Conservative Party argue the youth scheme will put young Britons under pressure in the jobs market, while ministers say the package will cut costs for businesses and help hold down prices in the shops.

Key points

  • The two sides published an 18-page joint declaration after the Brussels summit, sealing the reset and adding closer defence cooperation.
  • Burnham said the package could add 17 billion pounds to Britain’s economy this parliament, with a bill to put the deals into law coming next week.
  • Youth mobility will let 100,000 young people a year travel each way for up to three years; food checks end in a deal valued at 5.1 billion pounds a year; emissions trading systems will be linked.
  • University tuition fees for EU students remain unsettled, with six months to reach agreement and formal signing held until then; von der Leyen said she was confident a solution could be found.
