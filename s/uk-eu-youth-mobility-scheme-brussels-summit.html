UK and EU Set to Agree Youth Mobility Deal for 200,000 Young People a Year at Brussels Summit

A youth mobility scheme is set to be agreed by Britain and the European Union at a summit in Brussels on Friday that would let up to 200,000 young people a year live, work and study on the other side of the Channel, in the biggest step yet in the post-Brexit reset of relations.

Under the plan taking shape, as many as 100,000 young Europeans a year would be able to come to Britain, with the same number of young Britons able to spend time in the EU. The scheme would cover people aged 18 to 30, with reports that the upper limit could be raised to 31, and stays of up to three years. Negotiators were still working on the final details into the last hours, with Downing Street describing the talks on Thursday as down to the wire.

Prime Minister Andy Burnham is meeting Ursula von der Leyen, the European Commission president, and Antonio Costa, who leads the European Council, at the summit, the second between the two sides since Brexit. The gathering was pushed back from July after the resignation of Burnham's predecessor, and ministers have presented it as a first step towards a closer relationship, with a serious discussion about future options to follow.

The youth scheme is part of a wider package expected to be agreed on Friday, including a deal to cut border checks on food and drink and closer cooperation on carbon emissions trading. But several items remain unresolved. The exact cap on numbers is still being argued over, with Britain having offered 50,000 and the EU pressing for 100,000, and Britain is expected only to promise a significant cut in university tuition fees for EU students, with the figure left for later talks.

The fine print is also contested. Reports suggest limits on how many young Britons could work in individual EU countries, tied to how many of each country's young people come to Britain, a point smaller member states have raised. The Conservatives have attacked the plan, arguing that more young Europeans competing for jobs would hurt young people already in Britain and pointing to nearly 200,000 fewer job vacancies since Labour took office. Ministers say the package would start to repair some of the losses from leaving the EU.

Key points

  • Up to 100,000 young Europeans a year could come to Britain, with the same number of Britons able to go to the EU, under a scheme set for approval at Friday's Brussels summit.
  • The scheme would cover ages 18 to 30, possibly 31, for stays of up to three years to live, work and study.
  • A wider package is also expected, cutting food border checks and linking carbon emissions trading, but the final numbers are still being negotiated.
  • University tuition fees for EU students would fall significantly, but the exact figure is left for later talks, and limits on Britons working in individual EU states remain contested.
