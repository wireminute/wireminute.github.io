Britain and EU Seal Brexit Reset Deal With 100,000 Youth Visas a Year

Britain and the European Union have concluded negotiations on a reset of their post-Brexit relationship at a summit in Brussels, agreeing a package covering youth mobility, food trade and carbon markets, Downing Street said on Friday.

The agreements were settled at Friday's summit in Brussels, where Prime Minister Andy Burnham met European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and European Council President Antonio Costa. Downing Street said the talks had finished and the reset deal was agreed, calling it the biggest change to Britain's relationship with the bloc since it left six years ago. A joint statement setting out the details is due to be published later on Friday.

At the centre of the package is a youth mobility scheme allowing 100,000 young people a year to move in each direction. People aged 18 to 31 can spend up to three years across the Channel, living, working or studying there. The scheme is the first limited return of free movement for young people since Brexit, and ministers say it will make it easier for young Britons to study and travel abroad.

The food deal will end checks on British food and drink exports to the European Union, with Britain aligning with EU rules on food safety and animal and plant health. The two sides will also link their emissions trading systems, so businesses on both sides face the same carbon price. Burnham said the package could add up to 0.5 percent to Britain's economy and described it as the single biggest growth step of this parliament.

One issue is still open. The two sides have not agreed what EU students should pay to study at British universities, and the European Union has given Britain six months to settle the question. The formal signing of the agreements will wait until the fees issue is resolved, with the deals to be signed together. Ministers expect the package to take effect before the decade is out. Critics in the Conservative Party argue the youth scheme will put young Britons under pressure in the jobs market, while ministers say the package will cut costs for businesses and help hold down prices in the shops.

Key points

  • Negotiations concluded at Friday's Brussels summit, with Downing Street saying a reset deal covering youth mobility, food trade and carbon markets has been agreed.
  • The scheme will let 100,000 young people a year travel each way, with eligibility from 18 to 31 and stays lasting up to three years for living, working or studying.
  • Checks on British food and drink exports to the EU will end as Britain aligns with EU food rules, and the two sides will link their emissions trading systems.
  • University tuition fees for EU students remain unsettled, with six months to reach agreement and formal signing of the deals held until then; a joint statement with full details is due later on Friday.
