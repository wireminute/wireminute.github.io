UN Tribunal Says UNRWA Lacked Evidence to Dismiss Gaza Aid Worker

A UN tribunal has found that the agency for Palestinian refugees could not back up its decision to sack a Gaza aid worker, ruling that the evidence behind the dismissal fell short, after Israel accused him of taking part in the 7 October 2023 attacks.

Abu Awad was among nine employees whose jobs were cut in January 2024, when Israel said they had joined in the Hamas-led assaults. The tribunal ordered one year’s salary for Awad, plus $5,000 for moral damages. It did not rule on the allegations against him themselves, only on whether the dismissal was properly supported.

The tribunal found the case against Awad rested almost entirely on material held by Israel, which it called unsupported and of little evidentiary value. UN investigators had never spoken to the 19 employees Israel first named, because of safety and security concerns, the tribunal noted. It also cast doubt on phone intercepts put forward as proof: one file turned on a case of mistaken identity tied to a cloned SIM, and voice-matching claims looked shaky.

Awad had been accused of being a commander in the armed wing of Hamas, and of being told in an intercepted call to follow the 7 October attacks while taking updates from Hamas. He denied the claim, and colleagues said he had spent the day on the usual UNRWA staff bus. The judges said Israel’s security services never explained who placed the call, or how the phone that took it was linked to Awad.

When asked to justify the dismissals, UNRWA pointed to the Israeli government, a report in The Times of Israel and a report by UN Watch, a pro-Israel pressure group. The tribunal said the claim that those sources had informed the decision was open to some doubt, noting that all supporting documents submitted had been produced after the decision was taken on 26 January 2024. Lazzarini, the agency’s chief at the time, ordered the nine dismissals after consulting Secretary-General António Guterres. A later UN inquiry backed the decision, saying the employees may have taken part. The International Court of Justice said in October 2025 that Israel had not backed up claims that a large share of the agency’s staff belonged to Hamas or other armed groups. It is not yet clear how the ruling will affect the other dismissed workers.

Key points

  • The UNRWA Dispute Tribunal backed Abu Awad, one of nine Gaza staff sacked in January 2024, ordering a year’s salary plus $5,000.
  • The tribunal said the case rested almost entirely on material held by Israel, which it called unsupported and of little evidentiary value.
  • It cast doubt on phone intercepts as proof, including a mistaken-identity case tied to a cloned SIM, and on voice-matching claims.
  • UNRWA’s supporting documents were all produced after the 26 January 2024 decision, the tribunal noted; it is not yet clear how the ruling affects the other dismissed workers.
