Fighting Near Congo Ebola Hospital Traps Health Workers as WHO Chief Pleads for Peace

Heavy fighting near a hospital in the Democratic Republic of Congo's Ituri province has trapped Ebola response workers inside a maternity ward for more than a day, the World Health Organization said on Sunday, as its chief warned that the violence is putting patients and health workers at grave risk.

The WHO's director-general, Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, said in a statement on Sunday that fighting had been going on since Saturday near a hospital in Ituri, close to where the outbreak first appeared. Medical staff and aid partners had been sheltering in the hospital's maternity ward for more than 24 hours, he said, and the agency was working with humanitarian partners to arrange a safe exit for the team. He called for the hospital, its staff and its patients to be protected, adding that peace was the best medicine for the outbreak.

Attacks across the east of the country have become more frequent since the end of October and are seriously disrupting the Ebola response, the health agency said. Its latest update, published on Saturday, found that while some areas had seen a slight fall in cases, transmission remained high across the country. Ituri and North Kivu, in the east, remain the worst-affected provinces.

The outbreak has killed 5,678 people among 11,613 confirmed cases recorded by 17 November, according to the WHO, with nearly half of confirmed patients dying. A separate tracker run by the United States disease control agency put the toll slightly higher, at 5,715 deaths among 11,680 cases by 18 November. The outbreak, caused by the rare Bundibugyo strain of the virus, was declared in mid-May in Ituri and has now spread to eight of Congo's 26 provinces. Children under five have been hit hardest: 1,207 have caught the virus and 844 have died, about seven in ten.

There is still no approved vaccine or treatment made for the Bundibugyo strain, but trials of two candidate vaccines are due to start next week in Bunia, the capital of Ituri province and the centre of the outbreak. Africa's top health agency said last weekend that the outbreak was still not under control, and the WHO kept it a global emergency on 18 November. Cases have also been confirmed beyond Congo's borders, including nine in Kenya, two of them fatal.
