Trapped Ebola Workers Evacuated From Congo Hospital After Three Days of Fighting

Health workers trapped by fighting at a hospital in eastern Congo have been safely evacuated after sheltering there for more than three days, the World Health Organization said, as its chief warned the violence is still disrupting the fight against the country’s worst Ebola outbreak.

Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, the World Health Organization’s director-general, said on Tuesday that WHO staff, health ministry officials from the Democratic Republic of Congo and other colleagues had been safely evacuated from a hospital in Akwe, in Ituri province, after sheltering there amid fierce fighting for more than three days. He said they were brought to safety by health, government and humanitarian partners working together in very difficult conditions, and thanked everyone involved in the operation.

The evacuation ends an ordeal that began on Saturday, when heavy fighting broke out near the hospital, close to where the outbreak first emerged. Staff and response partners had taken shelter in the facility’s maternity ward. Tedros said the WHO had also temporarily moved its Ebola team out of Mongbwalu, another town in Ituri, because of rising insecurity, and would keep it away until the situation calms. He repeated that health care, health workers and humanitarians must be protected at all times, and has said peace is the best medicine for the outbreak.

The fighting is part of a wider surge in attacks across eastern Congo since the end of October, which the WHO says is seriously disrupting the Ebola response. Its latest figures put the toll at 5,775 deaths among 11,762 cases, making this the worst Ebola outbreak the country has recorded. Nearly half of confirmed patients have died. The outbreak, caused by the rare Bundibugyo strain, was declared in mid-May in Ituri and has spread to eight of Congo’s 26 provinces, with cases also confirmed beyond its borders, including nine in Kenya, two of them fatal. Children under five have been hit hardest: 1,207 have caught the virus and 844 have died, about seven in ten.

There is still no approved vaccine or treatment made for the Bundibugyo strain, but trials of two candidate vaccines are planned in Bunia, the capital of Ituri province and the centre of the outbreak. Africa’s top health agency has said the outbreak is still not under control, and the WHO kept it a global emergency on 18 November.
