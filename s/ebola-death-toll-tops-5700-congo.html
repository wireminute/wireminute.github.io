Congo Ebola Cases Pass 12,000 as Fresh Fighting Disrupts the Response

Confirmed Ebola cases in the Democratic Republic of Congo have passed 12,000, health ministry data showed on Thursday, as fresh fighting in the east forced more people from their homes and kept response teams from reaching communities at the centre of the fastest-growing Ebola outbreak on record.

The data put confirmed infections at 12,021, with 5,904 deaths, meaning nearly half of the people confirmed to have the virus have died. It is the second-largest and deadliest Ebola outbreak on record, behind only the 2014 to 2016 West Africa epidemic, which infected more than 28,600 people and killed more than 11,000. Health officials say the current outbreak is the fastest-growing in history, and it is on track to pass the West Africa death toll.

Fighting between the Congolese army and fighters from the Convention for the Popular Revolution resumed on Thursday morning in Iga-Barriere, a mining town about 25 kilometres north of Bunia, sending new waves of displaced people toward the city. Earlier in the week the fighting had already prevented teams from reaching some communities, disrupted the monitoring of people exposed to the virus and left some treatment centres short of staff, according to Dr Catherine Smallwood, the World Health Organization's Ebola incident manager.

About a dozen people, including two WHO personnel and Congolese health officials, were evacuated to Bunia on Tuesday after three full days trapped in a hospital in Akwe, northeast of the provincial capital, amid intense crossfire. The WHO has also temporarily moved its Ebola team out of Mongbwalu, another town in Ituri, because of rising insecurity, and will keep it away until the situation calms. The WHO chief has said health care, health workers and humanitarians must be protected at all times, and that peace is the best medicine for the outbreak.

The outbreak, caused by the rare Bundibugyo strain, was declared in mid-May in Ituri province and has since spread to eight of Congo's 26 provinces, with cases also confirmed beyond its borders, including nine in Kenya, two of them fatal. Children under five have been hit hardest: 1,207 have caught the virus and 844 have died, about seven in ten. Only 15 percent of new cases come from contacts already known to health authorities, according to data presented by Africa's health agency earlier this month, a sign the outbreak is still not under control. There is still no approved vaccine or treatment made for the Bundibugyo strain, but the WHO has approved trials of two candidate vaccines, planned in Bunia, and kept the outbreak a global emergency on 18 November.

Key points

  • Confirmed cases have reached 12,021 with 5,904 deaths, health ministry data showed on Thursday, making this the fastest-growing Ebola outbreak on record.
  • Fighting resumed on Thursday morning in Iga-Barriere, north of Bunia, displacing residents and blocking response teams from communities and exposed contacts.
  • The outbreak has spread to eight of Congo's 26 provinces, and only 15 percent of new cases come from contacts already known to health teams.
  • There is still no approved vaccine or treatment for the Bundibugyo strain, but the WHO has approved trials of two candidate vaccines.
