Putin's Nuclear Briefcase Spotted at China Summit Ahead of Rare Three-Way Talks

A naval officer accompanying President Vladimir Putin in Shenzhen confirmed on camera that the black briefcase he was carrying is Russia's nuclear command case, a day before Putin is due to meet the American and Chinese leaders together.

Footage broadcast by Russian state television showed a naval officer carrying a black briefcase beside President Vladimir Putin's delegation in Shenzhen, and when a journalist asked whether it was the case used for nuclear command, the officer confirmed that it was.

The case is known as Cheget, part of Russia's nuclear command and control system. It is a secure communications terminal that keeps the president linked to the country's strategic nuclear forces while he travels, and it can be used to pass on orders about their use. It was developed in the Soviet era and takes its name from Mount Cheget in the Caucasus. It is not a weapon itself, and it does not launch missiles at the press of a button.

Putin arrived in Shenzhen in the early hours of Wednesday for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit, his second visit to China this year. He met President Xi Jinping on Wednesday and addressed the summit's business forum. On Thursday he is expected to sit down with Xi and President Donald Trump for the first three-way meeting of the three leaders, with nuclear arms control among the subjects expected to come up. The last remaining treaty limiting the American and Russian nuclear arsenals expired in February, and no formal talks on a replacement have begun.

The briefcase travels with the Russian president on his trips abroad as a matter of routine, and nothing in the footage suggested any change in Russia's nuclear alert status. Its appearance on camera drew attention because it came a day before a meeting that brings the leaders of the world's three largest nuclear-armed states to the same table, and because the United Nations secretary-general has said he hopes the summit will be used to discuss cutting nuclear arms.
