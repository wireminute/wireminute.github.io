North Korea Expels Swiss Envoy and Orders Pyongyang Office Shut in Sanctions Row

North Korea has ordered Switzerland's envoy in Pyongyang to leave within 48 hours and demanded the closure of the Swiss diplomatic office, weeks after it reopened, in a row over Swiss sanctions on a North Korean children's camp.

North Korea's foreign ministry declared Astier Sylvain, the head of the Swiss diplomatic office in Pyongyang, persona non grata and ordered him to leave North Korea within two days, the country's state news agency reported on Wednesday. Pak Hyon Su, a director general in the ministry, summoned Sylvain and handed him a memorandum demanding the closure of the office, which reopened only last month after being shut for more than five years.

The move is retaliation for Switzerland's decision to add the Songdowon International Children's Camp, in the coastal city of Wonsan, to its sanctions list earlier this month. Swiss authorities say the camp takes part in programmes that move Ukrainian children from Russian-occupied territory to North Korea and expose them to propaganda promoting Russian narratives. The camp is also under sanctions from the European Union and Britain.

Pyongyang called the Swiss measure an open hostile act and a violation of its sovereignty. Kim Yo Jong, the North Korean leader's sister and a senior party official, warned on Tuesday that the sanctions had stripped the office of its reason to exist, and said she would advise the foreign ministry to look at shutting it down.

Few Western countries keep a diplomatic presence in Pyongyang; Switzerland is one of them. The two countries established relations in 1974, and Switzerland ran a humanitarian office in the capital from 1997 until its staff withdrew in March 2020, when North Korea closed its borders during the Covid-19 pandemic. The office reopened as a diplomatic mission on 19 October, and Swiss Foreign Minister Ignazio Cassis visited Pyongyang at the end of that month, meeting his counterpart Choe Son Hui and speaking of relaunching dialogue. The mission reports to the Swiss embassy in Beijing, while Pyongyang keeps an embassy in Bern.

Switzerland's foreign ministry said on Tuesday that reopening the office was part of its policy of keeping channels of dialogue open, including with states with which it disagrees, and was separate from its sanctions policy. Bern has not yet said publicly how it will respond to the expulsion order.

Key points

  • North Korea declared Swiss office head Astier Sylvain persona non grata and gave him 48 hours to leave.
  • Pyongyang demanded the closure of the Swiss diplomatic office, which reopened only on 19 October.
  • The row follows Swiss sanctions on the Songdowon children's camp over alleged transfers of Ukrainian children.
  • Kim Yo Jong warned on Tuesday that the office had lost its reason to exist; Switzerland says it exists to keep dialogue open.
