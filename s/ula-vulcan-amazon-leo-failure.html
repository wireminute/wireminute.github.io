Vulcan Rocket Fails Minutes After Liftoff, Losing 40 Amazon Internet Satellites

A United Launch Alliance Vulcan rocket failed about 10 minutes after liftoff from Florida on Sunday, destroying 40 Amazon internet satellites in the company's first outright mission failure since it was formed in 2006.

The rocket lifted off from Space Launch Complex 41 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station at 12:40 p.m. Eastern time, after two days of delays and a countdown hold with 20 seconds left to resolve a technical issue. Its six solid rocket boosters and two methane-burning main engines appeared to work normally, and the boosters fell away less than two minutes into the flight. The trouble came during the burn of the Centaur upper stage, about 10 minutes after launch, and the company confirmed roughly 50 minutes later that the mission had not reached orbit.

It was the fifth flight of the Vulcan, the rocket built to replace the Atlas 5, and its first in nine months. The company, a joint venture of Boeing and Lockheed Martin, had never lost a mission outright before. No cause has been given yet, and the company says it will work with Amazon and other organisations to find what went wrong and decide what needs fixing before the rocket flies again.

The loss is a setback for Amazon's Leo constellation, its planned rival to SpaceX's Starlink network. The 40 satellites would have been the largest single batch launched for the project so far, taking the number in orbit from 396 to 436. Amazon has bought 38 Vulcan launches to build the constellation, which must reach 3,232 satellites by July 2029 under the terms of its licence, with half of them in orbit by July 2028. The company plans to start its first service before the end of this year in the United States and Britain.

Earlier Vulcan flights were also troubled: nozzle problems with the solid boosters marred two of them, and engineers spent months working on a fix before this launch. It is not yet known whether the countdown hold, the boosters or the upper stage played any part in Sunday's failure, and the company has given no timetable for its investigation.

Key points

  • A Vulcan rocket failed about 10 minutes after liftoff from Cape Canaveral, losing all 40 Amazon Leo internet satellites on board.
  • It is the first outright mission failure for United Launch Alliance since the company was formed in 2006.
  • No cause has been given yet; the company says it will investigate with Amazon before the rocket flies again.
  • Amazon has bought 38 Vulcan launches for its Leo constellation, which must reach 3,232 satellites by July 2029.
