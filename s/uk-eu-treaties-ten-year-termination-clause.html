UK-EU Reset Treaties Carry 10-Year Clause on Border Rebuild Costs

Britain and the European Union will publish the texts of two treaties agreed last week as part of their post-Brexit reset on Thursday afternoon, with the documents set to include a 10-year clause that would make either side pay to rebuild border checks if it walks away from the deal.

The two texts cover food trade and emissions trading, and they will be published alongside the government's Cutting Costs Bill, which is designed to put most of the deal into law. A third text, covering the youth experience scheme, is not among the two due on Thursday. Fresh studies of how the deal will affect different industries and regions are also due.

Under the termination clause, a party that ends the agreement within ten years would have to pay for rebuilding the border-check infrastructure the deal is designed to scale back. Food exporters stand to gain the most: checks and paperwork on British food and drink heading to the EU will be scrapped, and border facilities will be dismantled. Government analysis puts the gain for agricultural exports at 16 percent. Ministers hope the savings will reach shoppers, though shoppers may see little difference at the till.

Linking the two sides' carbon markets will bring more of Britain's aviation and shipping sectors under the same system. European affairs minister Hamish Falconer said the bill would let the government put two major trade agreements into effect, cutting paperwork for food and drink traders and shielding British exports worth billions of pounds from carbon charges. The texts will also set out in finer detail how Britain will observe and help shape EU rules it does not vote on. Ministers will also gain powers, through secondary legislation, to align British and EU laws with less scrutiny from Parliament.

The prime minister told MPs earlier this week that the agreements were pragmatic and not a step towards rejoining the bloc, adding that rejoining without a referendum would be unthinkable. Andy Burnham dismissed former prime ministers who have warned him against reopening the Brexit debate, saying they were stuck in the past. Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch said he had oversold the deal, while she and Reform UK leader Nigel Farage have signalled they would refuse to pay if a future government ended it. The reset was agreed at a summit in Brussels on 20 November, where the two sides also settled a youth mobility scheme and closer defence ties.

Key points

  • Two treaty texts will be published on Thursday afternoon alongside the Cutting Costs Bill; the youth scheme text follows later.
  • A 10-year termination clause would make either side pay to rebuild border checks if it ends the deal within a decade.
  • Food exporters gain from scrapped checks and paperwork, with government analysis putting the rise in farm exports at 16 percent.
  • The emissions trading link will cover more UK aviation and shipping, and ministers gain powers to align laws with less parliamentary scrutiny.
