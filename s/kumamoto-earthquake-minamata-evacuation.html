Whole Japanese City Ordered to Evacuate After 5.7 Quake Jolts Kumamoto

An earthquake of magnitude 5.7 has struck Kumamoto Prefecture in south-western Japan, prompting the city of Minamata to order its entire population of more than 22,000 people to evacuate.

The tremor hit at 2:22 a.m. local time on Thursday, centred in the Amakusa-Ashikita area about 10 kilometres below the surface, according to Japan's weather agency. It reached lower 5 on the country's seven-point shaking scale in parts of the prefecture, the strongest jolt there since 1 August. No tsunami warning was issued, and a run of smaller aftershocks followed the main tremor.

Minamata issued its evacuation order at 4:05 a.m., and it was still in force after sunrise. NHK put the order's reach at 11,126 households, or 22,133 people. The nearby village of Kuma issued an evacuation order of its own.

Rail services were disrupted across the region. The operator JR Kyushu halted eight bullet trains on the line linking Hakata and Kagoshima-chuo, leaving about 5,600 passengers affected, and a stretch of the Kyushu Expressway was closed. Police said no serious injuries or damage had been reported by 3 a.m., although a man in his seventies in the town of Mashiki fell from his bed when the early-warning alarm sounded and was taken to hospital with bruises.

The quake belongs to the seismic sequence set off by the magnitude 7.1 tremor that struck Kumamoto on 28 July, killing 43 people and damaging more than 83,000 homes, according to the Fire and Disaster Management Agency. The weather agency urged people in the worst-hit areas to stay alert for another quake of similar strength over the coming week.

Key points

  • A magnitude 5.7 earthquake struck Kumamoto Prefecture at 2:22 a.m. local time, roughly 10 kilometres deep, Japan's weather agency said.
  • Minamata ordered its entire population, 22,133 people in 11,126 households, to evacuate, and the order was still in force after dawn.
  • Eight bullet trains were halted, affecting about 5,600 passengers, and part of the Kyushu Expressway was closed; no serious injuries or damage were reported.
  • The quake is part of the sequence that began with July's magnitude 7.1 tremor, which killed 43 people; the weather agency warned of further strong shaking for about a week.
