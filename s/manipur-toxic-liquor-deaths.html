Death Toll in Manipur Toxic Liquor Tragedy Rises to 13 as 346 Are Hospitalised

At least 13 people have died and 346 others are in hospital in India's north-eastern state of Manipur after drinking alcohol that officials suspect was tainted, police said on Saturday, as the toll from the suspected methanol poisoning climbed through the week and hospitals in the state capital filled up.

Police in Chandel district said 13 people had died by 9pm on Friday and 346 patients had been admitted to hospitals since November 18, after drinking the suspected spurious liquor in the Chakpikarong area and nearby villages. Some news reports put the death toll at 14. The patients, mostly from Chakpikarong and surrounding areas, are being treated at hospitals in Imphal, the state capital, including the Jawaharlal Nehru Institute of Medical Sciences, the Regional Institute of Medical Sciences, Shija Hospital and Babina Hospital.

Shija Hospital alone has attended to more than 312 patients, the hospital said, and its beds were overwhelmed, forcing it to seek extra support from the state health department and other medical institutions. More than 140 patients were admitted to Shija Hospital and over 41 to Babina Hospital. The dead were all men aged between 32 and 50. At least a dozen patients were reported to be in a critical condition, and some were undergoing haemodialysis, a procedure that cleans the blood when the kidneys cannot.

Chief Minister Yumnam Khemchand Singh visited Shija Hospital and Babina Hospital on Friday to meet patients and review their treatment, and said more than 180 patients had been admitted to the two hospitals at that point, with the toll then at 11. He has ordered an inquiry to identify those responsible and said they would be arrested and punished. The exact cause of the deaths has not yet been medically established and remains under investigation, the district administration said.

Police in Chandel said the liquor was bought in Sugnu, a town in the neighbouring district of Kakching, and was not brewed in Chandel itself. The district administration has advised people not to drink locally brewed liquor or alcohol of doubtful origin, particularly from the suspected source, until further notice. Deaths from illicit or adulterated alcohol are reported regularly in India, where cheap liquor sold outside regulated channels can contain methanol, a highly toxic industrial chemical that can kill in doses as small as 60 millilitres.

Key points

  • At least 13 people have died and 346 are in hospital after drinking suspected tainted alcohol in Manipur's Chandel district since November 18; some reports put the death toll at 14.
  • Shija Hospital alone has attended to more than 312 patients, and its beds were overwhelmed; at least a dozen patients are in a critical condition.
  • Police say the liquor was bought in Sugnu, in neighbouring Kakching district, and was not brewed in Chandel.
  • The chief minister has ordered an inquiry, and district officials have warned people not to drink liquor of doubtful origin while the cause is investigated.
