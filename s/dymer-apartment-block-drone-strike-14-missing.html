Three Killed in Dymer Drone Strike as Third Body Pulled From Rubble

Rescue workers have recovered a third body from the rubble of a five-storey apartment building in Dymer, in the Kyiv region, taking the death toll from an overnight Russian drone strike to three, as the search continued for people still listed as missing.

Rescue workers have recovered the bodies of three people from the ruins of a five-storey apartment building in Dymer, in the Kyiv region, after a Russian drone strike partly brought the block down overnight. Emergency services identified two of the victims as a woman of 48 and an 18-year-old, and the search for more people under the rubble was still going on, with dog handlers working through the debris. Families of the missing waited outside the ruins for news.

The number still listed as missing was put at about 10, including two children, after earlier figures were revised down. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy had earlier said 14 people were unaccounted for and may be trapped beneath the debris. Nine residents have been evacuated, including one child, and the fire that followed the strike has been contained. Regional officials had earlier put the number possibly trapped at seven or eight.

At least four people were injured. A 16-year-old boy was taken to hospital with shrapnel wounds to his arm, and a 22-year-old woman was treated at the scene for cuts to her foot. A third person, a woman aged about 54, suffered shock and was treated by medics at the scene without needing hospital care. The blast also damaged a kindergarten and a shop close to the building.

The strike hit the settlement of Dymer in the Vyshhorod district overnight into Sunday, destroying flats from the first to the fifth floor. Ukraine's air force said Russia launched 186 drones across the country overnight, of which 161 were shot down. Zelenskyy said Russia had launched about 2,700 drones, nearly 1,460 guided aerial bombs and 165 missiles at Ukraine over the past week, across 20 regions, and renewed his call for the United States to authorise the transfer of Patriot air defence interceptors already funded by allies. Russian shelling also killed one person in Zaporizhzhia, where a child was wounded when guided bombs hit residential buildings.

The search was briefly halted on Sunday afternoon when a new air threat forced rescuers to take cover, and three more explosions were heard in the town as an unidentified object fell from the sky in flames. Elsewhere in the Kyiv region, a drone strike on a grocery store in Vyshneve killed one person and injured three, local officials said. The search and rescue operation in Dymer was continuing, with emergency crews still picking through the rubble.

Key points

  • Three people confirmed killed after rescuers recovered a third body from the rubble in Dymer; the search for more people under the debris is continuing.
  • About 10 people were still listed as missing, including two children; nine residents were evacuated and the fire has been contained.
  • At least four people injured: a 16-year-old boy hospitalised with shrapnel wounds, a 22-year-old woman treated at the scene, and a woman of about 54 treated for shock.
  • The search was briefly halted by a new air threat in the afternoon; a separate drone strike on a shop in Vyshneve killed one person and injured three.
