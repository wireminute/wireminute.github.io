Death Toll in Dymer Drone Strike Rises to 12 as Rescued Woman Dies in Hospital

The death toll from the Russian drone strike on a five-storey apartment building in Dymer has risen to 12, after the 79-year-old woman pulled alive from the rubble died in hospital on Tuesday, and rescue work at the site has now ended.

The death toll from the Russian drone strike on a five-storey apartment building in Dymer has risen to 12, after the 79-year-old woman pulled alive from the rubble died in hospital. Valentyna Mykolaevska spent nearly 22 hours trapped under the wreckage before rescuers freed her in the early hours of Monday and took her to hospital. The Kyiv region's military chief, Tymur Tkachenko, announced on Tuesday morning that she had died despite doctors' efforts to save her. She became the twelfth person killed in the strike, which hit the building in the early hours of Sunday while families were asleep.

The most dramatic moment of the rescue came on Sunday evening, when emergency workers found seven-year-old David alive under the rubble, nearly 14 hours after the strike. The boy spoke calmly to his rescuers as they freed him and is in stable condition and receiving medical care. Nine residents were evacuated from the building, including one child, and at least six people were injured. A 16-year-old boy was taken to hospital with shrapnel wounds to his arm, and a 22-year-old woman was treated at the scene for cuts to her foot. The blast also damaged a kindergarten and a shop close to the building.

Rescue workers recovered a tenth and an eleventh body from the ruins on Monday morning, the tenth at about 6am and the eleventh at about 7:20am, and the search ended later that day. Monday was declared a day of mourning across Kyiv region for the victims of Sunday's attacks, in which at least 15 people were killed across the region. Two other deadly strikes hit the region the same day: a drone attack on a supermarket in Vyshneve killed three people and wounded 15, including three children, and a strike in Bila Tserkva left one man dead and four others injured.

Ukraine's air force said Russia launched 186 drones across the country overnight, of which 161 were shot down. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Russia had launched about 2,700 drones, nearly 1,460 guided aerial bombs and 165 missiles at Ukraine over the past week, across 20 regions, and renewed his call for the United States to authorise the transfer of Patriot air defence interceptors already funded by allies. Among the dead in Dymer was an 11-year-old girl, killed with her mother.

Key points

  • The death toll in Dymer has risen to 12 after Valentyna Mykolaevska, 79, rescued alive after nearly 22 hours under the rubble, died in hospital on Tuesday; she is the twelfth victim of the strike.
  • Seven-year-old David was found alive under the rubble after nearly 14 hours and is in stable condition; the search for people still trapped has now ended.
  • At least 15 people were killed across Kyiv region in the overnight attacks; Monday was declared a day of mourning.
  • Two more deadly strikes hit the region the same day: a supermarket attack in Vyshneve killed three and wounded 15, including three children, and a strike in Bila Tserkva left one man dead.
