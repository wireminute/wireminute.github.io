Death Toll in Dymer Drone Strike Rises to 11 as Rescuers Keep Searching the Rubble

The death toll from the Russian drone strike on a five-storey apartment building in Dymer has risen to 11, after rescuers pulled an eleventh body from the rubble on Monday morning, as the search continues for people still trapped.

Rescue workers in Dymer recovered a tenth and an eleventh body from the ruins of the five-storey apartment building on Monday morning, the Kyiv region's military chief, Tymur Tkachenko, said. The tenth, a woman, was pulled from the debris at about 6am, and the eleventh was uncovered at about 7:20am. The building was hit by a Russian drone overnight into Sunday, destroying flats from the first to the fifth floor and starting a fire now contained. More than 130 rescuers are working at the site, and the search has repeatedly been suspended during air raid alerts, when the threat of another attack forces teams to take cover.

The most dramatic moment of the rescue came on Sunday evening, when emergency workers found seven-year-old David alive under the rubble, nearly 14 hours after the strike. The boy spoke calmly to his rescuers as they freed him and is in stable condition and receiving medical care. At about 3:30am on Monday, rescuers freed a 79-year-old woman alive from the rubble and took her to hospital. Nine residents were evacuated from the building, including one child, and at least six people were injured. A 16-year-old boy was taken to hospital with shrapnel wounds to his arm, and a 22-year-old woman was treated at the scene for cuts to her foot. The blast also damaged a kindergarten and a shop close to the building.

Monday has been declared a day of mourning across Kyiv region for the victims of Sunday's attacks. At least 14 people were killed across the region in the overnight strikes, officials said. Two other deadly strikes hit the region the same day: a drone attack on a supermarket in Vyshneve killed at least two people and injured at least eight, and a strike in Bila Tserkva left one man dead and four others injured. The emergency service said earlier it was searching for 11 people, including two children, who could not be reached.

Ukraine's air force said Russia launched 186 drones across the country overnight, of which 161 were shot down. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Russia had launched about 2,700 drones, nearly 1,460 guided aerial bombs and 165 missiles at Ukraine over the past week, across 20 regions, and renewed his call for the United States to authorise the transfer of Patriot air defence interceptors already funded by allies. The story is still developing.
