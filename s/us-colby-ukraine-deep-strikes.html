Pentagon Tells Allies Ukraine Deep Strikes Do Not Work as Germany and Britain Push Back

The Pentagon's policy chief has told Ukraine's allies that Kyiv's long-range strikes deep inside Russia are not working, setting off an open split with Germany and Britain at a meeting of the country's military backers in Brussels.

Elbridge Colby, the under secretary of defense for policy, told the Ukraine Defense Contact Group on Tuesday that the deep strikes had failed to bring the war closer to an end and had done nothing to slow Russia's advances on the front line. He said attacks on civilian sites, including warehouses of the online retailer Wildberries, had probably hardened Russian public resolve and the Kremlin's willingness to escalate, and he urged allies to direct their support toward equipment for front-line fighting instead.

The criticism drew a sharp public rebuke. German Defence Minister Boris Pistorius said it was "quite a stupid thing" to criticise strikes aimed at strategic military targets, and Britain also rejected the Pentagon's assessment, according to reports from the meeting. Ukraine's defence minister, Yevhenii Khmara, said the strikes were a response to Russian attacks in October and pointed to the damage to the bridge linking Russia with Crimea, adding that Kyiv planned to expand its deep-strike capacity more than threefold next year.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy used the same gathering to press allies to deliver air defence systems without delay as Russia steps up strikes on civilian infrastructure ahead of winter. NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte said earlier on Tuesday that Ukraine had the right to defend itself, including by hitting Russian logistical hubs. Colby, by contrast, said Ukraine's forces were at their most effective when fighting on the front line, using drones to slow Russian advances and strike supply lines, and he described the deep-strike campaign as a spectacle aimed at inflicting pain on ordinary Russians.

The disagreement comes as Ukraine has used long-range drones to hit oil refineries and other sites far inside Russia, while Moscow has answered with heavy strikes on Ukrainian cities and power infrastructure. The Ramstein group of around 50 countries, which coordinates military aid to Kyiv, met to discuss weapons supplies for the winter. The split leaves Washington publicly at odds with its closest European allies over a tactic Kyiv regards as one of its few ways of carrying the war to Russia.

Key points

  • Colby told the Ramstein group that Ukraine's deep strikes have not shortened the war or slowed Russia's front-line advances.
  • He said strikes on civilian sites such as Wildberries warehouses have probably hardened Russian resolve and the Kremlin's willingness to escalate.
  • Germany's Pistorius called the criticism "quite a stupid thing"; Britain also rejected it, while Zelenskyy urged allies to rush air defences.
  • Colby urged allies to focus aid on front-line equipment; Ukraine says it will expand deep strikes more than threefold next year.
