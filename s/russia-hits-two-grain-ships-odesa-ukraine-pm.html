Two Grain Ships Hit Near Odesa in Overnight Russian Attack, Ukraine Says

Russia attacked two foreign cargo ships carrying more than 100,000 metric tons of grain overnight near Ukraine's Black Sea port of Odesa, Ukraine's prime minister said on Thursday.

Prime Minister Sergiy Koretskyi said the two foreign vessels were hit overnight near the Odesa port hub. He did not say where the grain came from or where the ships had loaded, and no casualty figures were given. Russia's defence ministry said its forces had struck a dry cargo vessel southeast of Odesa overnight, claiming it was carrying military equipment and dual-use goods for Ukraine. Moscow says it does not attack civilian targets.

Grain shipments from the Odesa ports have all but stopped since late July, after Russia intensified attacks on grain terminals and merchant vessels. Before the escalation, the three ports handled about 90 percent of Ukraine's agricultural and metallurgical exports. Kyiv has not banned port operations, but shipowners have avoided the ports, and insurance costs have deterred traders. Ukraine has exported 7.6 million tons of grain this season, down from 11.5 million tons a year earlier.
