Delta Restores Weight-and-Balance System After More Than 1,600 Flights Delayed

Delta Air Lines said on Sunday afternoon that the automated system that calculates aircraft weight and balance was working again, after a failure in the system delayed more than 1,600 flights, about half the airline's mainline schedule, at the start of the Thanksgiving travel rush.

The airline said the automation that tabulates the weight-and-balance calculations required for planes to take off was working again by about 3:30 p.m. Eastern time, ending a day of manual workarounds in which crews calculated each aircraft's loading by hand. Flight tracking data showed more than 1,600 Delta mainline flights delayed on Sunday, about half of the airline's planned operation, and more than 40 flights cancelled. At Atlanta, the airline's largest hub, delayed flights passed 1,000 during the day. Delta said about 70 percent of the day's disruptions were caused by the technology failure, with the rest blamed on weather and air traffic restrictions in New York and the Northeast.

The fault was in the airline's Automated Load Instruction System, which works out each aircraft's weight and balance before take-off. When it failed, crews had to complete the calculations by hand, slowing every departure. In a message to staff, the airline described the problem as a database issue affecting the system that tracks aircraft-loading information, and warned the manual workaround drives high call volumes and workload. Delta has not said publicly what caused the failure. The airline said it was focused on completing the rest of Sunday's schedule, and would rebook affected customers, provide meals and hotel rooms where needed, or refunds for cancelled flights.

The Federal Aviation Administration halted Delta flights bound for Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport on Sunday morning, at the airline's request, holding departures at their origin airports. The stop was eased to a ground delay, with flights resuming under restrictions and average delays of 57 minutes, and by late morning the delay programme had ended. The disruption spread well beyond Atlanta. Passengers reported Delta delays at airports including Newark, New York LaGuardia, Reagan National, Washington Dulles, Boston, Baltimore, Miami and Minneapolis, as late turnarounds rippled through the network.

The timing adds pressure: Thanksgiving falls on Thursday, and Atlanta airport expects more than four million travellers to pass through it between now and 1 December. The FAA also briefly halted flights at Houston and New York LaGuardia on Sunday morning over low visibility, in unrelated ground stops; both have since been lifted. The disruption comes nine days after a nationwide ground stop on 13 November that also hit Delta hard. Knock-on delays were expected into the evening as the airline worked through the backlog. The story is still developing.
