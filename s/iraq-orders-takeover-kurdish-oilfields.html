Iraq Orders Federal Takeover of Kurdish Oilfields and Border Crossings

Iraq's prime minister has ordered the federal government to take over oilfields and border crossings in the country's Kurdistan region, a sweeping move the Kurdistan regional government immediately rejected.

Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi ordered the federal Oil Ministry to manage oilfields in areas under Kurdish security control, his office announced on Tuesday. The order covers ministry-owned fields in the provinces of Diyala, Kirkuk, Salahuddin and Nineveh, and follows an investigation into suspected oil smuggling at the state-run North Oil Company.

In the same set of directives, the prime minister ordered the Border Ports Authority to take over all crossings in the Kurdistan region, which borders Turkey, Iran and Syria, and told the regional government to approve exports of crude oil and petroleum products only through the federal Oil Ministry. The Energy Police were ordered to secure oilfields across the country and monitor them with cameras, including fields under Kurdish security control, and border forces were told to close unofficial crossings.

The prime minister also removed the head of the border crossings authority and suspended the North Oil Company's director general and his deputies pending further investigation. The oil minister had said on Monday that the northern fields should be managed federally, like fields in central and southern Iraq, and that investigators had not yet established how much oil had been smuggled. The orders also require electronic meters at every operating oil well and a digital system to monitor pipelines for theft.

The prime minister of the Kurdistan region, Masrour Barzani, rejected the decisions. Khormala, the main oilfield under Kurdish security control, appeared to be covered by the order, and people familiar with the matter said Kurdish authorities would probably resist any attempt by the federal ministry to take over its management. Kurdish leaders had already rejected Baghdad's proposals for broader federal control of the region's oil sector, a senior Kurdish source said on Sunday.

The orders are part of a wider crackdown on an oil smuggling network that officials say generated hundreds of millions of dollars from stolen oil. Iraqi forces have moved against the network's stronghold at Jurf al-Sakhar, south of Baghdad, and senior officials have been arrested. Baghdad already oversees Kurdish crude exports through Turkey, with the proceeds going to the federal treasury, but Tuesday's orders go further, directing federal agencies to operate and guard fields where Kurdish forces provide security. The measures are the first of their kind ordered by an Iraqi prime minister since 2003.

Key points

  • Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi ordered the federal Oil Ministry to manage oilfields under Kurdish security control in Diyala, Kirkuk, Salahuddin and Nineveh provinces.
  • The Border Ports Authority is to take over all crossings in the Kurdistan region, and oil exports must go through the federal ministry.
  • The Kurdistan region's prime minister, Masrour Barzani, rejected the orders.
  • The move follows a smuggling inquiry at the North Oil Company and a wider crackdown on an oil smuggling network.
