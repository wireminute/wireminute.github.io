Six Wounded in Strike on Sanaa Oil Company Fuel Station as Saudi Defences Down Drone and Missile

Six workers were wounded when a strike hit a fuel station run by Yemen's state oil company in the capital Sanaa early on Sunday, Houthi-run health authorities said, as Saudi air defences reported downing a Houthi drone and a ballistic missile.

Anees al-Asbahi, a spokesman for the Houthi-run health ministry, said the strike hit a fuel station belonging to Yemen's state oil company in Sanaa and wounded six workers, damaging the company building. Houthi-run media attributed the attack to Saudi aircraft. Saudi authorities had not issued an immediate comment, and the casualty figures could not be independently verified.

The Saudi-led coalition said on Sunday that a Houthi drone heading for Riyadh had been shot down by its air defences. It said it had also brought down a ballistic missile aimed at the western region along the Red Sea coast, prompting a civil defence alert in the Mecca region before the danger was declared over. No damage or casualties from the interceptions were reported.

In the south, a security official said a Houthi drone strike on a residential neighbourhood in Aden's Al-Buraiqah district killed three people and wounded seven. A separate report said the three killed were children. The Houthis did not comment on the Aden strike.

The exchange followed a heavy weekend. Houthi-run television al-Masirah, citing health authorities in Sanaa, reported that 41 people were killed and 28 injured in airstrikes attributed to Saudi Arabia across Yemen in the past 24 hours, including 11 children and seven women among the dead. The figures could not be independently verified, and the broadcaster did not say where the strikes took place.

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said the group had targeted the Al-Shuqaiq power plant and Jazan port in southwestern Saudi Arabia, claiming direct hits and extensive damage. Those claims also could not be verified. The coalition said its air defences had intercepted two ballistic missiles targeting Jazan, with debris falling in the Al-Shuqaiq area, and three ballistic missiles fired towards Riyadh in the early hours of Saturday, the third night in a row that emergency alerts sounded in the capital.

The fighting is the latest in a renewed round of cross-border attacks that reignited in July. A Houthi missile hit Riyadh's tallest tower, the Public Investment Fund skyscraper, two days before the weekend, blowing out windows across several floors, and an attack on King Khalid International airport earlier this month killed at least 13 people and wounded more than 300. Saudi authorities say Houthi attacks have killed 26 people in the kingdom since September, including 23 in strikes on airports. The story is still developing.
