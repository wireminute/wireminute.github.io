Saudi Defences Down Three Houthi Missiles Over Riyadh on Third Night of Alerts

Saudi air defences intercepted three ballistic missiles fired towards Riyadh in the early hours of Saturday, the Saudi-led coalition said, as emergency alerts sounded in the capital for a third night in a row.

Coalition spokesman Maj. Gen. Turki al-Maliki said the missiles were intercepted and destroyed after being launched by the Houthi militia in Yemen. He announced the interceptions on X shortly after 2 a.m. local time, following a civil defence alert issued at 1:53 a.m. warning residents of an aerial threat. Two explosions were heard in the capital, witnesses said, before civil defence lifted the alert and said the danger had passed.

It was the third consecutive night that Riyadh residents received emergency warnings, two days after a Houthi missile hit the capital's tallest tower, the Public Investment Fund skyscraper in the King Abdullah Financial District, blowing out windows across several floors. No damage or casualties from Saturday's interceptions were reported.

The Houthis have threatened to widen their targets, naming a power station at Shuqaiq, Red Sea ports including Jeddah, and Riyadh's financial district. The threats came after the coalition said it had struck Hodeidah port and targets in the Yemeni capital Sanaa earlier in the day. The Houthis have also warned airlines to avoid Saudi airspace and told people to stay away from the financial district.

The exchange is the latest in a renewed round of cross-border fighting that reignited in July, with the Houthis stepping up attacks on Saudi cities as the wider regional war spreads. The coalition has struck Houthi missile and drone facilities across Yemen in response. The story is still developing. An attack on King Khalid International airport in Riyadh earlier this month killed at least 13 people and wounded more than 300, the deadliest attack on the kingdom in more than a decade, and the Houthis have since claimed further strikes on the airport and on oil pumping stations.
