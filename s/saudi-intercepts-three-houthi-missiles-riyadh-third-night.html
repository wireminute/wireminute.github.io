Houthis Say 41 Killed in Yemen Strikes as They Claim Retaliation on Saudi Power Plant and Port

The Houthis said at least 41 people, including 11 children and seven women, were killed in airstrikes across Yemen in the past 24 hours, as the group claimed retaliatory strikes on a power plant and a port in southwestern Saudi Arabia.

Houthi-run television al-Masirah, citing health authorities in the Houthi-controlled capital Sanaa, reported that 41 people were killed and 28 others, including three children, were injured in airstrikes attributed to Saudi Arabia across Yemen in the past 24 hours. The broadcaster did not say where the strikes took place. The casualty figures could not be independently verified, and Saudi authorities had not issued an immediate comment.

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said the group had targeted the Al-Shuqaiq power plant and Jazan port in Saudi Arabia's Jazan region, claiming direct hits and extensive damage. Those claims also could not be independently verified. Saree said the attacks were retaliation for reported Saudi strikes on power stations in Yemen's northern Saada province and the Red Sea city of Mocha, as well as an attack earlier on Friday on Hodeidah port.

The Saudi-led coalition said its air defences had intercepted and destroyed two ballistic missiles targeting Jazan, with debris falling in the Al-Shuqaiq area. Houthi-run media broadcast images of a damaged power station in Mocha after a coalition air raid. The United Nations said it was following with concern reports of coalition airstrikes on Hodeidah port and ongoing military operations in the governorates of Sanaa, Saada and Taiz.

Hours earlier, Saudi air defences intercepted three ballistic missiles fired towards Riyadh in the early hours of Saturday, the coalition said, as emergency alerts sounded in the capital for a third night in a row. Coalition spokesman Maj. Gen. Turki al-Maliki said the missiles were intercepted and destroyed after being launched by the Houthi militia in Yemen. Two explosions were heard in the capital, witnesses said, before civil defence lifted the alert and said the danger had passed. No damage or casualties from Saturday's interceptions were reported.

The exchange is the latest in a renewed round of cross-border fighting that reignited in July, with the Houthis stepping up attacks on Saudi cities as the wider regional war spreads. It came two days after a Houthi missile hit the capital's tallest tower, the Public Investment Fund skyscraper in the King Abdullah Financial District, blowing out windows across several floors. An attack on King Khalid International airport in Riyadh earlier this month killed at least 13 people and wounded more than 300, the deadliest attack on the kingdom in more than a decade. The story is still developing.
