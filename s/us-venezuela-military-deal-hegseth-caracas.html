US and Venezuela Sign Military Cooperation Deal as Hegseth Makes Surprise Caracas Visit

The United States and Venezuela have signed a military cooperation agreement covering joint training and intelligence sharing, announced by US Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth during a surprise visit to Caracas on Wednesday.

US Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth announced the agreement alongside interim President Delcy Rodriguez at the Miraflores presidential palace in Caracas, describing it as a re-establishment of the two countries' defence partnership. He said the shared aim was to stabilise Venezuela and to confront the drug trafficking groups operating in the region, and he described the country as having moved from the region's most aggressive threat to a compliant security partner.

The deal covers joint training, intelligence sharing and other areas of military cooperation, as well as work to strengthen Venezuela's armed forces and border security. Rodriguez said the arrangement would help confront common threats and improve training for Venezuelan troops. Neither side gave further details of what the cooperation will involve in practice, and the Pentagon offered no additional information.

The visit was the first by a US defence secretary to Venezuela since American special forces captured then-president Nicolas Maduro in a raid in January, and the highest-level American visit to the country since then. Rodriguez has led an interim government since she was sworn in on January 5, and state television broadcast images of the two leaders side by side.

The trip comes as Washington deepens its role in Venezuela after the January operation, including a separate arrangement giving a US-linked energy producer long-term access to Venezuelan oilfields. It also follows a difficult period for Hegseth at home, where the House earlier this month voted to send an impeachment resolution against him to committee, and where he has faced sustained questions over the Iran war and the legality of American strikes on suspected drug boats.

Hegseth also spoke at the US embassy in Caracas during the visit, which had not been announced in advance. His office gave no timetable for when the joint training or intelligence sharing under the new agreement would begin.

Key points

  • Hegseth announced the deal in Caracas on Wednesday alongside interim President Delcy Rodriguez, covering joint training and intelligence sharing.
  • He called it a re-establishment of the US-Venezuela defence partnership, aimed at stabilising the country and confronting drug trafficking groups.
  • It is the first visit by a US defence secretary since American forces captured Nicolas Maduro in January; Rodriguez has led the interim government since January 5.
  • Neither side gave details of the cooperation in practice, and the Pentagon offered no further information.
