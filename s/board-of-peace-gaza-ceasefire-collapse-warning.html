Gaza Ceasefire Could Collapse Without Action, Peace Board Warns UN

The board overseeing the Gaza ceasefire has warned the United Nations Security Council that the year-old truce could collapse without immediate action, saying a ceasefire that delivers nothing visible cannot be sustained indefinitely.

The United States-backed Board of Peace delivered its first annual assessment to the United Nations Security Council on Tuesday, warning that without immediate steps to fully implement the 2025 ceasefire deal, the truce between Israel and Hamas may be on the brink of collapse. The board was created in 2025 at President Donald Trump's behest to oversee the ceasefire and the reconstruction of Gaza, under a 20-point peace plan. The ceasefire formally began on October 10, 2025, though attacks have continued since.

The distance between what has been agreed and what is happening on the ground nonetheless remains the principal risk to the process, the report said. A ceasefire that delivers nothing visible cannot be sustained indefinitely, it added. Should it collapse, there would be no road map to return to, no administration to deploy and no enabling environment to rebuild.

The report called on both Israel and Hamas to uphold the commitments made last year and urged the Security Council to lend its support. It called out Israel for restrictions on the entry of winter shelter equipment, which the board has been requesting for months, leaving Gaza facing another winter in tents and rubble. Restrictions on supplies and equipment, compounded by funding shortfalls, continued to impede preparedness and response efforts, the report said.

The report said continued Israeli strikes in Gaza and Hamas military activity have stalled efforts by both sides to follow the road map, echoing the board's first report in May, delivered by its director-general Nickolay Mladenov, a former United Nations Middle East envoy. It also said Israel has still not decided on the arrangements for training, deploying and returning civilian police to Gaza, and the technocratic civil administration set up to run Gaza on an interim basis has still not been allowed to take up its job.

Key points

  • The Board of Peace told the UN Security Council the Gaza ceasefire could collapse without immediate steps to fully implement the 2025 deal.
  • A ceasefire that delivers nothing visible cannot be sustained indefinitely, the board warned, saying collapse would leave no road map to return to.
  • The report criticised Israeli restrictions on winter shelter equipment, leaving Gaza facing another winter in tents and rubble.
  • Israel has yet to decide on arrangements for civilian police in Gaza, and the interim technocratic administration has still not been allowed to start work.
