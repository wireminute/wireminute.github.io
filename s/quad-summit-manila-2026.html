Quad Leaders Warn on Economic Coercion in First Summit in Two Years

The leaders of the United States, India, Japan and Australia warned of grave concerns about economic coercion at their first full summit in two years in Manila, and announced five joint initiatives, including a first maritime surveillance exercise and an 85 million dollar push on trusted artificial intelligence.

The leaders of the United States, India, Japan and Australia met in Manila on Tuesday for their first full summit in two years, warning of grave concerns about economic coercion and announcing five joint initiatives. President Donald Trump joined Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi of Japan, Prime Minister Narendra Modi of India and Prime Minister Anthony Albanese of Australia for the four-way meeting on the sidelines of the ASEAN meetings, hours after arriving in the Philippine capital.

In a joint statement issued after the meeting, the four leaders announced a first Quad Business Leaders' Forum, to be held in Mumbai in early 2027, closer counterterrorism cooperation, and a new Indo-Pacific Maritime Surveillance Collaboration under which their patrol aircraft will fly a joint exercise in 2027, the first in a Quad setting. They also widened their cable partnership to cover electricity cables, promised deeper cooperation on cybersecurity, and backed an 85 million dollar push to expand access to trusted artificial intelligence across the region. Their warning covered economic coercion, including arbitrary export restrictions, and they opposed any destabilising or unilateral action to change the status quo by force or coercion.

At a group photograph before the talks, Trump praised his counterparts in personal terms, calling Modi a very tough man and adding that Takaichi may be even tougher. Outside the venue, about 2,000 demonstrators faced off with police, carrying banners reading 'Trump out now' and 'Reject Pax Silica', a reference to a United States supply chain initiative that has divided opinion in the Philippines. Trump also used the day to press his trade message, saying the United States would catch China in rare earths production within 10 months.

Trump meets Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos on Wednesday before heading to Shenzhen for the APEC summit, where he said he hopes to hold one-on-one talks with Russian President Vladimir Putin and North Korean leader Kim Jong Un. Putin arrived in China on Wednesday ahead of a summit with Xi Jinping and Trump, while Beijing said it had no information about a meeting between Trump and Kim. The ASEAN summit closed on Tuesday with a Digital Economy Framework Agreement, though leaders failed to deliver a code of conduct on the South China Sea.

Key points

  • The Quad leaders held their first in-person summit in two years in Manila, warning of grave concerns about economic coercion.
  • Five initiatives were announced, including a first joint maritime patrol-aircraft exercise in 2027, a business leaders' forum in Mumbai, and an 85 million dollar artificial intelligence push.
  • Trump called Modi a very tough man and said Takaichi may be even tougher, as about 2,000 people protested outside the venue.
  • Trump heads to Shenzhen for APEC, eyeing talks with Putin, who arrived in China on Wednesday, and with Kim Jong Un.
