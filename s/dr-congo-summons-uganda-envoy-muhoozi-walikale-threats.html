DR Congo Summons Uganda Envoy After General Backs Rebel Capture of Walikale

The Democratic Republic of Congo has summoned Uganda's top diplomat in Kinshasa after Uganda's military chief publicly backed a rebel bid to seize Walikale, a mineral-rich territory in the country's east.

Kinshasa called in Uganda's head of mission on 24 November and demanded clear explanations from Kampala, after Gen Muhoozi Kainerugaba, Uganda's chief of defence forces and the son of President Yoweri Museveni, posted a series of messages on X backing the advance of the Front for the Refoundation of the Nation, a movement led by former Congolese president Joseph Kabila. The foreign ministry described the remarks as particularly serious, saying they undermined the restraint expected between sovereign states.

In the posts, published on Monday, Kainerugaba said Uganda's military would not oppose the group's capture of Walikale, in North Kivu province, and warned that Ugandan forces could seize the town themselves if the rebels delayed. He wrote that Uganda approved the group's capture of Walikale and that its army would not oppose it, adding that if the group wasted a lot of time, Uganda would capture Walikale itself. He said Uganda's presence in eastern Congo was driven by security concerns, including the fight against the Allied Democratic Forces and the protection of the Bahema community, and accused a Rwandan rebel group of targeting Bahema members.

Kinshasa said it expected Uganda to clarify its official position and take steps to prevent similar statements in future, while reaffirming its commitment to regional peace and its readiness to defend its sovereignty. Uganda's government had not publicly responded to the protest by Thursday. Kainerugaba's X account, which had close to 1.4 million followers, has since become inaccessible.

The row is sensitive because the two countries' armies have fought alongside each other against armed groups in eastern Congo since 2021, and the dispute comes amid a wider conflict in the region. It is not the first time the general's posts have strained relations: in February last year he threatened to seize the eastern city of Bunia, and in 2024 Kinshasa summoned Uganda's representative after his threats against foreign mercenaries in Congo. His latest posts followed reports that a Congolese military aircraft had been shot down in the east, with foreign personnel reportedly among those on board.

Key points

  • DR Congo summoned Uganda's head of mission in Kinshasa on 24 November and demanded explanations, calling Gen Muhoozi Kainerugaba's posts particularly serious and a breach of the restraint expected between states.
  • Kainerugaba, Uganda's military chief and President Museveni's son, wrote on X that Uganda's army would not oppose the FRN rebel movement's capture of Walikale, and warned Ugandan forces could take the town themselves if the rebels delayed.
  • Kinshasa wants Kampala to clarify its official position and prevent a repeat; Uganda's government had not publicly responded by Thursday, and the general's X account, with close to 1.4 million followers, has become inaccessible.
  • The dispute strains a joint military campaign against armed groups in eastern Congo running since 2021; it is the general's third such diplomatic incident, after threats over Bunia last year and foreign mercenaries in 2024.
