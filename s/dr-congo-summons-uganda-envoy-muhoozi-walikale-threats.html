Uganda to Pull Troops From DR Congo Town as Diplomatic Row Deepens

Uganda's army says it will withdraw its troops from Lubero town in eastern DR Congo within two weeks, handing security to Congolese forces, as a diplomatic row over its military chief's backing for a rebel advance continues.

Uganda's army said on Friday it will pull its troops out of Lubero town in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo within two weeks, handing responsibility for security there to the Congolese armed forces. The announcement, made by the army's acting spokesman Col Chris Magezi, said the withdrawal would begin in the coming fortnight. Ugandan forces have held the town in North Kivu province since January 2025, when they moved in to pursue fighters of the Allied Democratic Forces who had fled joint operations elsewhere in the east.

The move comes days after Kinshasa summoned Uganda's head of mission and demanded explanations over posts by Uganda's military chief, Gen Muhoozi Kainerugaba, on X backing a rebel bid to seize Walikale, a mineral-rich territory in North Kivu. The general, the son of President Yoweri Museveni, wrote that Uganda's army would not oppose the advance of the Front for the Refoundation of the Nation, a movement led by former Congolese president Joseph Kabila, and warned Ugandan forces could take the town themselves if the rebels delayed. Kinshasa called the remarks particularly serious and asked Kampala to clarify its official position and prevent a repeat.

The army did not say whether the withdrawal was linked to the diplomatic row, and it gave no indication that the wider joint campaign against the Allied Democratic Forces would change. The two countries' armies have operated together against armed groups in eastern Congo since 2021, and the joint offensive against the ADF continues elsewhere in the country. The statement also did not say whether the pullback from Lubero pointed to a broader reduction of Uganda's military presence in eastern Congo.

The announcement lands as fighting has resumed between Congolese forces and rebels across the wider Lubero territory, raising fresh concern for civilians there. The row is the general's third diplomatic incident of its kind: he threatened to seize the eastern city of Bunia last year, and in 2024 Kinshasa summoned Uganda's representative after his threats against foreign mercenaries in Congo. His X account, which had close to 1.4 million followers, has become inaccessible since the latest posts.

Key points

  • Uganda's army says its troops will leave Lubero town in North Kivu within two weeks, handing security to the Congolese armed forces; they have held the town since January 2025 while pursuing ADF fighters.
  • The announcement follows Kinshasa's summons of Uganda's envoy on 24 November over Gen Muhoozi Kainerugaba's posts backing the FRN rebel movement's capture of Walikale and warning Ugandan forces could take it themselves.
  • The army did not say whether the withdrawal was linked to the row or whether it signals a broader pullback; the joint campaign against the ADF continues elsewhere.
  • Fighting has resumed across the wider Lubero territory, and the row is the general's third diplomatic incident, after threats over Bunia last year and foreign mercenaries in 2024.
