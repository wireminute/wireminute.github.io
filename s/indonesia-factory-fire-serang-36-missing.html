24 Body Bags Removed From Indonesia Candy Factory as Search Resumes After Fire

Indonesian police have removed 24 body bags from a candy factory destroyed by fire in western Java, as the search for dozens of missing workers resumes on Monday after being suspended overnight.

The fire broke out on Saturday evening at the PT Fonusa Agung Mulia factory in Cikande, in the Serang regency about 50 kilometres west of Jakarta. The plant makes Milkita, a brand of milk-based candies, and belongs to United Family Food, an Indonesian food manufacturer founded in 1981. Firefighters were still tackling hotspots in the burned building on Sunday, and officials warned parts of it remained at risk of collapse.

Police removed 24 body bags from the factory on Sunday, up from seven recovered earlier in the day. Police said a single bag could contain the remains of more than one person, so the number of bags does not by itself give a death toll. The intensity of the fire has made visual identification impossible, and a police disaster victim identification official said the process would rely on DNA testing and could take two to three weeks.

Figures on the missing have shifted through the day as officials reconciled lists of who was at work. In a later statement the factory said that of 65 workers involved, 24 had returned home, four were receiving medical treatment, 29 remained missing and the whereabouts of another eight had yet to be confirmed. That accounting differs from earlier figures: police had put about 50 workers unaccounted for, and the factory's owner said about 300 people had been present during the shift, far more than the 60 the provincial governor had first believed were inside. Rescue officials said 15 people were pulled out alive.

The search was suspended overnight and is scheduled to resume on Monday morning, the Banten provincial police chief, Hendra Kurniawan, told local television. The cause of the fire is not yet known. Survivors told officials it started in a cooling unit on the third floor, and the local disaster agency said the cause was still under investigation.
