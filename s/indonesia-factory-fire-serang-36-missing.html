Seven Body Bags Recovered as 50 Remain Missing After Indonesia Candy Factory Fire

Indonesian police have recovered seven body bags from the ruins of a candy factory in western Java, while about 50 workers remain unaccounted for more than a day after a fire tore through the plant.

The fire broke out on Saturday evening at the PT Fonusa Agung Mulia factory in Cikande, in the Serang regency about 50 kilometres west of Jakarta. The plant makes Milkita, a brand of milk-based candies, and belongs to United Family Food, an Indonesian food manufacturer founded in 1981. Firefighters were still tackling hotspots in the burned building on Sunday, and officials warned parts of it remained at risk of collapse.

Police said the seven body bags did not necessarily represent seven individual victims, because a single bag could contain the remains of more than one person. The intensity of the fire has made visual identification impossible, and a police disaster victim identification official said the process would rely on DNA testing and could take two to three weeks.

Figures on the missing have shifted through the day as officials reconciled lists of who was at work. The latest police figure puts about 50 workers unaccounted for, up from 36 earlier on Sunday. The factory's owner said about 300 people had been present during the shift when the fire broke out, far more than the 60 the provincial governor had first believed were inside. Rescue officials said 15 people were pulled out alive, including four who needed hospital treatment.

The cause of the fire is not yet known. Survivors told officials it started in a cooling unit on the third floor. The local disaster agency said the cause was still under investigation, and the search continued on Sunday in the charred remains of the building.

Key points

  • Seven body bags recovered from the candy factory in Cikande, Serang; a bag may hold remains of more than one person.
  • About 50 workers still unaccounted for, up from 36 earlier on Sunday; the factory owner said about 300 people were on shift.
  • Visual identification is impossible after the intensity of the fire; DNA testing could take two to three weeks.
