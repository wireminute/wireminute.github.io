Blast Heard in Riyadh as Houthi Missile Threat Causes Damage

A missile fired by Yemen's Houthis towards the Saudi capital caused material damage on Thursday, the Saudi-led coalition said, after a loud blast was heard across Riyadh and debris came down in the city.

The coalition said its forces had dealt with the threat, and an early assessment found debris on the ground and damage to property. How much damage had been done was still unclear. The Saudi Civil Defense agency had earlier warned people in the capital of a possible danger, without giving details. There was no immediate confirmation from the Saudi authorities or from the Houthis themselves.

Saudi aircraft struck the Houthi-held Yemeni capital, Sanaa, in the early hours of Thursday, according to the Houthi-run Saba news agency, which gave no further details. The coalition said a cruise missile aimed at the southern region had also been shot down. The blast in Riyadh came shortly after the state news agency said Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman had just held talks with the head of US Central Command, Admiral Brad Cooper, in the capital.

Riyadh has been struck repeatedly during the war, including a fatal strike on the city's main airport. The Houthis, who are aligned with Iran, have stepped up attacks in recent weeks as the wider regional war has spread, with mine strikes on tankers near the Strait of Hormuz and fighting in Gaza and Yemen running in parallel.

The attack came as President Donald Trump, speaking from Shenzhen before the first trilateral meeting with the leaders of China and Russia, said Iran appeared to be looking into chemical and biological weapons after strikes on its nuclear programme. Trump played down reports of a plot to detonate a dirty bomb in Washington, saying people in the capital should not be worried, and warned of forceful retaliation if such an attack happened. He also said the United States did not need help from China or Russia to end the war with Iran. Reports said Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu was pressing Washington to join a four-to-seven-day air campaign against Iran, but Trump has not made a decision, and some American officials question the intelligence behind the warnings.

Damage teams were still assessing the scene on Thursday morning, and the coalition gave no casualty figures. The incident is the latest sign of how far the regional war has reached into the Gulf's capitals, and the story is still developing.

Key points

  • The Saudi-led coalition said it dealt with a Houthi missile threat towards Riyadh on Thursday after a loud blast, with debris falling and material damage reported.
  • Saudi jets struck Sanaa early on Thursday, and the coalition said a cruise missile aimed at the southern region had also been shot down.
  • Neither the Saudi authorities nor the Houthis immediately confirmed the incident, and how much damage had been done was still unclear.
  • The incident came as Trump said Iran appeared to be looking into chemical and biological weapons, and as reports said Netanyahu was pressing for a joint air campaign.
