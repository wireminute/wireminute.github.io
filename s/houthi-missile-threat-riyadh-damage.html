Saudi Wealth Fund Tower Damaged as Houthi Missile Debris Falls on Riyadh

Debris from a Houthi missile threat damaged the Riyadh tower housing Saudi Arabia's sovereign wealth fund on Thursday, witnesses said, hours after a loud blast shook the capital and the Saudi-led coalition said its forces had dealt with the attack.

Witnesses said the facade of the 80-storey tower in the capital's financial district was left scarred over several floors, its windows shattered, with damage confirmed by two other sources. The coalition said an early assessment found debris on the ground and material damage, and the full extent was still being determined. There was no immediate comment from the Saudi authorities or from the fund itself.

The Saudi Civil Defense agency had warned people in the capital of a possible danger in the early hours, and an all-clear followed minutes after the blast. Civil defence issued seven emergency warnings across the country during the morning. The boom came shortly after the state news agency said Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman had just held talks with the head of US Central Command, Admiral Brad Cooper, in the capital.

The coalition said its air defences had brought down four ballistic missiles and a cruise missile fired towards the country's southern region on Thursday, and that its forces had destroyed two missile launchers in Yemen, one in Saada province and one in Dhamar. Later in the morning, it said it had also brought down two cruise missiles heading for Riyadh and two drones, one over the capital and one over the south. Saudi aircraft also struck the Houthi-held Yemeni capital, Sanaa, in the early hours of Thursday, according to the Houthi-run Saba news agency.

The Houthis, aligned with Iran, have stepped up attacks in recent weeks as the wider regional war has spread. The Houthi military spokesman said the group had carried out four operations overnight, with cruise missiles aimed at King Khalid International Airport and at the financial district in Riyadh, and ballistic missiles aimed at Khamis Mushait air base and at Jizan. He also counted 87 coalition air strikes across nine Yemeni provinces in the past 24 hours, and about 5,350 since the escalation began.

The Houthi-run Health Ministry in Sanaa said 11 civilians had been killed and 36 wounded in Saudi airstrikes in the past day, and put the total since the latest escalation began at 397 killed and 895 wounded. The World Health Organization put the toll from the latest escalation higher, at 2,260 killed and 10,519 wounded. Yemen's Presidential Leadership Council issued a law designating the Houthis a terrorist organisation, with penalties of 20 to 30 years in prison or death. Damage teams were still assessing the scene in Riyadh. The story is still developing.

Key points

  • Witnesses said debris damaged the 80-storey Riyadh tower housing Saudi Arabia's sovereign wealth fund, blowing out windows across several floors; the coalition said the full extent was still being assessed.
  • The coalition said it brought down two cruise missiles heading for Riyadh and two drones later in the morning, after earlier bringing down four ballistic missiles and a cruise missile aimed at the southern region.
  • The Houthi military spokesman claimed four operations, including cruise missiles aimed at Riyadh's airport and financial district; civil defence issued seven emergency warnings across Saudi Arabia.
  • The World Health Organization put the toll from the latest escalation at 2,260 killed and 10,519 wounded, and Yemen's government designated the Houthis a terrorist organisation; the story is still developing.
