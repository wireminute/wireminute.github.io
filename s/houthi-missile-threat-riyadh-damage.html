Saudi Wealth Fund Tower Damaged as Houthi Missile Debris Falls on Riyadh

Debris from a Houthi missile threat damaged the Riyadh tower housing Saudi Arabia's sovereign wealth fund on Thursday, witnesses said, hours after a loud blast shook the capital and the Saudi-led coalition said its forces had dealt with the attack.

Witnesses said the facade of the 80-storey tower in the capital's financial district was left scarred over several floors, its windows shattered, and two other sources confirmed damage to the building, the district's tallest tower. The coalition said an early assessment found debris on the ground and material damage, and the full extent was still being determined. There was no immediate comment from the Saudi authorities or from the fund itself.

The Saudi Civil Defense agency had warned people in the capital of a possible danger in the early hours, and an all-clear followed minutes after the blast. The boom came shortly after the state news agency said Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman had just held talks with the head of US Central Command, Admiral Brad Cooper, in the capital.

The coalition said its air defences had brought down four ballistic missiles and a cruise missile fired towards the country's southern region on Thursday, and that its forces had destroyed two missile launchers in Yemen, one in Saada province and one in Dhamar, used in the attacks on the south and Riyadh. Saudi aircraft also struck the Houthi-held Yemeni capital, Sanaa, in the early hours of Thursday, according to the Houthi-run Saba news agency, which gave no further details.

Riyadh has been struck repeatedly during the war, including a fatal strike on the city's main airport, and debris from an earlier attempt damaged a building belonging to the state oil company in the capital. The Houthis, who are aligned with Iran, have stepped up attacks in recent weeks as the wider regional war has spread. Mine strikes on tankers near the Strait of Hormuz have added to the wider regional war.

The Houthi-run Health Ministry in Sanaa said 11 civilians had been killed and 36 wounded in Saudi airstrikes in the past day, including three deaths in an attack on Al-Talahi in Ibb province, and put the total since the latest escalation began at 397 killed and 895 wounded. The Houthi military spokesman claimed the group had fired ballistic missiles at Jizan and a drone at Khamis Mushait air base. The attack came as President Donald Trump, speaking from Shenzhen before the first three-way meeting with the leaders of China and Russia, said Iran appeared to be looking into chemical and biological weapons. Damage teams were still assessing the scene in Riyadh, and the coalition gave no casualty figures of its own. The story is still developing.

Key points

  • Witnesses said debris damaged the 80-storey Riyadh tower housing Saudi Arabia's sovereign wealth fund, blowing out windows across several floors; the coalition said the full extent was still being assessed.
  • The coalition said it dealt with the Houthi missile threat towards Riyadh on Thursday after a loud blast, with debris falling and material damage reported.
  • The coalition said its air defences brought down four ballistic missiles and a cruise missile aimed at the southern region and destroyed missile launchers in Saada and Dhamar provinces.
  • The Houthi-run Health Ministry said 11 civilians were killed and 36 wounded in Saudi strikes in the past day; no Saudi casualty figures were given, and the story is still developing.
