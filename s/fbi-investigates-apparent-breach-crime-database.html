Trump Confirms Hackers Breached the FBI's National Crime Database

President Donald Trump has confirmed that hackers breached the FBI's National Crime Information Center, the nationwide database that police forces across the United States use to share warrants, fingerprint records and background checks.

Trump spoke about the incident in a television interview on Saturday, saying he had only just heard about it. "We just heard about it, and I will say that it's not good. I don't like that," he said. He added that the breach might still reveal things investigators would not otherwise have learned, pointing to past inquiries into his 2016 campaign.

The apparent intrusion was first reported on Friday, citing two people briefed on the matter, and was later confirmed through a person familiar with the investigation. The FBI has declined to comment. It has not said what data, if anything, was accessed or altered, and the full scope of the intrusion is still being established.

Two people familiar with the matter said hackers got into the NCIC system, which holds fingerprint records and information on wanted persons, violent felons and missing persons. The database is restricted to law-enforcement use but is not a classified system, and officials are also concerned about whether the accuracy of records officers rely on could have been affected.

It is the second apparent breach to hit the bureau in months. In September, a group calling itself ShinyHunters targeted an FBI jobs website and exposed sensitive personal data of employees. A Canadian cybersecurity executive was arrested in October and faces extortion conspiracy charges in connection with that investigation.

Reports also said the Agriculture Department was hit in a separate incident. The discovery follows a run of major outages affecting banks, the National Security Agency and air traffic control, which administration officials said were caused by routine upgrade work rather than cyberattacks. There is no indication the FBI incident is linked to those outages, and no suspects have been named.

Key points

  • Trump confirmed in a television interview on Saturday that hackers breached the FBI's National Crime Information Center, the first official confirmation of the intrusion.
  • The FBI is investigating and has declined to comment; it has not said what data, if anything, was accessed or altered.
  • Sources say hackers got into the NCIC, which holds fingerprint records and details of wanted persons, violent felons and missing persons; the system is restricted to law enforcement but is not classified.
  • It is the second apparent breach of the bureau in months, after the September attack on its jobs portal, and reports also said the Agriculture Department was hit.
