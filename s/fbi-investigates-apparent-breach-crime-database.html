FBI Investigates Apparent Breach of National Crime Database

Two people briefed on the investigation say the FBI is looking into an apparent intrusion into its National Crime Information Center, the nationwide database that police forces across the United States use to share warrants and background checks.

The incident came to light in recent days, and bureau officials learned of it on Thursday. The FBI has not said what data, if anything, was accessed, and how far the suspected compromise reaches is still being established. A former FBI official called the centre among the most important systems police use to share information.

It is the second apparent breach involving the bureau in recent months. In September, a group calling itself ShinyHunters targeted an FBI jobs website and exposed sensitive personal data of employees. Hours after officials learned of the latest incident, FBI Director Kash Patel appeared on television to praise the bureau's cybersecurity work.

The discovery follows a run of major outages that have hit banks, the National Security Agency and air traffic control. Officials have not named any suspects or said how the intruders got in, and the investigation is continuing.
