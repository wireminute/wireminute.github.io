Israel Says It Can Hit Iran Alone as Netanyahu Convenes Security Cabinet

Israel's military says it is ready to strike Iran at any moment, even without American help, as Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu prepares to convene his security cabinet on Thursday night.

The military spokesperson, Brigadier General Effie Defrin, told the Israeli public broadcaster on Thursday that Israel was ready to strike Iran with force at any moment, even acting alone without American involvement. He said Tehran still posed an existential threat to Israel and that its aim of destroying the country had not expired.

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu will bring his security cabinet together on Thursday night as Israel weighs its next steps. A minister's office confirmed the meeting would take place. There has been no official word that a strike has actually been ordered, and Defrin said there was no change in the public safety guidelines for civilians.

The warning comes as the American envoy Jared Kushner said President Donald Trump was keeping all options on the table, and confirmed he had held several meetings with Iranian officials as part of diplomatic efforts led by Vice President JD Vance.

The United States has enforced a naval blockade of Iranian ports for months, while Iran has targeted shipping it says is breaking its transit rules, pushing oil prices to nearly 95 dollars a barrel. Talks hosted by Qatar have yet to produce a deal, with Iran seeking an end to the blockade, the lifting of some sanctions and a halt to bombing in exchange for reopening the strait. Washington's secretary of state has told allies the economic pressure on Iran is so effective that major combat operations are not needed for now.

The statement also follows a fresh round of attacks across the region, with an explosion in the Yemeni capital Sanaa, missiles fired towards Riyadh intercepted by the Saudi-led coalition, and incidents involving tankers near the Strait of Hormuz. Yemen's Houthis have claimed several of the recent attacks on Saudi targets.
