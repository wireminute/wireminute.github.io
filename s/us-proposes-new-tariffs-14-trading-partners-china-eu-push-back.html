US Proposes New Tariffs on 14 Trading Partners as China and EU Push Back

The United States has proposed new tariffs on imports from 14 trading partners, including China and the European Union, drawing protests from governments across Asia and Europe that say they will negotiate but could hit back.

The US Trade Representative's office announced the duties on Wednesday after an eight-month investigation under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 into what Washington calls excess industrial capacity. The investigation covered 16 trading partners, though Mexico and Norway were left out of the proposals. The move rebuilds tariff leverage after the Supreme Court struck down the administration's earlier broad duties in February.

China would face the highest rate, 20 percent, though the administration kept it within a cap agreed under a trade truce with Beijing that runs until 10 January. The rate could rise to 40 percent if talks to extend the truce fail. The European Union would face 15 percent, with a possible rise to 20 percent, while India would face 17 percent if a deal is reached and 25 percent if not. Vietnam would face 20 percent with a deal and 35 percent without one. Japan, South Korea, Taiwan and Switzerland are capped at 15 percent, and five South and Southeast Asian economies at 18 percent. Brazil faces separate proposed duties, including 10 percent on finished aircraft.

Responses came quickly on Thursday. China's Commerce Ministry said Beijing kept the right to respond with measures of its own and would watch developments closely. The European Union said it did not accept the reasoning behind the new duties and expected Washington to honour last year's deal. Japan called the planned 15 percent levy on its goods regrettable, and Singapore said it disagreed with the findings and would urge the United States to reconsider duties of up to 20 percent. For now, most governments signalled they preferred negotiation to retaliation.

The duties will not take effect until after a public comment period and a hearing on 25 January, with implementation possible later that month. US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said the rates stayed within existing trade deals and that about 30 billion dollars of Chinese imports were being considered for reductions under a bilateral process. Washington said it did not expect Beijing to retaliate, and trade is expected to feature at a December meeting between the two countries' leaders.

Key points

  • The United States proposed tariffs on 14 trading partners after an eight-month Section 301 investigation into excess industrial capacity; Mexico and Norway were spared.
  • China faces 20 percent, the highest rate, capped under a trade truce until 10 January but possibly doubling to 40 percent if talks fail; the European Union faces 15 percent.
  • China, the European Union, Japan and Singapore all pushed back on Thursday, keeping the right to respond while signalling they prefer negotiation for now.
  • The duties face a comment period and a 25 January hearing before they can take effect, with implementation possible later in January.
