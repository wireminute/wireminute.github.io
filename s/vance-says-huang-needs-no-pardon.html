Vance Says Nvidia Chief Jensen Huang Needs No Pardon and Was Never Under Investigation

Vice President JD Vance has said Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang does not need a presidential pardon, telling reporters he has seen no evidence Huang committed a crime and that federal officials assured him the chipmaker's boss was never under investigation.

Speaking to reporters on Wednesday after serving a pre-Thanksgiving lunch to service members at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base near Dayton, Ohio, Vance said he had been told by the Justice Department, the attorney general and multiple national security officials that Huang was under no investigation, and that he did not think the Nvidia chief needed a pardon at all. He added that he had seen no evidence Huang had committed a crime.

Vance said the accusations against Huang rested on anonymous reporting rather than real evidence, and argued that people should not be tried in the court of public opinion. He said he had spoken to the attorney general and other officials, and that if Huang had been under investigation the administration would have said so.

The remarks followed President Donald Trump telling an interviewer on Tuesday that pardoning Huang was always an option but that he did not think it would be necessary, and urging Huang to tell the truth in his public appearances. Trump had pressed Nvidia's board to bring Huang back, and the board voted unanimously on Monday to reinstate him, 11 days after he resigned following reports of extramarital affairs, including one with a woman suspected of links to Chinese intelligence.

A classified briefing for congressional staff on Tuesday pointed in the same direction: US officials said there was no evidence China directed the woman, Alice Huang, to spy on the Nvidia chief, and that the evidence suggested the relationship may have been amorous rather than a coordinated operation. Officials also said China's intelligence service had approached her to provide information, as Chinese law compels, but that there was no indication she passed on intelligence of significance. Huang still faces an internal Nvidia investigation and is due to testify publicly before the Senate Banking Committee on 3 December, while inquiries into suspected chip smuggling remain unresolved.

Key points

  • Vance said Huang needs no pardon and that he has seen no evidence the Nvidia chief committed a crime.
  • Vance said the Justice Department, the attorney general and national security officials told him Huang was under no investigation.
  • Trump said on Tuesday that a pardon was always an option but that he did not think it would be necessary.
  • US officials told congressional staff in a classified briefing that there was no evidence China directed Alice Huang to spy on the Nvidia chief; Huang is due to testify before the Senate Banking Committee on 3 December.
