Xiamen Airlines Signs $7.4 Billion Deal for 50 Boeing 737-8 Jets

Xiamen Airlines has agreed to buy 50 Boeing 737-8 jets in a deal worth about $7.4 billion at list prices, the planemaker's first big order from a Chinese carrier in nearly ten years.

The agreement was signed on Tuesday and disclosed in a stock exchange filing by Xiamen's parent company, China Southern Airlines. The jets are due to arrive in batches from 2031 through 2034, and the purchase still needs approval from Xiamen's shareholders and from Chinese government authorities. Boeing offered a significant discount, so the amount actually paid will be below the headline figure, which is standard practice for large aircraft orders.

The order comes after a commitment China made for 200 jets at the May summit in Beijing between the US and Chinese leaders, although the filing did not make clear whether these 50 jets count toward that pledge. Boeing had gone almost a decade without a major order from China. Last year, Beijing reportedly told its airlines not to take delivery of Boeing jets and paused purchases of US aircraft equipment and parts as the tariff-driven trade war deepened.

Jamieson Greer, the US trade representative, said in September that about 140 jetliner orders were in a good state, with another 10 being finalised. Trump said on Monday that he believed China would buy another 250 Boeing planes, without giving details, and Beijing has not confirmed any further purchases.

According to China Southern, the order will help Xiamen take advantage of its relocation to the new Xiang'an international airport in Xiamen, help build an aviation hub in Fujian and reshape its fleet. Xiamen flew only Boeing jets for almost four decades until its first Airbus arrived in early 2023, an A321neo taken on lease. The two airlines also agreed in April to buy 137 Airbus A320neo-family jets.

Key points

  • Xiamen Airlines has signed an agreement to buy 50 Boeing 737-8 jets, worth about $7.4 billion at list prices, with deliveries planned from 2031 to 2034.
  • It is Boeing's first big order from a Chinese carrier in nearly ten years, after Beijing halted deliveries and purchases during the trade war.
  • The purchase still needs approval from Xiamen's shareholders and Chinese government authorities, and the final price will be below the list figure after a significant discount.
  • The order follows China's commitment for 200 jets at the May summit in Beijing, though the filing does not say whether these 50 are part of it.
