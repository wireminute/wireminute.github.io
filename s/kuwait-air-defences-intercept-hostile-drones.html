Kuwait Says Its Air Defences Are Engaging Hostile Drones as Gulf Fighting Widens

Kuwait said early on Wednesday that its air defences were engaging hostile drones over the country, as the war between the United States and Iran spread to a new Gulf front and Bahrain issued a fresh public safety alert.

Kuwait's military headquarters said in a post online at about 1.39am Gulf time that air defence batteries were engaging hostile drones and that any blasts people heard were interceptions. It told residents to follow the security authorities' instructions. The announcement did not say who had sent the drones, what they were meant to hit, or if anyone was hurt.

The announcement came hours after American fighter jets hit an Iranian missile site on Qeshm Island, just off Iran's southern coast. The strike followed an Iranian drone attack on a radar run by US forces at Bahrain's Sheikh Isa base. US Central Command said several of the drones were stopped before they could hit, and that American equipment was undamaged and no US troops were hurt.

Bahrain's Defence Force said it had stopped a number of aerial attacks and blamed Iran for targeting the kingdom, while Bahrain's Interior Ministry warned residents of a potential threat and urged them to move to safe locations. Kuwait's Foreign Ministry separately condemned what it called continued Iranian attacks on Bahrain. Kuwait has come under attack before in this war, with earlier strikes reported on the international airport, on refineries, and on a power and desalination plant at Shuaiba.

A Saudi-led coalition said early on Wednesday that its jets had hit 60 Houthi military sites in Yemen. The war began on February 28, when the United States and Israel launched strikes on Iran, and has since been fought in bursts across the Gulf, with shipping, air bases and energy sites repeatedly targeted. No end to the fighting is in sight, and the latest exchanges have complicated efforts to reach a settlement.

Key points

  • Kuwait's army said its air defences were engaging hostile drones early on Wednesday and that explosions heard were interceptions.
  • The Kuwaiti statement named no origin for the drones and reported no casualties or damage.
  • Bahrain issued a fresh safety alert after its forces said they stopped aerial attacks, and Kuwait condemned the attacks on its neighbour.
  • The exchanges followed a US strike on Qeshm Island and a coalition attack on 60 Houthi targets in Yemen.
