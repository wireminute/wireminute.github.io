Kuwait Names Iran Over Drone Attacks and Reserves Right to Self-Defence

Kuwait has publicly blamed Iran for a wave of drone attacks on the Gulf state and warned it will take every step needed to defend its territory, after its air defences opened fire on hostile drones in the early hours of Wednesday.

Kuwait has publicly blamed Iran for a wave of drone attacks on the Gulf state, hours after its air defences opened fire on hostile drones in the early hours of Wednesday. The foreign ministry called the strikes repeated and criminal, described them as a flagrant breach of Kuwait's sovereignty, and said the country would take every step needed to defend its territory, pointing to its right of self-defence under the United Nations Charter.

Iran's army had said earlier that its drones struck American military equipment at Ali Al-Salem Air Base, naming Patriot missile defences and launch platforms for drones and missiles. Kuwait's military said its batteries were engaging hostile drones and that blasts heard by residents were interceptions. Neither Kuwait nor the United States has confirmed any damage from the claimed strike, and Kuwait reported no casualties.

The United Arab Emirates condemned the attack on Kuwait in the strongest terms and said it stood fully behind its neighbour. Bahrain's defence force said separately that its air defences had stopped a fresh wave of Iranian air strikes early on Wednesday, and the Gulf Cooperation Council condemned what it called repeated attacks on Bahrain and urged the United Nations Security Council to act.

The Houthis in Yemen said they had also flown drones against pumping stations on Saudi Arabia's East-West oil pipeline. Saudi officials said a drone was stopped near Riyadh, and the Saudi-led coalition said it had destroyed a missile fired towards the capital. The exchanges followed an American strike on an Iranian missile site on Qeshm Island on Tuesday, which Washington described as self-defence after Iranian drones targeted a radar used by American forces at Bahrain's Sheikh Isa base. The war between the United States, Israel and Iran began on 28 February, and the latest round of strikes has spread across the Gulf, hitting shipping, air bases and energy sites, with no settlement in sight.

Key points

  • Kuwait's foreign ministry blamed Iran for repeated drone attacks, calling them criminal and a flagrant violation of its sovereignty.
  • Iran said its drones targeted American military equipment at Ali Al-Salem Air Base; Kuwait and the United States have confirmed no damage or casualties.
  • Kuwait said it would use every means at its disposal to defend its territory, citing its right to self-defence under the UN Charter; the UAE condemned the attack.
  • Bahrain said its defences stopped a fresh wave of strikes, and the Houthis claimed drone attacks on Saudi oil pipeline stations as the Gulf exchanges widened.
