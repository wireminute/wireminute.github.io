Iran Says It Targeted US Base in Kuwait as Two American Carriers Mass in Arabian Sea

Iran said on Wednesday that its drones struck American military equipment at a base in Kuwait, hours after Kuwait said its air defences were engaging hostile drones, as two American aircraft carriers sailed together in the Arabian Sea and Bahrain reported a fresh wave of attacks.

Iran's army said on Wednesday that its drones had targeted American military equipment at a base in Kuwait, hours after Kuwait said its air defences were engaging hostile drones over the country. In a statement carried by state television, the army said combat and kamikaze drones had targeted Patriot air defence systems and drone and missile launch platforms belonging to the American military at Ali Al-Salem Air Base. Neither Kuwait nor the United States has confirmed any damage from the claimed strike, and Kuwait's own statement named no origin for the drones and reported no casualties.

Kuwait's military headquarters had said in a post online at about 1.39am Gulf time that air defence batteries were engaging hostile drones and that any blasts people heard were interceptions, telling residents to follow the security authorities' instructions. Bahrain's Defence Force said separately that its air defences had stopped a fresh wave of Iranian air strikes early on Wednesday, describing them as part of a continuing, systematic campaign against the kingdom. The Gulf Cooperation Council condemned what it called repeated Iranian attacks on Bahrain and urged the United Nations Security Council to act, saying any attack on the kingdom infringed on the security of all Gulf states.

The exchanges followed an American strike on an Iranian missile site on Qeshm Island on Tuesday, which Washington described as self-defence after Iranian drones targeted a radar used by American forces at Bahrain's Sheikh Isa base. A Saudi-led coalition said its jets had hit 60 Houthi military sites in four Yemeni cities, Sanaa, Hodeidah, Saada and Amran, including underground tunnels used to store ballistic missiles and workshops used to assemble drones. United States Central Command also released images showing the aircraft carriers USS Theodore Roosevelt and USS George Washington sailing together in the Arabian Sea.

Air travel across the region remained disrupted, with flydubai extending cancellations to Riyadh and Abha until November 27 and Etihad extending its Riyadh and Dammam cancellations until November 30. The war began on February 28, when the United States and Israel launched strikes on Iran, and has since been fought in bursts across the Gulf, with shipping, air bases and energy sites repeatedly targeted. No end to the fighting is in sight, and the latest exchanges have complicated efforts to reach a settlement.

Key points

  • Iran's army said its drones targeted Patriot systems and launch platforms at Ali Al-Salem Air Base in Kuwait; neither Kuwait nor the United States has confirmed damage from the claimed strike.
  • Kuwait's army said its air defences were engaging hostile drones early on Wednesday and that explosions heard were interceptions.
  • Bahrain said its forces stopped a fresh wave of Iranian air strikes, and the Gulf Cooperation Council condemned the attacks and urged the UN Security Council to act.
  • Two American aircraft carriers were pictured sailing together in the Arabian Sea as Gulf airlines extended Saudi flight cancellations into late November.
