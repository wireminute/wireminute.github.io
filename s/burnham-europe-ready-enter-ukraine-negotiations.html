Burnham Says Europe Is Ready to Join Talks to End Russia's War in Ukraine

Britain's prime minister says Europe is ready to enter negotiations to end Russia's war in Ukraine, after a meeting of Kyiv's allies that also brought the American mediators trying to broker a settlement to the table.

Andy Burnham described Europe's readiness as one of three positive outcomes from the Coalition of the Willing meeting, which gathered Ukraine's president, the leaders of Germany and France, and the US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner. He said Europe had reached a clear, consistent and united position on the negotiations.

Burnham insisted that a peace agreement between Ukraine and Russia must come before any wider talks on European security, and said it was not for the aggressor to dictate unrealistic demands. Kushner said bringing Europe into the process would be essential to ending the war, but Russia's president has voiced scepticism about a European role, and no date has been set for a fifth round of talks.

Key points

  • Burnham says Europe is ready to enter negotiations after a Coalition of the Willing meeting with Zelensky and the US envoys.
  • A peace deal between Ukraine and Russia must come first, before any wider talks on European security, he says.
  • Kushner calls a European seat at the table essential, but Putin has voiced scepticism and no date is set for a fifth round of talks.
