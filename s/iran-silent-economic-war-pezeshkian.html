Pezeshkian Says Iran Faces 'Silent Economic War' Tougher Than Missile War

Iran's President Masoud Pezeshkian said on Saturday that his country is fighting a 'silent economic war' that is far more difficult than a military conflict, as the war with the United States and Israel squeezes its economy.

President Masoud Pezeshkian told an annual capital market conference in Tehran on Saturday that Iran is engaged in a silent economic war, far more difficult than a conflict fought with missiles and destruction, according to the state news agency IRNA. "We are engaged in a silent war that is far more difficult than one in which missiles are launched and destruction is caused. It is an economic war, one that you, as economic actors, are dealing with, and it is no easy battle," he told the gathering of investors and producers.

Pezeshkian said a group of "bullies, oppressors, and coercers" had sought to bring the country down, but Iranian businesses had managed market conditions well. He said the country's adversaries had expected famine and shortages of essential goods, yet production had continued and those expectations had not materialised. He praised investors, manufacturers and producers for keeping domestic markets supplied during the fighting.

The remarks come as the war strains Iran's economy and energy system. The United States and Israel began strikes on Iran in late February, and the fighting has since damaged power stations and gas facilities. Iran announced this weekend that rolling power cuts will begin on Sunday, with households facing outages of about two hours every second day, and fuel queues have formed in several cities. Attacks on shipping and a United States naval blockade have also disrupted trade through the Strait of Hormuz.

Iran said on Saturday it had held talks with Qatari mediators and urged the United States to accept its conditions for a settlement, while the commander of the Revolutionary Guard toured an underground missile facility. There was no sign of a breakthrough, and fighting continued across the Gulf, with strikes reported on shipping and on targets in Bahrain.
