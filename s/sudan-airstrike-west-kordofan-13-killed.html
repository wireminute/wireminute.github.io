At Least 13 Killed in Sudanese Army Airstrike in West Kordofan, Monitors Say

At least 13 civilians were killed when Sudanese army warplanes struck the Abu Qalb area of West Kordofan state on Sunday, according to a rights group, which said women and children were among the dead.

The Emergency Lawyers group, which documents abuses in Sudan's war, said the strike hit the Abu Qalb area of Abu Zabad locality and destroyed several homes. Its early findings put the civilian death toll at 13, though the figure has not been independently confirmed. A second monitor, the Kordofan Human Rights Observatory, counted 15 dead, including four children, and described the attack as a drone strike. The strike took place on Sunday, November 15, and the group announced its toll on Monday.

The rights group said videos circulating from the area showed scenes of destruction, and condemned strikes on residential neighbourhoods as a serious breach of the laws that protect civilians in war. The group said the continued use of airstrikes on residential areas posed a direct threat to civilian lives. It demanded a full independent inquiry, punishment for those behind the strike, and an end to bombing residential areas.

The army has not publicly addressed the allegation. Mohamed Didan, spokesman for the Special Operations Forces fighting alongside the army, wrote on social media that his troops had moved through Abu Zabad and cleared out positions belonging to the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces.

The war between the army and the Rapid Support Forces, which broke out in April 2023, has created a humanitarian emergency that aid groups rank among the worst in the world. The fighting has killed at least 59,000 people and forced about 13 million from their homes, with famine taking hold in parts of the country, according to aid groups.

Key points

  • Emergency Lawyers says at least 13 civilians, including women and children, were killed in an army airstrike in Abu Qalb, Abu Zabad locality, on Sunday
  • Several homes were destroyed; a second monitor puts the toll at 15, including four children
  • The toll has not been independently confirmed, and the army has not publicly addressed the allegation
  • The war between the army and the Rapid Support Forces has killed at least 59,000 people and displaced about 13 million since April 2023
