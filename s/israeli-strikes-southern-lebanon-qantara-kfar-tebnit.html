Israeli Strikes Hit Southern Lebanon Towns as Border Attacks Continue

Israeli forces carried out a series of strikes across southern Lebanon on Saturday, hitting the towns of Qantara and Kfar Tebnit and setting off demolitions near Khiam, Lebanese state media reported, in the latest round of attacks along the border.

Lebanon's National News Agency said warplanes struck Qantara and Kfar Tebnit in the morning, while Israeli forces set off explosions near Khiam and shelled the outskirts of Zawtar al-Sharqiya and Mayfadoun. Drones exploded in the towns of Shoukin and Nabatieh al-Fawqa, one of them on the roof of a house, and machine-gun fire was reported at Baraashit as military vehicles moved along the town's edge. No casualties were reported in the first accounts of the attacks.

The attacks are the latest in a campaign that has continued since March 2, despite a framework agreement between Lebanon and Israel reached in June with US backing. Lebanon's government has repeatedly called for the strikes to stop, while people in the border towns have lived under near-daily attacks for months.

Key points

  • Israeli warplanes struck the southern Lebanese towns of Qantara and Kfar Tebnit on Saturday, with demolitions and artillery fire reported in Khiam, Zawtar and Mayfadoun.
  • Drones exploded in Shoukin and Nabatieh al-Fawqa, and machine-gun fire was reported at Baraashit; no casualties were reported in the first accounts.
  • Attacks across southern Lebanon have continued since March 2 despite a framework agreement between Lebanon and Israel reached in June with US backing.
