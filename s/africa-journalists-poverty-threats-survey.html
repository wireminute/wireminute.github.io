Poverty, Threats and Fear Push African Journalism to Breaking Point, Survey Finds

Most journalists across Africa cannot live on what their work pays, while threats, intimidation and self-censorship have become part of the job, according to a continent-wide survey of 1,586 journalists in 45 countries.

The report, titled Journalism Under Siege, was published by the Federation of African Journalists on 29 October with support from UNESCO. It is the first journalist-led survey of its kind to cover pay, contracts, safety and media freedom across all five regions of the continent.

Its central finding is economic: 61 percent of respondents said their income from journalism does not meet their basic living needs, while only 4 percent said their pay adequately covers essential costs. Pay often arrives late, with 58 percent reporting delayed salaries, and the work itself is insecure: 62 percent have no written contract and 71 percent have no social protection. Many journalists keep side businesses simply to support their families, the report says.

The dangers are just as stark. Sixty-five percent of those surveyed said they had faced threats or intimidation linked to their work in the past three years, and only 17 percent said they always feel safe while reporting. More than half, 55 percent, said they rarely or never feel safe. At least 18 journalists and media workers were killed in Africa in 2025, according to the International Federation of Journalists.

Fear is also shaping what gets reported. Eighty-four percent of respondents said they censor themselves frequently or occasionally, and 69 percent said threats had forced them to avoid certain stories altogether. Federation president Omar Faruk Osman said the survey shows a profession struggling to survive while trying to inform the public, and warned that the public loses out when reporters cannot earn a living or work without fear. Financial insecurity was named the leading source of stress, cited by 38 percent, ahead of fear of attack at 24 percent.

Key points

  • 61 percent of 1,586 journalists surveyed in 45 African countries said their pay does not meet their basic living needs; only 4 percent said it covers essential costs.
  • 58 percent reported delayed salaries, 62 percent work without written contracts and 71 percent have no social protection.
  • 65 percent faced threats or intimidation in the past three years; only 17 percent always feel safe reporting, and 55 percent rarely or never do.
  • 84 percent censor themselves at least occasionally and 69 percent have avoided stories because of threats; at least 18 journalists were killed in Africa in 2025.
