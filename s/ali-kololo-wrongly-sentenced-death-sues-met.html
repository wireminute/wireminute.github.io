Kenyan Man Wrongly Sentenced to Death Over Attack on British Couple Sues London's Metropolitan Police

A Kenyan woodcutter who spent more than a decade in prison after being wrongly sentenced to death over a 2011 attack on a British couple says London's Metropolitan Police destroyed his life, as he sues the force over the evidence that put him behind bars.

Somali gunmen broke into David and Judith Tebbutt's beach bungalow on the Kenyan coast at Lamu in 2011. They shot David dead and abducted Judith, holding her captive in Somalia for six months. Ali Kololo, a woodcutter who had once worked at the resort, was arrested within a day. He says he was beaten and burned with cigarettes for days, then made to thumbprint a statement claiming he had led the attackers to the couple. Unable to read or write, he says the statement was a pack of lies he signed without understanding it.

Detectives from London's Metropolitan Police flew to Kenya within days to help the investigation, led by then Detective Superintendent Neil Hibberd. The British government had told the officers not to accept information obtained by interrogating detainees, but Hibberd requested Kololo's statement and relied on it in evidence for the prosecution. He told the court Kololo had worn a shoe matching footprints in the sand at the resort, although the officer who arrested Kololo said he had been barefoot when he was handed to police. Hibberd did not tell the court the force's own forensic tests could not establish whether the shoe had made the marks.

Kololo was the only person ever convicted over the attack. He was sentenced to death in 2013 and spent three years on death row before his sentence was commuted to life imprisonment. Britain's Independent Office for Police Conduct later found Hibberd had omitted significant forensic evidence at the trial, and that he would have faced a gross misconduct case had he not retired. In April 2023, Kenya's High Court quashed Kololo's conviction because of doubts over the prosecution's evidence, after work by the legal charity Reprieve and a Kenyan lawyer who represented him for free. Judith Tebbutt herself believed Kololo was innocent and said he had been made a scapegoat.

In his first media interview, Kololo said he had lost contact with his children in prison and is now too frail for the woodcutting he once lived by. He said he still lives with the effects of his torture and his decade in prison. He is now suing the Metropolitan Police, claiming Hibberd gave misleading evidence at his trial and acted unlawfully to secure his conviction. The force is contesting his compensation claim, has not apologised or admitted wrongdoing, and declined to comment.

Key points

  • Ali Kololo, a woodcutter from Lamu, was sentenced to death in 2013 over the 2011 attack on David and Judith Tebbutt, and spent more than a decade in prison before Kenya's High Court quashed his conviction in April 2023.
  • He says Kenyan police tortured him into thumbprinting a confession he could not read, and that evidence from a Metropolitan Police detective helped secure his conviction.
  • Britain's police watchdog found the detective omitted significant forensic evidence at the trial, including that tests could not link a shoe to footprints found at the scene.
  • Kololo is now suing the Metropolitan Police for compensation; the force is contesting the claim and has not apologised or admitted wrongdoing.
