Haiti Formalises Security Pact With El Salvador as Crowds Back Bukele's Troops

Haiti has formalised a security cooperation agreement with El Salvador, the same day thousands of Haitians marched in support of President Nayib Bukele's troop deployment against the country's armed gangs.

Crowds marched through Port-au-Prince and other Haitian cities on Tuesday in support of El Salvador's troop deployment, carrying signs praising President Nayib Bukele and calling for an end to gang rule. On the same day, Haiti's Council of Ministers approved a formal cooperation agreement with El Salvador, putting the deployment on a legal footing.

The deployment began after four Salvadoran aviation technicians serving with the United Nations-backed anti-gang force were kidnapped near the capital on October 18. Bukele answered by sending more than 2,000 troops under a separate bilateral deal with Haiti's government, and his forces have since promoted the mission with dramatic footage, including video of robot dogs carrying rifles moving through a stadium that Haitian and Salvadoran forces said they had retaken from gangs.

On Monday, El Salvador confirmed that the deployment, numbering about 2,500 troops, would end once the kidnapped technicians are recovered. Security Minister Gustavo Villatoro said the troops would leave as soon as the airmen reappear. But Haiti's Justice Minister Patrick Pelissier travelled to San Salvador the same day to thank El Salvador and urge the troops to stay, and no date has been set for any withdrawal.

Analysts cautioned that the high-profile offensive has produced few clear gains nearly a month after it began, and warned that operations without a long-term plan could deepen the conflict. The stakes are stark: United Nations figures show 4,595 people were killed and 2,050 injured in armed violence in Haiti in the first 10 months of this year. Armed gangs still control much of Port-au-Prince and the surrounding areas, and Haiti's leaders have long appealed for outside help to take the country back.

Key points

  • Haiti's Council of Ministers has approved a formal cooperation agreement with El Salvador covering the troop deployment.
  • Crowds marched in several Haitian cities on Tuesday backing El Salvador's troop deployment, with signs reading "Long live Bukele" and "Down with the gangs".
  • El Salvador says the deployment of about 2,500 troops will end once the missing airmen are found, but Haiti's justice minister has urged the troops to stay.
  • Analysts say the operation has shown few clear gains after nearly a month, as UN data puts the toll at 4,595 killed and 2,050 injured in the first 10 months of 2026.
