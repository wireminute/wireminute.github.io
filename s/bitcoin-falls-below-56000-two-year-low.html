Bitcoin Falls Below $56,000 to Lowest in Two Years as $2 Billion in Bets Are Wiped Out

Bitcoin has fallen below $56,000 for the first time in more than two years, extending a steep slide that has erased over half its value since October, as a rout in technology shares and heavy selling by funds pushed the cryptocurrency through one support level after another.

The coin dropped 6 percent in the United States on Friday and a further 5.7 percent in Asia on Saturday, touching $55,533, its weakest since September 2024. It has now lost more than half its value since reaching a peak above $126,000 in October 2025, and is worth less than it was when Donald Trump retook the White House.

The fall set off about $2 billion in forced sales of leveraged crypto bets over 24 hours, market data showed, most of them long positions held by traders betting that prices would rise. When those positions are closed automatically, exchanges sell the underlying coins, pushing prices lower and triggering the next round of sales.

Money has also been flowing out of the exchange-traded funds that hold bitcoin. Investors pulled $582 million from United States spot bitcoin funds on 19 November alone, and BlackRock's fund recorded its largest single-day outflow since listing, at $541 million. The funds have now lost more than $3 billion this year.

Analysts link the slide to a wider retreat from risky assets. Shares in artificial intelligence and technology companies have fallen sharply on doubts about stretched valuations, while uncertainty over the Federal Reserve's next move on interest rates has added to the pressure. The slide follows a hack of Binance, the world's largest crypto exchange, at the end of October, and United States midterm results that raised doubts about how quickly friendlier rules for the industry will arrive.

Bitcoin is down about 25 percent in November, putting it on course for its worst month since June 2022 and a seventh straight weekly fall. The next level traders are watching is $55,000, with some analysts pointing to the $50,000 to $54,000 range as the likely floor.

Key points

  • Bitcoin fell below $56,000 on Saturday, its lowest since September 2024, after dropping 6 percent on Friday and a further 5.7 percent in Asia.
  • The coin has lost more than half its value since its peak above $126,000 in October 2025.
  • About $2 billion in leveraged crypto bets were liquidated in 24 hours, most of them long positions.
  • United States spot bitcoin funds saw $582 million withdrawn on 19 November, with BlackRock's fund recording its largest single-day outflow.
